# Handling A Lockout

> [!abstract] In one line
> Confirm it's real, notify the team immediately, call the client within minutes — speed
> is what saves the job, not sympathy.

## Definition

The procedure for when a cleaner arrives and can't get into the home: confirm the
cleaner actually can't get in, notify the office immediately, contact the client fast,
hold for a defined wait window, then apply a consistent lockout fee policy and
compensate the cleaner for time lost. [[SOPs]] cites lockouts as the canonical example
of "wait until you've had three, then document it" — this is that document.

## Why It Matters

An unhandled lockout costs the business twice: lost revenue on a job that doesn't
happen, and a frustrated cleaner who wasted a trip. Handling it emotionally instead of
systematically means inconsistent fees, inconsistent client communication, and cleaners
who don't trust that their time is protected.

## Symptoms

- Cleaners don't know how long to wait or who to call
- Lockout fees get applied inconsistently, or not at all
- Cleaners aren't compensated for wasted travel/wait time, so they resent lockout jobs
- No record exists of repeat-lockout clients

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming a lockout instead of confirming it (knock, ring, wait a few minutes first)
- Letting the cleaner sit indefinitely with no wait-window limit
- Treating the client as at fault by default instead of assuming error, not bad intent
- Not compensating the cleaner for wait time, which erodes trust in the team

## Models

**The 5-step lockout system:**

1. **Confirm it's real** — cleaner knocks, rings, waits a few minutes. Don't assume.
2. **Notify the team immediately** — client name, address, scheduled time.
3. **Contact the client fast** — call within minutes; if no answer, leave a voicemail
   and send a text immediately after.
4. **Wait window** — cleaner waits a defined time (roughly 15 minutes past arrival). If
   the client responds, proceed with the clean. If not, confirm the lockout and move on.
5. **Apply policy** — a lockout fee (typically a percentage of the job, capped),
   applied consistently, with a defined dispute process. Follow up with the client after
   with a summary of what happened, any fee, and rescheduling options.

**Cleaner compensation:** pay the cleaner for wait time at a fair rate with a maximum
cap — this protects the team without eating margin on every lockout.

## Checklist

- [ ] Lockout confirmed (knock, ring, wait a few minutes — don't assume)
- [ ] Team notified immediately with client name, address, scheduled time
- [ ] Client contacted within minutes; voicemail + text if no answer
- [ ] Wait window observed (~15 minutes), then confirm lockout or proceed
- [ ] Lockout fee policy applied consistently
- [ ] Cleaner compensated for wait time, per policy cap
- [ ] Client follow-up sent: what happened, fee if any, reschedule options
- [ ] Incident logged: client, date, outcome, fee applied
