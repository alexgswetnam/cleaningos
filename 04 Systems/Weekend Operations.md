# Weekend Operations

> [!abstract] In one line
> Weekend staff aren't just answering phones — they're a sales team, a support team, and
> an ops team running in parallel, or Monday starts as chaos.

## Definition

Staffing weekends (Saturday/Sunday) with the same three functions that run on weekdays:
reactive support (calls, texts, cleaner issues), proactive sales (following up on old and
quoted leads, pushing recurring bookings, filling schedule gaps), and operational
maintenance (CRM cleanup, schedule review, catching conflicts before Monday). Reinforces
[[Speed To Lead]] and [[Leads Don't Disappear]] into the two days most businesses
silently let leads go cold.

## Why It Matters

Leads that arrive Friday evening through Sunday routinely sit untouched until Monday at
most cleaning companies, and Monday becomes reactive chaos instead of a clean start.
Treating weekend staff as a phones-only reactive role — rather than sales + support +
ops — is a direct, avoidable revenue leak.

## Symptoms

- Leads submitted over the weekend get their first contact Monday or later
- Monday mornings are consistently chaotic
- Weekend staff describe their job as "just answering the phone"
- Schedule conflicts for the coming week are discovered on Monday, not caught in advance

## Common Mistakes

- Treating weekend coverage as purely reactive
- Letting quoted or unresponsive leads sit untouched over the weekend
- Not using weekend downtime for CRM cleanup and schedule review
- No documented weekend workflow, so it depends entirely on who's on shift

## Models

**Three core functions, run in parallel:**

1. **Reactive support** — incoming calls, client texts, cleaner issues, customer
   support. Keeps operations from stalling.
2. **Proactive sales** — follow up with old and previously-quoted leads, attempt to
   close bookings, push recurring service, fill open schedule gaps. This is where most
   companies leave money on the table by doing nothing.
3. **Operational maintenance** — use slower periods to clean CRM data, review the
   upcoming schedule for conflicts, improve route efficiency, prevent double bookings.

**Follow-up workflow for weekend leads:** review the pipeline and prioritize (recent
leads, recurring opportunities, higher-ticket jobs, engaged prospects) → call with the
goal of reconnecting, answering questions, and offering scheduling help → if no answer,
leave a voicemail, send a follow-up text, and log the attempt with a next action and
updated status in the CRM.

## Checklist

- [ ] Weekend staff respond to inbound calls/texts promptly, checking voicemail regularly
- [ ] Weekend staff actively work the lead pipeline — not just inbound
- [ ] Priority order: recent leads → recurring opportunities → higher-ticket jobs →
      engaged prospects
- [ ] Every follow-up attempt logged: date/time, outcome, notes, next action, updated
      status
- [ ] Upcoming week's schedule reviewed for gaps, conflicts, double bookings
- [ ] Unconfirmed appointments verified: client confirmation, cleaner confirmation,
      entry instructions, payment method
- [ ] Downtime used for CRM cleanup: dedupe leads, update statuses, fix tagging

## Templates

**Weekend follow-up call opener:**
> "Hi [Lead Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company Name]. I wanted to follow up
> regarding your cleaning inquiry and see if you had any questions or wanted help
> scheduling a cleaning."
