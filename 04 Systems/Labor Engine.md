# Labor Engine

Hub page. What this engine is → [[Four Engines]]. What lives here is how it **runs**.

> [!abstract] In one line
> The system that keeps demand and capacity to fulfill it in balance — finding,
> onboarding, paying, and retaining enough dependable cleaners that a booking is never
> stronger than the team's ability to actually show up and do the work.

## By Season

What matters in this engine at each stage, per [[Labor 101]]'s "What is a Labor
Bottleneck" lesson — the clearest season-by-season breakdown of this Engine found in the
vault so far.

**Graduation test, accepted 2026-08-07** (`00 Inbox/graduation-criteria-proposal.md`): the
owner picks the measurement window; a test has to hold **twice** in it, not once. Tests, not
thresholds. Note: "team leads" in the Scale row below is retired language — the leadership
layer in this business is the VA, not a cleaner promoted over other cleaners. See
[[The VA Is Your Leadership Layer]].

| Season | Focus | Ignore | Graduation Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survival | Get one or two reliable helpers, or the owner keeps cleaning personally until cash is steady. A labor bottleneck here looks like demand existing but staffing being unstable — turnover, no-shows, inconsistent quality, or simply not being able to find cleaners at all. | Building elaborate onboarding systems or backup depth before there's steady, provable demand | A job you didn't touch was completed to standard and the client noticed no difference |
| Stability | Onboarding checklists and packet, a written [[Cleaner Handbook]] and Client Experience Guide — reliability starts coming from standards instead of the owner personally training every hire | Hiring management roles before there's enough stable, recurring work to manage | Someone called out and the job still happened — without you cleaning it |
| Scale | Backup coverage — see [[Backup Cleaner]]. Growth collapses the moment one person quits without redundancy already built | Assuming the Survival-season habit of doing it all yourself still scales | Someone other than you judged whether a new cleaner was good enough, and you accepted the judgement |
| Harvest | Reduce risk by continuously onboarding new cleaners every month and running them through a real quality-testing phase — see [[Testing A New Cleaner]] — building an asset, not reacting to the next departure | Treating labor as solved just because it's currently stable — a static roster is a future bottleneck | Cleaners were onboarded and quality was audited during the window, and you did neither |

## Health Check

How an owner knows this engine is working or broken, per the same lesson:

- **Working:** demand can be fulfilled without the owner scrambling; a single cleaner
  calling out doesn't threaten booked revenue; the roster has depth beyond the current
  week's schedule
- **Broken:** turnover, no-shows, and inconsistent quality are recurring rather than
  occasional; the business has leads or bookings it can't actually staff; one or two
  cleaners leaving would be a crisis rather than an inconvenience
