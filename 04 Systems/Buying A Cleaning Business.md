# Buying A Cleaning Business

Workflow. The concepts it uses are defined once in `03 Concepts/` — this page is the
sequence, not a second home for the ideas.

> [!abstract] In one line
> Find out how much of the business is the owner, and how much would still be there without him.

> [!warning] Scope
> Built from a single recorded evaluation call. It covers what to ask and in what order. It
> does not cover sourcing deals, valuation modelling, or financing mechanics beyond
> [[Deal Structure]], and it is not legal or financial advice.

## Why Buy Rather Than Build

Not addressed directly in the source, but the buyers' positioning is the argument:

> "We excel in growth. We excel in sales and marketing."

They describe the target as *"a house that looks like it could use a pretty good
facelift"* — an established book of business run by an operator without modern systems, where
the buyer's edge is the [[Four Engines|Leads]] and [[Four Engines|Logistics]] capability the
seller never built.

## The Sequence

```
NDA signed
    ↓
Request the CIM / deal materials
    ↓
Rapid-fire evaluation call with the broker
    │   financials & add-backs      → [[Add-Backs And SDE]]
    │   asset condition
    │   owner dependency            → [[Owner-Dependent Revenue]]   ← the one that matters
    │   client concentration        → [[Client Concentration]]
    │   contracted vs. recurring    → [[Uncontracted Recurring Revenue]]
    ↓
Protections agreed in principle    → [[Non-Compete]] + transition period
    ↓
Deal structure explored            → [[Deal Structure]]
    ↓
LOI signed
    ↓
Due diligence window               → [[Due Diligence]]
    │   CPA review, tax reconciliation
    │   time on site watching the owner work
    ↓
Close, or walk
```

**The order is deliberate.** Protections and structure are raised *before* the LOI, so that
a seller unwilling to sign a non-compete is discovered while walking away is still cheap.

## The Rapid-Fire Question Set

Announce the format first — *"I'm just going to rapid fire if it's cool with everyone"* — and
then work the list. Each links to the concept that explains what you're listening for.

| # | Ask | Concept |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Will we see every transaction, or line summaries? | [[Add-Backs And SDE]] |
| 2 | Why did the add-backs change between years? | [[Add-Backs And SDE]] |
| 3 | Can we get the current year, not just the last two? | [[Add-Backs And SDE]] |
| 4 | Service records on the vehicles and equipment? Is anything idle because it's broken? | — |
| 5 | **How many hours a week does the owner work in the business?** | [[Owner-Dependent Revenue]] |
| 6 | **Does he pay himself for that work?** | [[Owner-Dependent Revenue]] |
| 7 | Which service lines does he personally deliver, and what % of revenue are they? | [[Owner-Dependent Revenue]] |
| 8 | How many clients, and do a few make up most of the revenue? | [[Client Concentration]] |
| 9 | What share of recurring revenue is under written contract? | [[Uncontracted Recurring Revenue]] |
| 10 | Is he open to a non-compete and a transition period? | [[Non-Compete]] |

**Questions 5 and 6 are the ones that pay.** In the source they were not volunteered, and
the answer changed the valuation.

## What Went Wrong In The Worked Example

A 25-year-old commercial cleaning business, ~8 long-tenured employees, priced on an SDE that
looked reasonable. What surfaced under questioning:

- The owner personally does or manages the one-time cleanups — **~25% of revenue**
- **15–20 hours a week**, sometimes more
- **He never paid himself for it**, so the SDE overstates real earnings
- **~75% of revenue is recurring but mostly uncontracted** — relationships, not agreements
- Pen-and-paper books; the client revenue distribution didn't exist and had to be assembled
- Personal expenses run through the business: self-insurance, life insurance, a vacation home

The buyers' conclusion held two things at once, which is the right outcome of a first pass:

> On the surface it's a fair deal, and now the price makes sense — but the books aren't a
> clean source of truth. The owner is "missing some pretty big stuff."

## Sourcing — an anecdote, not yet a method

> [!info] Still explicitly out of scope
> The page-level warning above stands: this workflow doesn't cover sourcing deals. What
> follows is one in-progress anecdote (2026-04-20), recorded because it names a real
> negotiating stance, not because it's a repeatable sourcing process yet.

Alex and Elijah are separately pursuing a carpet cleaning business acquisition, sourced
through an unplanned personal connection (met the new owner of a carpet cleaning business
on the street; that owner connected him to the previous owner for a coffee meeting).
[[Deal Structure|Seller financing]] is the preferred structure once terms are discussed.

The stated negotiating posture: don't get attached to any single target.

> "Don't be too hungry... if this doesn't work out, it doesn't work out."

They're committed to buying *a* business in this category, not necessarily *this* one —
which is presented as the source of their negotiating leverage, not a throwaway line.

**Update, 2026-04-27.** The asking price ($200K) was judged too high — Courtney's read
was to offer roughly half. Still in the "don't be too hungry" posture from the prior call:
willing to walk, and still committed to buying a business in this category regardless of
whether this specific one closes.

## Health Check

- Have you asked whether the owner pays himself for labor he performs? *If not, you have not
  begun.*
- Can you state what share of revenue survives the owner leaving?
- Do you know which clients are contracted?
- Have you spent a day watching the business run?
- Do you have a written right to withdraw?
