# Owner Role Evolution

> [!abstract] In one line
> How the owner's own job changes as the business matures — Self-Employed → Supervisor → General Manager → Owner — and why you have to act like the next role up before the business obviously requires it.

## Definition

A model of the **owner's leadership role**, not a model of the business.

Four ways an owner can spend their week, in the order they usually appear:

| Role | What you're doing |
|---|---|
| **Self-Employed** | Everything. Cleaner, marketer, salesperson, scheduler. |
| **Supervisor** | You have cleaners, but you're managing all of it. You're the glue. |
| **General Manager** | Managing managers. Systems run things, not you personally. |
| **Owner** | You own an asset. Someone else could step in and run it. |

> [!warning] This does not define the Seasons
> **A Role is not a Season, and a Season does not assign you a Role.**
>
> [[Four Seasons]] describes the maturity of **one Engine**. Each of the four Engines
> carries its own. This page describes what the **owner** is doing. The two readings are
> independent, and forcing them into a one-to-one table is what made this model wrong as
> Canon — see the superseded block below.
>
> **A Survival-stage Engine does not mean the owner personally cleans houses.** An owner
> with Leads in Harvest and Labor in Survival isn't a self-employed cleaner. They have a
> hiring problem.

## When This Matters

It converts an abstract question — *"how do I get to the next stage?"* — into a concrete
one: **what would the next role up do this week that I'm not doing?**

It also names a specific failure mode that revenue hides. An owner can be doing $30K/month
and still be acting self-employed: answering every call, cleaning when someone's out,
holding every process in their head. The revenue says one thing. The behaviour says
another. **The behaviour wins**, and the business stalls.

## Key Ideas

**You pull the business. It doesn't pull you.**

> "You pull the business to the next stage. The business doesn't pull you."

You move by **acting like the next role up**, before the business obviously requires it.
Keep acting self-employed and you stay self-employed regardless of revenue. Start acting
like a supervisor — bringing cleaners on, delegating, holding standards — and the
structure follows.

This is the practical version of [[Five Laws|Build In Order]]. The stage doesn't change
and then permit you to change your behaviour. Your behaviour changes and drags the stage
with it.

**Where it connects to the Engines.** The Role you're playing is mostly a readout of the
**Leadership** Engine, and it constrains how fast the other three can mature. An owner
still acting self-employed can push Leads into Scale by spending money, but cannot hold
it there — there's nobody to absorb the work. See [[Owner-Dependent Revenue]].

**Diagnostic question:** which role am I actually playing this week — not which one do I
want to be playing? Then: what does the next role up do that I don't?
