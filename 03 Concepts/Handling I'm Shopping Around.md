# Handling I'm Shopping Around

> [!abstract] In one line
> Affirm the shopper instead of fighting them — then take the spot off the table.

## Definition

When a prospect says they're calling other companies, you agree with them, reframe
shopping as evidence they care, and give them a reason to come back to you.

## Why It Matters

Fighting it makes you one more pushy vendor. Affirming it makes you the one they
remember — and per Alex, the ones who shop and come back are the best clients.

Rick, on the call: *"everybody's shopping around because everything's expensive and
nobody has any money. That happens all the time."*

## Symptoms

- "You're the first person I called, I just want to do my research"
- "Can you just text me the quote?"
- They're polite, engaged, and gone

## Common Mistakes

**The big one — don't coach them on how to shop.** Alex tried the helpful-friend approach:
telling prospects what to watch for at other companies (no insurance, no English speakers,
no satisfaction guarantee).

> "I literally tell them how to go shop and find someone else and just didn't have as
> many people come back."

It feels generous. It loses deals. **Close them, don't educate them.**

Other mistakes:
- Treating it as a price objection when it isn't. Jack quoted $180 against a $150 budget on
  two working professionals with a cottage — *"it's not like their lack of money."* Alex:
  even at her number she probably still shops.
- Getting defensive.

## Models

**Affirm, then own it:**

> "Honestly, I'm just like you. I call five people before I decide who I'm going to go
> with. And that just tells me you really care about your home. Our best clients are the
> ones that talked to five different people and then ended up calling us back."

Then go straight to [[The Soft Close]].

## Checklist

- [ ] They say they're shopping around
- [ ] Affirm: "I'm just like you, I call five people"
- [ ] Reframe: "that tells me you really care about your home"
- [ ] Social proof: "our best clients are the ones who talked to five people and came back"
- [ ] Soft close: hold the spot
- [ ] Do **not** give tips on what to look for elsewhere

## Student Examples

**Jack** — lost a weekly-clean prospect who already had a cleaner and was looking to
switch. His diagnosis on review: *"I forgot to go into why that is... really build up the
current negative side of why she's looking to switch. Listening back to my call, I could
have really explored that a lot more."*

**Courtney** — sees it less, and reads it differently: *"if they're calling us, yes
they're shopping around, but they also want to book."*

## FAQ

**Q:** They asked me to text the quote. Is that a loss?
**A:** Not yet — but it's the exit. Affirm and soft-close before you agree to text anything.
