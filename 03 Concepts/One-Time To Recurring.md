# One-Time To Recurring

> [!abstract] In one line
> The percentage of one-time cleans that become recurring clients — where the profit actually lives.

## Definition

Conversion rate from a single booking into an ongoing schedule, and the three concrete
moves that raise it: a call, an email, and a timed discount offer.

## Why It Matters

[[Client Lifetime Value]] depends entirely on it. A one-time client is worth one clean; a
recurring client is worth roughly double the one-time lifetime value — Courtney's real
numbers put it at $2,564 for a one-time customer vs. **$4,800** for a recurring one, from
BookingKoala's own reports. Doubling this conversion rate changes the whole business
without adding a single lead.

## Symptoms

- You don't know what share of one-time cleans ever rebook
- The post-clean call is skipped once the job is marked complete
- No email goes out with an actual recurring price attached
- Clients who loved the clean never hear a recurring offer at all

## Common Mistakes

- Not tracking it at all
- Treating a booking as the finish line rather than the start
- Making the client do the math themselves — send the exact recurring price, don't make
  them ask for a quote a second time
- Running only one of the three strategies below instead of layering all three

## Models

**What actually drives the conversion — answered.** The February call that first raised
this concept flagged the mechanism as unknown. A later call answers it directly with
three complementary strategies, stacked so a client who misses the first catch gets a
second and third chance:

**Strategy 1 — the post-clean phone call (Alex's method).** Call right after the first
clean. Ask how it went, what they'd rate it. If five stars, ask for a review and send the
link while still on the phone. Then ask directly: *"Would you like [cleaner name] to come
back? Are you looking for monthly or bi-weekly?"* — assume the close rather than asking
*if* they want to continue. If they hesitate, name the loss: *"You do have a credit on
the account — I don't want you to lose it. Want to go ahead and schedule?"*

**Strategy 2 — the follow-up email with pricing (Courtney's method).** Send an email
stating exactly what their recurring plan would cost — monthly price and bi-weekly price,
already calculated, no math left for them to do. No sticker shock, because they already
know what the first clean cost. Typical response: *"Yeah, that's great. Do I get the same
cleaner?"* → *"Yes, of course."* → booked.

**Strategy 3 — the 30-day, 25%-off recurring offer (Rick's method).** About a month after
the first clean, send: *"If you sign up for recurring, we'll give you 25% off."* This
catches the client who loved the clean but didn't commit on the spot — the group most
likely to be lost without a second nudge.

**A fourth, automated backstop.** If none of the three land, the CRM's own 60-day
follow-up text reminds the client they have a store credit they'll lose — see
[[Sales Pipeline Stages]]'s Booked (One Time) stage for where this sequence lives.

**The mechanism behind that backstop, in more detail.** A fifth source names the specific
[[Harvest CRM]] trigger and message sequence: a one-time client who hasn't become
recurring within a set window (stated here as 60 days, matching the backstop above) enters
an automatic text sequence rather than relying on a human to remember. The sequence
escalates: a reminder that a credit is about to expire, a mention of currently available
spots, and a direct nudge before the credit is lost — *"You're about to lose the credit.
Are you sure you don't want to book?"* The stated reason this matters at all: without a
CRM, an owner is already manually tracking new leads, cleaners, scheduled cleans, old
leads, and one-time clients, and this specific follow-up is the one most likely to get
dropped under that load.

**The seasonal swing that still needs a mechanism.** One member's earlier number — **70%
conversion in December, 16% in January** — is still not explained by anything ingested so
far. The three strategies above are candidate levers (a missed post-clean call, no
pricing email, no timed offer), but nothing confirms which one actually moved that
specific member's number. Treat the strategies as the fix to try, not a diagnosis of that
swing.

## Checklist

- [ ] Call after every first clean — ask how it went, get the rating
- [ ] Five stars → ask for a review, send the link on the phone
- [ ] Ask directly: monthly or bi-weekly? Assume the close
- [ ] If they hesitate, name the credit they'd lose
- [ ] Send a follow-up email with the exact monthly and bi-weekly price calculated
- [ ] At ~30 days, if still not recurring, send a 25%-off recurring offer
- [ ] Confirm the CRM's 60-day store-credit reminder is live as the automated backstop
- [ ] Track the conversion rate monthly — don't let it go unmeasured
