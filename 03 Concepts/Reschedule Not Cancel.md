# Reschedule Not Cancel

> [!abstract] In one line
> Before you accept a cancellation, ask whether moving the date would help instead — most "cancellations" are a timing problem, not a service problem.

## Definition

When an existing recurring client calls to cancel, don't treat every cancellation as a
lost client by default. Listen for whether they're unhappy with the service or dealing
with a timing/life circumstance, and if it's timing, offer to move the appointment to a
specific future date before accepting the cancellation.

## Why It Matters

A recurring client who cancels because of travel, a family change, or a possible move is
not the same event as a client who cancels because the clean was bad. Treating both the
same way — accepting the cancellation and moving on — throws away [[Client Lifetime
Value]] on clients who were never actually unhappy with you. The whole point of this
technique is that the client tells you which situation you're in, if you listen for it,
before you decide how to respond.

## Symptoms

- A client says they need to cancel, and you accept it without asking why
- Clients who say positive things about the company ("you guys have been great") still
  get canceled outright
- You leave cancellation calls with "call us when you're ready" instead of a specific date
- You don't ask for a review from a client who is clearly still happy, because the call
  felt like a loss

## Common Mistakes

- Accepting a cancellation immediately when the actual issue is only timing
- Treating a life circumstance (travel, family changes, a move) like a service complaint
- Ignoring positive signals in what the client says — if they're praising the company,
  that's information, not small talk
- Leaving the next step vague ("reach out whenever") when you could offer a specific date
- Missing the chance to ask for a review because the call felt like it was already lost

## Models

**Listen first.** Let the client explain what's going on before responding. The signal to
listen for: do they say anything negative about the company, or do they volunteer that
it's been good?

**If nothing negative comes up, don't accept the cancellation as final — ask about moving
it instead:**

> "But would moving the appointment due to another date or month help instead of canceling
> the entire appointment with us?"

This reframes the decision. Instead of "keep this appointment or cancel forever," the
client is choosing between two ways to stay a client. Offer a specific date once they give
you a rough window — don't leave it open-ended:

> "Maybe we can reschedule the cleaning starting June, like maybe June 1?"

**If the client is happy, ask for the review while you have them:**

> "With regards to giving us feedback, I will send you a link via text."

Do this in the same call. A client who is actively rescheduling rather than leaving is a
client in a good mood about the company — that's the moment to ask, not after.

## Checklist

- [ ] Client calls to cancel — let them explain before responding
- [ ] Listen for service complaint vs. timing/life issue
- [ ] If they say anything positive about the company, do not accept the cancellation as
      final
- [ ] Ask whether moving the appointment to another date or month would help
- [ ] Offer a specific date, not an open-ended "call us when you're ready"
- [ ] Confirm the new date and update the booking
- [ ] If they're happy, ask for a review and send the link on the spot
- [ ] End the call warmly and restate the new appointment date

## Templates

- "What happened? What made you decide to..."
- "But would moving the appointment due to another date or month help instead of
  canceling the entire appointment with us?"
- "Actually, we have you down on [date]. So that will be amazing."
- "With regards to giving us feedback, I will send you a link via text."

## FAQ

**Q:** What if the client really is done — not just pausing?
**A:** The technique doesn't force anyone to stay. It just makes sure you don't accept a
cancellation by default when the client hasn't actually said they're unhappy. If they
decline the reschedule offer, you've lost nothing you wouldn't have lost anyway.
