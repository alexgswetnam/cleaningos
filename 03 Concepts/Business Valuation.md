# Business Valuation

> [!abstract] In one line
> Annual profit times two to two-point-two — which is why you don't sell in the season before Harvest.

## Definition

What a cleaning business is worth, and the arithmetic that tells you whether an offer is
serious.

## Why It Matters

Without the math, an offer sounds like a number. With it, an offer is either good or
insulting, and you can tell in thirty seconds.

## Models

**The formula:**

```
monthly revenue × profit margin = monthly profit
monthly profit × 12            = annual profit
annual profit × 2 to 2.2       = sale price
```

**Alex's worked example, verbatim from the call:**

> "Let's say it's just doing 60K a month. 60K a month at about 25% profit — you're looking
> at like 15K a month actual profit. And then you do that across the year. So 15K profit
> times 12, so that's 180K profit in a year. And then you sell for like two, 2.2x that.
> So you could sell for 360 to 400, something like that."

## Examples

**The $50K offer Alex declined.** A California owner doing close to six figures a month,
300+ GBP reviews in San Diego, running Meta ads for cleaning businesses, already operating
two companies and starting a third. He wanted to buy rather than start from scratch.

His own words on the offer:

> "I know where you guys are at, Alex and Elijah... you have the infrastructure, you have
> the VAs. You're not going to get stressed by it. I can offer you 50K — I'm kind of
> hurting my own offer here by saying that, but I can't offer you enough. I think you
> generally should hold."

Alex's reasoning:

> "Would it be nice for a 50 grand payday? Sure. But would it be a lot nicer to wait six
> months, wait a year, and a $200,000, $300,000 payday? Yeah, a lot better."

The decisive point is that holding costs nothing: *"me holding on, it looks like me going,
okay, no, we're not going to do anything and just continue to pay payroll on at the VA and
run it."* The infrastructure runs without him. Waiting is free.

## Symptoms

- Someone offers to buy and you have no idea whether it's fair
- You're thinking about selling because you're tired, not because the number is right
- You don't know your actual profit margin

## Common Mistakes

- Selling in Scale instead of [[Four Seasons|Harvest]]
- Not knowing your margin, so you can't run the formula
- Treating a buyer's interest as flattery rather than information — this buyer told Alex
  outright the business was about to be worth much more

## Checklist

- [ ] Know your monthly revenue
- [ ] Know your real profit margin
- [ ] Run the formula before responding to any offer
- [ ] Ask whether the business still needs you — if not, holding is nearly free

**Q:** Is Alex going to sell?
**A:** Eventually. *"We will sell probably at some point in the future, but I definitely

actually want to get out of it."*
