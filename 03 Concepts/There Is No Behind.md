# There Is No Behind

> [!abstract] In one line
> Comparing your timeline to someone with a different life is unfair to yourself, and feeling behind makes you act behind.

## Definition

The reframe for owners who feel like they're lagging their peers: "behind" isn't a real
position, it's a comparison against people with different time, experience, and
obligations — and believing it becomes self-fulfilling.

## Why It Matters

Nicole, three years into her business, felt behind because she hadn't set up a business
bank account or learned CRM terminology yet, comparing herself to further-along members.
Alex and Jack both pushed back hard and directly, live on the call — this isn't a soft
platitude, it's treated as an operational problem: a founder who believes she's behind
makes worse decisions than one who's just doing the next right thing.

> "There is no behind. There's nowhere you should be. That's a lie."

## Symptoms

- Comparing your progress to someone else's in the group and feeling worse for it
- Feeling behind becoming a reason to freeze rather than act
- Listing everything you haven't done yet instead of the next thing to do

## Common Mistakes

- Comparing outcomes without comparing starting conditions (time available, experience,
  family obligations)
- Treating "behind" as information instead of a story you're telling yourself
- Putting ten things on your plate because you're trying to catch up all at once

## Models

**Comparison is almost always apples to oranges.** Alex's own example: dropped out of
high school at 17, has worked in 30+ businesses, consults for $100M+ companies, works
14-hour days, no spouse or kids. Nicole has a husband, a house, and a full cleaning
schedule. Jack's first two businesses failed before this one. Desiree works a 9-to-5 with
kids on top of the business. None of these are comparable timelines.

**Reframe hard as expected, not as a warning sign:**

> "This is what hard feels like. You knew it would be hard when you signed up. Don't get
> down on yourself — just go do the hard things."

**The practical antidote to overwhelm: pick three.** Don't put ten things on the plate.
Pick three, finish them, report back next call. For Nicole specifically: set up a
business bank account, shoot the GBP verification video, call existing clients for
referrals — see [[Referral Program]].

## Checklist

- [ ] Notice when you're comparing your timeline to someone else's outcome
- [ ] Check whether their starting conditions actually match yours
- [ ] If overwhelmed, cut your list to three things
- [ ] Do those three, then come back for the next three
