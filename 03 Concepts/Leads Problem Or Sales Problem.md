# Leads Problem Or Sales Problem

> [!abstract] In one line
> Most owners who feel stuck are stuck on one of two things — and they're not equally hard to fix.

## Definition

The first diagnostic fork in the [[Leads Engine]]. Either you can't get enough leads, or
you can't close the ones you get. The fix is completely different, so guessing wrong costs
months.

## Why It Matters

> "It's usually almost really like 95% of the time — getting leads, closing those leads."

That's Alex's own spoken estimate from a pattern across many coaching conversations, not a
measured statistic — **no dataset backs "95%," and this page does not restate it as fact.**
Read it the way it was said: an emphatic "almost always," not a tracked percentage. The
takeaway is the fork itself, not the number — the two most common answers to "why am I
stuck" are lead volume and close rate, and owners routinely misattribute between them: they
conclude marketing is broken when the close rate is the problem, or they grind on sales
scripts when they simply aren't getting enough calls.

**And the two are not equally hard to fix:**

> "Your problem is much easier to fix than a sales problem."

A lead problem can be copied — Alex can take a working PPC account structure and hand it
over. A sales problem is a skill, and skill has to be built.

`Cleaning Biz 101 — Convert` corroborates the sales-side diagnosis independently, from
running weekly sales-call breakdowns across many owners: *"most cleaning biz owners
aren't good at sales."* If objection handling, "it's too pricey," or "I'm just looking
around" consistently derail calls, that's the sales-problem signal from this table, not a
lead-volume issue — see [[Word Tracks]] and [[Price Objection]].

## Symptoms

- Flat revenue for months with no explanation
- "I need to stop marketing, it isn't working"
- Lots of calls, few bookings — or few calls, good bookings

## Common Mistakes

- Switching channels when the real issue is the close rate
- Building sales scripts when you only get four leads a week
- Diagnosing from feel instead of from [[Marketing Math]]

## Models

**The fork:**

| Signal | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Good close rate, too few leads | **Lead problem** | Easier. Copy a working channel setup. |
| Enough leads, low close rate | **Sales problem** | Harder. Word tracks, training, reps. |

Get the signal from your close rate. Anything meaningfully under ~50% with decent lead
volume points at sales — see [[Word Tracks]].

## Checklist

- [ ] Calculate close rate from real data ([[KPI Tracking Sheet]])
- [ ] Compare lead volume against your capacity
- [ ] Name the constraint before spending anything
- [ ] Lead problem → [[Channel Prioritization]]
- [ ] Sales problem → [[Word Tracks]], [[Handling Let Me Think About It]], [[The Soft Close]]

## Student Examples

**Courtney, 2025-02-03** — 67% conversion on her own data. No sales problem at all. Her
constraint was lead volume, and she was running almost entirely organic. Diagnosis: set up
PPC properly, keep Thumbtack free leads, optimize GBP.

Alex's note on scale: she does great without a script, but at $30K/month she can't take
every call herself — at that point a VA or team member needs the script she never needed.

> [!info] Same student, later
> By [[2025-03-25 Weekly Coaching Call|March 25]] Courtney's constraint had moved to
> **labor**, and the answer became [[Labor Before Leads]]. The constraint moves. Re-diagnose.

**Jack** — $40 cost per lead, 20% close rate. Concluded marketing was broken. It was sales.
The same Jack example (same numbers) reappears in [[Leads 101 part 1]]'s "Acquire vs.
Convert: Which One Is Broken?" lesson — same underlying case, not a second student.

same 95%-of-the-time claim as a pattern across many owner conversations), and "How do I
know if marketing is working" (defines "working" as profitable and routes a non-profitable
channel through the same Acquire-or-Convert fork).
