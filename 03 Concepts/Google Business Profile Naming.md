# Google Business Profile Naming

> [!abstract] In one line
> The two moves that matter most: a physical address (the pin) and the service + city in the name — signage has to match exactly.

## Definition

Originally just the GBP naming convention (brand, divider, service, city); has grown to
cover the two highest-leverage GBP moves together, since they're taught as a pair and
both determine map pack ranking. Naming: `Business Name | House Cleaning Service [City]`.
Address: a physical pin via a virtual office, without which the naming convention alone
can't rank the profile in the map pack. See [[GBP Verification]] for how the address gets
verified.

## Why It Matters

Together, name and address are what the profile ranks for and what determines whether a
business shows in Google's top-3 map pack — where more than half of all map-search clicks
go. The format is `Business Name | House Cleaning Service [City]` — choose the city you
most want clients from — and it does nothing without a verified physical presence behind
it.

## Common Mistakes

- Editing the name during video verification. You cannot change it before the check — only after. On a live call the reviewer can edit it with you in real time.
- Signage that does not match the profile name
- Running service-area-only with no physical address — invisible on the map regardless of naming
- Using a P.O. box as the address — Google will suspend the profile
- Casting too wide a service radius instead of pinning to where ideal clients actually are

## Models

**Traction after a rename shows up within about a week.** A member changed the profile
name to the `Business Name | House Cleaning Service [City]` format and Google didn't flag
the change; an uptick in activity appeared roughly five to six days later. Not instant,
but fast enough that it's worth doing early rather than treating it as a someday task.

**Posting keeps a ranked profile active — see [[GBP Posting Cadence]] for the full
routine.** Harvest CRM's AI can schedule and generate GBP posts.

**The address is arguably the higher-leverage move of the two, and it's not optional.**
A service-area-only profile with no physical address has no pin on Google Maps — and
without a pin, a business is largely invisible to nearby searches, however good the
naming and reviews are. Get a physical address via a virtual office or coworking space
(~$70/month, see [[GBP Verification]] for the verification mechanics) rather than
operating pin-less.

**The pin radiates relevance from its location — place it deliberately.** The closer a
searcher is to the pin, the higher the ranking. A business spread across a 50–70 mile
service area with no pin picks up occasional leads from underserved areas, but those
leads are often too far away to profitably service. If the actual office isn't in the
ideal service area, get a virtual address that is.

**How to actually pick the spot, step by step — before signing anything.** Start by
finding the options: look for coworking spaces and office suites, and talk to the
owners directly rather than just booking online — asking in person can surface options
the listing doesn't show (one owner offered to have mail sent over immediately and put
the requester's name on the building directory the same day). Then evaluate the spot
itself in three passes: (1) **competition** — who else shows up for the same search, how
many reviews they have, and whether their own site and GBP are actually optimized (a
competitor with more reviews but no SEO work is beatable; treat review count and
optimization as two separate checks, not one); (2) **population and median income** of
the area — a low-competition spot with ten residents earning minimum wage is still the
wrong spot; (3) confirm the space itself can support GBP verification (see [[GBP Verification]]) before committing. Worst case if a location doesn't work out: fall back
to service-area-only rather than losing the spend already made. Source: [[Google Biz Profile Setup and Growth]].

**Only the top three results in the map pack matter.** Google Maps shows three businesses
in the main view; everything else requires clicking "more businesses." The top three
capture over half of all clicks — the rest compete for what's left. This is the reason
address and naming optimization are worth the effort rather than a marginal tweak.

**Scaling past one profile's radius: multiple GBP locations.** A single GBP can only
dominate a limited radius. To cover more area, open additional locations — each its own
Google account, its own virtual address (~$70/month), same naming format (`[Business
Name] House Cleaning Service [City]`) — all funneling to the same website and booking
system. No separate LLC is needed; a DBA (~$25) covers each one. The hardest part of a
new location is starting from zero reviews again. Use **Local Falcon** (or a similar
tool) to check actual map ranking from different points around the pin, rather than
guessing at coverage.

**A live cross-industry comparison confirms address beats review count.** A window
cleaner with 385 Google reviews still ranked 7th in the map pack because they had no
physical address (service-area-only) — beaten by a much newer, address-verified profile
with far fewer reviews. Reviews build trust once someone's looking at the profile; the
address determines whether the profile shows up at all. See [[Website Technical SEO Basics]] for the WhiteSpark ranking-factor breakdown (GBP signals ≈ 32% of map-pack
ranking) this example illustrates.

**A verified, 4x'd profile still needs upkeep beyond the initial rename.** On a live
walkthrough of one profile (2026-04-20), the remaining to-do list was small but real:
post consistently (see [[GBP Posting Cadence]] — this source's own stated floor was
looser than that page's Mon/Wed/Fri routine, noted there rather than here), upload the
business logo if it's missing, and get the business email properly set up in [[Harvest CRM]] (DNS records) so notifications and outreach don't get flagged as spam. None of
these are hard fixes; they were just unfinished.

## Checklist

- [ ] Set the name to `Business Name | House Cleaning Service [City]`, using Google
      Keyword Planner to confirm the top local keyword (usually a variant of "house
      cleaning service")
- [ ] Make signage match the GBP name exactly
- [ ] Before signing for a spot: check nearby competitors' review counts *and* whether
      their own site/GBP is actually optimized — don't rule a spot out on review count
      alone
- [ ] Check the area's population and median income, not just the competition
- [ ] Get a physical address (virtual office/coworking, not a P.O. box) before expecting map visibility
- [ ] Pin the address in the actual area you want clients from, not just where the office happens to be
- [ ] Once one location is dominant, consider a second GBP location (own address, own Google account) rather than widening one profile's radius
- [ ] Use Local Falcon to verify actual ranking rather than assuming it from anecdote
