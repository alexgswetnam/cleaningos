# Quality Complaints

> [!abstract] In one line
> First decide whose fault it is — that single question determines who pays.

## Definition

The framework for handling a client complaint about the work, and for deciding whether the
cost comes out of your margin or the cleaner's pay.

## Why It Matters

> **How you handle mistakes keeps clients more than perfection does.**

Many current clients had a bad first experience and stayed because of the recovery. The
complaint isn't the risk; the handling is.

The second reason: without a rule, owners default to absorbing every cost. That's
expensive and it teaches cleaners nothing.

## The Matrix

**One question first: is the client asking for more than what was booked, or did the
cleaner miss something they should have done?**

| | Client's fault — asking beyond scope | Cleaner's fault — missed the work |
|---|---|---|
| **The cleaner** | **Don't penalise them.** They did what they were supposed to. | Goes back and fixes it, **uncompensated**. |
| **The client** | Point to the checklist, pre-email, website. You may still discount to protect the relationship. | Offer options: next visit, someone comes back, or money off. |
| **Who pays** | You absorb it | Reduce the cleaner's pay — they're paid a percentage of the job, not hourly |

## Scripts

**To the client:**

> "Sorry we missed that. I can have a cleaner come back out, we can get it on the next
> visit, or I can take $40 off this clean."

**Client asking beyond scope:**

> "We made it really clear in our pre-email, on the website, and with the checklist that
> we don't include that."

**To the cleaner:**

> "I'm not paying you to go there and be there — I'm paying you to complete this service.
> You need to go back and fix that, and I'm not compensating you for it, because you
> should have done it the first time."

**If they refuse:**

> "If you don't go back, I need to pay another cleaner to finish the job you didn't
> finish, so I'd have to reduce your pay."

## The Re-Clean Process

A second source formalizes the operational side of the same decision — once you know
it's a missed-work situation, here's the actual re-clean workflow:

**Eligibility window.** Re-clean requests must be reported within a defined guarantee
period (the source example: 48 hours) and be related to missed or incomplete work
against the original scope — not a new request.

**Triage severity first.** A minor issue (small miss, quick touch-up) can be handled by
apologizing and folding the fix into the client's *next* visit, if they're okay with
that — no separate trip. A larger issue (multiple missed areas, a noticeably incomplete
job) goes to the full re-clean process below.

**Default to fixing the work, not refunding the money.** Offer 2–3 time windows within
the guarantee period, confirm access/pets/special instructions, and decide who returns:
the original cleaner (with a clear list of what to correct) or a replacement (compensated
fairly for a job that's now smaller in scope, following the [[Quality Complaints|fault
matrix]] above for whether that pay comes out of the original cleaner's pay).

**Refunds are the fallback, not the first move.** Small refunds within a defined team
limit can be approved on the spot; anything larger escalates to a manager. If a client
delays reporting the issue for several days, conditions may have changed (dust,
re-use) — a partial refund can replace a re-clean at that point instead of sending a
cleaner back to a home that's no longer in the same state.

**The guarantee explicitly does not apply** to pre-existing damage or permanent stains,
declined add-ons the client is now complaining weren't done (e.g. deep-clean items),
misrepresented property condition, or a job that access/utilities actually prevented from
being done correctly. Use these exceptions to protect margin, not to dodge a legitimate
complaint — the goal stated in the source is trust, not avoidance.

## Checklist

- [ ] Get details from the client — **ask for pictures**
- [ ] Decide: beyond scope, or missed work?
- [ ] Beyond scope → point to the checklist, don't touch the cleaner's pay
- [ ] Missed work → cleaner returns uncompensated
- [ ] Offer the client three options rather than one
- [ ] If you discounted for a cleaner error, reduce their pay accordingly
- [ ] **Ask your best cleaners** what happened before judging
- [ ] Reinforce [[The Walkthrough]]
- [ ] Confirm the complaint is within the guarantee window (e.g. 48 hours) before
      proceeding
- [ ] Triage severity: minor → fold into next visit; larger → full re-clean process
- [ ] Offer 2–3 re-clean time windows before offering a refund
- [ ] Escalate refunds above the team's approval limit to a manager
- [ ] Confirm the issue isn't excluded (pre-existing damage, declined add-ons,
      misrepresented condition, access/utilities blocker)
- [ ] Log every complaint: what went wrong, who handled it, resolution

## Common Mistakes

- Absorbing the cost when it's clearly the cleaner's error
- Penalising a cleaner when the client was asking beyond scope
- Not pointing clients to the checklist for services they never booked
- Judging a cleaner without asking your best cleaners for context

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't reducing a cleaner's pay harsh?
**A:** They're paid a percentage of the job, not by the hour. The pay is for the completed
service. A cleaner who did complete it keeps all of it.
