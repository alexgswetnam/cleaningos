# Monthly Revenue Tracking

> [!abstract] In one line
> Screenshot recurring revenue at the start and end of every month — that's the whole growth benchmark.

## Definition

A manual habit — not a BookingKoala feature — of screenshotting projected total and
recurring revenue at the start of each month, then screenshotting actuals at month end, to
see how much new business was added and how much recurring revenue was kept versus lost.
Distinct from [[Marketing Math]] (lead-level unit economics: cost per lead, close rate,
CPA) and [[KPI Tracking Sheet]] (lead-source logging) — this is a business-level growth
trendline, not a per-lead diagnostic.

## Why It Matters

Without a month-over-month recurring-revenue trendline, an owner has no way to tell
whether a given month's numbers are normal seasonal variation (see [[Marketing
Seasonality]]) or a real problem. A single month read in isolation invites panic or false
confidence; the trend across many months is what's actually informative.

## Symptoms

- Judging a month's revenue in isolation instead of against a trend
- Conflating a high one-time-revenue month (e.g. move-outs) with real recurring growth
- Not knowing whether last month's growth is normal or unusually good/bad

## Common Mistakes

- Not tracking start-of-month vs. end-of-month at all
- Treating recurring and one-time revenue as the same number
- Comparing this month only to last month instead of to the same month a year prior

## Models

**The habit itself.** On the 1st of each month, screenshot BookingKoala's total projected
revenue and recurring revenue. At month end, screenshot again. Compare: how much new
business was added, how much recurring was kept vs. lost. There's no built-in report for
this in BookingKoala — it's manual, done every month, without exception.

> "I take screenshots of what the month projects at and what the month starts at. There's
> not a way for BookingKoala to do this — I just do it manually."

**Separate recurring from total.** A month can look strong on total revenue purely from
one-time work (move-outs, one-off deep cleans) while recurring — the number that actually
compounds — barely moved. Track both, and treat recurring as the primary signal.

**Use a peer or your own history as the reference curve**, not a flat target. Alex's own
recurring-revenue progression across a full year (see [[Marketing Seasonality]] for the
month-by-month figures) becomes the yardstick he uses to tell members whether their own
number is on track.

## Checklist

- [ ] Screenshot BookingKoala's projected total + recurring revenue on the 1st of the month
- [ ] Screenshot actuals again at month end
- [ ] Record new business added vs. recurring kept/lost
- [ ] Track recurring revenue separately from one-time revenue
- [ ] Compare against your own trend or a peer's, not an arbitrary target
