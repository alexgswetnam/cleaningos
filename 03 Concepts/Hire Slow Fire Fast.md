# Hire Slow Fire Fast

> [!abstract] In one line
> Take the time to really qualify who you bring on — then the moment someone's proven
> not to be a fit, move on quickly instead of holding on for emotional reasons.

## Definition

A hiring and management principle applied to both cleaners and VAs/office staff:
qualify carefully before bringing someone on ("hire slow"), then end the relationship
promptly once the evidence says it isn't working ("fire fast") — rather than letting
emotional attachment or guilt keep an underperformer in place. It's a business-decision
discipline, not a personality trait: the goal is separating "I like this person" from
"this is good for the business," not becoming cold toward people.

## Why It Matters

Holding onto the wrong person costs more than the discomfort of ending it. A real
example from the business: a first VA looked strong on paper (relevant past experience),
but was slow with the software and weak at sales — the opposite of what the role
required. He was kept roughly a month longer than he should have been specifically
because of personal fondness, while systems kept slipping. Elsewhere, a friend's cleaning
business struggled to get off the ground in its first two months specifically because he
kept working with a cleaner who repeatedly rescheduled, canceled, and no-showed — sympathy
for her circumstances directly cost his business momentum. **The pattern in both cases is
the same: personal feeling delayed a decision the numbers had already made.**

## Symptoms

- A team member's performance metrics (close rate, quality, reliability) are clearly
  below where they need to be, and the owner already knows it
- The owner is making excuses for someone's performance based on personal circumstances
- "I really like them" is doing the work that "this is working" should be doing
- A hiring decision was made quickly, based on how someone presented, without real
  qualification

## Common Mistakes

- Rushing the hiring decision because there's an urgent staffing need
- Keeping someone on because letting them go feels unkind, even after the evidence is
  clear
- Waiting for a struggling hire to improve without a specific timeline or benchmark
- Believing a C-level performer can be coached into an A-level one — see Models below
- Treating every underperformer the same way regardless of whether the gap is coachable

## Models

**Hire slow: qualify for real, don't rush because you're desperate.** The interview and
test-clean process exists precisely to prevent the fast, hopeful hire — see
[[Hiring a Cleaner SOP]] and [[Testing A New Cleaner]].

**Fire fast: numbers over feelings, once the pattern is clear.** *"You can either like her
and keep her and your business fails, or you can like her and tell her, sorry, she's not
a good fit, and find someone better, and your business is going to succeed. It's kind of
your only options."* The framing deliberately removes the false middle option — liking
someone and keeping them despite bad numbers isn't actually a neutral choice, it's a
choice against the business.

**The letter-grade ceiling — you can develop someone half a grade, not two.** *"You can
take someone from like a B minus to a B, or B to a B plus, but you're not going to take a
C player and turn them into an A player no matter how hard you train them."* This sets a
practical limit on how much coaching effort is worth spending before the honest
conclusion is "wrong fit," not "needs more training."

**Keep personal and business separate — as a discipline, not a personality change.** The
person applying this framework can simultaneously be someone who brings personal warmth
into business relationships elsewhere; the point isn't becoming cold, it's recognizing
that *this specific decision* — is this role working — needs to be made on evidence, with
personal fondness held separately.

**A model from outside cleaning, cited directly.** Watching a founder run a
multi-million-dollar meeting with his president, COO, and CFO — discussing real financial
and even legal decisions with zero emotional entanglement — was the moment this became
clearer: *"the lack of personal whatever was such an advantage to them because they were
running through numbers and making business decisions based on numbers."* Business
decisions, made on evidence, without the decision quality degrading through personal
attachment.

## Checklist

- [ ] Take real time to qualify before bringing someone on — don't skip steps because of
      urgency
- [ ] Set a specific, observable standard for what "working" looks like before the hire
      starts
- [ ] When the evidence says someone isn't a fit, act — don't wait for the feeling of
      certainty to arrive
- [ ] Separate "I like this person" from "this is working" as two different questions
- [ ] Don't invest heavy coaching effort trying to move a clearly-C-level performer to
      A-level
- [ ] Treat firing/ending the relationship as a business decision, communicated with
      respect, not an emotional confrontation

## FAQ

**Q:** Doesn't "fire fast" contradict giving new hires a fair warm-up period?
**A:** No — [[Testing A New Cleaner]] and [[Hiring a Cleaner SOP]] both build in a deliberate
on-ramp and coaching window for new hires. "Fire fast" applies once a real, observed
pattern exists, not to a first rough day.
