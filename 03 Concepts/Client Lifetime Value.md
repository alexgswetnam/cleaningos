# Client Lifetime Value

> [!abstract] In one line
> The right question isn't what you made on the first clean — it's what the client is worth over a year.

## Definition

Total profit a client generates across their whole relationship with you. It's the number
that justifies acquisition cost.

## Why It Matters

Judge acquisition against one clean and almost no paid channel survives. Judge it against
twelve months and the arithmetic changes completely.

**Retention — the thing that makes lifetime value real — starts before the first
clean, not after it.** `Cleaning Biz 101 — Fulfill` makes this point directly: clear
confirmations, consistent reminders, professional arrival, and set expectations
automatically increase repeat bookings, reviews, and referrals. Its framing: *"Fulfill is
one of the biggest 'silent' drivers or killers of retention."* This is the operational
half of the LTV math above — the number only gets realized if the delivery experience
earns the next booking. See [[Sales Pipeline Stages]]'s Booked stages for the specific
confirmation/reminder sequence this runs on.

## Models

```
cleans per year × profit per clean = lifetime profit
```

**Alex's worked example — one real client:**

Started in March, still cleaning monthly in December. Twelve cleans at $179, about $80
profit each.

> **$960 in profit from one client.**
>
> "Was it worth for me to pay even $100 to get this one guy? Yes."

Worth $200. Worth $300. The acquisition cost stops being scary once you know the number
on the other side.

`Cleaning Biz 101 — Fulfill` shows where the $179 itself comes from: it's a reverse-
engineered flat rate ($60/hour target × ~3 measured hours for this job profile), not a
round number picked at random. See [[Pricing Methodology]] for the full method.

**A member's LSA math:** $560 spend → 31 leads → 22% close → 7 bookings → 3 recurring →
**$3,500 in recurring revenue over the following four months.** $560 bought $3,500.

**Courtney's full LTV, pulled straight from BookingKoala's own reports** (Reports →
Trends → Revenue per Booking, Average Bookings per Customer, Average Recurring Bookings
per Customer) — the exact source and method behind the number, not a manual estimate:

```
average booking × average bookings per customer = revenue per customer (LTV)
```

- Average booking: $199
- Average bookings per customer: 12.82 → **$2,564 revenue per customer**
- Average *recurring* bookings per customer: 24 → **$4,800 revenue per recurring customer**

At ~55–61% total expenses (50% labor + 5–11% overhead/software/sales tax), that's roughly
**$964 profit per customer lifetime**, and close to double for a recurring one. This is
the number that turns [[Marketing Math]] into an equation: anything spent to acquire a
customer below the profit figure is worth spending, no matter how the CPA compares to
another channel's CPA in isolation — see [[Channel Prioritization]] for the Thumbtack-vs-
LSA case this settles.

**Against industry benchmarks, Courtney's numbers run about 20% higher** — roughly $2,000
LTV / $4,000 recurring is the stated industry average, against her $2,564 / $4,800. Named
as a sign her team is unusually good at keeping clients, not a typical result to expect
by default.

## Symptoms

- Acquisition cost feels unaffordable
- You're evaluating channels on first-clean profit
- You don't know what share of clients recur, or for how long

## Common Mistakes

- Measuring on clean one — see [[Month One Loses Money]]
- Not tracking [[One-Time To Recurring]] conversion
- Not knowing how long clients actually stay

## Checklist

- [ ] Average profit per clean
- [ ] Average cleans per client per year
- [ ] Multiply — that's your ceiling on acquisition cost
- [ ] Compare against CPA from [[Marketing Math]]
- [ ] Pull the real numbers from BookingKoala → Reports → Trends rather than estimating:
      revenue per booking, average bookings per customer, average recurring bookings per
      customer
- [ ] Calculate profit per customer lifetime using your actual expense percentage
- [ ] Compare any channel's CAC against the profit figure, not against another channel's
      CAC
