# Social Media Strategy

> [!abstract] In one line
> Social media won't bring you a lead — it makes the leads you already have trust you more.

## Definition

The role social media plays for most cleaning businesses: a credibility check for
prospects who already found you through another channel, not a lead-generation channel
in its own right. The framing follows Alex Hormozi's approach.

## Why It Matters

Owners either over-invest in social media hoping it generates leads directly, or ignore it
entirely and lose a free conversion boost. Neither read is accurate — the actual function
is narrower and cheaper to satisfy than either extreme assumes, which matters because
attention spent here is attention not spent on [[Channel Prioritization|higher-leverage
channels]].

## Symptoms

- Expecting a post to generate bookings and being disappointed when it doesn't
- Not posting at all and wondering if you're leaving something on the table
- Spending real time on content when paid marketing or GBP is the actual constraint

## Common Mistakes

- Treating social media as a lead source and measuring it that way
- Investing significant time here at the expense of [[Channel Prioritization|paid
  marketing or GBP optimization]]
- Expecting a small following (e.g. 22 followers) to surface posts organically — it won't
- Posting nothing at all and losing the credibility check entirely

## Models

**What it actually does: increases conversion on leads from elsewhere.** A prospect finds
the business through GBP, a Google search, or a referral, then "snoops" the social
profiles before deciding to call. What they find there — team photos, before/afters,
review screenshots, an "about us" story — makes them more confident, not less. The lead
didn't originate on social media, but social media raised its close probability.

> "For most businesses, social media is not going to be how you get clients. It's going to
> increase the conversion rate of the clients that find you."

**What to post.** About us / how the business started, the team, before/afters, a
satisfaction guarantee, review screenshots, "why choose us" content — the credibility
building blocks a snooping prospect is looking for.

**Minimum viable cadence.** Once a week is enough to check the box of "we're legit, we
still exist, we still clean." More is fine but not necessary — one member's VA posts about
five times a week, largely unsupervised, and he hadn't looked at the account in months.

**It's acceptable to be hands-off about it.**

> "I don't care to do that. I want to be hands off. Do I recommend it? Yeah, sure. I don't
> want to do it because I want to be hands off."

**A minor, secondary benefit: connecting social profiles to GBP.** This modestly helps
local search presence — worth doing, but not the primary reason to post.

## Checklist

- [ ] Post at minimum once a week — about us, team, before/afters, reviews
- [ ] Don't expect posts to generate leads directly
- [ ] Connect social profiles to your GBP listing
- [ ] Don't reallocate time from paid marketing or GBP to chase social media growth
- [ ] Delegate posting to a VA rather than doing it personally if it's a drag
