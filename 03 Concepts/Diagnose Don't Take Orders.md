# Diagnose Don't Take Orders

> [!abstract] In one line
> "What type of clean are you looking for?" is a menu. A doctor doesn't hand you a menu.

## Definition

Opening the call by finding out what's actually going on, rather than asking the prospect
to select a service from a list. Open-ended questions first; the prescription comes after
you've listened.

> "You don't go into the doctor and the doctor goes 'here's the menu, which treatment do
> you want?' — they diagnose you."

## Why It Matters

Asking "deep clean, standard, or move-in?" hands the prospect a decision they aren't
qualified to make and gives you nothing to sell with. They pick the cheapest thing they
recognise, and you've learned nothing about why they called.

Ask what's going on instead and they tell you the thing you actually need: the old cleaner
who stopped showing up, the six months of dissatisfaction, the renovation dust, the guests
coming Saturday. That's the material for [[Price Objection]] handling, for
[[Drop Scope Not Price]], and for the connection that makes [[Reciprocity]] work later.

**The order-taker version of this call is the one a VA runs by default.** If your VA sounds
like a menu, this is why.

## Symptoms

- Your first question is a list of options
- You know the square footage and nothing about the person
- You reach the price with nothing to say when they hesitate
- Calls take four minutes and close at 20%

## Common Mistakes

- Opening with service types
- Opening with beds/baths/square footage — necessary information, wrong first question
- Asking an open question and then interrupting with a closed one
- Assuming a short-answer prospect won't give you anything. They will — just less of it.

## Models

**The three openers:**

> "What's going on?"
>
> "What's the situation?"
>
> "What's got you reaching out today?"

All three do the same job: they make the prospect drive, and whatever they choose to say
first is the thing that matters most to them.

**Then diagnose, then prescribe.** You are the one who names the service. That's the whole
difference — "based on what you're describing, here's what I'd do" is a position of
authority; "which one do you want?" is a position of service.

**Type A prospects give you a summary, not a story.** That's not a failure of the question.
A quick summary is enough to diagnose from — you don't need ten minutes. Don't keep digging
for depth the person has already told you they won't provide. See [[Mirroring]].

**A concrete trigger rule for the prescription itself.** A second source gives a specific
decision rule for the most common prescription — first-visit deep clean vs. standard:
recommend a deep clean when the home hasn't had a professional clean in 6+ months, or the
prospect describes the condition as 5+ out of 10, then standard going forward. Having a
stated rule, rather than a gut call each time, is what makes "I'd recommend X" sound
confident instead of improvised.

## Checklist

- [ ] First question is open-ended
- [ ] Let them finish before asking anything else
- [ ] Note the actual reason they called, in their words
- [ ] Gather the specs (beds, baths, sqft, condition) after, not before
- [ ] Name the service yourself
- [ ] Check: could you now say something specific if they push back on price?
- [ ] Apply the deep-clean trigger rule: 6+ months since last clean or condition 5+/10 →
      recommend deep clean first, standard after

## Templates

> "What's going on? What's got you reaching out today?"
>
> "What happened with your last cleaner?"
>
> "Did you have a problem, or you just wanted the place to look better?"

**Not:**

> "Are you looking for a deep clean or a standard clean or a move-in?"

**The prescription itself, once diagnosed:**

> "Based on what you told me, I'd recommend a deep clean for the first visit, then
> standard going forward."

## Student Examples

**Jack / Marcella** — opened with "what type of clean are you looking for?" Marcella had had
a cleaner for ten years who moved back to Ukraine, then six months of dissatisfaction with a
replacement. None of that surfaced early, and Jack had nothing to work with when the price
landed. His own later diagnosis, from a different call: *"I forgot to go into why she's
looking to switch."* Same failure twice — see [[Call Breakdowns]].

**Untrained VAs** — named in the source as the default failure mode. A VA with no training
asks the menu question every time.

## AI Prompts

> When reviewing a call opening, check the first question asked. If it presents options, the
> call was an order-taking call regardless of how well the rest went, and the price
> conversation later will have nothing behind it.

## FAQ

**Q:** What if they open by asking "how much for a deep clean?"
**A:** Answer the question they're really asking by asking yours: *"Happy to get you that —
what's going on over there?"* You still need the diagnosis; you just can't ignore them to
get it.

**Q:** Doesn't this make the call longer?
**A:** With a chatty prospect, yes. With a Type A, no — they hand you a two-sentence summary
and you move on. See [[Mirroring]].
