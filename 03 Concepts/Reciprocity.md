# Reciprocity

> [!abstract] In one line
> If you're going to reduce the price anyway, make it a favour with your name on it — never a silent adjustment.

## Definition

Framing any price reduction as a personal decision you made for *this* person, rather than
as a standard discount or a quiet change in the system. The reduction costs you the same
either way; the framing decides whether you get anything back for it.

## Why It Matters

A discount that appears silently is worth exactly its dollar value. The client notices a
smaller number and nothing else.

The same reduction, named and given, creates an obligation. They feel chosen. What comes
back is loyalty, tips, reviews, and tolerance the first time something goes wrong — none of
which you could have bought for $25.

> "She's grateful. She feels special. She reciprocates later."

## The Precondition

This does not work standalone, and used standalone it actively backfires.

> "This only works if you've built connection first. If the whole call was 'beds? baths?
> square footage? price.' and then you try 'I like you, I'll drop the price' — it sounds
> like a sales tactic because there was no relationship to base it on."

"I like you" is only a favour if there's evidence you formed an opinion. That's what the
diagnosis conversation and the mirroring were for. See [[Diagnose Don't Take Orders]] and
[[Mirroring]].

**So the order is: connect, then reduce, then name it.** Reduce first and you've spent the
money and bought nothing.

## Symptoms

- You've knocked fees off in the system and the client never knew
- Clients who got a lower price behave exactly like clients who didn't
- Your discounts don't produce reviews
- "I like you, so I'll drop the price" lands flat

## Common Mistakes

- Removing a fee silently — the most common version, and it wastes the money entirely
- Framing it as a standard promotion. *Everyone gets this* is the opposite of a favour.
- Using the language without the relationship
- Reducing more than you meant to because the framing felt good

## Models

**Three worked examples**, all from the same call:

| Situation | What you say |
|---|---|
| System shows a $25 pet fee | *"Usually we'd charge the $25 for pets, but Marcella, it sounds like you take really good care of your home. I don't mind taking that off for you."* |
| Home is 3,050 sqft, rounds into a higher tier | *"I don't usually do this, but honestly, I like you. I'm going to round that down and take some off the price. Don't tell my boss."* |
| Quote assumes four hours | *"Usually it's four hours at this price, but since your home is well-maintained and you've had a cleaner — if our cleaner gets done in three and a half, I'll reduce the price to reflect that."* |

Each has the same three parts: **the normal price, the specific reason it's you, the gift.**
The reason has to be about *them* — how they keep their home, that they've had a cleaner
before — not about your margin or your calendar.

**"Don't tell my boss."** You are the boss. The joke works anyway, because what it really
communicates is that a rule is being bent, and rules bent for you feel different from prices
that were always going to be that.

**The third example is the safest one.** It's conditional — the price drops only if the work
takes less time. Nothing is given away; a fair exchange is made visible. Worth preferring
when the relationship isn't strong enough to carry a gift.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm you actually built connection earlier in the call
- [ ] Decide the reduction before you frame it, not during
- [ ] State the normal price out loud first
- [ ] Give a reason that is about them, specifically
- [ ] Say you're taking it off — don't let them discover it
- [ ] Use their name

## AI Prompts

> Before suggesting a price reduction to a student, ask what happened earlier in the call. If
> the call was specs-and-price with no diagnosis, reciprocity framing will read as a tactic —
> the correct advice is to fix the opening, not to add the favour language.

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't "I don't usually do this" a lie if I do it often?
**A:** It is, if you do. The conditional version — the time-based reduction — stays true
however often you use it, which is a reason to prefer it.

**Q:** What if they don't reciprocate?
**A:** Some won't. The framing costs nothing over the silent version, so it's free upside
either way.
