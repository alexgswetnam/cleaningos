# Sales Pipeline Stages

> [!abstract] In one line
> No lead gets lost: a visual answer to "where is this lead right now, and what happens next," with automation doing the remembering.

## Definition

A six-stage CRM pipeline structure — New Lead, Follow-Up, Quoted, Booked (One Time),
Booked (Recurring), Long Term Follow Up — with a defined automatic and manual action at
each stage. Built in [[Harvest CRM]], integrated with [[BookingKoala]] for the booking
stages.

## Why It Matters

Relying on memory, a notes app, or scattered texts to track leads guarantees chaos as
volume grows. A pipeline isn't valuable because of its stages — it's valuable because an
accurate one stops leads from being forgotten, stops random re-texting, makes bottlenecks
visible (e.g. lots of quotes sent but nothing booked signals a follow-up problem, not a
marketing problem), and is what allows scaling past 100 bookings a month without chaos.

Most lost deals in this business aren't lost because of cleaning quality — they're lost
to slow response, forgotten follow-up, no clear process, or friction in booking. The
entire purpose of this pipeline is to make those four failure modes structurally
difficult.

## Symptoms

- Tracking leads by memory, notes app, or scattered texts
- Leads "double-texted" randomly, or not followed up with at all
- Lots of quotes sent, few bookings, and no visibility into why
- Growth stalling because volume outpaces manual tracking

## Common Mistakes

- Having stages but no defined automation or manual action at each one
- Letting a lead sit in "Quoted" without a defined follow-up cadence
- Treating "Long Term Follow Up" as a dead end instead of a real, monitored stage
- Not tagging the marketing source at the moment a lead enters the pipeline — see
  [[Conversion Tracking]]

## Models

**The six stages, each with a goal and a defined action set:**

**1. New Lead.** The moment someone reaches out. Goal: instant contact, don't let them
slip. Automatic: tag the marketing source (PPC/Thumbtack/GBP/LSA/website/etc.), fire an
immediate auto-text (*"Hey this is [name] with [business]. Saw you reached out for
cleaning — when is a good time to call you?"*), an optional confirmation email, and a
missed-call text if they called and weren't reached. Manual, same day: call immediately —
see [[Speed To Lead]] — and if no response, double-dial twice a day until contact or a
week of automatic texts passes.

**2. Follow-Up.** For leads who gave a callback time, or who are being actively texted but
haven't yet been quoted by phone. Automatic: reminders/tasks for the team to call.
Manual: call at the time requested; once reached, move them to Quoted, book them
directly, or move to Long Term Follow Up if timing isn't right.

**3. Quoted.** Where most bookings happen — with good follow-up. Anyone given pricing by
any channel (phone, text, email, in-person) or sent a booking link with a price attached.
Automatic: reminders/tasks to call/text. Manual: if hesitating, ask why (timing, price,
trust, decision maker); offer the simplest next step (*"I can get you booked in 2
minutes"*); follow up every 1–2 days — this is not bothering them, it's getting a yes or
no. Exit criteria: books once → Booked (One Time); starts recurring → Booked (Recurring);
"not now" → Long Term Follow Up; bad fit → Do Not Contact.

**4. Booked (One Time).** Goal: a perfect first experience, with an eye toward converting
to recurring later. Automatic: confirmation email/text with expectations and policy
reminders; a 5-day-out reminder, a 24-hour-out reminder, and an "on the way" text (via
[[BookingKoala]]); enrollment in a one-time-clean follow-up sequence that offers
expiring store credit 2–3 months later. Manual: clear job notes for the cleaner, and
assign an available cleaner.

**5. Booked (Recurring).** Goal: keep them long-term — this is where the business becomes
stable. Same confirmation/reminder automations as one-time. Manual: track preferences
(pets, priorities, focus areas); handle issues fast, since retention lives or dies here;
periodic quality checks or a short manager touch.

**6. Long Term Follow Up.** Goal: turn old leads and past clients into future bookings,
automatically. Who lands here: "not right now" leads, ghosted quotes, past one-time
clients who never rebooked, and optionally paused/canceled recurring clients. Automatic
reactivation cadence: 30 days ("Still need help?"), 60–90 days ("Want to get back on the
schedule?"), 6 months (seasonal check-in), 12 months (annual check-in) — deliberately
paced to feel human, not spammy. Manual, on response: move back to Quoted or New Lead as
appropriate, and get them on the phone quickly.

**Why this specific cadence matters.** See [[Reactivating Past Clients]] for Rick's
independently-built VIP workflow, which used a single 60-day trigger for lapsed recurring
clients — this pipeline's 30/60–90/180/365-day cadence is a more granular version of the
same idea, staged rather than single-trigger.

**A simplified teaching version of the same pipeline, and two statuses worth naming
explicitly.** A second source teaches the same pipeline as five active stages (New Lead,
Follow-Up, Quoted, Booked, Long Term Follow-Up) plus two closed-out statuses:
**Cancelled/Declined** (the lead was worked but didn't convert) and **Do Not Contact**
(the lead explicitly asked to stop being contacted). Do Not Contact isn't new — it's
already this page's exit criteria for a "bad fit" out of Quoted — but this source elevates
it to an explicit, always-visible status rather than an implicit exit path, and adds
Cancelled/Declined as a similar closed status for tracking why leads didn't convert. Its
follow-up cadence is coarser than the 30/60–90/180/365 schedule above (initial contact →
4–6 hours → 24–48 hours → 30–90 days for long-term), which reads as a simplified version
of the same structure rather than a genuine conflict.

**The daily operating habit that keeps the pipeline from silently rotting.** Check the
dashboard daily; clear every due/overdue task, and either assign the next task or move the
lead's stage when a task completes. Leads in Follow-Up/Quoted with many touches and no
response move to Long Term Follow Up; leads with fewer touches get the current task
completed and a new one assigned two days out. New "hot" leads get called once daily
(texting is automated). Before ending the check, confirm every lead sitting in
Follow-Up/Quoted actually has a task assigned — an untasked lead in an active stage is
how leads get silently lost.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every lead tagged with source at the moment it enters the pipeline
- [ ] New Lead: auto-text + same-day call, double-dial if no response
- [ ] Follow-Up: call at the requested time, move forward or to Long Term Follow Up
- [ ] Quoted: follow up every 1–2 days until a clear yes or no
- [ ] Booked stages: confirmation + 5-day + 24-hour + on-the-way reminders automated
- [ ] One-time bookings enrolled in a 2–3 month store-credit follow-up sequence
- [ ] Long Term Follow Up cadence live: 30 / 60–90 / 180 / 365 days
- [ ] Bottlenecks reviewed by stage (e.g. many Quoted, few Booked = follow-up problem)
- [ ] Dashboard checked daily; every due/overdue task cleared same day
- [ ] Every lead in Follow-Up/Quoted has an assigned task — none left untasked
- [ ] Cancelled/Declined and Do Not Contact tracked as explicit statuses, not silent
      drop-offs

## Templates

- "Hey this is [name] with [business]. Saw you reached out for cleaning — when is a good

- "I can get you booked in 2 minutes." (Quoted stage close)
- "Still need help?" (30-day reactivation)
- "Want to get back on the schedule?" (60–90 day reactivation)
