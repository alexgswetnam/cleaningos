# Legacy Curriculum Crosswalk — Five Pillars

> [!abstract] In one line
> A migration aid, not a model: how Cleaning Biz 101's Acquire / Convert / Fulfill / Operate / Finances material maps into Four Engines × Four Seasons.

## Definition

**Five Business Pillars is retired as a customer-facing framework.** The one model is
[[Four Engines]] × [[Four Seasons]], governed by the [[Five Laws]].

This page exists for one job: **to move useful legacy material into CleaningOS without
losing it.** It is a translation table, not a second map of the business.

> [!warning] Do not teach the Five Pillars
> New curriculum uses Four Engines × Four Seasons. Any second customer-facing business map
> needs Alex's explicit approval before it exists — see `CONSTITUTION.md` §II.
>
> Old Cleaning Biz 101 material still teaches the pillars. That is expected and is not a
> contradiction to re-flag.

## The Crosswalk

**These mappings are approximate. They are not exact, and nothing claims they are.** Their
purpose is to tell you where legacy material probably belongs, so a human can check.

| Legacy pillar | Maps primarily to | Notes on the fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Acquire** | **Leads** | Cleanest mapping of the five. "Your lead engine — the stuff that makes strangers become interested enough to raise their hand." |
| **Convert** | **Leads** | Also Leads. The Engine model puts marketing and sales in one Engine that splits in two — see [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]]. The pillars split them into two peers, which is the main structural difference between the frameworks. |
| **Fulfill** | **Labor + Logistics** | Splits across two Engines. Capacity to do the work is Labor; making delivery repeatable is Logistics. Legacy Fulfill material has to be sorted, not moved wholesale. |
| **Operate** | **Logistics + Leadership** | Also splits. Systems and SOPs are Logistics; delegation, accountability and ownership culture are Leadership. |
| **Finances** | **Measurement across all four** | Not an Engine. [[Four Engines]]: finance is *"the health of all four, not a separate production Engine."* The legacy course agrees with itself here — its own Finances lesson calls money *"the scoreboard for the whole system"* that *"connects to everything."* See [[Business Finances]]. |

**Where the fit is worst:** Fulfill and Operate. Each spans two Engines, so a legacy lesson
filed under either could belong in either Engine, and the decision needs a human reading of
the lesson. Those are the rows to be careful with when working the
Legacy Content Crosswalk.

**Where the fit is best:** Acquire → Leads, and Finances → measurement. Both are close to
one-to-one.

## When This Matters

Only when migrating legacy material. If you are writing something new, you should not be
on this page.

It also matters as a record of a decision: the vault spent time treating the pillars as a
rival taxonomy, and that was an agent's inference from a single beginner call, not
something Alex taught. Left in place, it would have justified building a second curriculum.

## Key Ideas

**One model, one map.** An owner who learns two vocabularies for the same business learns
neither. The pillars gave beginners a starting vocabulary; Four Engines gives them a
diagnostic. Only one of those is worth teaching.

**The real question the pillars were answering was pacing, not structure.** A brand-new
owner freezes when shown the whole business at once. That problem is real and it survives —
but the answer is [[Five Laws|One Step Wins]] and Season-sequenced teaching, not a second
framework. *"How do you eat an elephant? One bite at a time"* is still good; it just
doesn't need five pillars to say it.

**Finances was never a peer.** Even inside the five-pillar course, Finances is taught as
the scoreboard for the other four. That is the Four Engines position exactly.

## Examples

**Keeley** — brand-new owner, strong on people skills, new to the business side (*"CRM — I
still don't even know what that means"*). Given the pillars plus a concrete homework
assignment rather than a general "learn the business." The thing that helped her was the
pacing and the specific assignment, not the taxonomy.

**Courtney, as the cautionary comparison** — four years essentially static before deciding
to grow three months prior to that call; then breaking revenue records monthly. Cited as
what happens when overwhelm is left unaddressed rather than broken into pieces.
