# Word Tracks

> [!abstract] In one line
> Knowing your exact line frees you to actually listen, instead of scrambling for what to say next.

## Definition

A word track is the exact sentence you say when a specific objection comes up. Not a
general approach — the literal words, known well enough that you don't have to assemble
them on the call.

## Why It Matters

> "There's a difference between me trying to put the words together versus I know that
> this is what I'm going to say."

When you're building a response mid-sentence, all your attention is on you. You're
thinking *what am I going to say, what am I going to say.* You can't hear the person.

When the line is automatic, attention goes back to them. You hear their tone, their
personality, what they're actually worried about — and you adjust the delivery of a line
you already know.

**The word track doesn't make you robotic. It's what makes you present.**

## Symptoms

- You feel anxious going into the objection part of the call
- You "feel out" each person and improvise
- You can't say what you'd reply to "let me think about it" without thinking first
- Deals die at the same moment every time and you can't say why

## Common Mistakes

- Improvising every objection and calling it "being natural"
- Having an approach but not a sentence
- Learning the words but not rehearsing them enough to stop thinking about them

## Models

Same words, adjusted delivery, per personality:

| Person | Delivery |
|---|---|
| Type A, wants the point | Direct, quick. *"Absolutely. What's the main concern you need to think about?"* |
| Chatty, warm | Softer. *"Absolutely, no worries. Of course you want to think about it. I don't want you to feel any pressure at all. I'm just curious — what's the main concern you need to think about?"* |

## Checklist

- [ ] Write the exact sentence for each objection you lose to
- [ ] Say it out loud until you don't have to think about it
- [ ] Run a call and notice whether you can hear them better

## FAQ

**Q:** Doesn't a script make me sound fake?
**A:** The opposite. Scrambling for words is what sounds fake — you're visibly not
listening. The line is what buys you the attention to be present.
