# Drop Scope Not Price

> [!abstract] In one line
> Meet their budget by cleaning less, never by charging less per hour.

## Definition

When a prospect genuinely can't afford the quote, you reduce what's included rather than
reducing your rate. Their total comes down. Your rate per hour doesn't move.

## Why It Matters

A discount permanently resets what that client expects to pay, and it drags your average
ticket down — one of the four numbers in [[Marketing Math]]. Reduced scope keeps the rate
intact while still solving their problem.

It also keeps the conversation honest. You're not pretending the work is worth less; you
are doing less work.

## Symptoms

- "That's more than I was hoping to spend"
- You're about to knock $40 off to save the booking
- Your effective hourly rate varies wildly by client

## Common Mistakes

- Cutting the rate to win the job
- Cutting scope without saying what's being cut — leads to disappointment and a bad review
- Confusing this with [[Not Every Client Is Your Client]]: if the gap is a whole league,
  reduced scope won't bridge it either

## Models

> "We can focus on bathrooms, kitchen, and high-touch areas. We skip the unused rooms.
> That brings it to a price that works for you."

Cut the rooms nobody uses. Keep the rooms that make the house feel clean.

**The only discount that exists:** $10–15 off the first clean in exchange for a review.
Small, rare, and used at the finish line — when someone is about to cross and wants to
feel like they got something.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm it's a real budget constraint, not a league gap
- [ ] Name exactly which areas you'll cover
- [ ] Name exactly what you're skipping — out loud, on the call
- [ ] Hold the rate
- [ ] Document the reduced scope so the cleaner doesn't over-deliver into a loss

## Templates

> not using. That brings it to a price that works for you."

## FAQ

**Q:** Doesn't this just look like a discount to them?
**A:** No — they can see what they're not getting. That's the difference. A discount says
the work was overpriced; reduced scope says less work costs less.
