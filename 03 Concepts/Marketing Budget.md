# Marketing Budget

> [!abstract] In one line
> About 10% of revenue — but only after you can convert the leads and keep the clients.

## Definition

How much to spend on marketing, and the two conditions that have to be true before
spending more is a good idea.

## Why It Matters

There's no universal number.

> "I can't give you an exact number. What I have seen across the industry as a recommended
> amount is about 10% of your revenue."

Below a certain size that percentage is meaningless, because 10% of very little is
nothing. **When you're small, marketing is an investment rather than a percentage.**

## The Two Gates

Before spending aggressively, both must be true:

1. **Your sales process is dialled in.** Can you call immediately, follow up consistently,
   and convert? See [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]] and [[Word Tracks]].
2. **Your cleaning team can deliver and retain.** Buying leads you'll disappoint is worse
   than buying none — you pay for the acquisition *and* the bad review.

> "I would not be spending money on marketing to get leads if you do not have your sales
> process dialed in."

Alex and Elijah spent hard early **because they came from sales and marketing.** That's
the reason, and it's not transferable by default.

## The Ranking It Implies

> "The hardest thing to nail down in the cleaning business is the marketing and sales. It
> is not that hard to find cleaners, use systems, find a VA, or handle logistics compared
> to marketing and sales."

Worth sitting with — it's the argument for why [[Leads Engine|Leads]] is the most common
constraint.

## Symptoms

- Asking for a budget number without knowing your close rate
- Spending more because growth feels slow
- Leads arriving faster than you can call them

## Common Mistakes

- Spending before either gate is met
- Copying Alex's early aggression without his sales background
- Using a credit card without a payoff plan

## Models

**Credit card ad spend.** Alex recommends it *with a specific condition*:

> "If you don't have 0% APR, then it's like you're renting money."

At 0% it's access to marketing dollars now, repaid from the clients they produce. Without
0%, the marketing has to be profitable enough to cover the borrowing too.

**His own sequence:** ~$4,000 on a card in month one against ~$6,000 in cleans.
$2,000–3,000 in month two. Then under $1,000/month for several months while retained
clients paid the cards down. See [[Month One Loses Money]].

## Checklist

- [ ] Start from ~10% of revenue as a reference, not a rule
- [ ] Gate 1: can you convert? Check your close rate
- [ ] Gate 2: can you deliver and retain?
- [ ] If borrowing, confirm 0% APR
- [ ] Have a payoff plan from retained clients
- [ ] Re-check against [[Marketing Math]] before increasing

## FAQ

**Q:** Is putting ad spend on a credit card safe advice?
**A:** It's what Alex did, and he's specific about the conditions: 0% APR, a proven sales
process, and profitable marketing. Outside those conditions it's borrowing to fund losses.
This is a real financial risk and the page states it as his practice, not a recommendation
for every situation.
