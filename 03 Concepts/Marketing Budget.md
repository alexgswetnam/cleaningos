# Marketing Budget

> [!abstract] In one line
> Spend is a function of how aggressive you're being and what stage you're at — not a fixed
> percentage of revenue.

## Definition

How much to spend on marketing, and the two conditions that have to be true before
spending more is a good idea.

## Why It Matters

**There's no fixed ratio, and Alex has explicitly ruled against teaching one.** The
governing variable is aggression and stage, not a percentage:

> Alex, 2026-08-07: *"the higher percent the more aggressive. its normal to go very high in
> the beginning, this number is arbitrary and changes massively. don't even make it a
> teaching point honestly, i'm not considering this really."*

Earlier material had floated **~10% of revenue** as an industry rule of thumb — both from
Alex directly ("I can't give you an exact number... about 10% of your revenue") and
independently from `Cleaning Biz 101 — Acquire` ("a healthy cleaning business usually needs
to spend at least 10% of gross revenue... if you want consistent growth"). **That figure is
kept here as historical record, not as teaching** — Alex has since called it arbitrary and
asked that it not be used as a benchmark. See Conflict History.

Below a certain size any percentage is meaningless anyway, because a percent of very little
is nothing. **When you're small, marketing is an investment tied to aggression and stage,
not a ratio of revenue.**

## The Two Gates

Before spending aggressively, both must be true:

1. **Your sales process is dialled in.** Can you call immediately, follow up consistently,
   and convert? See [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]] and [[Word Tracks]].
2. **Your cleaning team can deliver and retain.** Buying leads you'll disappoint is worse
   than buying none — you pay for the acquisition *and* the bad review.

> "I would not be spending money on marketing to get leads if you do not have your sales
> process dialed in."

Alex and Elijah spent hard early **because they came from sales and marketing.** That's
the reason, and it's not transferable by default.

## The Ranking It Implies

> "The hardest thing to nail down in the cleaning business is the marketing and sales. It
> is not that hard to find cleaners, use systems, find a VA, or handle logistics compared
> to marketing and sales."

Worth sitting with — it's the argument for why [[Leads Engine|Leads]] is the most common
constraint.

## Symptoms

- Asking for a budget number without knowing your close rate
- Spending more because growth feels slow
- Leads arriving faster than you can call them

## Common Mistakes

- Spending before either gate is met
- Copying Alex's early aggression without his sales background
- Using a credit card without a payoff plan

## Models

**Credit card ad spend.** Alex recommends it *with a specific condition*:

> "If you don't have 0% APR, then it's like you're renting money."

At 0% it's access to marketing dollars now, repaid from the clients they produce. Without
0%, the marketing has to be profitable enough to cover the borrowing too.

**His own sequence:** ~$4,000 on a card in month one against ~$6,000 in cleans.
$2,000–3,000 in month two. Then under $1,000/month for several months while retained
clients paid the cards down. See [[Month One Loses Money]].

## Checklist

- [ ] Set spend by aggression and stage, not a percentage of revenue
- [ ] Gate 1: can you convert? Check your close rate
- [ ] Gate 2: can you deliver and retain?
- [ ] If borrowing, confirm 0% APR
- [ ] Have a payoff plan from retained clients
- [ ] Re-check against [[Marketing Math]] before increasing

## FAQ

**Q:** Is putting ad spend on a credit card safe advice?
**A:** It's what Alex did — **while in personal debt** — and he's specific about the
conditions: 0% APR, a proven sales process, and profitable marketing. Outside those
conditions it's borrowing to fund losses. Even inside them, his own spend didn't convert
as expected because the fulfilment side wasn't ready. This page states it as his practice,
not a recommendation for every situation, and the risk belongs in any retelling.

## Conflict History

**Not a contested claim in the Type 1/2/3 sense — a direct ruling.** The page previously
taught ~10% of revenue as a reference figure. Alex, in passing on 2026-08-07, called that
number arbitrary and said not to teach it as a benchmark at all — see the quote above.
Per `00 Inbox/review-queue.md`'s framing, this was **not** treated as a formal supersession
of a Canon claim (Alex hadn't fully thought it through when he said it), but it settled the
open question of what this page should teach going forward: aggression and stage, not a
ratio. Already applied: removed as a graduation criterion for Leads Scale.
