# Augusta Rule

> [!abstract] In one line
> Rent your own home to your own business for up to 14 days a year, at fair market rate, and that expense is deductible to the business without being taxable income to you.

## Definition

A tax provision letting a business owner rent their personal residence to their own
business for up to 14 days per year — for a genuine business purpose (a team meetup,
client workshop, planning/vision day) — at fair market rent. The business deducts the
rent as an expense; the owner does not have to declare it as personal income on their
1040. Not tax or legal advice — see FAQ.

## Why It Matters

Used correctly, it can turn a profitable month into a paper loss for tax purposes,
directly reducing (or eliminating) that period's tax liability — without changing
anything about how the business actually operates.

## Symptoms

- Owner is paying tax on profit with no fringe/write-off strategy in place
- Owner holds team meetings, planning days, or workshops at a rented office or venue
  when the same event could legitimately happen at their own home

## Common Mistakes

- Overstating the day rate — it has to be genuine fair market rent (what the home could
  actually rent for, e.g. on Airbnb), not an inflated number
- Using it without an actual business purpose for the day (a real meetup, workshop, or
  planning session)
- Treating this page as tax advice rather than a prompt to talk to a CPA

## Models

**The worked example.** A business with $10K income and $7K expenses shows $3K profit.
Using the Augusta Rule: rent the owner's residence for 14 days at a genuine fair-market
rate — in this example, $400/day — for $5,600. That $5,600 becomes a business expense,
taking total expenses to $12.6K against $10K income: the business now shows a loss for
tax purposes, and the owner has a tax credit to carry forward, instead of owing tax on
$3K profit.

**Multi-member LLCs multiply it.** Each owner-member can rent their own residence
separately. A two-member LLC (filing a 1065, not a 1040 — treated as a partnership) can
do this twice, once per member's home.

**The instruction, not the specifics:** *"Use ChatGPT to look for things you can do for
your taxes... and look for the fringe stuff."* Vehicle expenses, cell phone, internet,
and a portion of rent/utilities are named as other write-off categories worth asking
about, alongside the Augusta Rule specifically.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm a genuine business purpose for the rental day (meetup, workshop, planning
      day)
- [ ] Use a real fair-market day rate for your area, not an inflated number
- [ ] Cap at 14 days per year, per residence
- [ ] Multi-member LLC: each member can do this once, for their own residence
- [ ] File correctly (1065 for a multi-member LLC, not 1040)
- [ ] Confirm the approach with a CPA before relying on it
