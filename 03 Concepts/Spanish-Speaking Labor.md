# Spanish-Speaking Labor

> [!abstract] In one line
> Spanish-speaking cleaners can cut labor from ~50% of revenue to 30–35% — but the language gap needs a real bridge, not luck.

## Definition

Hiring Spanish-speaking cleaners as a margin lever, and the two ways owners bridge the
language gap between an English-speaking owner/client base and a Spanish-speaking labor
pool: learning Spanish personally, or using a bilingual VA or team member as a
real-time translator.

## Why It Matters

Labor typically runs about 50% of revenue. Spanish-speaking cleaners in the sources
command $12–$22/hour versus $28–$50+ for English-speaking cleaners in the same market,
which can bring labor down to 30–35% of revenue — a margin swing large enough to matter on
its own, without changing anything else about the business.

## Symptoms

- Labor cost consistently eating close to half of revenue
- Turning away cleaner candidates purely because of a language barrier
- A margin ceiling that isn't explained by pricing or overhead

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming the language gap is unsolvable without a bilingual owner
- Treating this as a mandatory move rather than one option among several for
  margin-constrained businesses
- Not having a defined translation path (owner, VA, or bilingual team member) before
  hiring, leading to communication breakdowns on the job

## Models

**Two ways to bridge the gap:**

1. **Learn Spanish yourself.** A real but slow option — one owner cited in the source
   (Salim, ~$90K/month) learned Spanish from scratch over 18 months specifically to work
   with a Spanish-speaking team.
2. **Use a bilingual VA or team member as a real-time translator.** A Spanish-speaking VA
   sourced from South America (priced somewhat above Philippines-based VAs) can translate
   between clients and cleaners by phone as issues come up. In the source, a bilingual
   subcontractor plays this role directly — when a communication issue arises between a
   Spanish-speaking cleaner and a client, he's called in to translate live.

**This is a margin lever to consider, not a directive.** Presented explicitly as something
for margin-constrained businesses to weigh, not a step every owner needs to take.

## Checklist

- [ ] Compare current labor cost as % of revenue against the 30–35% target this approach
      can reach
- [ ] Decide on a translation path before hiring: owner-learns-Spanish, bilingual VA, or a
      bilingual team member on call
- [ ] Confirm the translation path can respond in real time when an on-the-job issue comes up
