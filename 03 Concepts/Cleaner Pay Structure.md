# Cleaner Pay Structure

> [!abstract] In one line
> Pay per job, not per hour — a percentage of that job's revenue, with tips passed
> straight through and no guaranteed minimum.

## Definition

The compensation model for cleaners: a percentage of each completed job's revenue
(varying by job type, difficulty, and market), rather than an hourly wage. Distinct from
[[Business Finances]], which covers *how fast and cleanly* cleaners get paid out
(banking/Zelle mechanics) — this is *how the pay amount itself gets calculated*. Also
distinct from [[Subcontractor Vs W-2]], the classification decision this pay model has
to stay compliant with.

## Why It Matters

Per-job, percentage-based pay aligns incentive with outcome: faster, better work earns
more, and the owner's costs stay predictable as a percentage of revenue rather than a
fixed hourly liability regardless of job difficulty. Getting this wrong — inconsistent
or unclear pay — is a direct driver of cleaner turnover and disputes.

## Symptoms

- Cleaners frequently ask "why was I paid less this time" with no clear answer
- Pay structure varies job to job with no consistent logic
- Cleaners are unclear whether they're guaranteed work or hours
- Disputes over add-on or scope-change pay

## Common Mistakes

- Switching between hourly and per-job pay inconsistently
- Guaranteeing earnings without a structure that actually supports it
- Ignoring scope changes instead of addressing them with a one-time adjustment
- Not passing add-on revenue increases through to cleaner pay proportionally

## Models

**Base pay:** a percentage of the job's revenue, varying by job type (standard vs. deep
clean), difficulty, and market conditions. This is project-based, not hourly — cleaners
are paid per completed job.

**Scope adjustments:** when a job turns out to be more than expected (extra rooms,
worse-than-expected condition, access/setup delays), the owner can apply a one-time pay
increase for that specific job, at their discretion. Not a standing bonus.

**Add-ons and tips:** add-on services (inside oven, fridge, etc.) increase both the job
price and cleaner pay proportionally. Tips go directly to the cleaner, paid on the normal
schedule.

**Cancellations and edge cases:** same-day cancellations, no-access jobs, and partial
completions are only compensated if the company's written policy allows it — see
[[Payment Verification & Collection]] for the specific $25 en-route/arrived rule.

**Guardrails:** cleaners are not guaranteed jobs, hours, or earnings. They can accept or
decline work. Pay structure has to stay compliant with contractor-vs-employee
classification and local labor law — see [[Subcontractor Vs W-2]].

## The Faithful Cleaners' Actual Numbers

A second, richer source — the company's own cleaner-facing handbook and its signed
subcontractor agreement — gives the real figures behind the abstract model above.

**Base pay:** 40–60% of **Net Job Revenue** (amounts actually collected from the client,
excluding sales tax, processing fees, discounts, refunds, and chargebacks). The handbook
states a target effective rate of $30/hour based on on-site time.

**Timekeeping:** BookingKoala check-in/check-out timestamps verify arrival and departure
and support quality and pay calculations, but both the handbook and the agreement are
explicit that this does **not** convert the pay model to hourly wages.

**Add-ons:** approved add-ons (fridge, oven, interior windows, baseboards, etc.) are
priced and approved before work, and pay the same percentage as the base job.

**Tips:** 100% pass through to the cleaner(s) who performed the job, unless the client
specifies otherwise (e.g. "I'd like to tip the office").

**Re-cleans:** unpaid if caused by workmanship. A 48-hour window to return and correct
the issue (or the client's requested time if later). Refusal or no-show gets the job
reassigned, and the cost of the replacement is offset against the original cleaner's next
payout. Scope changes, client-preference changes on work that met checklist standards,
post-visit new mess, and company-caused errors (scheduling/notes mistakes) are explicitly
**not** re-cleans and are paid normally.

**Payout:** weekly, every Friday, via ACH (or the cleaner's designated method), for the
Pay Week running Saturday–Friday. Cleaners must keep a current W-9 on file. Pay disputes
must be raised within 5 business days.

**1099 status, stated plainly in both documents:** cleaners are independent contractors;
the company does not withhold taxes; cleaners are responsible for their own income and
self-employment taxes.

The rest of this page's Checklist and general model above (percentage pay, add-ons, tips,
guardrails) is not affected by the dispute below — only the specific "is there a floor"
question is contested. See Conflict History.

## Corroborating Detail From Labor 101

A third source — the Labor 101 course — doesn't settle the floor dispute, but adds two
kinds of evidence.

**Corroborates the general percentage model, undisputed.** "Determining Cleaners' Pay"
describes a variable percentage ranging from **35% to 70%** of a job depending on margin
and job type, averaging **~50–55%** across a month — consistent with the 40–60% of Net
Job Revenue already on this page. It also states plainly, in the owner's own words, that
hourly pay is never guaranteed: *"I can't guarantee anything because you're 1099... I
can't guarantee hourly. I can't, you just can't do that legally."* "Labor Basics: Survival
Season Coaching" corroborates the no-pay-for-incomplete-work rule directly: *"She's
getting paid to [complete] a complete job. I'm not paying hourly. I'm paying for you to
complete the job that you said you were going to complete."*

**Adds a third position to the floor dispute itself — see Conflict History below.** The
course's own interview script promises a **$25.00/hr top-up guarantee** using language
that reads as a floor, which is neither the Handbook's $28 nor the Subcontractor
Agreement's $25-non-floor. See Conflict History for the full statement.

## Checklist

- [ ] Base pay set as a percentage of job revenue, not an hourly rate
- [ ] Percentage varies deliberately by job type/difficulty/market, not ad hoc
- [ ] Scope-change adjustments are one-time and discretionary, not a new baseline
- [ ] Add-on pay increases proportionally with add-on price
- [ ] Tips passed through directly, on the normal payout schedule
- [ ] Cancellation/no-access pay follows a written policy, not case-by-case judgment
- [ ] Cleaners get a job-level earnings breakdown, not just a lump payout
- [ ] Structure checked against contractor/employee classification rules

## Conflict History

> [!warning] Contested — is there an earnings floor or not?
> **Claim A (the cleaner-facing handbook):** describes a **Minimum Earnings Adjustment
> (MEA)** as an actual floor: *"if your Effective Hourly ... is < $28.00, we add a
> one-time MEA top-up so your Effective Hourly equals $28.00 (capped at 35 Verified
> Hours/week)."* Read on its own, this sounds like a guaranteed hourly minimum for
> qualifying weeks.
>
> **Claim B (the signed Subcontractor Agreement, Exhibit A):** describes a **Discretionary
> Project Supplement (DPS)** using a **Reference Effective Rate (RER) ≈ $25.00/hour** as
> a benchmark for evaluating whether one specific Job was mis-scoped — and states outright:
> *"Not hourly wages, not a weekly floor, not an employment benefit... Not automatic —
> each Job is reviewed on its facts. No averaging across multiple Jobs."*
>
> These aren't just two names for the same mechanism: they name **different dollar
> figures** ($28 vs. $25), and one is written as an earnings floor while the other is
> written specifically to *deny* being one. Both documents are from the same company
> (The Faithful Cleaners) and neither is dated relative to the other.
>
> **Why this matters beyond wording:** worker-classification tests (see
> [[Subcontractor Vs W-2]]) weigh whether a worker has a guaranteed minimum income —
> that's an employee-like signal. A cleaner-facing document that promises a weekly
> earnings floor, even a modest one, sits in tension with a legal agreement built
> specifically to avoid creating that floor. This vault takes no position on which
> document is legally controlling — that's outside what a concept page should rule on.
>
> **Unresolved since:** 2026-08-06. **What would settle it:** ask Alex which document is
> the current, operative one, and whether the handbook's MEA language has been
> reconciled with counsel against the agreement's DPS language.

> [!failure]- Third position found — Labor 101, 2026-08-06 — does not settle, deepens the dispute
> Reading Labor 101 for this ingestion surfaced a third figure/framing, live in the
> company's own hiring materials, that matches neither existing claim cleanly:
>
> **Claim C (Labor 101 interview script, "How to Conduct an Interview"):** *"We don't pay
> hourly - we pay per job. However, we do have a unique payment structure to help you get
> at least $25/hr. This means that if your pay breaks down to less than 25/hr for the
> time you spent in the home, **we'll pay the difference to make sure you got at least
> that much.** This is outlined further in the subcontractor agreement."* This is said to
> every cleaner candidate, in the actual hiring script. The dollar figure ($25) matches
> Claim B's Reference Effective Rate — but the language ("we'll pay the difference to
> make sure you got at least that much") is unambiguous floor language, structurally
> identical to Claim A's MEA promise, not Claim B's explicit "not a weekly floor" denial.
>
> **This is not just Claim A vs. Claim B with a different number — it's internally split
> within the same course.** "Determining Cleaners' Pay," a coaching lesson in the same
> course, has the owner saying the opposite about the same $25 figure: *"I want you making
> at least $25 an hour... but I'm not guaranteeing anything... I can't guarantee hourly."*
> Same course, same $25 number, one place framed as a guarantee spoken to candidates in
> the actual interview, another framed explicitly as non-guaranteed in coaching. A separate
> real interview call (Cleaner Interview Example, "Michelle") shows a third framing again:
> *"We base our flat rate off of 25 an hour"* — a pricing input, not a promise of any kind,
> with no top-up language at all.
>
> **Reading across all three sources now on this page:** the $25 figure appears
> consistently (Subcontractor Agreement RER, coaching lesson target, flat-rate pricing
> basis) — but whether $25 is ever actually *guaranteed* to a cleaner varies by which
> company document or script is speaking, independent of the $28 MEA question entirely.
> This does not settle Claim A vs. Claim B. It shows the "is there a floor" question isn't
> a two-document disagreement — it's inconsistent across at least four company artifacts
> (Handbook, Subcontractor Agreement, interview script, coaching call), none of which is
> dated relative to the others.
>
> **Not a Type 1 supersession:** none of the four sources carries a date establishing it
> as newer than the others, and per Constitution §X, undated material defaults to
> Contested rather than superseding. **Not a genuine third *position*** in the sense of a
> clean alternative claim — it's evidence that the dispute is worse than two documents
> disagreeing once. Filing this as a deepening of the existing Type 3 contested item, not
> a new classification.
>
> **What would settle it, updated:** in addition to the original ask (which document
> governs), also ask whether the interview script's "$25/hr, we'll pay the difference"
> line is something candidates are actually told verbatim today, and if so, whether that
> makes the $25 figure a de facto floor regardless of what the Subcontractor Agreement's
> Exhibit A says on paper.
