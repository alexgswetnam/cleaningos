# Building Connection

> [!abstract] In one line
> The first two minutes of a call are for a real question about them, not silence while you pull up your screen.

## Definition

Deliberately using the opening of a sales call — before any specs, before any diagnosis
question — to ask something that makes the prospect feel like a person rather than a
transaction. Distinct from [[Mirroring]], which is about matching *how* you deliver
whatever you say; this is about *what you choose to say first*, before you know enough
about the prospect to mirror anything.

## Why It Matters

Most reps waste the first two minutes on dead air or a throwaway "how's your day going,"
while they pull up their screen. That time is free — the call hasn't started producing
information yet — and it's the highest-leverage moment to build the trust that makes
everything after it easier.

> "You're not wasting time — you're building the connection that makes the sale easier."

A prospect who feels like a person by minute two is a different call than one who's been
handed a form disguised as a conversation.

## Symptoms

- Calls open with "how can I help you today" or straight into specs
- You fill the pull-up-the-screen silence with nothing, or with small talk you didn't
  choose on purpose
- Rapport-building happens accidentally, if at all, rather than as a deliberate first move
- Prospects sound guarded through the whole call, not just the beginning

## Common Mistakes

- Treating "how's your day going" as sufficient — it's real but shallow, and doesn't open
  into anything
- Skipping the opening question because your screen is already pulled up — the delay is
  worth taking even when you don't need it
- Asking the connection question and then not listening to the answer before moving to specs

## Models

**The loaded question that opens further than small talk.** Ask where they're located,
then:

> "How long have you been around there?"

If they've been there a while, it opens into something they're likely to have an opinion
about: *"So you've seen a lot of change in the area, huh?"* If they're newer:
*"Oh, so you're kind of new around here. What made you move?"* Either branch produces
something personal, not a status update.

**Use the pull-up-your-screen moment on purpose, even when you don't need it.**

> "Give me one second here. Let me pull this up. How's your day going?"

The pause reads as normal admin time either way — the difference is whether you fill it
with a real question or nothing.

**Elijah's version, used almost every call:** "How long have you been around?" — named
independently as his go-to opener, for the same reason: it reliably crosses into personal
territory without feeling invasive.

## Checklist

- [ ] Open with a question about them, not the job
- [ ] "How long have you been around [area]?" as the default opener
- [ ] Follow whichever branch their answer opens — change, or what brought them there
- [ ] If you need the screen-pull-up pause, fill it with the question, not silence
- [ ] Listen to the answer before moving to specs — don't ask and steamroll past it

## Templates

> "How long have you been around there?"
>
> "So you've seen a lot of change in the area, huh?"
>
> "Oh, so you're kind of new around here. What made you move?"
>
> "Give me one second here, let me pull this up. How's your day going?"

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't this the same as [[Diagnose Don't Take Orders]]'s opening question?
**A:** No — that page's opener ("what's going on?") is diagnostic and about the job. This
page's opener is personal and has nothing to do with the clean. Both come before specs;
they serve different purposes and can both be used, in this order: connection question
first, diagnostic question second.
