# Backup Cleaner

> [!abstract] In one line
> Even with no growth plans, one cleaner leaving should not be able to take you down.

## Definition

At least one more cleaner than the schedule strictly requires, working alongside the
team, so a departure or a sick day is absorbed rather than catastrophic. At scale, this
means a full roster with backups for the backups, plus standing relationships with other
local cleaning companies who can be called in an emergency — not just a second employee.

## Why It Matters

Alex, to Courtney: *"if one of your girls just had to go — something happened, she had to
move — you're in big trouble. Even if you weren't planning to scale, you still would want
to have at least one more kind of backup working alongside people in case someone left or
two people left."* It also underwrites every high-pressure job — see [[Airbnb Clients]].

A real revenue example makes the cost concrete: in January, two cleaners got sick in the
first week. The business should have done roughly **$30K** that month and didn't, because
even though other cleaners on the roster had capacity, many affected clients specifically
wanted *their* assigned cleaner and simply moved the appointment to the following week
instead of accepting a substitute. One cleaner is not a labor system. Two is still risky.
A roster — with backups for the backups — is what actually protects revenue.

## Symptoms

- One or two cleaners carry the whole schedule
- A single callout forces rescheduling clients rather than absorbing the gap
- No relationships exist with other local cleaning companies before an emergency hits
- Clients who prefer a specific cleaner have no acceptable fallback when that cleaner is
  out

## Common Mistakes

- Staffing exactly to current demand
- Treating backup capacity as a growth expense rather than insurance
- Waiting until a cleaner calls out to start looking for backup coverage
- Protecting margin over client service in a genuine emergency — turning down a
  low-margin emergency job costs more in trust than the margin is worth
- Assuming every client will happily accept a substitute cleaner — many won't, and that's
  a planning input, not a failure

## Models

**Backups for backups, not just a second cleaner.** The roster needs enough depth that
losing any single cleaner — or two at once — doesn't create bookings the business can't
fulfill. A two-cleaner "backup" is still a thin roster if both can plausibly be out at
the same time (illness spreads, life happens in clusters).

**Build relationships with other small cleaning companies before you need them.**
Reaching out to nearby cleaning companies with an explicit "we want backups, and you can
use us too" framing creates a standing emergency-coverage option. When an assigned
cleaner calls out right before a move-out, calling one of these relationships and
accepting a lower margin (10–20% instead of the usual cut) to get the client serviced is
the correct trade — the point is the client experience, not that specific job's margin.

**Recurring clients need their own version of redundancy planning.** Because some clients
will decline a substitute cleaner and simply reschedule, backup capacity has to account
for lost bookings on the affected clients' preferred days, not just for the raw hours a
sick cleaner would have worked.

**Backup needs change by season** (see [[Labor Engine]] for the fuller season breakdown):
in Survival, backup means one or two reliable extra helpers, or the owner still cleaning
personally when needed; in Stability, it's formalized through onboarding checklists and a
Client Experience Guide so a substitute cleaner can step in without chaos; in Scale, it's
team leads plus real bench depth; by Harvest, it's continuous onboarding of new cleaners
every month specifically to keep the roster deep, not just reactive hiring when someone
leaves.

## Checklist

- [ ] List current cleaner coverage and identify what breaks if each one is out
- [ ] Maintain more cleaners than the schedule strictly requires, not exactly enough
- [ ] Build relationships with 2+ other local cleaning companies before an emergency
- [ ] Have a plan for clients who won't accept a substitute cleaner
- [ ] Accept lower margin on emergency subcontracted jobs to protect the client
      relationship
- [ ] Revisit backup depth every time the business adds recurring volume
