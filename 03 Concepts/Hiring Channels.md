# Hiring Channels

> [!abstract] In one line
> Boosted Indeed post, Google Maps cold calls, and a local Facebook group post — then interview in bulk.

## Definition

Where cleaner applicants actually come from, and how to process them without spreading interviews across weeks.

## Why It Matters

Hiring is the slow half of [[Labor Before Leads]]. Compressing it into one bulk interview day is what makes hiring-first practical.

## Common Mistakes

- Interviewing one candidate at a time across several weeks
- Posting without sponsoring and waiting

## Models

**Cold calling off Google Maps, live-demoed on a 2026-02-20 call.** Search Google Maps
for small cleaning companies (few reviews) in your area, and call them directly — you're
not selling a job, you're telling them you have more work than you can handle:

> "Hi there, do you do cleanings? Awesome. I'm in the [area] area. I've got a cleaning
> business and I have too many cleans. So I'm looking for other cleaners — would you be
> interested? Do you know anyone else? We need cleaners really bad."

When they ask about pay, answer with a real number rather than a percentage in the
abstract:

> "We give a percentage of the clean. Last week, three of our cleaners made more than $40
> an hour because they're just good cleaners. I'm happy to have you talk to them."

Close for the interview, not the job:

> "What we do from here is set up a time, like an interview. I don't want to take 30
> minutes right now, but interviews — you can ask questions, I can give full information.
> We really need more cleaners because we have so much work. Can we book a time?"

Text to confirm the time. That's the whole call — simple, human, direct.

**When Indeed alone isn't producing enough fit, widen the pipeline rather than lowering
the bar.** Post on Facebook (adapt an existing hiring ad) and Nextdoor with a short,
casual pitch:

> "We pay at least [X] per hour-ish. If you're a cleaner, just reach out. We're super
> slammed. We just really need help."

**Follow up with applicants who never responded — they're not dead leads.** A short mass
blast to non-responders, nothing elaborate:

> "Have you given up on this?"

The best candidate may have meant to reply and life happened; this is the hiring
equivalent of [[Reactivating Past Clients|calling an old lead]] — the same principle,
applied one stage earlier in the funnel.

**Pair a weaker hire with a strong lead cleaner instead of passing on them.** Personality
and customer-facing fit matter more than cleaning skill, which can be taught — but a
candidate who's good, quiet, and gets the job done without being especially client-facing
doesn't have to be sent solo. Pair them with a strong lead cleaner: the lead handles
client interaction, the support cleaner handles the work. This widens who's hireable
without lowering quality on the client-facing side.

**When you're in a genuine labor crisis, multiply interview volume, don't just add a
channel.** A member with only 10 interviews in two weeks, personally cleaning because
there wasn't enough staff, was told directly: *"3x that and you're good. If you want to be
certain, 10x it."* At that severity ("mission critical"), volume itself is the fix — more
channels, more posts, more calls, until the funnel is wide enough to guarantee fit, not
just hope for it.

**Every applicant who will get on the phone is worth the call, even a bad fit.** Someone
who isn't right for the role personally might still know someone who is — treat a phone
conversation with a non-fit candidate as a referral opportunity, not a wasted call.

**Indeed, boosted.** A plain post gets little traction; boosting it with roughly $100 does.
Posted and boosted, ~80 applicants arrived within two days, with several qualified
candidates — two were hired from that single batch. The exact post copy is in the Labor
Fundamentals module.

## Checklist

- [ ] Search Google Maps for small cleaning companies (few reviews) in your area
- [ ] Call directly — don't message first
- [ ] Open with the pitch: too many cleans, looking for cleaners, do they know anyone else
- [ ] If asked about pay, give a real number ("three cleaners made $40+/hour last week")
- [ ] Close for an interview time, not a job offer
- [ ] Text to confirm
- [ ] It's a numbers game — talk to 10, find 1; talk to 50, find 3–4
- [ ] Boost the Indeed post (~$100) rather than posting unsponsored
- [ ] Expand past Indeed to Facebook and Nextdoor when volume/fit is still short
- [ ] Blast non-responders: "Have you given up on this?"
- [ ] Pair a quiet-but-competent hire with a strong lead cleaner instead of passing
- [ ] In a genuine labor crisis, multiply interview volume 3–10x rather than tweaking one channel
- [ ] Take a call from every applicant who'll talk, even an obvious non-fit — they may know someone

## Student Examples

**Alex and Elijah** — hired three cleaners off a sponsored Indeed post plus a local Facebook group post. Elijah scheduled seven or eight interviews in a single day, got three strong candidates, expected two to work out.

**Courtney** — planned to copy the method Rashaan shared.

**Courtney, 2026-04-20** — four to five interviews a day on Indeed alone, still short on
fit, since the gap was customer service/personality rather than cleaning skill. Widened to
Facebook, Nextdoor, and a non-responder blast to increase pipeline volume rather than
loosen standards.

**Courtney, 2026-04-27** — hired a new cleaner on the spot after a great attitude carried
her through a move-in/move-out clean that went wrong in every other way; still
interviewing for at least two more hires to build a comfortable bench.

**William, 2026-04-27** — lost key cleaners who took clients with them (non-compete
agreements exist but enforcement was judged impractical). Only 10 interviews in two weeks
while personally cleaning out of necessity — the "mission critical" case behind the 3–10x
volume guidance above.

**Alex, second confirmation.** Found a cleaner off the Google Maps method in under 10
minutes on a live call — an independent repeat of the same result reported on the
2026-02-20 call. Some of these one-call finds became recurring cleaners.

**Indeed boosted post, ~$100 spend.** ~80 applicants in two days, several qualified,
two hired from the batch.

**The full channel list, from the workshop:** Indeed · Housekeeper.com · Care.com ·
Facebook groups · Facebook Marketplace · Nextdoor · Craigslist · **Google Maps** (call
small cleaning companies with few reviews).

**It's a numbers game.** Talk to 10 → find 1 good one. Talk to 50 → find 3–4.

**Set expectations up front:** the first two weeks are a test period and the schedule
ramps. Rick frames it the same way — *"it's a slow start, you have to earn our trust."*

**Courtney's second round:** an in-the-field interview alongside an existing cleaner. The
team gets a say in who joins. See [[Testing A New Cleaner]].
