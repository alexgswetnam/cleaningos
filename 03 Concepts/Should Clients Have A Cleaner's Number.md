# Should Clients Have A Cleaner's Number

> [!abstract] In one line
> No — the business stays the single point of contact between client and cleaner in
> nearly every case, because direct contact opens the door to poaching, not because it's
> a communication nicety.

## Definition

The default policy that clients don't get a cleaner's direct phone number, and cleaners
don't get a client's — all communication routes through the business. Rare exceptions
exist and are tolerated, not designed around. Distinct from [[Internal Communication Via Discord]] (how cleaners communicate with the *business*) — this is specifically about the
client-cleaner boundary.

## Why It Matters

The client is doing business with the company, not with an individual contractor — the
company needs to remain the point of contact for scheduling, preferences, complaints, and
expectations. Beyond communication control, the concrete risk is poaching: direct contact
between a client and a cleaner creates the opening for a side arrangement that cuts the
business out entirely. In this business's own experience, only about 1–2% of all cleans
ever involve any direct client-cleaner contact — evidence that the exception can stay rare
without hurting service.

## Symptoms

- A recurring client starts messaging the assigned cleaner directly instead of the
  business
- The business loses visibility into a client's concerns because they were raised
  directly to the cleaner and not relayed
- A cleaner has a client's contact information without a clear, rare-exception reason

## Common Mistakes

- Letting direct contact become the normal workflow because it's more convenient in the
  moment
- Assuming an exception, once made, should become the standing default for that client
- Ignoring the poaching risk because a cleaner "seems trustworthy"
- Not proactively redirecting a client back to the business channel when they do reach
  out directly

## Models

**Default: the business is the sole communicator.** Scheduling, complaints, preferences,
and updates all route through the company — never directly between client and cleaner as
the standard operating pattern.

**Rare, tolerated exceptions exist — mostly for finicky, single-cleaner recurring
clients.** A client who only accepts one specific cleaner, on a tight recurring schedule,
and who isn't always home, is the realistic case where some direct contact happens
despite repeated redirection. Even then, the expectation is that the cleaner relays
anything relevant back to the business — the exception doesn't remove the business from
the loop, it just means the client sometimes messages the wrong person first.

**Why this matters beyond convenience — poaching.** Direct contact creates the structural
opening for a client and cleaner to arrange service privately, cutting the business out.
This is the primary reason the boundary is enforced by default rather than left to
convenience, not because direct contact is inherently unprofessional.

**Good cleaners generally don't want this responsibility anyway.** Strong cleaners tend to
prefer just doing the work well and being paid fairly, without taking on the business
owner's communication and relationship-management responsibilities — which is itself a
mild, informal check against the exception spreading.

## Checklist

- [ ] Client and cleaner phone numbers are not shared with each other by default
- [ ] All scheduling, complaints, and preference changes route through the business
- [ ] If direct contact happens (rare exception), the cleaner relays it back to the
      business
- [ ] Continue redirecting a client back to the business channel even after repeated
      instances of them reaching out directly
- [ ] Treat any pattern of direct contact as a poaching-risk signal worth monitoring
