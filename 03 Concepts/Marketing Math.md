# Marketing Math

> [!abstract] In one line
> Four numbers turn marketing spend from a gamble into an equation.

## Definition

**Cost per lead · Close rate · Cost per acquisition · Average ticket.**

```
cost per lead  = spend ÷ leads
close rate     = bookings ÷ leads
CPA            = cost per lead ÷ close rate
```

Together with your average ticket, these tell you whether marketing is profitable — and
if it isn't, which of the four is the problem.

## Why It Matters

> "It should be an equation for you to spend money on marketing. It should not be
> guesswork. Most people, it's guesswork."

This is [[Five Laws|Stop Guessing]] applied to the [[Leads Engine]]. Without these numbers
you cannot tell whether you have a marketing problem, a sales problem, or a pricing
problem — see [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]].

And Alex can't help you either: *"I don't know how to help you if you don't know what's
going on in your business."*

## Symptoms

- You can't state your cost per lead or close rate
- You've been at the same revenue for months and can't say why
- You decide ad spend by feel
- You think "marketing is broken" without knowing which number is broken

## Common Mistakes

- Not tracking at all
- Tracking overall instead of per channel — [[Close Rate By Channel]] varies enormously
- Blaming the channel when the problem is the close rate
- Classifying leads as "organic" when you don't actually know the source

## Models

**Worked example — all three numbers bad:**

$55 cost per lead × 25% close rate = **$220 CPA**. Average ticket $220. Labor takes 50%,
overhead 20%. Nothing left. Cost per lead is too high, close rate is too low, *and* the
ticket is too low.

**Worked example — only one number bad:**

$40 cost per lead × 20% close rate = **$200 CPA**. Average ticket $300 at 35% profit =
$105. Losing money per client. Diagnosis: it's not the channel — $40 is fine. Fix the
close rate to 50% and CPA drops to $80, which is breakeven on clean one, and then
[[Client Lifetime Value|recurring]] makes it profitable.

**Close rate alone can swing cost-per-booking dramatically at a fixed lead cost.**
`Cleaning Biz 101 — Convert` makes this vivid with one lead cost held constant at $40:

| Close rate | Cost per booking |
|---|---|
| 10% | $400 |
| 30% | $133 |
| 60% | $66 |

Nothing about the marketing changed between these rows — only the close rate. This is the
same [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]] diagnostic already on this page, restated as a
single clean table: when CPA looks bad, check close rate before touching spend. The
source's own VA hit a 60% close rate in December, which is the top row realized.

**Why cleaning is harder than high-ticket.** Roofing: $20K ticket, $100 cost per lead, 5%
close = $2,000 CPA, ~$8K net per client. You can be bad at marketing and still win. In
cleaning the ticket is $200–$400, so margins are thin and the marketing has to be tight.
**That's why most owners under $20K/month are stuck.**

## Checklist

- [ ] Log every lead with source, status, outcome ([[KPI Tracking Sheet]])
- [ ] Cost per lead, per channel
- [ ] Close rate, per channel
- [ ] CPA, per channel
- [ ] Know your average ticket and profit per clean
- [ ] If CPA > profit per clean, identify which of the four is off
- [ ] Fix that one thing
- [ ] Hold the numbers for 2–3 months, then scale spend

## Student Examples

**Alex's Q4:** $3,000 spend, 245 leads, **$12 cost per lead across everything** — because
about half the leads were free. Thumbtack: 90 leads (51 free), $15.64 CPL, 26% close, $58
CPA. PPC: $1,500 spend, $43 CPL, 26% close, $100 CPA. Organic: 119 leads, 70 bookings, $0.

That organic half is what drags the average down — see [[Channel Prioritization]].

**A December figure from a separate resource, likely not the same measure.** A different
source states December's overall **qualified close rate at 60%**, with Thumbtack at
$17.88 CPL / $40 CPA and Google Ads (PPC) at $35.31 CPL / $98 CPA — average ticket $377.
The per-channel CPL/CPA figures are close to (not identical to) the Q4 numbers above, but
the 60% figure is far above the 26% channel-level close rates recorded elsewhere. Not
flagged as a hard contradiction: "qualified close rate" explicitly excludes unqualified
leads from the denominator (see [[KPI Tracking Sheet]]'s lead-analytics breakdown), so a
blended, unqualified-leads-excluded rate reading meaningfully higher than a single
channel's raw close rate is plausible rather than inconsistent. Worth reconciling with an
exact date and matching methodology if it comes up again.

**A member stuck at $9K/month for a year** couldn't state his cost per lead or close rate.
There was no way to diagnose him.

## FAQ

**Q:** How long before I trust the numbers?
**A:** Two to three months of consistent tracking. Then increase spend as an equation.

## Conflict History

> [!warning] Type 2 — same metric, different value, same quarter
> `Cleaning Biz 101 — Acquire` (course lesson "The Best Paid Marketing Methods") states
> PPC's **2025 Q4** close rate as **41%** ($43 CPL → ~$104 CPA). The worked example above,
> from the 2025-02-03 coaching call, states Q4 PPC as **26% close** ($43 CPL → $100 CPA).
> The cost-per-lead figures agree almost exactly ($43 both) and the CPA figures are close
> ($100 vs $104), but the close-rate figures don't (26% vs 41%) — and a close-rate
> difference of that size at the same CPL should move CPA much further apart than it
> actually did, which is itself odd. Both sources now agree the quarter is **2025 Q4**
> (the course lesson is explicit about it; the coaching call previously wasn't dated to a
> specific year). Not resolved — could be a typo in either source, a mid-quarter change,
> or two different measurement windows within the same quarter. Flagged per the
> Contradiction Protocol rather than silently picking one number.
