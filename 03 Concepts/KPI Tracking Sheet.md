# KPI Tracking Sheet

> [!abstract] In one line
> The spreadsheet that makes every other number in the Leads Engine possible.

## Definition

Free resource in Skool: **Classroom → Free Resources → Sales and Marketing KPI Tracking Sheet.** Logs every lead by source, status, and outcome, and produces cost per lead, close rate, and CPA per channel.

## Why It Matters

It's the precondition for [[Marketing Math]], [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]], and [[Channel Prioritization]]. Alex, plainly: *"I don't know how to help you if you don't know what's going on in your business."*

## Common Mistakes

- Not using it
- Logging bookings but not leads
- Not recording the source
- Refusing to do it yourself and not paying anyone else to either — the gap doesn't close
  itself, it just gets more expensive to fix later

## Models

Alex walked several members through their own sheets live on this call, which is how Rick's $55 cost per lead and Courtney's 67% close rate got diagnosed.

**The sheet's structure, tab by tab — answers the "not in the vault" question this page
previously flagged.**

- **Leads tab.** One row per lead, logged daily (weekly logging lets leads slip through).
  Columns: name, date created, source (LSA, Thumbtack, referral, etc.), cost (only if
  known per-lead — e.g. Thumbtack; leave blank for flat-monthly channels like PPC, see
  below), who took the call, and **outcome**: No Contact, Follow Up (in conversation, no
  yes/no yet), Booked, Declined (said no explicitly), Unqualified (wrong-fit request, not
  a price objection — e.g. someone calling a house-cleaning line for carpet cleaning).
  Recurring/cancellation status, review status, and cancellation date get updated on the
  same row as they happen. Notes are sales notes only — why they booked or didn't — not
  scheduling notes.
- **Transactions tab.** One row per cleaner per completed job: pay date, cleaner, client,
  hours worked, pay amount, tips, and an adjustment note (only filled when pay was
  changed — e.g. reimbursed gas). This is what auto-populates the **Pay Stub** tab per
  cleaner per pay period — see [[Business Finances]] for the weekly cleaner-pay process
  this tab supports.
- **Lead Analytics tab (auto-populated from the Leads tab).** Per person and per month:
  leads taken, booked, close rate, and **qualified close rate** (excludes Unqualified
  leads from the denominator — a materially higher number than raw close rate, since a
  wrong-fit inquiry was never going to close regardless of sales skill). Per channel:
  cost per lead, cost per qualified lead, cost per acquisition — using the Leads tab's
  per-lead cost where available (Thumbtack), or a separate **manual spend** entry for
  flat-cost channels (PPC/Google Ads) where you log total period spend rather than a
  per-lead figure, and the sheet divides it across that period's leads from that source.
- **Referral tab.** Present, described as rarely used in practice — optional.

**How to read it once it's populated:** a low cost-per-lead with a low close rate isn't
automatically a sales problem — check the channel first. A 10% close rate is roughly
normal for Facebook leads; the same 10% on Google/organic leads signals a real sales
problem, because those leads should close much higher (people who found you and called
in are already partway sold). Always break performance out by source before diagnosing
what's wrong — see [[Marketing Math]] and [[Close Rate By Channel]].

**If you won't do it, pay someone $10/week rather than let it not happen.** Not every
owner wants to maintain a tracking sheet personally, and that's fine as long as it still
gets done — a VA can handle 15 minutes a day of data entry for a small weekly fee (2026-
04-20 call). Alex's framing on why this matters more than it feels like at low revenue:

> "It's hard to make financially responsible decisions at scale without it."

The cost of skipping it is deferred, not avoided — fine at $18K/month, genuinely limiting
at $50K–$100K/month. Fix it by choice now, or it becomes a forced problem later. See
[[Monthly Revenue Tracking]] for the complementary, business-level (not per-lead) version
of the same discipline.

## Checklist

- [ ] Log every lead daily, not weekly
- [ ] Record source, cost (if known per-lead), and who took the call
- [ ] Set outcome to one of: No Contact, Follow Up, Booked, Declined, Unqualified
- [ ] Log every cleaner transaction (job, hours, pay, tips, adjustments) for pay-stub
      generation
- [ ] For flat-cost channels (PPC), log manual period spend rather than leaving cost blank
- [ ] Read close rate against qualified close rate, and always by channel, before
      diagnosing a sales problem
- [ ] If you won't maintain it yourself, pay someone $10/week rather than let it lapse

## AI Prompts

> Before diagnosing any close-rate or cost-per-lead problem, ask whether the person is
> looking at a blended number or a per-channel one — blended numbers hide the actual
> problem (see [[Marketing Math]]). Also check whether they mean raw close rate or
> qualified close rate before comparing it to a benchmark; the two can differ by a wide
> margin on the same business and aren't interchangeable. If someone says they "don't
> have time" to log leads, the answer in this source is to pay someone $10/week rather
> than let tracking lapse — not to skip it.

## FAQ

**Q:** What's the difference between close rate and qualified close rate?
**A:** Qualified close rate excludes leads marked Unqualified (wrong-fit inquiries, not
price objections) from the denominator — it isolates sales performance from lead-quality
noise. A channel showing a low raw close rate but a healthy qualified close rate has a
targeting problem, not a sales problem.
