# Uncontracted Recurring Revenue

> [!abstract] In one line
> Recurring isn't the same as committed. Revenue held together by a relationship leaves with the person who holds it.

## Definition

Work that repeats reliably — weekly, bi-weekly — with nothing in writing. It shows up in the
financials looking exactly like contracted revenue, and it isn't.

## Why It Matters

For a buyer this is the single most misleading line on a summary. "75% recurring" reads as
stability. If most of that 75% is uncontracted, what you're actually buying is a set of
relationships that belong to the person leaving.

The failure mode is specific and documented: the seller departs, the clients follow, and the
thing you paid a multiple for evaporates. This is why the [[Non-Compete]] and the transition
period exist — they're the substitute for contracts that were never signed.

For an owner it cuts the other way and it's easy to fix. Papering long-standing recurring
clients costs nothing and converts relationship revenue into transferable revenue. That's
value you create with a signature.

## Symptoms

- "About 75% of our revenue is recurring" with no contract count attached
- Clients who've been served for years and have never signed anything
- The owner is the only person the clients have ever dealt with
- Contracts exist for a handful of accounts and nowhere else

## Common Mistakes

- Reading "recurring" as "contracted." They are different columns.
- Not asking what share of recurring revenue is papered
- Buying uncontracted revenue at the same multiple as contracted revenue
- As an owner, not papering long-standing clients because "they'd never leave" — which is
  true right up until the business changes hands

## Models

**The worked example.** ~75% of revenue was recurring, weekly or bi-weekly. Only a few
buildings were actually under contract. The rest was longstanding relationship work.

So the real question isn't "how much is recurring" but:

```
recurring revenue
  ├── contracted        → transfers with the business
  └── uncontracted      → transfers with the relationship
```

**Ask who holds the relationship.** A client who deals with the company transfers. A client
who deals with *Dave* goes where Dave goes. In a business where the owner personally
delivers a quarter of the work — see [[Owner-Dependent Revenue]] — the answer is usually
Dave.

**Two things substitute for a contract**, and you want both:

- A **[[Non-Compete]]** — stops the seller competing for what you bought
- A **transition period** — the seller introduces you, on site, so the relationship moves
  before he does

Neither is as good as a signature. Both are better than nothing.

## Checklist

- [ ] Ask what share of recurring revenue is under written contract
- [ ] Get the contracts and read the termination terms
- [ ] For uncontracted accounts: how long-standing, and who holds the relationship?
- [ ] Discount uncontracted revenue in your valuation. Don't pay the same multiple.
- [ ] Require a transition period long enough to be introduced to the major accounts
- [ ] Require a [[Non-Compete]]

## FAQ

**Q:** Should I put my own recurring clients on contracts?
**A:** If you might ever sell, yes — it's free enterprise value. The trade-off is that asking
a happy long-term client to sign something can feel like distrust, so timing and framing
matter.

**Q:** How much less is uncontracted revenue worth?
**A:** **Not established in any ingested source.** The principle is documented, the discount
isn't.
