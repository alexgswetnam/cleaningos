# AI Chatbots For Sales

> [!abstract] In one line
> Technically buildable, but humans always outperform on the sales call — the only sound use for AI here is after-hours, non-sales support.

## Definition

Whether to let an AI chatbot run sales conversations — pricing questions, objection
handling, custom-plan negotiation — versus routing everything to a human. Distinct from
[[AI CRM Auditing]], which uses AI to review CRM data after the fact rather than to talk
to prospects in real time.

## Why It Matters

It's tempting precisely because it's possible: pricing logic, objection-handling
parameters, and even custom-plan negotiation can all be programmed. The reason not to
isn't a capability gap — it's a channel and conversion problem. AI sales conversations
run over text, and text loses to phone on every number this vault has for it. See
[[Sales Happen On The Phone]] and [[Channel Prioritization]]'s text-vs-phone data.

> "When you compare it to a human, the human always outperforms. And it radically
> outperforms in certain industries."

## Symptoms

- Considering an AI chatbot to "handle overflow" on sales conversations
- Text-only lead handling already showing much lower close rates than phone
- Older clients (60–80) getting routed through the same chat flow as everyone else
- After-hours messages going completely unanswered, with no fallback at all

## Common Mistakes

- Deploying AI for the sales conversation itself instead of only for after-hours support
- Assuming a technically working chatbot means a profitable one — the tradeoff is close
  rate, not functionality
- Treating all age groups the same — older clients are named as especially poor fits for
  a chat-based sales interaction
- Building the chatbot before confirming text-only close rates are already survivable in
  the business — if they aren't, an AI layer on top doesn't fix the underlying problem

## Models

**The concrete comparison this claim rests on.** Jock's text-only sales approach converts
at roughly **10%**. Phone-based selling in this vault runs **45–55%** close rate. That gap
— not a general skepticism about AI — is the entire argument.

**The one use case that holds up: after-hours support for non-sales questions.** Example
given: a client texts at 9 PM panicking about a cancellation. An AI bot answering with the
cancellation policy and reassurance that someone will call in the morning prevents
overnight anxiety without needing a human awake to do it. That's information delivery, not
a sales conversation — no price is being negotiated, no objection is being handled, no
close is being attempted.

**The line between the two cases.** If the message requires judgment, negotiation, or a
close, it's a sales conversation and belongs on the phone. If it's a policy lookup or a
reassurance, AI can hold the line until a human is available.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm text-only close rates in your own business before considering AI for sales
      at all — see [[Channel Prioritization]]
- [ ] If considering an AI chatbot, scope it to after-hours, non-sales questions only
- [ ] Keep pricing, objection handling, and closing on the phone
- [ ] Don't route older clients through a chat-first flow by default

## AI Prompts

> If a student asks about adding an AI chatbot for sales, don't answer the technical
> question first. Ask what their current text-based close rate is. If it's already far
> below their phone close rate, the chatbot doesn't fix the gap — it automates the losing
> channel. Only recommend AI for after-hours, non-sales support.
