# Picking The Right VA

> [!abstract] In one line
> Don't hire from a resume — run a paid test (roleplay, a real system task, one to two
> weeks) and let the test decide, not the paper.

## Definition

The vetting decision: how to choose which VA to bring on, before [[When To Hire A VA]]'s
timing question and before [[Managing A VA]]'s ongoing oversight. Distinct decision from
both — this page answers "which candidate," not "when" or "how do I run them once hired."

## Why It Matters

A resume tells you nothing about whether someone can close a call, follow a booking
process, or hold to the company's values under pressure. Hiring on paper credentials
produced the exact failure this page exists to prevent: a VA who looked great going in
and quietly cost the business real revenue before the problem was even diagnosed as a
hiring-process failure rather than a training failure.

> "Everyone can look a certain way on paper, but they don't pan out. Can you do the job?
> Can you do the job well? The only way that you know that is by doing a bit of a test
> run."

## Symptoms

- Judging a candidate mainly by resume, past titles, or years of experience
- Skipping a live roleplay in the interview
- Not testing the actual software (BookingKoala, the CRM pipeline) before hiring
- Promising long-term work before any test period has run
- A VA's first real mistake being a company-values violation nobody screened for

## Common Mistakes

- **Hiring the best-looking resume instead of the best practical performer.** The first
  VA hired this way looked great on paper; in the business, work fell through and
  training didn't fix it.
- **Skipping the roleplay.** The VA who worked out was tested live, first call: "Ring,
  ring — hey, I'm looking for a cleaning," with objections layered in afterward.
- **Not testing system ability.** Ask the candidate to pull up BookingKoala and show how
  they'd make or adjust a booking. Not knowing a tool isn't disqualifying if they can
  learn it fast from a process video — that's different from being unable to do the job.
- **Letting a values violation slide because the candidate "seems" capable.** One VA sent
  an unprofessional text to a lead who hung up on him 12 seconds into a call — grounds for
  immediate termination regardless of how confident he'd sounded in the interview.
- **Assuming higher pay signals better fit.** Cost and quality are not correlated — see
  the pay range in [[When To Hire A VA]]'s Economics section. Some $2–3/hour candidates
  outperform $8–10/hour ones; conversation and testing settle it, not the number quoted.

## Models

**The test-run structure.** Tell the candidate up front: one or two weeks to see if the
fit works for both sides. Don't promise the world before it's proven — the point of the
period is that you genuinely don't know yet.

**What gets tested, in order:**

1. **A live sales roleplay in the interview itself**, not scheduled for later. Play the
   customer calling for a cleaning; layer in objections progressively — easy ones first,
   then harder ones — and watch whether the candidate keeps control of the call, asks
   questions, and pushes toward a booking rather than folding.
2. **A practical systems check.** Pull up BookingKoala (or the CRM pipeline) and ask the
   candidate to walk through making or adjusting a booking. Watching them learn a process
   video on the spot is a pass; being unable to follow it is a fail.
3. **The one- to two-week working trial**, watching real performance: close rate, speed
   to lead, follow-through, tracking accuracy, and communication tone with real leads.

**What disqualifies regardless of skill:** a company-values violation. The specific case —
an unprofessional text sent to a lead after a hang-up — ended the trial immediately,
independent of how strong the candidate's phone skills were otherwise.

**Credentials are a weak signal; demonstrated results are the real one.** Alex applies the
same standard to himself as a teacher (no college degree, doesn't finish high school) as
to a VA candidate: performance and track record decide, not the paper. Twenty years of
industry experience with a bad close rate is a worse hire than six months of experience
with a good one.

## Checklist

- [ ] Do not shortlist by resume or years of experience alone
- [ ] Run a live sales roleplay in the interview, with escalating objections
- [ ] Test the actual system (BookingKoala, CRM pipeline) — a walkthrough, not a
      description
- [ ] Set explicit test-period length (one to two weeks) before making any promises
- [ ] Watch for a values violation during the trial — treat it as immediate grounds for
      termination, not a coaching moment
- [ ] Judge on close rate, speed to lead, follow-through, tracking, and communication —
      not credentials
- [ ] Confirm pay quoted doesn't correlate with quality before deciding — see
      [[When To Hire A VA]]

## FAQ

if they pick it up quickly — that's a different signal than being unable to do the work
at all.
