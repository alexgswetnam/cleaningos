# Labor Before Leads

> [!abstract] In one line
> When both are broken, fix the slower one first — and hiring is always the slower one.

## Definition

When an owner has both a lead constraint and a labor constraint, the correct order is
**hire first, then turn on marketing.** This is the sequencing argument in its most
common concrete form — a direct application of [[Five Laws|Build In Order]].

## Why It Matters

Owners almost always want leads first. It feels like the growth move, and hiring feels
scary when you have no work to give someone. **The instinct is backwards.**

**Leads first:** marketing on → leads arrive → they want a clean next Tuesday → you can't
fulfill → you've paid for leads you can't serve → *now* you scramble to hire, under
pressure, while burning ad spend → you turn marketing back off. You end up with a labor
constraint anyway, minus the money.

**Labor first:** hire → partner them with your best cleaners → two or three test cleans →
your best cleaners confirm they're good → *then* turn on leads. No constraint. You can
scale.

> "Which one is going to take longer? For the most part, it's probably going to be
> hiring."

Start the slow process first. That's the whole argument.

## Symptoms

- "I need leads, I can hire all I want but I need leads"
- You've been putting off hiring for weeks and calling it a leads problem
- Your calendar has 10–15 open slots but you're planning a marketing push
- You have no backup cleaner

## Common Mistakes

- **Doing the easy thing first.** Alex named it directly: *"I know you probably are not
  even thinking through it as much as you're like, I just don't want to hire. I'm just
  going to do the other thing first."*
- Turning on marketing before testing the new cleaner — you're putting a paying client on
  someone unproven
- Waiting for work before hiring. See Rick's example below.
- Assuming you need one person when you're heading into growth season

## Models

**The diagnostic question:** which takes longer — finding and testing a cleaner, or
turning on a marketing channel?

Almost always hiring. Marketing is a switch; hiring is a pipeline. And leads are the
easier problem to get help with — Alex: *"it's easier for me to help you solve the leads
one, because I can just show you exactly what we did and you can copy it. Labor's like,
you just got to call, you got to interview, you got to test. That's all inside your own
business."*

## Checklist

- [ ] Count your genuinely open slots for next month
- [ ] Ask: could I fulfill a booking a day right now?
- [ ] If no → hire first
- [ ] Post the role ([[Hiring Channels]])
- [ ] Partner the new cleaner with your best cleaner
- [ ] Two or three test cleans
- [ ] Get your best cleaners' opinion
- [ ] Only then turn on leads

## Student Examples

**Courtney** — had both constraints and wanted leads first: *"Leads. Cause I can hire all
I want, but I need leads."* Her actual capacity for April was 10–15 cleans. Alex walked
her through both scenarios and she changed her mind on the call: *"Okay, labor. Fine.
Yeah, I need to hire anyway."*

**Rick** — the counter-fear, answered:

> "We were definitely afraid of hiring somebody when we had no work to give them. But we
> were advised to hire first. We hired our first cleaner, said, we don't know when we'll
> have work for you, but we'll let you know. And it was a day or two. We had work for
> them."

His method: tell them up front it's a slow start. *"You have to earn our trust before we
really give you a lot of work. And so they understand that going in."*

## FAQ

**Q:** I'm not trying to grow. Do I still need to hire?
**A:** Yes. You need at least one backup. Alex: *"even if you weren't planning to scale,
you still would want to have at least one more kind of backup working alongside people in
case someone left."* If one cleaner has to leave, *"you're in big trouble."* See
[[Backup Cleaner]].

**Q:** What if the new cleaner is bad and ruins my reviews?
**A:** That's why you test before you market. Partner them up and ask your best cleaners.
See [[Testing A New Cleaner]].
