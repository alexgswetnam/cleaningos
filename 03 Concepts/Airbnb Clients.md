# Airbnb Clients

> [!abstract] In one line
> High pressure, thin margin, and one sick cleaner turns into a one-star review and a chargeback.

## Definition

Short-term-rental turnover cleaning. Attractive because of frequency. Usually not worth
it — and if you take it, you charge full price.

## Why It Matters

The frequency is the bait. The economics and the risk are the problem.

**The margin.** Airbnb owners are optimizing for the cheapest possible clean. Alex:
*"if you own an Airbnb, you're trying to spend as little on expenses as you can. You're
going to hire and find a single mom on Facebook marketplace, and hopefully she does a good
enough job, but she's desperate enough for work."*

**The window.** Checkout 11, next guest 3. Three laundry cycles inside four hours. *"There's
no real room for error."*

**The failure cost.** Your cleaner calls out Sunday morning:

> "That's a one-star review. That's a chargeback. That's someone screaming at you over the
> phone that you told them you were going to do it, and now they have to figure out what
> to do on their Sunday."

You're taking on heavy responsibility for work you aren't personally performing.

## Symptoms

- A rush of short-term-rental calls (events drive this — Jack saw it with the World Cup coming to Toronto)
- Being asked to quote below your normal rate for "volume"
- Being asked to hold multiple weekly slots

## Common Mistakes

- Discounting for frequency. Jack ran the math at $37.50/hr against a normal $55/hr floor
  to make it work — that's the trap.
- Taking it without bench depth
- Blocking recurring-client slots for turnover work

## Models

**The question that settles it:**

> "Would you rather have to deal at the beck and call of an Airbnb three times a week, or
> leave the space open for someone who's going to pay full price — that is a natural
> recurring?"

**If you take it anyway:**
- Full price. No frequency discount.
- Have a bench you can call.
- Push liability to the subcontractor in writing — damages, chargebacks, and Alex
  suggested even a cash value attached to a one-star review, *"because you're taking on so
  much responsibility but you aren't the one actually going out and personally cleaning."*

## Checklist

- [ ] Quote your full rate
- [ ] Confirm you have backup coverage for that window
- [ ] If subcontracting, get liability in writing
- [ ] Compare against filling the slot with a recurring client

## Student Examples

**Jack** — two Airbnb calls ahead of the World Cup in Toronto, first ever. Quoted, no
response. After the call: *"the negatives do outweigh."*

**Courtney** — did them early on. *"The profit margin didn't make sense for the amount of
work they wanted us to do."*

**Alex** — does occasional Airbnb cleans **at full rate**, and they keep coming back:

> "I think they keep having to come back to us because someone else isn't doing it right."

The pattern he sees in the booking history is a client cycling through cheaper cleaners
and returning when each one fails. That's the only version of this that works — being the
expensive backup, not the cheap regular.
