# Managing A VA

> [!abstract] In one line
> Measure availability, not activity — and never invent busy work to fill their hours.

## Definition

How to onboard and manage a virtual assistant without surveillance theatre.

## Why It Matters

The instinct is to monitor. Screen sharing, activity trackers, filling their day. It
damages trust and measures the wrong thing.

> "I really don't mind if Elle is working 30 minutes a day or if she's working 12 hours a
> day. As long as she's doing the job and she's doing it well."

## Symptoms

- Your VA pushes back on screen sharing
- You're checking whether they're "working" rather than whether work is done
- You're inventing tasks to justify their hours
- Response times are slipping and you don't have a standard

## Common Mistakes

- **Screen recording as a permanent practice.** Reasonable for the first week or two of
  onboarding. Not forever. It also genuinely slows their machine — *"you got to think they
  don't have a super nice computer."* Both Elle and a previous VA found it frustrating.
- **Busy work.** Explicitly rejected:
  > "I'm not going to give you extra tasks just to be busy. I'm not going to give you busy
  > work. Unless there's something that is going to drive results, drive revenue, I'm not
  > going to throw it on your plate. I'm not going to say, actually, I kind of want the
  > colors on the website to look different. It's just stupid."
- **Monitoring without saying why.** If the real goal is availability, say so — and then
  solve for availability instead.
- **Under-scoping the role.** The opposite failure to busy work, and easier to miss because
  it looks like restraint. The VA is handed one narrow task in one area when they could
  carry a function. Nothing looks wrong — the work gets done — and the capacity you paid
  for never arrives. Ask what they could own, not what you can safely hand over.

## Models

**Discord as the office.** The VA sits in a voice channel during work hours. You drop in
whenever. That's presence without surveillance.

> "Our VAs go into Discord, a group chat, a voice chat — and they're in office. So they're
> in that chat, and I can jump in that chat at any point."

Used for the first two weeks to a month of training. After that it isn't needed.

**The one metric: response time.**

> "I've never had her take more than literally 30 seconds to respond. If your VA is taking
> three to five minutes to respond every single time — I mean, they might be in the
> bathroom, whatever. But if it's every single time, how often are they really checking?"

**The one acceptable exception:** *"I'm on the phone with a client."* Alex: *"that's really
the only excuse for ever not picking up."*

## Checklist

- [ ] Weeks 1–2: screen share or activity tracking, framed as temporary onboarding
- [ ] Say explicitly why, and when it ends
- [ ] Move to Discord voice channel as the "office"
- [ ] Set the standard: respond within 30 seconds during work hours
- [ ] Only exception: on a call with a client
- [ ] Never assign work that doesn't drive results
- [ ] Once trust is established, drop the channel — just call

## Templates

> "We're trying to on-ramp you. We want to see what your workflow looks like, and it's only
> temporary — probably a week of this. I know it's intensive."

> "I'm not going to give you extra tasks just to be busy."

## Student Examples

**Jack** — his VA Eva pushed back: *"I don't like that you're making me share my screen as
I'm working. I feel like it's a little too much."* Jack also felt bad he couldn't give her
full attention (8–9am, then work, then handoff). Alex's read: figure out what the screen
share is actually for. If it's availability, use Discord instead.

**Alex** — used activity tracking for the first week with Elle and a previous VA, Alan.
The difference in output was visible and informative. It was still frustrating for Elle.
He no longer uses it.

**Alex, 2026-04-15** — catches himself under-scoping a VA hired that same day.

> [!quote] Alex, [[2026-04-15 Weekly Coaching Call]]
> "We just hired another virtual assistant today, so it's like, okay, like, that one, I'm
> like, just do this one thing in this area. But I'm like, no, like, he could be, like, an
> assistant. Like, he could do more."

> [!quote] Alex, [[2026-04-15 Weekly Coaching Call]]
> "I'm looking at the thing I wrote down, which is every leader underestimates their team's
> potential. And I'm like, yeah, I think I'm underestimating my team's potential."

He names trust as the constraint rather than the VA's capability — he had avoided handing
over video editing because *"they're going to do it wrong… they're not going to do how I
like it."* The fix he lands on is not a better VA. It's giving the existing one more.

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't 30 seconds aggressive?
**A:** It's a standard for availability during agreed work hours, not a stopwatch. The
signal is the pattern, not any single instance.
