# Cleaner Job Notes

> [!abstract] In one line
> Structure every client's job notes under three fixed headers — preferred cleaner, clean
> exclusions, unique notes — instead of a free-form scribble, and access details (gate
> codes, apartment routes) belong there from the very first booking.

## Definition

A structured, per-client note system inside the scheduling platform (BookingKoala,
ScheduleDrop, ZenMaid, or equivalent) that gives cleaners the context they need without
having to ask the same questions every visit. Distinct from [[Client Expectation Setting]] (the message sent to a client before their first clean) — this is internal,
cleaner-facing, and lives inside the booking record itself, updated indefinitely as more
is learned about a client.

## Why It Matters

Recurring clients accumulate real preferences and access quirks over time — cash left on
the counter, a gate code, a room that's excluded, a specific mop the client wants used on
a certain surface. Without a consistent place and format for this information, notes
become a messy free-text field that different cleaners read (or don't read) differently,
and the same client-specific mistake — missing a gate code, cleaning an excluded room —
repeats across multiple visits and multiple cleaners.

## Symptoms

- Job notes are free-form and inconsistent from booking to booking
- Cleaners repeatedly ask the business (or the client) questions that were already
  answered on a previous visit
- Access problems (gate codes, apartment routing, parking) recur because nothing was
  written down after the first visit
- A cleaner does something excluded, or misses a client preference, because it wasn't
  captured anywhere

## Common Mistakes

- Typing whatever comes to mind into a single unstructured notes field
- Not updating notes after learning something new about a client
- Treating access instructions as something to remember rather than document
- Assuming a normal, no-preference client doesn't need the fields at all — most won't
  have much to say, but the structure should exist so it's ready when they do

## Models

**Three fixed categories, copy-pasted into every client record:**

1. **Preferred cleaner** — who the client likes or expects, if relevant.
2. **Clean exclusions** — what is *not* being done in this home (specific rooms, tasks).
3. **Unique notes** — everything else that's specific to this client: gate codes, using
   the client's own supplies, cash left on the counter, a strict or flexible schedule,
   special attention to a particular surface, or anything else that would otherwise be
   re-discovered the hard way.

Most clients won't need much in any of these fields — a typical clean has nothing unusual
to note — but using the same three headers every time keeps the system scannable instead
of accumulating an unstructured wall of text as the client base grows.

**Access information belongs here, collected proactively, not reactively.** For gated
communities and apartment complexes specifically: ask about gate codes or access
restrictions at booking, gather the full instructions, confirm the best route with the
cleaner *after* the first visit (not just from the client's description), and save the
confirmed version as a unique note for every future visit. This turns a first-visit
scramble into a solved problem for every subsequent booking.

**Update notes as you learn, not just at setup.** The system only stays useful if new
preferences and issues get added the moment they surface — a note field that's accurate
at onboarding but frozen afterward degrades the same way an unstructured system does.

**The tool matters less than the discipline.** Whether the platform is BookingKoala,
ScheduleDrop, or ZenMaid, the value is in the consistent structure and habit of updating
it — not in any specific software's notes feature.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every client record uses the same three headers: preferred cleaner, clean
      exclusions, unique notes
- [ ] Access instructions (gate codes, apartment routing, parking) are asked for at
      booking and documented before the first visit
- [ ] The confirmed best route/access method is re-verified with the cleaner after the
      first visit and saved for future ones
- [ ] Notes are updated the moment a new preference or issue surfaces — not left static
- [ ] Cleaners are expected to read notes before starting, not learn preferences by
      trial and error

## Templates

- "Preferred cleaner: _____ / Clean exclusions: _____ / Unique notes: _____" (the
  three-header structure, copy-pasted per client record)
- Access-collection questions: "Is there a gate?" / "Is this an apartment complex?" /
  "What's the easiest way to get there?"
