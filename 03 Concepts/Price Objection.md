# Price Objection

> [!abstract] In one line
> Don't get on your heels — push back and find out what their cheap cleaner did to them.

## Definition

Handling "that's more than I wanted to pay." The parent page for the price-specific
objections; see [[Word Tracks]] for the general principle.

## Why It Matters

The instinctive response is to justify — start listing what makes you worth it. That's
defensive, and prospects hear it as defensive.

> **Don't get on your heels.**

The move is to turn the question around and dig into why they're shopping at all. Someone
with a cheap cleaner they're happy with isn't on the phone with you.

## Symptoms

- You start listing your differentiators unprompted
- You're talking more than they are
- The call ends with "let me think about it" — see [[Handling Let Me Think About It]]

## Common Mistakes

- **Rambling about why you're worth it.** Defensive, and it hands them control.
- Treating a league gap as an objection — see [[Not Every Client Is Your Client]]
- Dropping the rate instead of the scope — see [[Drop Scope Not Price]]
- Responding to every pushback. Some don't need a response.

## Models

**Push back, don't defend:**

> "What happened to your old cleaner?"
> "Why are you looking for someone new?"

Dig into the pain. Their cheap cleaner failed them, and there's a reason.

**The price is the price.** Sometimes there's nothing to say:

> It's the Snickers bar at the stadium. $7? The cashier looks at you like *what do you
> want me to do about it?* And you buy it.

Not every pushback needs an answer.

**The receptionist technique** — Courtney's, and it works because it's true:

> "I'm Laura, the receptionist. That's the company's price — it's out of my hands."

She doesn't use her own name on sales calls. The price is genuinely not hers to move, so
clients stop negotiating and often book out of sympathy.

## Checklist

- [ ] They push back on price
- [ ] Don't justify — ask what happened with the last cleaner
- [ ] Listen for the failure. That's your opening.
- [ ] League gap? Let them go
- [ ] Real budget? [[Drop Scope Not Price]]
- [ ] Finish line and they want a win? $10–15 off first clean for a review
- [ ] Otherwise: the price is the price

## Templates

- "That's a great price. Why are you even looking around?"

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't the receptionist thing dishonest?
**A:** Only if it isn't true. Courtney genuinely can't change the price on the call — that
authority sits elsewhere. It works because the constraint is real.
