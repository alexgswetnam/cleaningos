# Channel Prioritization

> [!abstract] In one line
> Rank channels by profit, max one out, then move to the next — don't spread money across all of them.

## Definition

The order in which to turn on lead sources, and the rule that you saturate one before
adding another.

## Why It Matters

Spreading budget across five channels means none of them get enough data to optimize and
none of them get enough spend to scale. Max the most profitable one first.

## Models

**The ranking, most profitable first:**

| # | Channel | Economics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Organic** (GBP, SEO, web form) | $0 per lead, highest close rate. Always first. |
| 2 | **Thumbtack** with the free-leads method | Very profitable when filtered properly. See [[Thumbtack]]. |
| 3 | **PPC** (Google Ads) | ~$100 CPA in Alex's area. Sustainable and scalable. |
| 4 | **LSA** | Wildly area-dependent — $18/lead in one member's market, $60–70 in Alex's. |
| 5 | **Facebook / Meta** | $4–15 per lead but only 3–5% close. Converts over *months*. Needs long-horizon tracking. |
| — | **Yelp** | **Avoid.** Sneaky fees, a "free trial" worth watching out for. No corroborating positive report from any member. |

**PPC is the recommended first paid channel.** Alex: he doesn't personally know anyone
who's run Google Ads PPC properly and been unhappy with it.

**Organic drags everything down — in the good way.** If half your leads cost nothing, your
average across all channels collapses. Alex's Q4 came to about $12 per lead overall
because 119 of 245 leads were free. This is why GBP work matters *alongside* paid, not
instead of it.

**"Free" marketing and "organic" aren't quite the same claim.** `Cleaning Biz 101 —
Acquire` argues that without *paid* marketing you don't have a lead engine, you have "a
social strategy" that "can't carry the business" — grouping FB groups, referrals,
word-of-mouth, and organic posts as unpredictable "free marketing." This isn't actually a
contradiction of this page's #1-ranked organic/GBP: that lesson's own accounting treats
GBP as a *paid* method (the $50–$200/month virtual address is a real cost), separate from
the zero-cost "free marketing" bucket it's warning against. Read together: don't rely on
unpaid social hustle as your lead engine, but a verified GBP profile is not what that
warning is about — it's the highest-ROI paid channel on this page for a reason.

**A concrete rule of thumb for total spend:** a healthy cleaning business should spend at
least 10% of gross revenue on marketing for consistent growth. See [[Marketing Budget]].

**LSA's price-shopper problem shows up here too.** ~$60+ per call in one market, with a
tendency to attract price shoppers — consistent with this page's existing "wildly
area-dependent" LSA data, now with an explicit reason given for staying away from it
until higher-ROI channels are maxed out.

**Channels have ceilings.** Jock in Florida hits a wall around $150/day on PPC — past that,
cost per lead climbs. When a channel maxes out, that's the signal to open the next one.

**Ranking by CPA alone can pick the wrong channel — rank by lifetime value instead.**
Rick's data: Thumbtack at $145 CAC vs. LSA at $100 CAC. By raw CPA, LSA is the better
channel. But the Thumbtack lead converted to a recurring client worth $4,800 in lifetime
revenue ([[Client Lifetime Value]]); the LSA lead was a one-time client worth $199.
Judged against what each channel actually produced, Thumbtack was the better spend
despite the higher CAC. See [[Marketing Math]] for the full profit-per-customer math this
runs on. The practical instruction: before ranking a channel down for looking expensive,
check what its leads convert *into*, not just what they cost to get.

## The Attribution Pause Test

If two channels overlap and you can't tell which is producing, **pause the lower-priority
one and watch.**

1. List every active paid channel
2. For each: can you see exactly which leads came from it?
3. If two overlap and you can't separate them, pause one
4. Observe 1–2 weeks
5. Lead flow unchanged → it wasn't adding value. Keep the money.
6. Lead flow dropped → turn it back on. It was contributing.

Alex's philosophy stated directly:

> "Find the one that gives you the highest ROI. Max that out — highest budget you can —
> until you've tapped it out. Then look at the next source."

**Don't split budget across channels you can't measure separately.** That's not
diversification, it's paying for something you can't evaluate.

## Symptoms

- Small budgets running on four channels at once
- No channel has enough data to judge
- Two channels running and everything reports through one dashboard
- You're adding channels instead of scaling the one that works

## Common Mistakes

- Spreading spend thin
- Adding a channel before the current one is saturated
- Judging Meta on 30 days — it converts over months
- Ignoring organic because it isn't a lever you can buy

## Checklist

- [ ] Max organic first — GBP, reviews, site
- [ ] Add one paid channel, usually PPC
- [ ] Track per channel ([[Marketing Math]])
- [ ] Scale until cost per lead starts climbing
- [ ] Only then open the next channel

## Student Examples

**A friend in Australia** — $20K/month on Google Ads producing $200K/month in revenue,
under three years in business.

**Jock, Florida** — $7 cost per click, 30% conversion, $21–25 per conversion. Ceiling
around $150/day.

**Alex** — $18 cost per click, 42% conversion.

**Rick, 2025-03-11** — running PPC and LSA at once, everything surfacing in the LSA
dashboard, unable to attribute. LSA producing at ~$22/lead in his area, GBP organic
"finally clicking." Recommendation: pause PPC and watch. Revenue context — $7K on March
1st, $12K by March 11th.

**A member with $18/lead LSA** — Alex's response: at that price he'd spend $5,000/month.

**2026-02-20 call, three named data points at once:** Rashaun (Jersey City / Montgomery
County) ~$19/lead ($850 spend ÷ 45 leads) — phenomenal. Rick ~$22/lead — also great. Alex
and Courtney, their own areas, ~$70/lead — unprofitable for most. Same claim as the
$18/$60–70 range above, now with three named examples in one place: *"'LSA works' and
'LSA doesn't work' are both true — it depends entirely on your area."*

**A full-quarter GBP proof point, the earliest one on record.** Q4 (tracked via the
Skool tracking sheet): 115 organic Google/web-form leads, 67 booked — a close rate the
source calls "stupid high," attributed to the high intent of someone who searched and
called rather than clicked an ad. This is the same organic-is-nearly-free pattern as the
single-month and live-before/after proof points below, just measured over a longer,
earlier window.

**A single-month GBP proof point (2026-04-01 call).** In March, one member's GBP produced
118 leads, 98 qualified, 33 booked, at an average first-booking ticket of ~$300 — roughly
$10,000 in revenue against a ~$80/month cost (the virtual office address behind the
listing). This is the concrete number behind "organic is $0 per lead": the real cost isn't
zero, but it's close enough that the math barely changes, and it's the reason he was
opening a third GBP location the same call.

**A second GBP proof point, this time a live before/after (2026-04-20 call).** Courtney's
GBP interactions went from ~15 total in February (pre-verification) to 40+ within just the
first 18 days of April — a 4x increase, with website clicks and calls both up, and zero
paid marketing running. The verification itself happened March 17th. This is the same
"organic is close to $0 per lead" claim as the single-month proof point above, now shown
as a direct before/after on one profile rather than a monthly total.

**The same GBP profile, one call later: 4x became 5–6x.** Courtney's interactions
continued compounding past the earlier before/after — by the 2026-04-27 call, calls were
running 5–6x the pre-verification baseline with website clicks up significantly further
still, on zero ad spend the whole time. The pattern reads as compounding, not a one-time
bump: verification unlocked growth that kept building rather than plateauing immediately.

**A second, sharper text-vs-phone data point (2026-04-27 call).** Jock runs text-only
lead handling by choice — optimizing for efficiency across multiple businesses — and gets
10–15% conversion. Alex's phone-first approach gets roughly 50%. The instruction given to
VAs: *"hammer their phone and get them on the phone."* If a lead insists on texting only,
push back repeatedly before accommodating; expect near-zero conversion from leads that
stay text-only regardless. This sets a concrete number on the qualitative claim already on
this page — text isn't just worse, it's roughly a third to a fifth of phone's close rate.

**Text vs. phone matters as much as the per-lead cost.** Rashaun's $19/lead LSA still
underperformed because he could only respond to texts (full-time job, can't take calls)
and booked one job out of 45 leads — the phone leads went unanswered. Text leads convert
far worse than phone leads on LSA: *"people that text aren't as serious."* Alex's
recommendation once you have a VA: turn off text on LSA entirely, phone only. See
[[When To Hire A VA]] — a VA who can answer the phone is sometimes the actual unlock for
a channel that already looks unprofitable.

**Alex's own channel-testing chronology, in order.** [[Leads 101 part 1]] narrates the
actual sequence, which corroborates and dates the ranking above rather than adding a new
channel: **Thumbtack first** (~$4,000 spend month one, ~$2,000–3,000 month two, continued
roughly six months, total spend "well over $10,000 and probably closer to $20,000" —
spent heavily early because there was no established close yet and labor was also still
being worked out) → **Google Business Profile next** (~2 months of heavy research and
setup, "great results," recommended as something every member can do themselves) →
**Facebook Ads tested and dropped** (a bust for this business specifically — spent
"a bunch of money," only 2 bookings) → **Google Ads, which worked well.** LSA was skipped
entirely because calls ran $70–90 in the area. The lesson's own closing point matches this
page's philosophy directly: *"Thumbtack might not work in your area... it depends on your
business and your area. That is why you need to test everything and see what works best
for you."*

**The same source restates the ranking logic with a concrete channel table.** "What Way
Should I Get Leads First" (also [[Leads 101 part 1]]) gives the same order as a direct
answer to "where do I start": free sources first (GBP foremost, plus referrals and calling
the existing database), then paid, with Google Ads as the standout example ($43 CPL, 41%
close, ~$104 CPA — the same figures already on [[Marketing Math]]'s Conflict History) and
Meta explicitly *not* recommended as a first paid channel based on this business's own
result (over $1,000 spent for 2 bookings). One of those 2 Meta bookings only closed because
of long-tail CRM follow-up — a lead sat for **seven months** before an automated Harvest
CRM text got a reply and a booking. See [[Reactivating Past Clients]] for the general
mechanism this is a specific example of.

**A near-identical Q4 data set appears again, restated.** "Paid Marketing Methods
Breakdown" ([[Leads 101 part 1]]) gives PPC at $43 CPL/41% close/~$104 CPA, Thumbtack at
~$15 CPL/26% close/~$58 CPA, and GBP at 115 Q4 leads / 67 booked — all numbers that already
appear on this page, [[Marketing Math]], and [[Thumbtack]] from other sources. Read as the
same underlying Q4 dataset repackaged into a different course bundle (the same pattern
[[Leads 101 Part 2]] found with `Cleaning Biz 101 — Convert`), not new evidence. It also
independently confirms **Yelp as a channel to avoid** ("sneaky fees," no positive report
from any owner) and states the same 10%-of-revenue marketing-spend rule of thumb as
[[Marketing Budget]].

## FAQ

**Q:** Meta looks cheapest. Why is it last?
**A:** Cost per lead isn't cost per client. At 3–5% close, cheap leads are expensive
clients — and they convert months later, so you need tracking that spans quarters.
