# Channel Prioritization

> [!abstract] In one line
> Rank channels by profit, max one out, then move to the next — don't spread money across all of them.

## Definition

The order in which to turn on lead sources, and the rule that you saturate one before
adding another.

## Why It Matters

Spreading budget across five channels means none of them get enough data to optimize and
none of them get enough spend to scale. Max the most profitable one first.

## Models

**The ranking, most profitable first:**

| # | Channel | Economics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Organic** (GBP, SEO, web form) | $0 per lead, highest close rate. Always first. |
| 2 | **Thumbtack** with the free-leads method | Very profitable when filtered properly. See [[Thumbtack]]. |
| 3 | **PPC** (Google Ads) | ~$100 CPA in Alex's area. Sustainable and scalable. |
| 4 | **LSA** | Wildly area-dependent — $18/lead in one member's market, $60–70 in Alex's. |
| 5 | **Facebook / Meta** | $4–15 per lead but only 3–5% close. Converts over *months*. Needs long-horizon tracking. |

**PPC is the recommended first paid channel.** Alex: he doesn't personally know anyone
who's run Google Ads PPC properly and been unhappy with it.

**Organic drags everything down — in the good way.** If half your leads cost nothing, your
average across all channels collapses. Alex's Q4 came to about $12 per lead overall
because 119 of 245 leads were free. This is why GBP work matters *alongside* paid, not
instead of it.

**Channels have ceilings.** Jock in Florida hits a wall around $150/day on PPC — past that,
cost per lead climbs. When a channel maxes out, that's the signal to open the next one.

## Symptoms

- Small budgets running on four channels at once
- No channel has enough data to judge
- You're adding channels instead of scaling the one that works

## Common Mistakes

- Spreading spend thin
- Adding a channel before the current one is saturated
- Judging Meta on 30 days — it converts over months
- Ignoring organic because it isn't a lever you can buy

## Checklist

- [ ] Max organic first — GBP, reviews, site
- [ ] Add one paid channel, usually PPC
- [ ] Track per channel ([[Marketing Math]])
- [ ] Scale until cost per lead starts climbing
- [ ] Only then open the next channel

## Student Examples

**A friend in Australia** — $20K/month on Google Ads producing $200K/month in revenue,
under three years in business.

**Jock, Florida** — $7 cost per click, 30% conversion, $21–25 per conversion. Ceiling
around $150/day.

**Alex** — $18 cost per click, 42% conversion.

**A member with $18/lead LSA** — Alex's response: at that price he'd spend $5,000/month.

## FAQ

**Q:** Meta looks cheapest. Why is it last?
**A:** Cost per lead isn't cost per client. At 3–5% close, cheap leads are expensive
clients — and they convert months later, so you need tracking that spans quarters.
