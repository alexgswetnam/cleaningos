# The Soft Close

> [!abstract] In one line
> Put them on the schedule without charging them — the commitment happens in their head before it happens on their card.

## Definition

Rather than ending an undecided call with "let me know," you hold a specific time for
them at no cost. Something clicks. They've committed.

## Why It Matters

> "If you don't even get a card, you can get them down on the schedule and it makes them
> like — something in their head goes, they've already committed."

An undecided prospect with nothing booked is a lead. An undecided prospect with a time
held is a client who hasn't paid yet. The difference costs you nothing.

It also creates real scarcity, honestly: you are not available whenever. **Alex:**
*"you have to make sure you're not like, yeah, we can clean your house whenever, whatever
time."*

## Symptoms

- Calls end on "I'll think about it and get back to you"
- Nothing on the calendar from a call that went well
- You're relying on follow-up to close what the call should have closed

## Common Mistakes

- Ending with "let me know" — no commitment, no scarcity
- Signalling unlimited availability
- Pushing for the card before the spot

## Models

**The hold:**

> "I just don't want you to lose your spot. What I usually do in this situation is just
> put a time down. We're not going to charge you. You can give me a call back — let me
> just save this spot for you."

**If a card is required:**

> "I have to put a card down. Like I said, it will not charge you. It's encrypted. The
> bank protects you."

## Checklist

- [ ] Objection handled ([[Handling Let Me Think About It]] / [[Handling I'm Shopping Around]])
- [ ] "I don't want you to lose your spot"
- [ ] Name a specific time — not "sometime next week"
- [ ] "We're not going to charge you"
- [ ] If they hesitate on the card: encrypted, bank protects you
- [ ] Confirm you'll reach back out

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't holding a spot for free risky?
**A:** You're holding a slot you'd otherwise leave empty on an undecided lead. The
downside is an unfilled hour; the upside is the booking.
