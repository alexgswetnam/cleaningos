# Transitioning Clients To New Cleaners

> [!abstract] In one line
> Move willing clients to your new cleaners first, keep the resistant ones yourself, and only later tell the holdouts you can't do their home anymore.

## Definition

The two-phase conversation for moving a solo cleaner's client base onto a growing team,
without losing clients who are attached to the owner personally.

## Why It Matters

Every solo cleaner who becomes an owner hits this: clients are used to *you*, specifically.
Handled badly, growth reads to them as a downgrade. Handled well, it's a non-event for
willing clients and a managed, eventual conversation for the rest — not something you have
to force all at once.

## Symptoms

- Growth is stalled because you're still personally cleaning most of your accounts
- Clients push back the moment you mention someone else coming instead
- You're avoiding telling long-time clients you can't keep doing their home

## Common Mistakes

- Forcing every client onto a new cleaner at once instead of starting with the willing ones
- Giving a brand-new cleaner a full client load immediately instead of a testing period
- Never having the eventual "I can't do this anymore" conversation, and quietly resenting
  the clients who never got moved

## Models

**Phase 1 — now.** Have the real conversation. Assign new cleaners to clients who are
open to it first. Leave resistant clients with you for the time being:

> "I'm growing the business. The girls are wonderful. I can guarantee you'll be happy."

**Phase 2 — months later.** For the clients still holding out, there's an eventual
conversation you have to be willing to have:

> "I'm at a point where I can't do your home anymore. This is exciting for me. I have
> wonderful cleaners and I can guarantee you'll be happy."

**New cleaners earn the schedule, they don't start with it.** Don't hand a new hire ten
houses in week one:

> "I can't just give you 10 houses right away. There's always a testing period. The
> better job you do, the more cleans I'm going to give you."

## Checklist

- [ ] Identify which clients are likely open to a new cleaner vs. attached to you
- [ ] Move the willing ones first
- [ ] Keep resistant clients with you temporarily — don't force it in one pass
- [ ] Start every new cleaner with one clean, check in, then increase gradually
- [ ] Months later, have the "I can't do this anymore" conversation with remaining holdouts

## Templates

  wonderful cleaners and I can guarantee you'll be happy."
- "I can't just give you 10 houses right away. There's always a testing period. The
  better job you do, the more cleans I'm going to give you."
