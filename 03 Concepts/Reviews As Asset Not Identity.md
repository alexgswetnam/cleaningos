# Reviews As Asset Not Identity

> [!abstract] In one line
> The business is an asset that supports your life, not a reflection of your worth — and reviews will destroy you if you confuse the two.

## Definition

Emotional separation between the owner and the business's public reputation. Care about
the people. Don't let the star rating be a verdict on you.

## Why It Matters

> "This was my baby. It's not my baby anymore. It's an asset that I'm able to use to
> bring money in for myself and for one day, my family."

The failure mode is real and it's a health problem, not a business problem. Alex's
friend: five stars all the way to 200 reviews, took enormous pride, fought every bad one.
Then cleaners didn't work out — five to ten one-star reviews across three months. Now
sitting at 4.7–4.8.

> "He really can't call people to try to get after them... part of it is that he's just
> too emotionally involved in the business as far as what it stands for."

Two years of serious work, and he needed to step back for health and family.

Alex's own version: *"It drove me insane. I saw it drop my friend, like, really crazy."*
Both he and Elijah felt heavy pressure early on *"of having an upset person really upset
us because we really cared."*

## Symptoms

- A bad review ruins your day or your week
- You want to call the reviewer and argue
- You feel personally attacked by feedback about a clean you didn't perform
- You're avoiding hiring because someone might damage your rating

## Common Mistakes

- Fighting every bad review
- Treating the rating as a scoreboard for you personally
- Letting review anxiety block hiring — which then guarantees the capacity problems that
  actually cause bad reviews

## Models

**Put the care in the right areas.**

> "As far as people to people, I'm going to take care of people as best as I can, both
> customers and cleaners and virtual assistants. But reputation, reviews and all that —
> it can drive you insane."

Care about people. Don't care about the number.

## Checklist

- [ ] Notice when a review has changed your mood for more than an hour
- [ ] Ask: is this about the client's experience, or about me?
- [ ] Fix the operational cause; don't fight the reviewer
- [ ] Keep hiring — capacity problems cause more bad reviews than bad hires do

## Student Examples

**Courtney** — 70 reviews, all five star, and it's the specific reason she was avoiding
hiring. Alex named her fear back to her: *"if someone comes in and ruins it, I kind of
want to punch them in the throat because that's years of work."* She confirmed: *"That was
the trust. It's not anything else."*

The answer wasn't to stop hiring — it was [[Testing A New Cleaner]].

**Alex's friend** — the 200-review story above.

## FAQ

**Q:** So I shouldn't care about reviews at all?
**A:** Care about the causes. A bad review is information about your operation. It is not
information about you.
