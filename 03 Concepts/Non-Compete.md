# Non-Compete

> [!abstract] In one line
> Without one, the seller can take the clients and the employees and rebuild the thing you just paid for.

## Definition

A contractual restriction stopping the seller from operating a competing business — bounded
by a **radius** and a **time window**.

> A "100, 150 mile radius" so the seller doesn't "go rip the shirt off that they just sold."

## Why It Matters

The whole story is in one family:

> Alex's parents bought a business. The seller went off and started another business, took
> the employees, took the clients. The parents lost in arbitration and paid the lawyer fees.

They lost twice — the business they bought, and the cost of arguing about it.

The exposure is largest exactly where it's least visible: a business whose revenue is
relationship-held rather than papered. See [[Uncontracted Recurring Revenue]]. If the
clients follow people rather than companies, then the seller keeps the ability to take them
back for as long as he's allowed to compete.

## Symptoms

- Recurring revenue with no client contracts
- The seller is well-known locally and plans to stay in the area
- The seller is young enough to start again
- Employees are loyal to the owner rather than to the business

## Common Mistakes

- **Skipping it because the seller seems decent.** The cautionary tale is not about a
  villain; it's about what becomes possible once the money has moved.
- A radius so small it's meaningless in a service business
- Covering the seller and not the employees
- Assuming enforceability without checking. Non-compete law varies by jurisdiction and has
  been moving. Get advice for your state — **nothing in the vault covers this.**
- Treating it as a substitute for a transition period. They do different jobs.

## Models

**Two dials:**

| Dial | The example |
|---|---|
| Radius | 100–150 miles |
| Time | Not specified in the source — establish it |

**Pair it with a transition period.** The non-compete is defensive; the transition is
constructive. One stops the seller taking the relationships, the other moves them to you
while he's still there to make the introduction.

**Ask early, and read the answer.** In the broker call the seller was reported as open to
both a non-compete and a transition, and as preferring to walk away cleanly. A seller who
resists either is telling you something worth more than the answer.

## Checklist

- [ ] Raise the non-compete before the LOI, not during closing
- [ ] Set a radius appropriate to how far the business actually serves
- [ ] Set a duration
- [ ] Cover soliciting employees as well as clients
- [ ] Negotiate a transition period alongside it
- [ ] Have a lawyer confirm enforceability in your jurisdiction
- [ ] Ask what the seller plans to do next. The honest answer is useful either way.

## Templates

> "We'd want a non-compete — say a 100 to 150 mile radius — and a real transition period, so
> the revenue actually transfers. Is he open to that?"

## Student Examples

**Alex's parents** — bought a business, seller started a competitor and took the clients and
employees, lost in arbitration, paid the fees. The reason this is non-negotiable for Alex.

**William, 2026-04-27 — the other direction: cleaners, not sellers.** A different but
related scenario — cleaners (not a business seller) leaving and taking clients with them.
William had non-compete agreements in place but judged enforcement impractical for
himself: *"am I really going to go to court?"* This is the employee/subcontractor version
of the same enforceability gap, not the buyer/seller scenario the rest of this page
covers — recorded here because the underlying doubt (having an agreement on paper doesn't
guarantee it's worth enforcing) is the same lesson in a different relationship. See
[[Hiring Channels]] and [[Managing A VA]] for the labor-side response to losing cleaners.

## AI Prompts

> Any discussion of buying a business must raise the non-compete and the transition period,
> even if the user didn't ask. Do not offer an opinion on enforceability — that is
> jurisdiction-specific and unaddressed in the vault. Point the user to a lawyer.

## FAQ

**Q:** Are non-competes even enforceable?
**A:** Varies by jurisdiction and has been changing. **The vault has no position on this and
shouldn't** — it's a question for a lawyer in your state.

**Q:** Is asking for one insulting?
**A:** It's standard. A seller who takes offence is worth watching.
