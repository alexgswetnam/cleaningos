# Differentiation And Trust Building

> [!abstract] In one line
> "We do great work, we're reliable, we're affordable" is what every competitor says too — a real differentiator is simple, stated immediately, and followed up with proof, not a menu of services.

## Definition

A stated reason a prospect should trust and choose this company specifically, distinct
from generic claims every cleaning company makes. Paired with a specific follow-up
resource — a "Trust Builder" document — sent to leads who don't book on the first call,
built to answer the unspoken fears behind hiring a stranger to be in your home, rather
than explaining service types.

## Why It Matters

From the customer's perspective, most cleaning companies sound identical. The real
fear behind "who do I hire to clean my house" isn't about mop technique — it's "am I
letting the wrong person into my home." A differentiator that speaks to that fear
converts better than one more list of services, and the same principle extends to
follow-up content: a generic "types of cleans" PDF doesn't reduce uncertainty, and a
trust-focused one does.

## Symptoms

- Sales calls and marketing lean on the same generic claims every competitor makes
- Follow-up material after a no-book call explains services instead of building trust
- No clear, simple answer exists to "why you, and not the other guy"

## Common Mistakes

- Believing you need an elaborate "unique mechanism" to stand out
- Competing on claims (reliable, affordable, great work) instead of a concrete
  differentiator
- Sending a "types of cleans" explainer as the primary follow-up after an unbooked call

## Models

**A real differentiator is simple and stated immediately.** The source's own example:
*"We're a locally-owned Christian cleaning company that puts customers first with a
satisfaction guarantee."* Said early in the call, it does three things at once: signals
this isn't a faceless national chain, signals a character/integrity standard, and
signals accountability if something goes wrong — the exact three things a homeowner is
quietly worried about before letting a stranger into their house.

**When a prospect names a specific competitor comparison, use it.** Ask what they're
comparing you to, and lean into whatever gap that reveals as the differentiator for that
specific call — see [[Word Tracks]] for the exact-line version of this.

**What you need, stated as a formula:** a clear reason to trust + proof you're legit + a
simple next step. Nothing more elaborate than that.

**The Trust Builder follow-up.** For leads who don't book on the call, the
highest-converting follow-up tested wasn't a service-explainer — it was a document that
answers the questions a lead is thinking but not saying: are you legit, are your
cleaners trustworthy, what happens if I'm unhappy, will this be a headache, can I feel
confident hiring you. A "types of cleans" PDF doesn't touch any of that.

**Naming the differentiator as a USP — the same decision, framed as "what's my ammo."**
[[Leads 101 part 1]]'s USP lessons give the same instruction from a different angle:
identify what's genuinely true and different about the business, then use it as ammo on
calls, on the website, and in marketing. A USP has to fit the business's own DNA — it
can't be copied wholesale from a competitor. Concrete examples given, several of which
overlap directly with the trust-fear formula above: a satisfaction guarantee (available to
any business, and the strongest single default), licensed/insured cleaners,
background-checked cleaners, English-speaking cleaners, cleaner experience, and — while the
business is still small — the owner personally taking the calls. Not every possible
differentiator applies to every market: eco/green cleaning is explicitly named as a USP
that doesn't fit this business's own customers, who rarely ask for it, even though it might
matter in a different, more health-conscious market. The lesson is not "collect every
differentiator" — it's "find the ones that are true and that your specific ICP (see
[[Ideal Customer Profile]]) actually cares about."

**A USP can expire as the business changes.** The owner-answers-the-phone differentiator
stopped being true once VAs started taking calls — a reminder that a USP should be
re-checked, not treated as permanently fixed once chosen.

## Checklist

- [ ] State your differentiator in the first minute of a call, not at the end
- [ ] Keep it simple: one sentence, not a list
- [ ] When a prospect names a competitor, ask what specifically they're comparing
- [ ] Build (or use) a trust-focused follow-up document, not a services explainer, for
      unbooked leads

## Templates

- "We're a locally-owned [values-based] cleaning company that puts customers first with
