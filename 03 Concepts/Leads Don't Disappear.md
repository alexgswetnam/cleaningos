# Leads Don't Disappear

> [!abstract] In one line
> Nobody who called about a cleaning stopped wanting one. They booked someone else.

## Definition

The claim that a lead going quiet is not a lead losing interest. Someone who picked up the
phone to find a cleaner still needs a cleaner. Silence means one of three things happened —
and only one of them is over.

> "People who call for cleaning and then go quiet didn't change their mind about needing a
> clean. They either stuck with their current cleaner, called someone else, or will close
> with you if you follow up."

## Why It Matters

This is a belief that changes behaviour, and the belief it replaces is the comfortable one.

"They must not have been serious" costs nothing to think and requires nothing of you. It is
also almost always false — and it is the reason follow-up doesn't happen. Nobody chases a
lead they've decided was never real.

Accepting that the demand was real makes silence into a data point about your call rather
than about their intent. Which is uncomfortable, and useful.

## Symptoms

- You describe unresponsive leads as "tire kickers"
- Your follow-up stops after one attempt
- Leads go quiet after the price and you don't know why
- You have no idea what your close rate actually is — see [[Close Rate By Channel]]

## Common Mistakes

- Treating silence as a decision
- Following up once and calling it followed up
- Deciding the lead was unqualified rather than reviewing the call — see [[Call Breakdowns]]
- Letting someone off the phone in the first place. The cheapest follow-up is not needing
  one — see [[Sales Happen On The Phone]].

## Models

**The three outcomes behind silence:**

| What happened | Recoverable? |
|---|---|
| Stuck with their current cleaner | Later — the dissatisfaction that made them call hasn't gone away |
| Called someone else and booked | Later — the new cleaner will eventually miss a week |
| Still deciding | **Now, if you follow up** |

Only the third is live today. The first two are the argument for
[[Reactivating Past Clients]] — a lead who booked elsewhere is a lead with a known problem
and a known phone number.

## Checklist

- [ ] Stop recording quiet leads as lost
- [ ] Follow up more than once
- [ ] Keep quiet leads in a list you actually work — see [[Smart Lists]]
- [ ] Review the call before concluding the lead was the problem

## Student Examples

**Marcella** — the source's own example. After Jack's callback at $162 she said *"I'll call
you if we move forward,"* then went quiet. The source's reading is that she booked somebody
else, and that the loss happened on the phone, not afterwards.

## AI Prompts

> When a student describes leads that "disappeared," do not accept the framing. Ask what
> happened in the last thirty seconds of the call and how many follow-up attempts were made.
> The lead going quiet is rarely the event worth examining.
