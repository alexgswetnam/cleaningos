# Marketing Seasonality

> [!abstract] In one line
> January, February and March are worse — that's the calendar, not your funnel breaking.

## Definition

Predictable variation in marketing performance across the year, and judging results
against the trend rather than against last month.

## Why It Matters

An owner who doesn't know the shape of the year will panic in Q1, turn off a working
channel, and conclude marketing is broken.

> "January, February, March... you are not going to have as profitable marketing."

People cancel. Demand is different. Performance changes. **None of that means anything is
wrong.**

This compounds with [[Month One Loses Money]] — an owner who starts spending in January
gets the worst of both patterns at once and is very likely to quit right before it works.

## Symptoms

- Q1 numbers down and you're rewriting your whole marketing
- Judging every month in isolation
- Cancellations clustering and feeling like a service problem

## Common Mistakes

- Panicking during a slow season without knowing the trend
- Not adjusting offers around holidays
- Letting referral and email follow-up go slack when volume is high
- Stopping creative testing once something works

## Models

**Zoom out.** Know when you expect an increase and when you expect a dip. Judge the year.

**Adjust to the season and holidays** — including special offers.

**As volume grows, capture more of what you already have.** Referral programs, email
marketing, and follow-up matter more at high lead volume, not less.

**Keep a pulse check:** are we increasing or decreasing? That requires accurate numbers —
see [[Marketing Math]].

**Never stop testing** new creative, copy, and landing pages.

## Checklist

- [ ] Map performance by month across a full year
- [ ] Mark expected dips before they arrive
- [ ] Plan holiday offers ahead of the season
- [ ] Confirm referral and email follow-up are live
- [ ] Run one creative or landing page test at a time, continuously
