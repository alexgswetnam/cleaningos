# Website Strategy

> [!abstract] In one line
> Get a simple website on day one — it builds trust and supports paid search, but it does not generate leads by itself.

## Definition

Whether and when a cleaning business needs a website, what job it actually does, and what
platform to build it on. Distinct from [[Website Technical SEO Basics]] (the technical
checklist once a site exists) and [[PPC Landing Page Strategy]] (the narrower decision of
where *paid* traffic should land — already answered: the real site, not a squeeze page).
This page is the earlier, broader question: do I need one, when, and on what platform.

## Why It Matters

A website is one of the few marketing assets with no real downside — but treating it as
either optional (skip it until later) or magic (build it and leads arrive) both cost time.
Skipping it means no place to send a skeptical prospect or support Google Ads. Expecting it
to generate leads on its own means disappointment when a live site with no SEO or traffic
produces nothing.

## Symptoms

- Waiting to launch until the website is "finished" or fully custom
- Running Google PPC with no website to send traffic to
- Believing a new website will start producing leads without SEO or traffic behind it
- A long, form-heavy contact page that makes reaching out feel like homework
- Building from a blank page or coding from scratch instead of adapting a template

## Common Mistakes

- Delaying launch because the first version has to be perfect
- Assuming the website itself is a lead source rather than a trust and conversion layer
- Hiring a builder without giving them a reference site to model
- Choosing a platform for ease alone with no thought to long-term SEO control
- Turning the site into a full self-checkout lane (instant quotes, long forms) instead of
  a trust-and-capture page that hands the close to the phone

## Models

**Yes, get one — there's no real downside.** A website lays the foundation for Google PPC
specifically (LSA and Meta can technically run without one, but a site still helps), and
gives skeptical callers somewhere to go mid-conversation. People who find the business
through the website, GBP, or organic SEO arrive **pre-sold** — in one example, their close
rate ran around 70%, well above cold-channel leads, because they'd already spent time on
the site before calling.

**Get it early, keep it simple at first.** A basic one-page site — company info,
trust-building content (reviews, photos, a clear offer, policies), a clear way to reach
out — is enough at launch. The goal isn't a finished SEO machine on day one; it's looking
legitimate. Upgrade later once the business is ready to invest in SEO and GBP optimization
together.

**It will not get you leads by existing.** A website with no SEO and no traffic sitting
online doesn't generate inbound leads on its own. Leads come from people finding it —
through SEO, backlinks, Google Business Profile, or traffic sent from other channels. The
site's job downstream of that traffic is to build trust and make the sale easier, not to
manufacture the traffic itself.

**Build from a template, not from scratch.** Don't code from zero and don't start from a
blank template — find a working reference site (a competitor's, the course's own, or a
purchasable template) and adapt the structure, copy, colors, logo, and location details to
the business. If building it yourself is too technical, hire on Fiverr — but hand the
builder a reference site, don't let them start from nothing, and confirm technical SEO is
handled (see [[Website Technical SEO Basics]]).

**Platform: WordPress for long-term SEO control; simpler builders (Booking Koala, Wix,
Squarespace) to launch fast.** The tradeoff is speed now versus SEO control later.
WordPress gives the most control over page speed, on-page SEO, service-area pages, schema,
and internal linking — the levers [[Website Technical SEO Basics]] runs on. A simpler
builder is a fine way to get something live quickly and can be upgraded or rebuilt once
SEO becomes a real priority.

**The site's three jobs, restated from [[PPC Landing Page Strategy]].** Build trust
quickly (reviews, photos, a clear offer, policies), make it easy to reach the business (a
call button and a short form — see [[Form Field Optimization]]), and route leads into the
CRM so follow-up happens automatically. Instant-quote tools are avoided deliberately: they
train visitors to compare on price like a commodity and remove the context a phone call
provides. The close still happens on the phone — see [[Sales Happen On The Phone]].

## Checklist

- [ ] Launch a simple one-page site as soon as the business starts — don't wait for a
      polished build
- [ ] Include trust-building content: reviews, photos, a clear offer, policies
- [ ] Keep the contact form short — name, phone, optional note
- [ ] Route website leads into the CRM automatically
- [ ] Send paid traffic (PPC) to the real site, not a separate squeeze page
- [ ] Choose WordPress if long-term SEO control matters; a simpler builder if speed to
      launch matters more right now
- [ ] If hiring the build out, give the builder a reference site — don't let them start
      from a blank page
- [ ] Once traffic exists, confirm technical SEO basics — [[Website Technical SEO Basics]]

## FAQ

**Q:** Do I need a website before I start marketing?
**A:** Yes, but it can be a simple one-page site. Don't delay launch waiting for a
polished build — see [[Five Laws|One Step Wins]].

**Q:** Will building a website get me leads?
**A:** Not by itself. It needs traffic — SEO, GBP, or leads sent to it from other
channels. See [[Website Technical SEO Basics]] for what makes it findable.
