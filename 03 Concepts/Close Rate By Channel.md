# Close Rate By Channel

> [!abstract] In one line
> A lead is not a lead — where it came from predicts whether it closes, and "healthy" means profitable, not a fixed percentage.

## Definition

Close rate measured per source rather than as a single overall number.

## Why It Matters

An overall close rate hides everything useful. A 3% Meta close rate and a 70% referral close rate average into a number that describes neither, and leads you to fix the wrong thing.

**"Healthy" is not a universal number — it's whether the channel makes money.** A close
rate that looks low can still be healthy if the channel is profitable at that rate; a
close rate that looks fine can still be a problem if it isn't. The distinction matters
because it changes what "improve this" means: *"I would define healthy as, are you making
money? I would define optimal as, what can we do to make this better?"* When a close rate
is genuinely bad, the fix isn't guessed at — it's diagnosed by listening to the actual
calls and checking whether the problem is lead quality, the salesperson, or pricing before
blaming the channel.

## Symptoms

- Comparing close rates across channels without accounting for lead source
- Treating one universal close-rate number as the target for every channel
- Concluding "marketing is bad" from a low close rate without listening to the calls first

## Common Mistakes

- Reporting one blended close rate
- Judging a channel on cost per lead alone
- Writing off Meta at 30 days when its leads convert over months
- Comparing a Meta lead to a Google Business Profile lead as if they carry the same intent

## Models

Ranked highest to lowest:

| Source | Close rate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral** | Highest | A friend vouched for you |
| **Organic inbound / GBP** | 60–70%+ | They searched, read your site for 10–30 minutes, then called. They sold themselves. |
| **PPC** | ~40–45% (one source), ~26% (another) — see [[Marketing Math]]'s Conflict History for the unresolved discrepancy | Moderate, area- and spend-dependent |
| **Thumbtack** | Moderate | ~26–29% |
| **LSA** | Moderate | Area-dependent |
| **Meta / Facebook** | ~10% (one source), 3–5% (another) | Converts over months, not days |

On organic inbound: *"I've watched recordings of people going through our website and people will spend 10, 20, 30 minutes on the website... By that time, they know that they want to go with us."*

On Meta: *"You get a bunch of leads, they're five to $15 cost per lead, but then it feels like nobody's booking. But then three months later, you see that quite a bit of people are converting over time."*

**Diagnosing a bad close rate, once you know it's bad.** Don't guess at the fix. Look at
the numbers by channel, then listen to the actual sales calls — see [[Call Breakdowns]].
The three usual suspects: lead quality, the salesperson's handling of the call, or the
price itself being mispositioned for that channel's leads.

## Checklist

- [ ] Break close rate down by lead source before judging any single number
- [ ] Ask "is this channel profitable?" before asking "is this close rate good?"
- [ ] If a channel's close rate is bad, listen to its calls before touching spend or
      abandoning the channel
- [ ] Don't compare a Meta lead's close rate to a GBP lead's as if they're equivalent

> single blended answer is close to meaningless. If the close rate is described as "bad,"
