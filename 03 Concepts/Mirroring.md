# Mirroring

> [!abstract] In one line
> Read who you're talking to in the first thirty seconds, then match their tone, pace, and professionalism — same words, different delivery.

## Definition

Adjusting *how* you talk — not what you say — to match the person on the other end. Over
the phone there are only three dials: **tone, pace, and professionalism.**

> "We like people that are more like us. Over the phone, it's going to be tone, pace, and
> professionalism."

## Why It Matters

The read happens in the first thirty seconds and it's not optional — you're going to sound
like *someone*, and if it isn't someone like them, you're working against yourself for the
rest of the call.

The failure is symmetrical. Being chatty and slow with a Type A who wants the point costs
you the call. Being crisp and transactional with someone who wants to talk costs you the
call too. Neither is a personality flaw; both are a failure to read.

This is also what makes [[Reciprocity]] possible later. "I like you, I'm going to take that
off for you" only lands if the call before it built something real.

## Symptoms

- The prospect sounds like they want to get off the phone from the first answer
- You're doing most of the talking and they're giving one-word replies
- Your calls all sound the same regardless of who answers
- You have good word tracks and still lose people early

## Common Mistakes

- Having one phone personality and using it on everyone
- Reading "short answers" as rudeness rather than as information
- Mirroring content instead of delivery — you match their words, not their pace
- Trying to build warmth with someone who's telling you they don't want warmth

## Models

**The two ends of the dial**, both from the source:

| You're talking to | You sound |
|---|---|
| A boardroom boss, walks in heels, only woman in the room | Crisp. Direct. Don't drag words. Don't be chatty. |
| Aunt Sherry, who wants to chat | You chat. |

**Time of day is part of the read.** The Marcella call was at 5 PM — done with work, maybe
tired, maybe has dinner plans. That's not a personality trait, and it changes the delivery
just as much as one.

**Same words, adjusted delivery.** This is where Mirroring meets [[Word Tracks]] — the
line is fixed, the delivery moves:

| Person | "Let me think about it" reply |
|---|---|
| Type A | *"Absolutely. What's the main concern you need to think about?"* |
| Chatty, warm | *"Absolutely, no worries. Of course you want to think about it. I don't want you to feel any pressure at all. I'm just curious — what's the main concern you need to think about?"* |

**Canned jokes, used to break formality** — worth having ready, because inventing one under
pressure produces the wrong one:

> "She didn't burn your house down, did she?"
>
> "When you say small dog — are we talking football size or coffee mug size?"
>
> "Did you have a problem, or you just wanted the place to look better?"

Use these on the chatty end of the dial. On a Type A call they cost you time she told you
she didn't have.

## Checklist

- [ ] Listen to the first two answers before deciding how to sound
- [ ] Name the read to yourself: fast or slow? warm or transactional?
- [ ] Match pace first — it's the dial people notice
- [ ] Note the time of day
- [ ] Keep the word track; change only the delivery

## AI Prompts

> When reviewing a sales call, separate two failures that look identical in a transcript:
> the wrong words (a [[Word Tracks]] problem) and the right words delivered at the wrong
> pace (a Mirroring problem). A transcript alone usually can't tell you which — ask for the
> recording or ask what the prospect sounded like.

## FAQ

**Q:** Isn't this just being fake?
**A:** You already change how you talk to your grandmother and your accountant. This is
noticing that you do it, and doing it on purpose.

**Q:** What if I misread them?
**A:** Adjust. The read isn't a commitment — it's a starting position you keep updating.
