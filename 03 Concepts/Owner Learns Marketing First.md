# Owner Learns Marketing First

> [!abstract] In one line
> Don't outsource marketing before you understand it yourself — you can pay someone to execute once you already know what good looks like.

## Definition

The claim that an owner should learn marketing fundamentals before handing the function
off entirely, rather than outsourcing it as a way to avoid the skill. Directly applies
[[Philosophy]]'s broader belief — that delegation before understanding spreads confusion
rather than removing it — to the specific case of marketing.

## Why It Matters

Marketing is one of the most valuable and most transferable skills a business owner can
build — it compounds into future businesses and doesn't depend on any one vendor. An owner
who always relies on someone else to run marketing never learns to judge whether that
person is doing a good job, never builds the philosophy behind why a campaign works or
fails, and stays dependent in a way that limits the business's flexibility.

## Symptoms

- Wanting to hand off marketing entirely before knowing your own cost per lead or close
  rate
- No way to evaluate whether an agency or freelancer is actually doing good work
- Treating marketing as a task to avoid rather than a core owner skill

## Common Mistakes

- Hiring marketing help before understanding what "good" looks like
- Confusing setup assistance (someone builds it, you take it over) with full,
  permanent dependence
- Assuming outsourcing is always wrong — it isn't, once the owner already has the skill

## Models

**The two legitimate exceptions.** It's fine to bring in help when someone sets marketing
up and the owner takes it over afterward — that's support, not dependence. It's also fine
to pay for execution once the owner already understands marketing and simply doesn't have
the time, or would rather spend money than hours.

**Otherwise: learn it first.** For an owner who doesn't yet know their own numbers, full
outsourcing means never developing the judgment to run or evaluate marketing at all —
even a well-run outsourced campaign doesn't teach the owner anything if they never see
inside it.

## Checklist

- [ ] Learn your own cost per lead, close rate, and CPA before hiring anyone to run
      marketing — see [[Marketing Math]]
- [ ] If bringing in help, decide up front whether it's setup-then-takeover or genuine
      ongoing outsourcing
- [ ] Don't outsource simply to avoid the discomfort of learning the skill
