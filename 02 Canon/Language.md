## Hard Naming Rules

These are the only absolutes. Break them and sentences become ambiguous or wrong.

### Harvest vs. Harvest CRM

Harvest means two things. The rule that keeps them apart:

| Write | Means |
|---|---|
| **Harvest CRM** *(always both words)* | The software product. **Never shortened to "Harvest."** |
| **Harvest** *(bare)* | The fourth Season. |

✅ "Harvest CRM handles the follow-up automatically."
✅ "Once you reach Harvest, the business runs without you."
❌ "Harvest handles that." — ambiguous, especially said out loud.

Agents must expand to "Harvest CRM" every time the product is meant, including second
and third mentions. No pronoun shortcuts.

### Owner / Client / Cleaner

| Term | Who |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | The cleaning business owner. Our student. |
| **Client** | The Owner's customer. Whose space gets cleaned. |
| **Cleaner** | The person doing the work. |

"How to get more clients" is advice **for** the Owner. "How to get more owners" is
**our** marketing. Confusing these produces sentences that are subtly nonsense, and it
happens in almost every first draft.

*Customer* is fine conversationally — Alex uses it. Just never use one word for both
roles in the same passage.

## Coined Terms

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| **CleaningOS** | The whole method. The operating system under the tactics. |
| **Engine** | One of four core business areas. [[Four Engines]] |
| **Season** | The stage an Engine is in. [[Four Seasons]] |
| **Law** | One of five governing principles. [[Five Laws]] |
| **One Step** | The single next action, given the Season. Never a list of ten. |
| **Business GPS** | The diagnostic — where am I, what's next. |
| **Roadmap** | The sequence. Where you are, where you're going, next step. |

Use these consistently. They carry the method — an owner who learns the words has half
learned the system.

## Preferences, Not Rules

Weak defaults. Override when the sentence is better for it.

- **Constraint** over *bottleneck* in teaching material — it implies something
  identifiable and removable. Both are fine; Alex uses both.
- **Cleaner** over *employee* or *staff*.
- **Build** for making something new. **Fix** or **strengthen** for repairing.

## Words Alex Dislikes In Others

Not banned in our writing — these are things he finds hollow when the industry says them:

"Just scale." "Crush it." "Seven-figure CEO." "Boss babe." "Hustle harder." "Automate
everything." "Just hire a VA." "Passive income" when the business still needs the owner
for every exception. "Six figures" without saying revenue, profit, or owner income.

Also vague coaching language used to dodge a concrete problem: "step into your power,"
"raise your vibration," "work on your mindset."
