# The Four Engines

**Leads · Labor · Logistics · Leadership**

## Leads → [[Leads Engine]]

Creates profitable clients by turning attention into conversations, quotes, booked jobs,
and recurring relationships through marketing and sales.

## Labor → [[Labor Engine]]

Creates the capacity to fulfill the promise by recruiting, vetting, onboarding,
scheduling, and retaining enough dependable cleaners.

## Logistics → [[Logistics Engine]]

Makes excellent service repeatable — a reliable way to move from booking through
scheduling, job completion, quality control, payment, and follow-up.

## Leadership → [[Leadership Engine]]

Gets people to own and improve the work without the owner directing every move. VAs, team
management, accountability, communication, decision ownership.

---

## Why Four

Every recurring growth problem in a cleaning business falls into one of four jobs:

| Job | Engine |
|---|---|
| Create demand and convert it | **Leads** |
| Create capacity to perform the service | **Labor** |
| Make delivery repeatable | **Logistics** |
| Make people responsible for operating and improving it | **Leadership** |

Sales belongs inside Leads. Recruiting belongs inside Labor. Software and SOPs belong
inside Logistics. VAs, managers, culture, and accountability belong inside Leadership.

**Finance measures the health of all four. It is not a separate production Engine.**

Three categories would combine problems that require different solutions. Five would
usually split one of these jobs into a subcategory and make the diagnostic less useful.

## Each Engine Splits In Two

From the workshop — useful when diagnosing, because the two halves fail differently:

| Engine | Splits into |
|---|---|
| **Leads** | Marketing (getting them) + Sales (closing them) — see [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]] |
| **Labor** | Finding · hiring · retaining · **backup** |
| **Logistics** | Systems · SOPs · CRM · scheduling · communication |
| **Leadership** | Delegation · ownership culture · accountability · someone else can step in |

## Which Breaks First

At the very beginning, **Leads** — there's no predictable way to create clients yet.

As soon as marketing starts working, **Labor** becomes the first serious growth ceiling.
A company can go from *"we need more jobs"* to *"we cannot cover the jobs"* very quickly.

## What Owners Over-Invest In

**Logistics**, because it feels productive and safe. They can spend weeks choosing
software, writing SOPs, building automations, and rearranging workflows **without facing
the rejection involved in sales or hiring.**

More established owners over-invest in **Leads**, because buying more demand feels easier
than repairing Labor or Leadership.

> [!abstract] The point
> There is no universal answer for every business, and that's exactly the point of the
> model: **diagnose the Engine instead of following whatever the industry is currently
> talking about.**
