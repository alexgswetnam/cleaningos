# The Four Engines

**Leads · Labor · Logistics · Leadership**

> [!warning] Unverified — AI-drafted
> The definitions and the "why four" argument came from a draft, not from Alex. See
> `VERIFY.md`.

Each Engine has a hub page in `04 Systems/` holding its workflows and SOPs. This page
defines what each Engine *is*; the hub holds how it *runs*.

---

## Leads → [[Leads Engine]]

Creates profitable clients by turning attention into conversations, quotes, booked jobs,
and recurring relationships through marketing and sales.

## Labor → [[Labor Engine]]

Creates the capacity to fulfill the promise by recruiting, vetting, onboarding,
scheduling, and retaining enough dependable cleaners.

## Logistics → [[Logistics Engine]]

Makes excellent service repeatable — a reliable way to move from booking through
scheduling, job completion, quality control, payment, and follow-up.

## Leadership → [[Leadership Engine]]

Gets people to own and improve the work without the owner directing every move. VAs,
team management, accountability, communication, decision ownership.

---

## Why Four

Every recurring growth problem in a cleaning business falls into one of four jobs:

| Job | Engine |
|---|---|
| Create demand and convert it | **Leads** |
| Create capacity to perform the service | **Labor** |
| Make delivery repeatable | **Logistics** |
| Make people responsible for operating and improving it | **Leadership** |

Sales sits inside Leads. Recruiting sits inside Labor. Software and SOPs sit inside
Logistics. VAs, managers, culture, and accountability sit inside Leadership.

**Finance measures the health of all four. It is not a separate production Engine.**

Three categories would combine problems requiring different solutions. Five would split
one of these jobs into a subcategory and make the diagnostic less useful.

---

## Which Breaks First

At the very beginning, **Leads** — there's no predictable way to create clients yet.

As soon as marketing works, **Labor** becomes the first serious growth ceiling. A
company goes from *"we need more jobs"* to *"we can't cover the jobs"* very quickly.

## What Owners Over-Invest In

**Logistics**, because it feels productive and safe. Weeks choosing software, writing
SOPs, building automations, rearranging workflows — without facing the rejection
involved in sales or hiring.

More established owners over-invest in **Leads**, because buying more demand feels
easier than repairing Labor or Leadership.

> [!abstract] The point
> There's no universal answer for every business. That's why this is a diagnostic:
> diagnose the Engine instead of following whatever the industry is currently talking
> about.
