# The Five Laws

The non-negotiables. Every concept page declares which Laws bear on it via `laws:` in
frontmatter, which makes these names a primary index across the whole vault.

---

## 1. Stop Guessing

Owners guess about almost everything: what to charge, where leads come from, why
prospects didn't book, which ad produced revenue, whether a cleaner is profitable, how
long jobs take, why clients cancel, whether recurring clients are worth more, what their
margins are, and which part of the business is actually preventing growth.

**Every guess creates a decision made from emotion.** A slow week makes them cut prices. A
busy week makes them hire too quickly. One complaint makes them rewrite the service. One
good lead makes them spend more on an unproven channel.

The cost is not just money. **Guessing destroys momentum**, because the owner keeps
changing direction before anything has time to work. It also makes the business
impossible to teach, delegate, or improve — because nobody knows what's actually true.

## 2. Clarity Creates Momentum

Owners usually don't need more motivation. **They move when they can see the actual
problem, the target, and the next action clearly.**

## 3. Build In Order

Every good tactic can become a distraction when used in the wrong Season. **Solve the
constraint in front of you before building for a future version of the company.**

## 4. The Roadmap Already Exists

A cleaning business is not an unknowable creative mystery. Other owners have already hit
the same categories of problems. **The details vary, but the path is learnable.**

## 5. One Step Wins

The business doesn't need to be transformed today. The owner needs to complete **the right
next step.** Doing that consistently beats consuming more information, rebuilding the
plan, or trying to repair all four Engines at once.

---

## How Laws Are Used

Every concept page tags the Laws it touches. That turns the Laws into a cross-cutting
index — you can ask the vault "everything that bears on Build In Order" and get an answer
across every Engine and Season.

**These names are expensive to change.** Renaming one means editing every page that cites
it. Decide slowly, then leave them alone.
