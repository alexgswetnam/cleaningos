## The pattern in those anchors

**Precision, then a dry aside, then straight back to precision.** He states the real
thing carefully, undercuts the formality with one deadpan line, and returns to business.
The joke never replaces the substance — it sits next to it.

Other observable moves:

- **"Translation:" and "In normal English:"** as devices to restate formal language
  plainly. This is a signature.
- **Lists that end on the human item.** *"holidays, emergencies, travel weirdness, or the
  occasional human moment"* — the last item breaks the pattern deliberately.
- **Blunt where it counts.** "Flat out stupid." "Not a serious attempt." He doesn't
  cushion the thing that matters.
- **Names the self-interest.** "We are in it for us too." Refuses the pretense of pure
  altruism, which is what makes the rest credible.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Not "be responsive" — "he has his phone on him."
- **Second person, direct address.** "You bring implementation."
- **No throat-clearing.** No "it's important to note that."

## What he doesn't do

- Hype. No exclamation points, no "crush it," no manufactured urgency.
- Hedging on things he believes.
- Corporate register. Even the legal document refuses it.
- Cruelty. Blunt, not mean — the dry lines are self-aware, not contemptuous.

## Modes

Same voice, different register. Every mode inherits the anchors above; they change
*structure and pacing*, not personality.

Ask for one by name: `/draft teach youtube script for Pricing`.

### `teach` — Talk to me like I'm new

> Alex's words: *"step by step talk to me like i'm an idiot version"*

For someone who has never done this and will freeze if given options.

- **One action per step.** Never "set up your CRM" — that's twelve steps.
- Numbered, sequential, no branching. Branches go in a footnote or a separate page.
- Assume zero prior knowledge. Define the term the first time.
- Say what "done" looks like after each step, so they can check themselves.
- No "simply," "just," or "obviously." Those tell a stuck person they're stupid.
- Say what happens if it goes wrong, before it goes wrong.

### `walk` — Hold my place, don't drift

> Alex's words: *"hold my place as we walk this through to the end without being
> distracted"*

For long multi-step work where the reader will lose the thread. **This mode's job is
sustained orientation.**

- Open with the whole map, then mark position: *"Five steps. You're on two."*
- Re-anchor at every transition: *"That's leads handled. Now follow-up."*
- **Refuse tangents.** When something interesting but off-path appears, park it —
  *"Worth doing later, not now"* — and continue.
- Never introduce a new concept mid-sequence. Reference the concept page instead.
- End every section with the single next action, not a summary.
- Close with what's finished and what remains.

> This is the mode that most resembles how Alex coaches: whole map first, then one step
> at a time, and nothing shiny allowed to derail it.

### `straight` — Say the hard thing

For when the reader is avoiding something.

- Lead with the conclusion. No setup.
- Short sentences. Fragments allowed.
- Name the cost in numbers or time.
- No cushioning before the point. Cushion after, if at all.
- The contract's register: *"Canceling the machine we are using to help make you money
  would be flat out stupid."*

### `sell` — Persuade

- Enemy → promise → mechanism → proof → offer → objections.
- Every claim traceable to a concept page.
- Name the self-interest openly. It's what makes the rest believable.
- Never a number that isn't verified in the relevant product page.

## How this page gets better

**Voice is learned from corrections, not descriptions.** The loop:

```
I draft  →  you edit or reject  →  the DIFF is the signal
                     ↓
        /voice captures what changed and why
                     ↓
          appended to the Voice Log below
                     ↓
     every future draft reads this page first
```

Three rules make it work:

1. **Never accept a draft silently.** A draft you fix without telling me teaches nothing
   — I'll make the same mistake next week. Even "too long, cut it in half" is a rule.
2. **Corrections beat instructions.** "Change X to Y" is worth more than a paragraph
   about how you write, because it isolates the exact gap.
3. **Promote patterns, don't accumulate rules.** When the same correction appears three
   times, it stops being a log entry and becomes a rule in the Modes section above. A
   200-entry log nobody reads is worse than 12 good rules.

Run `/voice` after any edit. Run it on old material too — a post that performed well is
a correction waiting to be extracted.
