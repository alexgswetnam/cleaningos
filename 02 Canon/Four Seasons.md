## The Structural Idea

**The entire business is not always in one Season. Each Engine has its own.**

Leads can be in Scale while Labor is in Survival. Logistics can be stable while
Leadership has barely begun.

**The owner's real job is to identify the Season of each Engine and work on the one
currently constraining the whole system.**

This is what makes the model a diagnostic rather than a description. It's also what lets
the vault answer *"what should I do next"* for a specific owner instead of dumping
everything it knows.

---

## Survival

Usually **$0–5,000/month**, though revenue alone doesn't define the Season.

They may have no cleaners, one dependable cleaner, or a loose list of subcontractors they
hope will be available when work comes in.

The day is a mix of posting for leads, answering every call, quoting jobs, scheduling,
finding someone to fulfill the work, checking quality, collecting payment — and sometimes
cleaning the house themselves when the plan breaks. **They don't have enough volume yet to
know which problems are patterns and which are one-offs.**

Worried about: the next client, covering the next job, paying for ads, whether this can
ever become a real business.

> **Their job is not to optimize. Their job is to survive long enough to prove demand,
> fulfillment, and basic unit economics.**

## Stability

The result is becoming repeatable. Leads don't appear only by luck. Jobs can be sold at
sustainable prices. The owner has dependable fulfillment, a growing recurring base, and
basic processes that work more than once.

**They've moved out of Survival when one bad day no longer threatens the existence of the
business.**

## Scale

The business can add volume without adding equal amounts of owner chaos. Unit economics
are proven, the core workflows are documented, and other people can carry recurring
responsibilities.

**They've entered Scale when growth is mainly a matter of adding capacity to a machine
that already works — not inventing a new machine every week.**

## Harvest

The business produces profit, freedom, and optionality beyond the owner's daily labor.
Leadership exists below the owner. Performance is visible through numbers. The business
can fund the owner's life, other investments, a new market, or an eventual sale.

**They've reached Harvest when the company can continue operating and improving without
needing their attention as its primary fuel.**

Harvest is not a stopping point. As Alex put it on the 2025-03-25 call: *"sustain is going
to actually continue to grow. It's not going to stop growing."* You can keep it or sell
it — see [[Business Valuation]].

## What Owners Try To Do Too Early

They try to automate, delegate, and scale **before they can consistently acquire and serve
a client profitably.**

They build giant SOP libraries before enough jobs have occurred to reveal the real
process. They hire management before there's enough stable work to manage. They expand
service areas, add services, obsess over SEO, or build elaborate CRM workflows while their
phone still isn't answered consistently.

> **They try to become passive before becoming predictable.** Owner freedom is an output
> of a proven, documented, delegated system. It is not the first setting you turn on.
