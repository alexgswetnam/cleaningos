## The Structural Idea

**There is no universal business-level Season. Each Engine has its own.**

Leads can be in Scale while Labor is in Survival. Logistics can be stable while Leadership
has barely begun. A company does not have *a* Season, and any sentence that gives it one
is wrong.

This is what makes the model a diagnostic rather than a description. It's also what lets
the vault answer *"what should I do next"* for a specific owner instead of dumping
everything it knows.

## The Four Questions

Ask them of **one Engine at a time.**

### Survival

> **Can this Engine reliably produce its required result at all?**

Not well, not consistently. At all. Leads that appear when you post and stop when you
don't. One dependable cleaner, or a loose list of subcontractors you hope will be
available when work comes in.

An Engine in Survival hasn't yet generated enough volume to tell you which of its problems
are patterns and which are one-offs.

> **The job here is not to optimize. It is to make the thing happen once, on purpose,
> repeatably enough to prove it can.**

### Stability

> **Can this Engine produce the result consistently and predictably?**

The result stops depending on luck or heroics. Leads don't appear only by chance. Jobs get
sold at sustainable prices. Fulfillment is dependable and the basic process works more than
once.

**An Engine leaves Survival when one bad day no longer threatens it.**

### Scale

> **Can this Engine increase output without proportional increases in complexity, errors,
> cost, or owner involvement?**

Turning the volume up doesn't multiply the chaos. The unit economics are proven, the core
workflow is documented, and other people can carry recurring responsibility for it.

**An Engine enters Scale when growth is a matter of adding capacity to something that
already works — not inventing a new machine every week.**

### Harvest

> **Can this Engine continue producing, monitoring, and improving its result without
> depending on the owner as its primary fuel?**

Someone other than the owner runs it, watches its numbers, and improves it. Performance is
visible without the owner asking.

Harvest is not a stopping point. As Alex put it on the 2025-03-25 call: *"sustain is going
to actually continue to grow. It's not going to stop growing."* An Engine in Harvest is
what makes a business sellable — see [[Business Valuation]].

> The Season is **Harvest**. The product is **[[Harvest CRM]]**, always written with both
> words. Bare *Harvest* always means this Season. See [[Language]].

## What Owners Try To Do Too Early

They try to automate, delegate, and scale an Engine **before it can produce its result
even once, reliably.**

They build giant SOP libraries before enough jobs have occurred to reveal the real process.
They hire management before there's enough stable work to manage. They expand service
areas, add services, obsess over SEO, or build elaborate CRM workflows while the phone
still isn't answered consistently.

> **They try to become passive before becoming predictable.** Owner freedom is an output of
> a proven, documented, delegated system. It is not the first setting you turn on.

This is [[Five Laws|Build In Order]] stated as a failure mode.
