## Core Teaching

# The Best Paid Marketing Methods

Here are the best lead engines for a cleaning business (based on what we’ve actually tested + what’s working right now).

The big idea: you don’t “try marketing.” You build a lead engine you can predict.

That means you track cost per lead, close rate, and cost per booking.

Use the Free Tracking Sheet in our Free Resources module. This is what I pulled the screenshot from the above - every lead goes into this sheet so we know our KPIs.

The below numbers are our ACTUAL statistics from 2025 Q4 (Oct-Dec)

1. PPC (Google Ads) — solid + predictable when built right

We use PPC and average $43 cost per lead with a 41% close rate.
That works out to about $104 cost per booking.

PPC is great because it captures intent. People are actively searching for a cleaner. The win is consistency and scale once you have it dialed in.

If you would like us to set up your PPC for you, just send me a DM. If it isn't set up properly, you may end up like my friend Jacob who has spent thousands with almost nothing to show for it.



2. Thumbtack — cheap bookings if you run it correctly

Thumbtack was about $15 cost per lead for us (factoring in the free leads hack I show in the free resources module under marketing). Our close rate there is 26%, which means roughly $58 cost per booking.

Thumbtack is especially good because you can set it up to target people looking for recurring service, not just one-time deep cleans. If you want more info on how to set this up correctly, refer to the Thumbtack settings under marketing in the Free Resources module.



3. Local Map Pack SEO (Google Business Profile) — our best source

This has been insane for us. I spent 2 months learning from Jackie Chou on YouTube to distill it down to what is most important for a cleaning business like ours.

In Q4 we got 115 leads from this and booked 67 of them.

GBP is “free,” but to get the best results you usually need a Google business address, which can run $50–$200/month depending on your city. We pay $80 per location and we have two locations — do the math! It's amazing.

This is our best lead source, but it’s not magic: you have to optimize your website and consistently post on your GBP. I made a video in the free resources on how to do this to get similar results, and there’s a more in-depth breakdown in the GBP setup course.



4. Facebook Ads — we failed (for now), but we’re retesting

We tried Facebook and spent more than $1,000 for only 2 bookings.

Most likely we did it wrong - so we’re currently testing new approaches with two friends who are getting under $100 cost per booking. We’ll share how our tests perform once they’re complete (by the end of February).



5. Yelp — avoid

We tested Yelp and it was terrible. They’re sneaky with fees and their “free trial” is something to watch out for. I personally haven’t heard one cleaning business owner say anything consistently good about Yelp.



6. Past clients + old leads — the hidden lead engine most people ignore

This is why your CRM matters.

Your CRM should be automatically sending long-term follow-up (think 2 months, 6 months, even 12 months). We regularly get bookings from:





one-time clean clients from months ago



old leads that reply 3-6 months later to an automated follow-up text

Most cleaning businesses burn money chasing new leads while they let warm leads die in their database!!!



7. Referrals — powerful, but we haven’t fully tapped it yet

Referrals can be one of the best engines, but we haven’t gone as hard on this as others. We’re working on improving our referral focus and building a more intentional system around it.



A note on LSA (Google Local Services Ads)

We haven’t used LSA because in our area it’s often $60+ per call, and from what others report, it can bring a lot of price shoppers. Once we have more marketing budget to allocate, we’ll test it on marketing methods with a lower ROI like this - but it hasn’t been our focus.



Rule of thumb

A healthy cleaning business usually needs to spend at least 10% of gross revenue on marketing if you want consistent growth.

You can’t build stability on “posting and hoping.” You build it with lead engines you can measure.

Our methodology is to max out the highest ROI lead engines first before moving on to the next. If we had a $20k/mo marketing budget, we would likely be doing all of these... For now, we cherry pick the best.

## Key Principles

* Speed-to-lead wins deals
* Systems beat memory and motivation
* Sales happens on the phone, not the website
* Tracking determines what to scale
* CRM is the center of operations
