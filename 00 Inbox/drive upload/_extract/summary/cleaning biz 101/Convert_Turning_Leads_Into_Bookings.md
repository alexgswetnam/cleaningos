## Core Teaching
Convert is where the money gets made. Acquire's job is to get people to raise their hand; Convert's job is to make sure those raised hands actually become booked jobs.

The key insight is that most cleaning businesses don't lose deals because they're bad at cleaning. They lose deals because they respond too slow, they forget to follow up, they don't have a clear sales process, and they don't have a system that makes booking feel easy. These are sales and systems failures, not service failures.

The goal of Convert is simple: no lead gets lost. To make that real, every lead should have four things — a source, a status, a next action, and a follow-up plan. That structure is what keeps leads from slipping through the cracks.

When Convert is weak, marketing starts to feel "expensive," but the real problem is that you're wasting leads you already paid for. Close rate is the lever. With leads costing $40, a 10% close rate means $400 per booking, a 30% close rate means $133 per booking, and a 60% close rate means $66 per booking. Same lead cost, wildly different economics — the difference is conversion. (The example references a VA who hit a 60% close rate in December.)

Finally, sales skill matters and is where most owners fall short. From breaking down live sales calls in weekly sales trainings, the takeaway is that most cleaning business owners aren't good at sales — especially handling objections and overcoming "it's too pricey" or "I'm just looking around for quotes."
