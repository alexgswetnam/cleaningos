## Core Teaching

# Tracking \& Attribution

Why tracking matters

Every lead source feels “kinda good” until you look at the numbers.





You might think Thumbtack is crushing it… but your close rate is low and you’re paying too much per booking.



You might think PPC is expensive… but it closes higher and the cost per booking is actually better.



You might be posting nonstop in FB groups… and still not know if it’s doing anything.

Tracking makes marketing honest.

The metrics that actually matter

At minimum, you should know these 3 things for every lead engine:





Cost per lead (CPL)



Close rate (bookings ÷ leads)



Cost per booking (CPL ÷ close rate)

If you don’t know those, you can’t confidently decide where to put your time or money.

Attribution = “Where did this lead come from?”

Attribution is simply making sure every lead gets tagged with its source:





PPC



Thumbtack



GBP / map pack



Referral



Past client



Organic website



Yelp (if you’re testing it)



etc.

Because when leads come in, your brain will lie to you. You’ll feel like one channel is working more than another based on whatever happened most recently.

Attribution protects you from that.

The hidden win: follow-up attribution

Here’s the part most people miss:

A lead source isn’t just “where the lead came from.”
It’s also “how long did it take to convert?”

Some leads book same day. Others book 2 weeks later. Others respond 6 months later to an automated follow-up text.

If you aren’t tracking and tagging properly inside your CRM, you’ll shut off campaigns too early and kill future bookings you don’t even realize you’re getting.

What “good tracking” looks like (simple version)





Every lead enters your CRM with a source tag (so you know where the lead came from)



Every booking can be traced back to the source that started it



Your tracking sheet needs to be accurate, so update it daily with leads and results.



You review performance weekly/monthly and reallocate budget accordingly



Tracking isn’t “extra work.” It’s the difference between:

“I think this is working” vs “I know exactly what’s working, and I can scale it.”
