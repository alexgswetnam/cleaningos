## Core Teaching

### Cold Calling to Find Cleaners (Live Demo)
Alex demonstrates calling a cleaner found on Google Maps. The script is simple and direct:

"Hi there, do you do cleanings? Awesome. I'm in the [area] area. I've got a cleaning business and I have too many cleans. So I'm looking for other cleaners — would you be interested? Do you know anyone else? We need cleaners really bad."

When they ask about pay: "We give a percentage of the clean. Last week, three of our cleaners made more than $40 an hour because they're just good cleaners. I'm happy to have you talk to them."

When they're interested: "What we do from here is set up a time, like an interview. I don't want to take 30 minutes right now, but interviews — you can ask questions, I can give full information. We really need more cleaners because we have so much work. Can we book a time?"

Then text them to confirm the interview time. That's it — simple, human, and direct. You're not selling a job; you're telling them you have more work than you can handle and asking if they want in.

### Rashawn's Automated Hiring Pipeline (Go High Level / Harvest CRM)
Rashawn built a fully automated hiring pipeline and walked the group through it step by step. Alex asked to template it for the school.

**Application.** A form on the website (built in Go High Level's form builder, embedded on the site). Applicants fill it out and it feeds directly into the CRM's hiring pipeline. Questions cover experience, requirements, vehicle, etc. One automation: if they select anything but "car" for transportation, it's an automatic decline — saves time.

**Pipeline stages (each triggers automated emails):**

1. **New Application** — review in the CRM. Go to Sites → Forms → view their application. Check experience and requirements. If unqualified, tag the opportunity as "lost" → automation sends a rejection email and removes them.

2. **Schedule Interview** — drag the applicant here. Automation fires an email with a calendar link to schedule the interview. Calendar is connected to Google Calendar, so it shows up immediately.

3. **Interviewed** — move them here after the interview. This stage is a holding step.

4. **Schedule Onboarding** — drag here if moving forward. Automation fires an email with a calendar link to schedule the onboarding call.

5. **Document Collection** — drag here after onboarding goes well. Automation fires a sequence of emails requesting documents one at a time: first the ID upload (goes directly into their CRM contact), then W-9, then background check consent, then the agreement to sign. Each fires after the previous is submitted. All documents are stored in the CRM contact record.

6. **Rejection** — at any stage, tag as "lost" → automated rejection email fires.

The whole process is automated except for the actual interview conversation. Indeed applicants are directed to the website form. Documents upload directly into the contact in the CRM, so everything lives in one place.

Alex loved it and committed to templating it for other members. Courtney wanted it for her business. This later became the hiring pipeline shown in the April 1 and April 8 calls (Rick and Melissa's version).

### LSA Cost Per Lead by Area
LSA (Local Services Ads) cost varies wildly by area:
- Rashawn (Jersey City / Montgomery County): ~$19 per lead ($850 spend ÷ 45 leads). Great economics.
- Rick (his area): ~$22 per lead. Also great.
- Alex and Courtney (their areas): ~$70 per lead. Unprofitable for most.

At $19/lead, LSA is one of the best channels. At $70/lead, it's "undoable" unless your close rate and average ticket compensate. This is why "LSA works" and "LSA doesn't work" are both true — it depends entirely on your area.

### Text vs. Phone for LSA Leads
Text leads from LSA convert at a much lower rate than phone leads. Rashawn was getting more phone leads than text but could only respond to texts (he works a full-time job and can't take calls). He booked one job — from text. The phone leads were going unanswered.

Alex's recommendation: when you get a VA, turn off text for LSA entirely. Only take phone calls. Conversion is much higher. "People that text aren't as serious."

The VA is the bottleneck — Rashawn needs someone answering phones to unlock the leads he's already paying for. Options: onlinejobs.ph for Philippines VAs, or Bangladesh ($3–4/hour with surprisingly good English).

Courtney has four solid cleaners and hired one around Christmas. She's at capacity and knows she needs more, especially with a new account starting in March. But she's been avoiding it: "I'm avoiding you like the plague." Alex's response: "Yes, that's a win" — being at capacity and needing to hire is the right problem to have. She needs to face it. Rashawn's automated pipeline is exactly what she needs to make it less painful.

## Key Principles
- Cold calling cleaners from Google Maps works — the pitch is simple: "I have too many cleans, are you interested?"
- The hiring pipeline can and should be automated — each stage triggers the right email without manual effort.
- The only part that can't be automated is the actual interview.
- LSA cost per lead is heavily area-dependent — $19 in some areas, $70 in others.
- Phone leads convert much better than text leads on LSA.
- A VA answering phones is the unlock for leads you're already paying for.
- Being at capacity and needing to hire is the right problem to have — don't avoid it.
- A cleaner handbook / onboarding packet sets standards from day one.
- Documents should upload directly into the CRM contact so everything lives in one place.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Cold Calling Cleaners from Google Maps
1. Search Google Maps for cleaning services in your area.
2. Call them directly.
3. Script: "Hi, do you do cleanings? I've got a cleaning business in [area] and I have too many cleans. I'm looking for other cleaners — would you be interested? Do you know anyone else?"
4. When they ask about pay: "We give a percentage of the clean. Our cleaners made more than $40/hour last week."
5. If interested: "Let's set up a time for an interview. Can we book a time?"
6. Text them to confirm the interview.

### Automated Hiring Pipeline (CRM)
1. Build an application form in your CRM (Go High Level / Harvest). Embed it on your website.
2. Set up pipeline stages: New Application → Schedule Interview → Interviewed → Schedule Onboarding → Document Collection → (and a rejection stage).
3. Automate: moving to "Schedule Interview" sends a calendar-link email. Moving to "Schedule Onboarding" sends another calendar-link email. Moving to "Document Collection" triggers a sequence of emails requesting ID, W-9, background check, and agreement — one at a time.
4. Tagging as "lost" at any stage sends an automatic rejection email.
5. Add a filter: if they select anything but "car" for transportation, auto-decline.
6. All documents upload directly into the CRM contact record.
7. The only manual step: the actual interview conversation.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Not cold calling cleaners — it's simple, fast, and works.
- Running the entire hiring process manually when each stage can be automated with emails and calendar links.
- Assuming LSA works (or doesn't) without knowing your area's cost per lead — it varies from $19 to $70+.
- Leaving LSA text leads turned on when phone leads convert much better.
- Paying for LSA leads you can't answer because you don't have a VA.
- Avoiding hiring when you're at capacity — that's the right problem, face it.
- Not having a cleaner handbook setting standards from day one.
- Storing onboarding documents outside the CRM instead of in the contact record.
- Hiring a VA from Bangladesh without vetting their phone manner — one sent an unprofessional text to a lead who hung up.
