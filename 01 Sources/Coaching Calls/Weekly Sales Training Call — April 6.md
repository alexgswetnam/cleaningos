## Core Teaching

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) — Whiteboard Breakdown
The numbers come directly from BookingKoala's reports (Reports → Trends → Revenue per Booking and Average Recurring Bookings per Customer).

**Courtney's real numbers (2022–2026):**
- Average booking: $199
- Average number of bookings per customer: 12.82
- Revenue per customer (LTV): $199 × 12.82 = **$2,564**
- Average recurring bookings per customer: 24
- Revenue per recurring customer: $199 × 24 = **$4,800**

**Profit calculation:**
- Expenses: ~55–61% (50% labor + 5–11% overhead/software/phone/sales tax)
- At 61% expense: $2,564 × 0.61 = ~$1,600 in expenses
- **Profit per customer lifetime: ~$964**
- For recurring: roughly double (~$1,900+)

**What this means for marketing:**
Anything you spend to acquire a customer below $964 is profitable. So a $50 CAC? Extremely profitable — you make $914. A $145 Thumbtack CAC? Still very profitable — you make $819. Even $500 to acquire a recurring customer who generates $4,800 in revenue over their lifetime is worth it.

This is why marketing is an equation, not a guessing game. You know the number. You know what you can spend.

**Channel comparison using LTV:**
Rick's data showed Thumbtack at $145 CAC vs. LSA at $100 CAC. On the surface, LSA looks cheaper. But Thumbtack brought in a recurring client — and a recurring client at $4,800 LTV is worth far more than a one-time client at $199. So $145 to acquire a recurring client is a bargain compared to $100 to acquire a one-time.

**Industry benchmarks:**
From what Alex has seen, industry average LTV is around $2,000 per customer and ~$4,000 for recurring. Courtney's numbers ($2,564 and $4,800) are about 20% higher — her team is excellent at keeping clients.

**How to find your numbers:**
Go to BookingKoala → Reports → Trends. Set the date range as far back as your accurate data goes. Look at: revenue per booking, average bookings per customer, and average recurring bookings per customer.

### Why Recurring Clients Are the Real Money
A one-time customer brings $2,564. A recurring customer brings $4,800 — nearly double. This is why every lesson, every sales script, and every follow-up sequence pushes for recurring. The conversion to recurring is where profit compounds.

At scale: if Courtney doubles her cleaner count (3.5 → 7), she hypothetically doubles her revenue — but only if she can keep converting to recurring. The recurring base is what makes the business sustainable and valuable.

### Converting One-Time to Recurring — Three Strategies

**Strategy 1: Post-clean phone call (Alex's method).** Call right after the first clean. Ask how it went, what they'd rate it. If five stars, ask for a review (send the link while on the phone). Then: "Would you like [cleaner name] to come back? Are you looking for monthly or bi-weekly?" Assume the close. If they hesitate: "You do have a credit on the account — I don't want you to lose it. Want to go ahead and schedule?" If they don't book, the 60-day follow-up text catches them later.

**Strategy 2: Follow-up email with pricing (Courtney's method).** Send an email stating how much their recurring plan would cost — monthly price and bi-weekly price, already calculated. No sticker shock because they already know what the first clean cost. Common response: "Yeah, that's great. Do I get the same cleaner?" → "Yes, of course." → Booked.

**Strategy 3: 25% off recurring offer (Rick's method).** About a month after the first clean, send: "If you sign up for recurring, we'll give you 25% off." This catches people who loved the clean but didn't commit.

### AI Chatbots: Not Recommended for Sales in This Industry
Alex has explored it. Technically possible — you can program pricing logic, objection handling parameters, and even custom-plan negotiations. But the problems are fundamental: it's all over text (no urgency, no connection), close rates are dramatically lower (Jock's text-only approach: ~10% vs. 45–55% on phone calls), and you miss prospects you could have closed on a call.

The only valid use case: after-hours AI support for simple, non-sales questions. Example: a client texts at 9 PM panicking about a cancellation. An AI bot could respond with the cancellation policy and reassure them someone will call in the morning. That prevents client anxiety overnight. But AI for sales — answering pricing questions, handling objections, booking — doesn't work in this industry because humans always outperform, especially with older clients (60–80 year olds).

## Step-by-Step Process

### Calculating Your Customer LTV
1. Go to BookingKoala → Reports → Trends.
2. Set the date range as far back as your accurate data goes.
3. Find: average revenue per booking (e.g., $199).
4. Find: average number of bookings per customer (e.g., 12.82).
5. Multiply: $199 × 12.82 = $2,564 revenue per customer.
6. Find: average recurring bookings per customer (e.g., 24).
7. Multiply: $199 × 24 = $4,800 revenue per recurring customer.
8. Estimate your total expense percentage (labor + overhead, e.g., 55–61%).
9. Calculate profit: $2,564 − ($2,564 × 0.61) = ~$964 per customer.
10. Now you know: any marketing spend below $964 per acquired customer is profitable.

### Converting One-Time to Recurring
1. **Post-clean call:** Call after the first clean. Ask how it went. If great, ask for a review (send link on the phone). Then ask: "Would you like [cleaner] to come back? Monthly or bi-weekly?"
2. **Email with pricing:** Send a follow-up email with their specific monthly and bi-weekly pricing already calculated. Remove sticker shock — they already know the first-clean price.
3. **30-day discount offer:** A month later, send: "Sign up for recurring and get 25% off."
4. **60-day credit reminder:** If they still haven't rebooked, the automated CRM text reminds them they'll lose their credit.
