## Core Teaching

### Website Technical SEO Evaluation
Alex uses a browser extension (Detailed SEO Extension) to evaluate a member's website. Key issues found:

**H1 headings:** You should only have one H1 per page. This member had four. The H1 is the most important heading telling Google what the page is about (e.g., "Top-Rated House Cleaning Service in [City]"). H2s are secondary topics, H3s expand on H2s. Multiple H1s confuse Google about your page's focus.

**Schema markup:** The site had a local business schema (good — most don't), but it wasn't connected to the Google Business Profile and didn't include hours or other key details. Improving schema helps Google understand your business and rank you higher.

**Google Maps embed:** The site didn't have the Google Maps embed connected. To do this: go to your Google Business Profile → Share → Embed a map → Copy the HTML → Put it on your site. This connects your website to your GBP and helps you rank higher in the map pack.

**Service area pages:** The website had no location/service-area pages. Adding pages for each area you serve helps Google associate your business with those areas.

**Form fields:** The member's quote form had too many fields. Studies consistently show: fewer form fields = higher conversion rates. More fields = more friction = people clicking off. Alex's form is just name, phone, and optional message — then the VA calls within seconds. A deeper form gives you a more "qualified" lead, but you lose people who would have been clients if the form weren't so long. Better to capture everyone and qualify on the phone.

### Microsoft Clarity (Free Website Session Recordings)
Clarity is a free tool that records every visitor's session on your website — you can see exactly where they click, scroll, hover, and bounce. Alex showed a real recording: a visitor came from GBP, explored service options, clicked on housecleaning, read the checklist, scrolled through reviews, and eventually interacted with the contact form. This data shows where people drop off and helps you optimize your site for conversion. "Freaking spyware" but incredibly useful.

### Form Optimization: Fewer Fields = More Conversions
This is a documented law in marketing: increased form fields = decreased form submissions. Studies consistently show this. Alex's form is bare-bones: name, phone, optional message. The VA calls back within seconds. William's form had many fields and was losing people.

The counterargument: a deeper form pre-qualifies leads. But you're missing people who would have booked but bounced at the form. And price shoppers who fill out a long form just to compare quotes may close for the wrong reason and become bad clients. "It's better to have no client than to have a bad client."

A/B test suggestion: take the long form off for a week, replace with a simple one, and see if more people reach out. Use Clarity to watch where people drop off.

### Sales Happen on the Phone, Not Text
William's challenge: he can't get text-based sales to work. He sends a probing question ("Looks like you're looking for a quote on your 3-bed/2-bath in Lexington — is that correct?"), gets a yes, then tries to escalate to a phone call and loses people.

Alex's approach: VAs are instructed to "hammer their phone and get them on the phone." If the lead insists on text, push back, push back, push back. If they absolutely will only text, proceed, but expect almost zero conversions. "I expect basically no sales to happen through text because it's just super difficult." The member Jock does text-only and has a 10–15% conversion rate vs. the host's ~50% on calls. Jock optimizes differently (multiple businesses, wants efficiency), but for most owners, phone is the way.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Website SEO Quick Fixes
1. Install Detailed SEO Extension in your browser and check your site.
2. Fix H1 headings — only one H1 per page with your most important keyword (e.g., "Top-Rated House Cleaning Service in [City]").
3. Check your schema markup — make sure your local business schema is connected to your GBP with hours and contact info.
4. Embed Google Maps: GBP → Share → Embed a map → Copy HTML → Put on your site.
5. Add service-area/location pages for each area you serve.
6. Simplify your quote form to name, phone, and optional message. Remove all other fields.
7. Install Microsoft Clarity (free) to record visitor sessions and watch where people drop off.

### Connecting AI to Your CRM (Claude + Harvest)
1. Connect Claude to Harvest CRM (config takes ~5 minutes).
2. Prompt: "What leads are untouched that should have a task or status note?" — get a list of missed follow-ups.
3. Prompt: "What's our speed-to-lead, Monday–Friday 9–5?" — get a breakdown per lead.
4. Set up a daily report sent to your VA: flagged leads, overdue tasks, leads without notes.
5. Use it to write/improve email sequences and mass-text cold or canceled leads with promotions.
6. Upgrade from free plan when it runs out — one extra booking over 12 months justifies the cost.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Having multiple H1 headings on your website — Google can't tell what the page is about.
- Not embedding Google Maps from your GBP on your website.
- Having a long quote form that causes visitors to bounce before submitting.
- Not installing Clarity to see where website visitors drop off.
- Trying to close sales over text instead of getting leads on the phone.
- Not following up on overdue CRM tasks — leads go cold while sitting in the pipeline.
- Having only 10 interviews in two weeks when you're in "mission critical" labor mode.
- Not having terms of service with clients — cleaners can take them when they leave.
- Relying on non-competes without enforcement — "am I really going to go to court?"
- Posting to GBP once a month instead of every other day.
- Not uploading your logo to GBP.
