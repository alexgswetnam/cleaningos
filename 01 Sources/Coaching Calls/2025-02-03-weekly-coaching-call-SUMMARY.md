## Core Teaching

### The Three Numbers You Must Know
The foundational marketing numbers are cost per lead, close rate, and cost per acquisition (CPA). Add average ticket and you can determine whether your marketing is profitable or not.

**Cost per lead** = total spend ÷ total leads. **Close rate** = bookings ÷ leads. **CPA** (cost per acquisition) = cost per lead ÷ close rate. **Average ticket** = what you charge on average per clean.

Example: $55 cost per lead × 25% close rate = $220 CPA. If your average ticket is $220, 50% goes to labor, 20% to overhead — there's nothing left. Marketing looks broken but the problem could be cost per lead, close rate, average ticket, or all three.

Another example: a member had $40 cost per lead and thought he needed to stop marketing or switch channels. His problem was a 20% close rate. At 50% close rate, the same $40 cost per lead becomes $80 CPA, which is breakeven on a $300 average ticket at 35% profit — and then recurring revenue makes it profitable.

### Why Tracking Makes Everything Visible
Without tracking, you're making guesses. One member was stuck at $9K/month for a year and couldn't answer what his cost per lead or close rate was — so there was no way to diagnose the problem. Is marketing the problem? Is sales the problem? Is pricing the problem? You can't know without data.

The tracking sheet (available in Skool under Classroom → Free Resources → Sales and Marketing KPI Tracking Sheet) tracks every lead by source, status, and outcome. It produces analytics: cost per lead, close rate, and CPA by channel. The host's Q4: $3,000 total spend, 245 total leads, $12 blended cost per lead. Thumbtack: 90 leads, 51 free, $15.64 cost per lead, 26% close rate, $58 CPA. PPC: $1,500 spend, $43 cost per lead, 26% close rate, $100 CPA. Organic (GBP/web form): 119 leads, 70 bookings, $0 cost per lead, $0 CPA — this is what brings the blended number down.

### Month One Loses Money — And That's Normal
A live breakdown of one member's January: $1,500 lead spend, $3,000 in revenue from new leads, $1,500 to cleaners — they lost money on new client acquisition that month. But they made money because of recurring business from prior months. This is the typical pattern.

The host's own first month: $4,000 in marketing, ~$8,000 revenue, lost ~$1,500 after cleaner pay and overhead. Month two: $3,000 marketing spend, still lost money. Month three: $0 marketing spend, $4,000 in bookings from recurring clients. Month four: breakeven. Then profitable and growing.

### Recurring Revenue Is Where You Make Money
The real question isn't "did I profit on the first clean?" — it's "how much does this client generate over their lifetime?" One client (Shahana's Con) started in March, was still cleaning monthly in December — 12 cleans × $179, with $80 profit per clean = $960 in profit from one client. Was it worth $100, $200, even $300 to acquire that client? Absolutely.

Another member calculated: $560 LSA spend → 31 leads → 22% close rate → 7 bookings → 3 recurring → $3,500 in recurring revenue over the next 4 months. So $560 in marketing generates $3,500+ downstream. That's why you can lose money month one and still win.

### Rank Channels by Profitability and Max Each One
Don't spread money across every channel. Find the most profitable one, max it out, then move to the next. The host's ranking: (1) Organic — always most profitable, $0 cost, highest close rate (people already sold themselves on you). (2) Thumbtack — with free leads method, very profitable. (3) PPC (Google Ads) — $100 CPA in their area, sustainable, scalable. (4) LSA — $60–$70 cost per lead in their area, expensive but works in cheaper markets ($18/lead in one member's area). Facebook/Meta — $4–$15 cost per lead but only 3–5% close rate; leads convert over time (months later), so you need long-term tracking.

PPC is recommended as the first paid channel for most people. The host doesn't personally know anyone who's done Google Ads PPC and hasn't been happy with it. One example: a friend in Australia spends $20K/month on Google Ads and does $200K/month in revenue, less than three years in business.

### Organic Lowers Your Blended Cost Per Lead
As one member pointed out: if you're spending $40/lead on paid but half your leads are organic, your blended cost per lead drops to $20. This is why GBP optimization and organic SEO matter alongside paid — they make your entire funnel more profitable.

### Close Rate Varies By Channel
Highest close rate: referrals (friend recommending you). Second highest: organic inbound (they searched, found you, spent 10–30 minutes on your website, and called — they already sold themselves). PPC: moderate. LSA: moderate. Meta/Facebook: lowest (3–5%), but leads can convert months later with follow-up. Thumbtack: moderate (~26–29%). Track close rate per channel, not just overall.

### Thumbtack Follow-Up Tactics
When a Thumbtack lead comes in: (1) Immediately message in Thumbtack — they measure your response time. (2) Call twice (or even three times if it looks like a serious lead — not a 1-bed/1-bath). (3) Leave a voicemail. (4) Text: "Hey, I just tried you. You just reached out. When's a better time to call?" (5) Let the CRM handle ongoing follow-up.

If someone gets upset about multiple calls: "Am I bothering you? You just reached out to us and I wanted to get ahold of you." If they're negative, they probably wouldn't have been a good client anyway.

No-contact rate is a tracked metric. Q4: 6 out of 90 Thumbtack leads never responded, 5 out of 36 PPC leads never responded. If the no-contact number is high, something's wrong — the host found a Google Ads "search network partner" setting that was generating bad leads and turned it off.

### PPC Landing Page / Website Strategy
The host sends PPC traffic directly to their website (not a squeeze page or separate landing page). Reasons: (1) The website converts well on its own. (2) PPC traffic going to the site helps SEO — Google sees more traffic, so the site ranks higher organically, creating a virtuous cycle. A squeeze page on a separate domain wouldn't give that SEO benefit.

They use Microsoft Clarity to record user sessions on the site — you can see where the mouse moves, where they scroll, how long they stay. Many PPC visitors spend 10–20–30 minutes reviewing the site before calling. By that time, they've already sold themselves.

Conversion tracking: UTM campaign builder tags PPC links (e.g., thefaithfulcleaners.com?campaign=PPC1). A cookie plugin captures the UTM data and submits it with the lead form, so every lead can be attributed to its source. Phone call clicks are tracked via Google Ads conversion tracking. This is how they know exactly which leads came from PPC vs. organic vs. GBP.

Jock in Florida: $7 cost per click, 30% conversion rate, $21–$25 cost per conversion. The host: $18 cost per click, 42% conversion rate. One member's area: $18 cost per lead on LSA — the host would spend $5,000/month at that price.

### Why Cleaning Is Harder Than High-Ticket
Comparison to roofing: $20K average ticket, 50% to subs, $100 cost per lead, 5% close rate = $2,000 CPA, but $10K profit after subs = $8K net. In cleaning, average tickets are $200–$400, so margins are much tighter and marketing must be locked down. This is why most owners under $20K are stuck — they don't understand marketing at a foundational level.

### Airbnb Clients
The host stays away from Airbnb cleans for two reasons: (1) Fast turnaround — someone can message the day before. (2) Airbnb owners are optimizing for cheapest possible clean because it's a cash-generating machine for them, so you can't charge full price. Better to keep that schedule slot open for a full-price client.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Diagnosing Your Marketing Health
1. Start using the tracking sheet (Skool → Classroom → Free Resources → Sales and Marketing KPI Tracking Sheet). Log every lead with source, status, and outcome.
2. Calculate cost per lead for each channel (spend ÷ leads).
3. Calculate close rate for each channel (bookings ÷ leads).
4. Calculate CPA for each channel (spend ÷ bookings, or cost per lead ÷ close rate).
5. Know your average ticket.
6. Determine profit per clean (average ticket minus labor, overhead, and marketing cost).
7. If CPA is higher than profit per clean, identify the problem: is it cost per lead (too high)? Close rate (too low)? Average ticket (too low)?
8. Fix the identified problem: cost per lead → optimize channel settings, switch channels. Close rate → improve sales (scripts, training, phone skills). Average ticket → adjust pricing.
9. Factor in recurring revenue: what percent of one-time clients convert to recurring? Over how many months do they stay? What's the lifetime value?
10. Once you know the numbers hold over 2–3 months, increase spend confidently — it's an equation, not a guess.

### Marketing Channel Prioritization
1. Maximize organic (GBP optimization, SEO) — highest close rate, $0 cost.
2. Thumbtack with free leads method — filter to recurring only, use prepaid card + unlimited setting.
3. PPC (Google Ads) — set up correctly, track conversions, scale spend as numbers confirm profitability.
4. LSA — varies by area; great if cost per lead is low ($18–$42), expensive in some markets ($60–$70+).
5. Facebook/Meta — low cost per lead ($4–$15) but very low close rate (3–5%); leads convert over months with follow-up.

### Thumbtack Lead Follow-Up
1. Immediately message in Thumbtack the moment the lead comes in (response time is tracked).
2. Call the lead — twice if needed, three times for serious-looking leads.
3. Leave a voicemail.
4. Text: "Hey, I just tried you. You just reached out. When's a better time to call?"
5. If someone's upset about multiple calls: "Am I bothering you? You just reached out to us. I wanted to get ahold of you."
6. Let the CRM handle ongoing follow-up automatically.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Not tracking leads, cost per lead, close rate, or CPA — making it impossible to diagnose problems.
- Blaming marketing when the problem is close rate (or vice versa).
- Panicking when month one of new client acquisition loses money — that's normal; recurring revenue makes the math work.
- Pocketing profit early instead of reinvesting in marketing during the growth phase.
- Spending on Thumbtack without filtering out unprofitable job types (1-bed/1-bath, one-times) — one member spent $220 in a day on four bad leads.
- Starting Thumbtack lead price slider at the top instead of working from the bottom up.
- Not using the free leads cycling method on Thumbtack.
- Classifying leads as "organic" when you don't actually know where they came from — misattribution.
- Running Google Ads with "search network partners" turned on — generates unqualified leads and no-contacts.
- Only texting Thumbtack leads instead of calling (and calling multiple times).
- Sending PPC traffic to a squeeze page on a separate domain instead of your main website (loses the SEO benefit).
- Taking Airbnb clients at below-rate pricing when that slot could go to a full-price client.
- Not converting one-time clients to recurring — this is where the real profit lives.
- Spending $6,000 on a course that doesn't teach you about sales tax or marketing math.
