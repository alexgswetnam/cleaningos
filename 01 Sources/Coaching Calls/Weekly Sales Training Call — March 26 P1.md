## Core Teaching

### Reading and Mirroring the Prospect
Within the first 30 seconds, read who you're talking to. Marcella was Type A — short responses, direct, professional, sounded like she wanted to get off the phone immediately. The response: mirror her. Match her tone, pace, and level of professionalism. Don't be chatty or slow. Don't drag words.

We like people who are more like us. Over the phone, mirroring is tone, pace, and professionalism. If you're talking to aunt Sherry who wants to chat, you chat. If you're talking to a boardroom boss who walks in heels, you're crisp and direct.

Time of day matters: this call was at 5 PM. She's probably done with work, maybe tired, maybe has dinner plans. Adjust.

### Don't Take Orders — Diagnose Like a Doctor
"What type of clean are you looking for?" is like a doctor handing you a menu of treatments without knowing your problem. An untrained VA asks: "Are you looking for a deep clean or a standard clean or a move-in?" — that's taking orders, not selling.

Instead: "What's going on? What's the situation? What's got you reaching out today?" Let them tell you. Then you diagnose. Open-ended questions let them drive and give you information to work with.

For a Type A person who gives short answers: they'll give you a quick summary. That's fine — you don't need a 10-minute story. You just need enough to diagnose and prescribe.

### Building Micro-Commitments Before Price
Never go straight from "what's your square footage" to "here's the price." Build a chain of small yeses first:

"What day would work best for you?" → "Thursday or Friday." → "Let me check if we even have availability — we're pretty packed." → "I've got this Thursday at 2 PM — would that work?" → "Yes." → "Morning or afternoon?" → "Afternoon." → "Great. Is it a gated community or do we need a code?" → "It's gated, here's the code."

By the time you give the price, they've said yes to the day, the time, the logistics. They're mentally booked. The price is just the final confirmation, not the start of a negotiation.

Then: "It's going to be $180 plus tax. We don't charge until after the clean because we want to make sure you're satisfied. Whenever you're ready, I can grab your card."

### Price Delivery: Confidence and Tonality
The biggest issue on Jack's call: upward inflection when giving the price. "Is that something you're comfortable moving forward with?" sounds like an apology — like you're hoping they'll say yes.

**Downward inflection** = confidence. The price is a statement, not a question. "It's just going to be $180 plus tax" — said with a period at the end, not a question mark.

**Chris Voss tonality techniques:**
- **Late night DJ voice** (slow, steady, deep): use for non-negotiables. "We cannot get on ladders. At all." No room for negotiation. That's just reality.
- **Making something land heavy**: "This is going to... change your life." Pause. Weight. Space.
- **Making something land lightly** (for prices): "It's just going to be five thousand dollars. Want to go ahead and get started?" The word "just" minimizes it. Your tone is casual. It's not a big deal.

Don't say: "I have my quote here at 180 plus tax... is that something you're comfortable moving forward with?" (upward, weak, question).

Do say: "So it's just going to be 180 plus tax. Go ahead and grab your card when you're ready." (downward, confident, assumed close).

**Practice:** Record yourself. Stand in front of a mirror. Say the price out loud until it sounds natural and confident. It will feel dumb — do it anyway.

**Actual confidence in your pricing:** Technique alone isn't enough. Do market research — call competitors, compare pricing, confirm your price is fair. When you genuinely believe your price is a good deal, your voice reflects it naturally. If you feel like you're overcharging, your prospect will feel it too.

### Reciprocity When Dropping Price
Anytime you reduce a price, frame it as a personal favor — not a standard discount. This triggers reciprocity: they feel like they owe you something back (loyalty, tipping, reviews).

**The pet fee example:** System shows $25 pet fee. Instead of silently removing it, say: "Usually we'd charge the $25 for pets, but Marcella, it sounds like you take really good care of your home. I don't mind taking that off for you." She's grateful. She feels special. She reciprocates later.

**The square footage example:** Home is 3,050 sqft (rounds up to a higher tier). "I don't usually do this, but honestly, I like you. I'm going to round that down and take some off the price. Don't tell my boss." (You are the boss — but the humor works.)

**The time reduction:** "Usually it's four hours at this price, but since your home is well-maintained and you've had a cleaner, I don't mind — if our cleaner gets done in three and a half, I'll reduce the price to reflect that."

This only works if you've built connection first. If the whole call was "beds? baths? square footage? price." and then you try "I like you, I'll drop the price" — it sounds like a sales tactic because there was no relationship to base it on.

### "Is It Just a Price Thing?"
When the prospect hesitates after the price, don't guess. Ask directly: "Is it just a price thing?"

This is disarmingly direct. If they say yes, you know exactly what to solve. If they say no (they need to talk to their spouse, or they want to compare), you can handle that specific objection.

Once they confirm it's price: "If I can find a fair price for you, would you be able to book right now?" Get the buy-in before negotiating. Then build a custom solution (cap the time, rotate rooms, skip the basement).

Jack was two questions away from closing this weekly recurring client. Marcella was giving buying signals ("I don't really use my basement," "if we could do it every other week"). She was already negotiating the scope — which means she wanted to buy. Jack just didn't close.

### Custom Solutions for Price Reduction
Instead of just lowering the number, restructure the service:

"Why don't we cap the time at three hours for this price? We'll focus on kitchens and bathrooms — the high-touch areas — and do the rest as we're able during that time."

"What if we did the basement every other week instead of every week? One week vacuum the living room, next week the basement. That keeps it manageable and keeps your price where you need it."

The prospect co-creates the plan. They feel ownership of the solution. It's not a discount — it's a custom package built for them.

## Key Principles
- Read and mirror the prospect's personality within the first 30 seconds.
- Don't take orders ("what type of clean?") — diagnose like a doctor.
- Build a chain of micro-commitments (day, time, logistics) before giving the price.
- Deliver price with downward inflection — a statement, not a question.
- "Just" makes big numbers land lightly: "It's just going to be $180."
- Frame every price reduction as a personal favor to trigger reciprocity.
- "Is it just a price thing?" is the most direct and useful question when they hesitate.
- Get buy-in before negotiating: "If I find a fair price, would you book right now?"
- You need genuine confidence in your pricing — do the market research.
- Never be the one to end the conversation (two questions away from closing).
- Practice price delivery in front of a mirror until it sounds natural.

## Step-by-Step Process

### The Micro-Commitment Close
1. Gather info naturally (beds, baths, sqft, situation, dirtiness level).
2. Before giving the price, check availability: "Let me see if we can even fit you in — we're pretty packed."
3. Get day commitment: "What day works best for you?"
4. Get time commitment: "Morning or afternoon?"
5. Get specific time: "I've got Thursday at 2 PM — would that work?"
6. Get logistics: "Gated community? Key or code?"
7. Recap: "So we've got 3 bed, 2 bath, 2000 sqft, Thursday at 2 PM."
8. Deliver price with confidence: "It's just going to be $180 plus tax."
9. Assumed close: "We don't charge until after the clean. Whenever you're ready, I can grab your card."
10. If they hesitate: "Is it just a price thing?"
11. If yes: "If I can find a fair price for you, would you be able to book right now?"
12. Build custom solution together (cap time, rotate rooms, skip low-use areas).
