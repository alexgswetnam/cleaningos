# Upload Leads and Contacts to Harvest CRM

Upload Leads & Contacts to Harvest CRM

## Problems This Solves
- They have old leads sitting in another system and do not know how to import them.
- They have one-time clients who never became recurring and want Harvest CRM to follow up automatically.
- They have recurring clients they want organized in the CRM without accidentally texting them promotional follow-ups.

## Quick Summary
This lesson teaches how to upload existing leads and customers into Harvest CRM when a business is already established. The student should export customers from their current platform, such as Booking Koala, Housecall Pro, Jobber, ScheduleDrop, or ZenMaid, and organize them in a sheet. The key step is separating contacts by booking history: people who never booked, one-time clients, and recurring clients. One-time clients and old leads can be added to workflows that follow up automatically. Recurring clients are simply tagged and placed in the booked recurring stage without being texted. The lesson also warns that A2P should be set up quickly because imported leads may start receiving texts after 14 days. The goal is to make the CRM start working on old opportunities without dumping everyone into the same bucket.

## Core Teaching
Harvest CRM can be set up with existing contacts from another platform without making it complicated. The student first exports their customers or leads from whatever system they already use. The list should be sorted by number of bookings so it is easy to separate people who never booked, people who booked one time or maybe twice but are not recurring, and people who are actual recurring clients.

Once those groups are separated, they should be imported into Harvest CRM and mapped to the proper fields. The important part is not just importing the contacts. The important part is sending each group into the correct workflow or pipeline stage.

Old leads who did not book should go into the did-not-book workflow and long-term follow-up. One-time clients should go into the one-time clean workflow so the system can follow up with them over the next year. Recurring clients should go into the booked recurring stage, but they should not be texted or placed into a reactivation sequence.

A2P setup matters because the automated texts do not go out immediately. The CRM waits 14 days and then drips texts slowly so the number does not get flagged and the business does not get overloaded all at once.

## Key Principles
- Do not import every contact into the same workflow.
- Sort contacts by number of bookings before importing.
- Separate did-not-book leads, one-time clients, and recurring clients.
- One-time clients should be followed up with automatically because they may still rebook later.
- Old leads who never booked should go into long-term follow-up.
- Recurring clients should be organized in the CRM but not blasted with follow-up texts.
- A2P approval should be completed as soon as possible because SMS automations depend on it.
- Dripping texts slowly protects the phone number and prevents a sudden flood of replies.

## Step-by-Step Process
- Export customers or leads from the current platform, such as Booking Koala, Housecall Pro, Jobber, ScheduleDrop, or ZenMaid.
- Open the export in a sheet.
- Sort contacts by the number of bookings they have had with the business.
- Separate old leads who never booked, one-time clients, and recurring clients into the correct groups.
- Save the file for the group you are importing.
- Go to the Contacts tab inside Harvest CRM.
- Click Import Contacts.
- Upload the file and match the fields, such as email, phone, and date booked.
- Choose the correct workflow for the imported group: did not book, one-time clean, or recurring.
- Start the bulk import.
- Make sure A2P setup is completed as soon as possible so SMS follow-up can work properly.
- Review the pipeline after import to confirm contacts landed in the correct stage.

- "Booked recurring" for current recurring clients.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Uploading all contacts together without sorting them first.
- Putting recurring clients into old-lead follow-up automations.
- Forgetting to import one-time clients into a sequence that can win them back.
- Not matching fields properly during import.
- Waiting too long to complete A2P setup after importing contacts.
- Texting too many old leads all at once and risking the number getting flagged as spam.
- Assuming old leads are dead instead of putting them into a long-term follow-up system.

## Cleaned Lesson Notes
If a business is already established, Harvest CRM should be set up with the leads and clients it already has. This includes old leads that never booked, one-time clients, people who booked once or twice but never became recurring, and recurring clients.

Start by exporting the customer list from the current platform. This could be Booking Koala, Housecall Pro, Jobber, ScheduleDrop, ZenMaid, or another scheduling system. Export the customers into a sheet and sort them by the number of bookings they have had with the business.

From there, separate the contacts into groups:

- Leads who never booked.
- One-time clients or people who booked only once or twice but are not recurring.
- Recurring clients.

Each group should be imported separately so it can go to the right workflow. In Harvest CRM, go to the Contacts tab, click import contacts, upload the file, and match the fields. Match obvious fields like email, phone number, and date booked. Do not import fields that do not matter.

When importing, choose the correct workflow:

- Old leads who never booked should go into the did-not-book workflow.
- One-time clients should go into the one-time clean workflow.
- Recurring clients should go into the booked recurring stage.

For did-not-book leads, the CRM adds a database upload tag and puts them into long-term follow-up. They are not texted immediately. They are texted in 14 days, and the texts are dripped slowly instead of all at once. This helps prevent the number from being flagged as spam and also prevents the team from suddenly being overwhelmed by too many replies at once.

For one-time clients, the logic is similar. They are tagged as booked for automation reasons and placed into a long-term 12-month sequence that follows up with them over time.

For recurring clients, they are placed in the booked recurring stage. They are not sent texts and nothing is changed with them other than being organized in the CRM.

A2P setup should be completed as soon as possible because the follow-up texts depend on it. The lesson emphasizes getting A2P approved early because it can take about a week, and using the provided template should make approval much easier.
