# Cleaning Biz 101 — Acquire

## 1. Acquire: Building a Lead Engine

This part of the business is your lead engine — the stuff that makes strangers become
interested enough to raise their hand. It's about getting in front of the right people,
building trust quickly, and giving them a clear next step (call, quote request, booking
link, etc.).

If Acquire is weak, everything else feels stressful because you're constantly trying to
"find business" instead of simply working the system.

The goal here is predictable lead flow: you know where leads come from, you know what a
"good lead" looks like, and you have a clean path for turning attention into contact info.

## 2. Lead Engine: Free vs. Paid Marketing

Most cleaning businesses don't actually have a lead engine.

If you want to operate like a real business (not a side hustle), you need inbound leads
showing up consistently, even when you're busy, tired, or not in the mood to "go hunt."

There are really only two buckets for your lead engine:

**Free marketing** — posting in FB groups, asking friends for referrals, commenting/
networking/word of mouth, organic posts. Free is fine… but it's unpredictable. It depends
on your energy, your time, and your motivation. Miss a week? Leads slow down. Get busy?
Leads slow down. Free marketing depends on manual effort rather than a lead engine.

**Paid marketing** is where a cleaning business starts acting like a machine. It's you
deciding: "I want leads every day, so I'm going to pay for attention." Paid marketing
gives you something free marketing almost never can: control — over how many people see
you, over consistency, over scale.

The hard truth: if you don't have paid marketing, you don't really have a lead engine, you
have a social strategy. Which can help… but it can't carry the business.

## 3. The Best Paid Marketing Methods

Here are the best lead engines for a cleaning business, based on what's actually been
tested. The big idea: you don't "try marketing." You build a lead engine you can predict —
tracking cost per lead, close rate, and cost per booking (Free Tracking Sheet in Free
Resources).

**The below numbers are ACTUAL statistics from 2025 Q4 (Oct–Dec):**

1. **PPC (Google Ads)** — solid and predictable when built right. $43 cost per lead, 41%
   close rate, ~$104 cost per booking. Captures intent — people are actively searching.
2. **Thumbtack** — cheap bookings if run correctly. ~$15 cost per lead (factoring in the
   free-leads hack), 26% close rate, ~$58 cost per booking. Especially good because you
   can target recurring service specifically, not just one-time deep cleans.
3. **Local Map Pack SEO (GBP)** — best source. 115 leads in Q4, 67 booked. "Free," but
   best results usually need a Google business address ($50–$200/month depending on
   city; $80/location, two locations run). Not magic — requires website optimization and
   consistent posting.
4. **Facebook Ads** — failed so far. $1,000+ spent for only 2 bookings. Retesting with
   two friends getting under $100 cost per booking; results to be shared once tests
   complete.
5. **Yelp** — avoid. Sneaky fees, a "free trial" to watch out for. Hasn't heard one
   cleaning business owner say anything consistently good about it.
6. **Past clients + old leads** — the hidden lead engine most people ignore. Requires a
   CRM sending long-term automated follow-up (2 months, 6 months, even 12 months).
   Regularly gets bookings from one-time clients from months ago and old leads replying
   3–6 months later to an automated follow-up text.
7. **Referrals** — powerful, not yet fully tapped. Working on a more intentional referral
   system.

**LSA (Google Local Services Ads)** — not used. Often $60+/call in this area, and can
bring a lot of price shoppers per other reports. Would test once more marketing budget is
available, but hasn't been the focus.

**Rule of thumb:** a healthy cleaning business usually needs to spend at least 10% of
gross revenue on marketing for consistent growth. Methodology: max out the highest-ROI
lead engines first before moving to the next.

## 4. Tracking & Attribution

Every lead source feels "kinda good" until you look at the numbers. Tracking makes
marketing honest.

**The metrics that matter, at minimum, for every lead engine:**
- Cost per lead (CPL)
- Close rate (bookings ÷ leads)
- Cost per booking (CPL ÷ close rate)

**Attribution** = making sure every lead gets tagged with its source (PPC, Thumbtack,
GBP/map pack, referral, past client, organic website, Yelp, etc.). Without it, your brain
lies to you — you'll feel like whatever channel produced the most recent win is "the one
that's working."

**The hidden win: follow-up attribution.** A lead source isn't just where the lead came
from — it's also how long it took to convert. Some leads book same day, others 2 weeks
later, others 6 months later off an automated follow-up text. Without tracking and tagging
properly inside the CRM, campaigns get shut off too early, killing future bookings that
were actually coming.

**What good tracking looks like:** every lead enters the CRM with a source tag; every
booking traces back to its source; the tracking sheet is updated daily; performance is
reviewed weekly/monthly and budget reallocated accordingly.

## 5. Website: Build Trust, Don't Sell

Your website isn't where sales happen — it's where trust happens. Most sales still happen
on the phone.

**Why not instant quotes:** they attract price shoppers (a number trains people to
compare you like a commodity) and they remove context (a phone call lets you ask
questions, set expectations, build trust, match them to the right service — that's how
you win higher-quality recurring clients). If someone wants a number without talking,
they're usually not your best client anyway.

**Why not long forms:** they feel professional but kill conversions — reaching out
shouldn't feel like homework. Prefer a short, frictionless form: name, phone, and an
optional quick note. The goal of the website is not to perfectly qualify the lead — it's
to capture it and let follow-up and the phone call do the real work.

**The website's three jobs:** build trust quickly (reviews, photos, clear offer,
policies); make it easy to reach you (call + simple form); route leads into the CRM so
follow-up is automatic.

**Why WordPress:** the most flexible platform for SEO. For long-term, compounding lead
flow (especially local map pack + organic search), a fully-optimizable site matters —
page speed, on-page SEO, service area pages, schema, internal linking, technical fixes.
All-in-one website builders are fine for a basic presence but can cap what's controllable
SEO-wise.
