Quick Summary
Respond fast, follow up consistently, and document every touch. Use simple pipeline stages—New Lead, Follow-Up, Quoted, Booked, Cancelled/Declined, Do Not Contact. Build a multi-touch follow-up schedule across hours, days, and months. Even a spreadsheet can work if you track status, last contact, next touch, and notes.

Core Teaching
You don’t need fancy tools—just a disciplined system. Speed plus consistency wins. Respect preferences and stop when asked. Keep notes tight so any teammate can pick up the thread.

Key Principles
Respond within minutes to hours.
Multiple follow-ups before giving up.
Document all calls, texts, emails, and notes.
Respect DNC requests immediately.
Step-by-Step Process
Check for New Leads Daily
Review inboxes and forms; reply within 30 minutes when possible.
Initial Contact
Friendly, professional opener; ask about needs and timing.
Follow-Up Schedule
4–6 hours, then 24–48 hours, then 30–90 days for long-term.
Track Leads
Name, contact, service interest, last contact date, next follow-up, status, notes.
Booked Leads
Confirm details; assign staff; record service/special requests.
Do Not Contact
Mark immediately; cease outreach.
Weekly Review
Sweep for stale leads; schedule next actions; update statuses.
Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
“Hi [Name]! Thanks for reaching out about [Service]. When would be a good time to chat about your cleaning needs?”
“Just checking in—happy to answer any questions about [Service]!”
“Are you still interested in scheduling your cleaning?”
Common Mistakes / Warnings
One-and-done outreach.
No next action date.
Ignoring DNC or preferences.
