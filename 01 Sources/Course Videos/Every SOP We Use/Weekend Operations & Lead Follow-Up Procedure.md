Quick Summary
Weekends shouldn’t be idle—they’re a lever for bookings, retention, and clean Mondays. A strong weekend team handles three functions at once: reactive support (calls, texts, cleaner issues), proactive sales (follow up old and quoted leads, push recurring, fill gaps), and operational maintenance (clean CRM, review appointments, catch conflicts, improve routes). Prioritize fast responses, disciplined follow-ups with clear documentation, and schedule reviews that prevent surprises.

Core Teaching
Treat weekends like three departments combined: support, sales, and operations. React fast to inbound messages, then switch to proactive lead follow-up and schedule optimization. Use downtime to clean your CRM, review the pipeline, verify confirmations, and remove bottlenecks before Monday.

Key Principles
Responsiveness wins: reply quickly and return missed calls.
Proactive beats passive: follow up, attempt closes, and fill gaps.
Discipline in CRM: document every touch with next steps and status updates.
Prevention mindset: fix conflicts and unassigned jobs before they explode.
Step-by-Step Process
Handle Inbound Calls & Messages
Convert inquiries, support clients, assist cleaners, prevent missed leads; respond quickly, check voicemail, return calls promptly, keep communication professional.
Manage New Leads
Contact fast; gather scope details; provide estimate; attempt to secure booking; update CRM accurately (source, notes, status).
Lead Follow-Up Workflow
Review pipeline; prioritize recent leads, recurring opportunities, higher-ticket jobs, engaged prospects.
Contact lead: reconnect, answer questions, offer scheduling support, attempt to book.
If no answer: leave voicemail, send text, update CRM notes, schedule the next follow-up.
Document each follow-up: date/time, outcome, notes, concerns, next action, updated status.
Review Upcoming Schedule
Identify and fill open gaps; resolve staffing conflicts; prevent double bookings; improve travel efficiency; assign any unassigned jobs.
Support Cleaning Teams
Clarify schedules; solve access issues; communicate with clients; handle supply/time adjustments.
Monitor Client Experience
Scan for complaints, cancellations, unanswered messages, negative reviews, and retention risks; act on them.
Review Unconfirmed Appointments
Verify client and cleaner confirmations, entry instructions, and payment methods.
CRM Cleanup
Merge duplicates, update statuses, improve notes, remove inactive prospects, ensure accurate tagging.
Productivity Expectations
Balance support, lead conversion, and operational organization; use downtime to strengthen scheduling efficiency.
Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
“Hi [Lead Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company Name]. I wanted to follow up regarding your cleaning inquiry and see if you had any questions or wanted help scheduling a cleaning.”
Voicemail + text after no-answer with next follow-up scheduled.
Common Mistakes / Warnings
Treating weekends as “answer phones only.”
Not documenting follow-ups with next action and status.
Ignoring schedule gaps, conflicts, and unassigned jobs until Monday.
Letting leads go cold due to slow response.
