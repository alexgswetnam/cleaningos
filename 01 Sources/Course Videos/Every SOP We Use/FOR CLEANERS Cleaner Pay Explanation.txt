Skool Lesson: FOR CLEANERS: Cleaner Pay Explanation

🧠 The Goal

Your pay structure should:





Attract and retain good cleaners



Protect your profit margins



Stay simple enough to manage



Scale as you grow

If you get this wrong, you’ll get:





High turnover



Complaints about pay



Thin margins



Constant operational headaches



⚙️ The Core Principle



Pay per job, not per hour — and keep it performance-based.

Why?





Aligns incentives (faster + better work = better earnings)



Keeps your costs predictable



Scales better than hourly pay



🔁 The 4-Part Compensation System



1. Base Pay Structure (Per Job)

Cleaners are paid:



A percentage of each job’s revenue

This percentage can vary based on:





Job type (standard vs deep clean)



Difficulty



Market conditions

👉 Important:





This is project-based pay, not hourly wages



Cleaners are paid per completed job



2. Adjustments for Scope Changes

Sometimes jobs aren’t what you expected.

When that happens:





You may increase pay for that specific job



This is done at your discretion

Examples:





Client adds extra rooms/tasks



Condition is worse than expected



Access or setup slows the job significantly

👉 Key rule:

These are one-time adjustments, not guaranteed bonuses



3. Add-Ons, Upsells & Tips





Add-ons (inside oven, fridge, etc.) should:





Increase job price



Increase cleaner pay proportionally



Tips:





Should go directly to the cleaner



Paid on your normal payout schedule



4. Cancellations & Edge Cases

Define policies for:





Same-day cancellations



No-access jobs



Partial completions

👉 Important:

Only compensate for these if your policy allows it



⚠️ Protecting Your Business

No Guaranteed Work





Cleaners are not guaranteed:





Jobs



Hours



Earnings



Set Clear Expectations

Cleaners should understand:





They can accept or decline jobs



They provide their own tools (if applicable)



Pay is tied to completed work



Follow Local Laws





Always structure pay in compliance with:





Contractor vs employee rules



Local labor laws



🧾 Tracking & Transparency

Provide cleaners with:





Job summaries



Earnings breakdowns



Any adjustments or deductions

👉 Transparency reduces disputes



🔥 Pro Tip

The best companies don’t just pay well…

They make pay feel fair, consistent, and predictable.

💰 CLEANER COMPENSATION SOP



Step 1: Base Pay Structure





Cleaners are paid per completed job



Compensation is based on:





A percentage of job revenue



Percentage may vary by:





Job type



Difficulty



Market conditions



Step 2: Handle Scope Adjustments

If job conditions differ significantly from expectations:

You may apply a one-time pay adjustment when:





Additional work is added on-site



Property condition is worse than expected



Access or setup delays the job

All adjustments:





Are discretionary



Apply only to that specific job



Step 3: Add-Ons & Upsells





When a client adds services:





Increase job price



Increase cleaner compensation proportionally



Step 4: Tips





Tips are passed directly to the cleaner



Paid on the standard payment schedule



Step 5: Cancellations & No-Access

Follow company policy for:





Same-day cancellations



No-access situations

Compensation for these cases:





Only applies if defined in your policy



Step 6: Payment Schedule





Pay cleaners on a consistent schedule (e.g., weekly)



Provide a breakdown including:





Job earnings



Add-ons



Tips



Adjustments



Step 7: Expectations

Cleaners:





May accept or decline jobs



Are paid per job (not hourly)



Are not guaranteed a minimum number of jobs



Step 8: Compliance





Ensure compensation structure follows:





Local labor laws



Contractor/employee classification rules



Step 9: Documentation

Maintain records for:





Completed jobs



Payments issued



Adjustments made



🚨 Rules





Do not switch between hourly and per-job pay randomly



Do not guarantee earnings unless structured properly



Do not ignore scope issues — address them quickly



Keep compensation consistent and transparent
