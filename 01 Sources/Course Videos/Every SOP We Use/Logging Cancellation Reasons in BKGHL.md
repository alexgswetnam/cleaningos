Quick Summary
Capture the reason when a client cancels, then record it cleanly in both the booking system and CRM. Use concise, specific wording and always include the date and your name. If the reason is temporary (travel, renovation, moving), create a follow-up task. Run a weekly quality check to ensure every cancellation has a logged reason in both systems. Clear, consistent notes make follow-ups smooth and reporting reliable.

Core Teaching
Ask for the reason politely, document it precisely in the booking and CRM systems, and set follow-ups when there’s a chance to win the client back. Keep tone professional, track accountability with date/name, and review weekly so nothing slips.

Key Principles
Always get and log the reason.
Write concise, specific notes (avoid “Canceled”).
Mirror in booking system and CRM with date and your name.
Create follow-ups for temporary situations.
Weekly supervisor review for completeness.
Step-by-Step Process
Receive Cancellation
Thank the client; ask politely for the reason; keep tone calm and professional.
Record in Booking System
Open booking; add the clear reason in “Cancellation Reason/Notes”; save; set status to Cancelled if applicable.
Add Notes in CRM (e.g., GHL)
Add date, reason, and your name; save; create follow-up task if temporary.
Optional Follow-Up
Schedule outreach after travel/renovation/move when appropriate.
Quality Check
Weekly, verify every cancellation has a reason in booking + CRM, and follow-up tasks where relevant.
Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
“Thanks for letting us know! May I ask what’s the reason for the cancellation? Your feedback really helps us improve.”
Example CRM note: “Cancelled on Oct 23, 2025 – Client out of town for 3 weeks. Noted by [Your Name].”
Common Mistakes / Warnings
Writing vague notes like “Canceled” without a reason.
Logging in only one system.
Forgetting attribution (date/name) and follow-up tasks.
