Quick Summary
Treat every cancellation as an opportunity: save it, reschedule it, or monetize it via a fee. Timing drives policy—72+ hours, no fee; under 72 hours, apply a cancellation fee with reasonable caps and documented exceptions. Always attempt retention first by asking why and proposing solutions. If a fee applies and the client resists, reinforce policy and offer options like paying now or adding it to the next cleaning. Log every incident with reason, date, and handler so you can reduce churn over time.

Core Teaching
Use a three-step system: identify timing, attempt to save the booking, then apply policy and confirm next steps. Keep discounts targeted and rare. Track cancellations in both the booking system and CRM to spot patterns and improve retention. Be clear, firm, and fair, with sensible exceptions for true emergencies.

Key Principles
“Every cancellation is either saved, rescheduled, or monetized.”
72+ hours: no fee. <72 hours: fee applies (example 25%, capped).
Attempt to save before cancelling.

Step-by-Step Process
Determine Timing
72+ hours: no fee. Less than 72 hours: fee applies.
Attempt Retention
Ask reason; offer reschedule, scope adjustments, targeted incentives when appropriate.
Apply Policy
72+ hours: cancel with no fee; ask to reschedule and confirm future appointments.
<72 hours: apply fee; handle exceptions (emergencies) with documented one-time waivers.
Handle Resistance
Reinforce policy; offer pay-now or add-to-next options; escalate repeat refusals and consider discontinuing service.
Update Systems
Booking: cancel and apply/waive fee; select reason.
CRM: add note with date, reason, action taken, who handled it.
Notify Cleaner (if within 7 days).
Optional Follow-Up
Set reminder if cancellation is temporary.
Completion Checklist
Timing confirmed, retention attempt made, fee applied/waived, payment handled/documented, systems updated, cleaner notified, notes added.
Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
“Thanks for letting us know — do you mind sharing what’s causing the cancellation?”
“Would you like to reschedule?” / “Should we keep your future appointments active?”
Common Mistakes / Warnings
Overusing discounts.
Skipping the retention attempt.
Not logging reasons and actions.
Inconsistently applying fees.
