Quick Summary
Pay per job, not per hour—keep it performance-based. Base compensation is a percentage of job revenue, variable by job type, difficulty, and market. Apply one-time discretionary adjustments only when scope changes meaningfully. Add-ons and upsells increase both job price and cleaner pay proportionally. Define rules for cancellations, no-access, and partial completions. Set expectations: no guaranteed jobs or hours; cleaners may accept/decline jobs; comply with local laws. Track and share earnings breakdowns to maintain transparency.

Core Teaching
A four-part system: base pay per job, scoped adjustments when warranted, proportional compensation for add-ons and tips going directly to cleaners, and clear policies for cancellations/edge cases. Protect the business with clear expectations, legal compliance, and transparent tracking so everyone understands earnings.

Key Principles
Performance-based, per-job compensation.
One-time discretionary adjustments for legitimate scope changes.
Add-ons and tips increase cleaner earnings; tips are passed through.
No guaranteed work; clarity and compliance matter.
Step-by-Step Process
Base Pay Structure
Pay per completed job; percentage of job revenue; percentage may vary by job type, difficulty, market.
Handle Scope Adjustments
Apply one-time discretionary increases for added work, worse-than-expected conditions, or access/setup delays.
Add-Ons & Upsells
Increase job price and cleaner compensation proportionally when clients add services.
Tips
Pass tips directly to the cleaner; pay on the standard schedule.
Cancellations & No-Access
Follow policy; compensate only if defined in policy.
Payment Schedule
Pay on a consistent schedule; provide breakdowns (job earnings, add-ons, tips, adjustments).
Expectations
Cleaners may accept/decline jobs; per-job pay; no guaranteed minimum jobs.
Compliance
Follow local labor laws and proper contractor/employee classification.
Documentation
Maintain records of completed jobs, issued payments, and adjustments.
Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
“Pay per job, not per hour — and keep it performance-based.”
“Aligns incentives (faster + better work = better earnings).”
Common Mistakes / Warnings
Switching randomly between hourly and per-job pay.
Ignoring scope issues instead of adjusting pay when justified.
Guaranteeing earnings informally without structure.
