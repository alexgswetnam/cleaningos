# leads 101 — Marketing Basics series

Three lessons. Confirms the course is structured as **Engine × Season** — there is a
"Marketing Basics [Season] Coaching" lesson per Season. Two of four are translated.

> [!danger] Uses a retired term
> These lessons say **"Sustain and Sell."** That Season is now [[Four Seasons|Harvest]].
> Do not teach the old name.

## 1. How Much To Spend On Marketing

**Benchmark:** ~10% of revenue. *"I can't give you an exact number."*

**Alex's own first months, on a credit card:**
- Month 1: ~$6,000 in cleans, ~$4,000 ad spend on a credit card
- Month 2: ~$2,000–3,000 spend
- Then: dropped under $1,000/month for several months, kept the clients, paid the cards
  down from the retained work
- **Started February 2025.** Still had weekly, biweekly and monthly clients from that
  first cohort.

**Why they were comfortable:** both partners came from sales and marketing. *"We knew we
could handle multiple leads a day, call them immediately, follow up consistently, get
cleans, and have a good conversion rate."*

**The two gates before spending:**
1. Is your sales process dialled in?
2. Can your cleaning team deliver and retain?

> "The hardest thing to nail down in the cleaning business is marketing and sales. It is
> not that hard to find cleaners, use systems, find a VA, or handle logistics compared to
> marketing and sales."

**On credit cards:** useful at 0% APR. *"If you don't have 0% APR, then it's like you're
renting money."*

**The failure, verbatim:**

> "We also messed up some relationships early because we did not know what we were doing
> on the cleaning side. We sent bad cleaners, priced incorrectly, quoted incorrectly,
> promised things, and underdelivered."

## 2. Marketing Basics — Scale Season

> "In the Scale season, you know your numbers. Marketing is an equation for you."

You've tested multiple sources and chosen the ones that work for your area and offer. The
job is optimisation: best cost per lead, best practices across GBP, Google Ads, Meta,
Thumbtack.

Moving toward Harvest means spending more, because the numbers are known. *"Now it's time
to bet on yourself."*

**The warning:**

> "It's very easy to put more money into marketing, but it's not necessarily easy to
> handle another three, five leads a day."

Sales follow-up and systems have to be ready first. The other failure: forcing the wrong
channel — *"trying to squeeze out of it what you can when really you should be doing PPC
instead of LSA."*

## 3. Marketing Basics — Sustain and Sell (Harvest) Season

**Zoom out to trends over time.** Judge the year, not the month.

> "January, February, March... you are not going to have as profitable marketing."

Tougher months. People cancel. That's seasonality, not breakage.

Adjust marketing to season and holidays, including special offers. With high lead volume,
**referral programs and email follow-up** matter more — capitalise on leads you already
have. Keep a pulse check on whether you're increasing or decreasing. Always be testing new
creative, copy, and landing pages.
