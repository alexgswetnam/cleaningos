## 1. I'm Getting Quotes From Other Companies First

# Handling "I'm Getting Quotes From Other Companies First" (How to keep the conversation alive and stay top of mind)
## Module
I'm getting quotes from other companies first(1)

## Course
Objection Handling Course

## Skool URL
Not provided.

## Best For
Cleaning business owners, VAs, and salespeople who hear prospects say they want to compare quotes before booking.

## Problems This Solves
- Leads using "I'm getting other quotes" as a way to get off the phone.
- Salespeople losing momentum when a prospect wants to shop around.
- Not knowing how to turn comparison shopping into connection.
- Not knowing how to assume the close without sounding desperate.
- Letting quote shoppers disappear without follow-up.
- Leaving open loops after a sales call.

## Quick Summary
This lesson teaches how to handle the objection, "I'm getting quotes from a couple other companies first." The first move is to affirm that they are doing research instead of making them feel wrong for comparing options. Then you reframe that behavior as a sign that they care about getting the cleaning done right, which makes them one of your favorite types of clients. After that, you ask what they are mainly looking for in a cleaning company and position your service as the solution. The second response is to assume the close by offering to get them on the calendar with no commitment and a reason-based discount. If they still need to shop around, the sale becomes a follow-up game. The key is not to leave open loops or wait passively for them to come back.

## Core Teaching
When someone says, "I'm getting quotes from a couple other companies first," they are often using that as a way to disconnect. They want to get off the phone and compare you against other companies.

The first response is not to fight them. Agree with them and flip the objection into a connection point.

Say something like:

"That's awesome. I'm really glad to hear that you're doing your research. And actually, those are our favorite clients, the ones who really care about getting their home cleaned and doing everything right. So what is the main thing you guys were looking for from a cleaning company?"

This works because the prospect is trying to use comparison shopping as a reason to leave the conversation. You are turning it into a reason to stay in the conversation. You are basically saying, "That thing you just said is exactly why you are the kind of client we like talking to."

Once they answer what they are looking for, connect your service to that need. Most people will say they want good service at an affordable price. That gives you room to explain how your company gives them the result they are looking for.

Then you can move into an assumed close:

"Okay, here's what I can do for you. I can get you guys on the calendar for Monday, the 31st for this first cleaning, no commitment, and give you guys a $10 discount if you're willing to leave us a couple Google reviews after the cleaning. So with this offer, you can go ahead, try it out, and then make a better decision after you've given us a try. What's a good email address for you guys to put on file so I can get the ball rolling here?"

The lesson notes that technically you are not allowed to offer discounts for Google reviews. The bigger principle is that if you offer a discount, you should give a reason. Do not offer discounts randomly, because it makes your price feel flexible.

If they still want to get other quotes, that is okay. You will not book 100% of people on the phone. At that point, the sale becomes a follow-up game. Message them later that night. Ask how the search went. Message them the next day. Call them. Stay on top of the lead and stay top of mind.

## Key Principles
- Do not make people wrong for getting multiple quotes.
- Affirm that research is a good thing.
- Reframe quote shopping as a sign that they care about quality.
- Turn their attempt to disconnect into a reason to connect.
- Ask what they are actually looking for in a cleaning company.
- Position your company as the solution to what they say they want.
- Assume the close after you have addressed the concern.
- Give a reason if you offer a discount.
- Do not leave open loops.
- Follow up if they still need to shop around.

## Step-by-Step Process
1. When they say they are getting other quotes, affirm it.
2. Tell them those are your favorite clients because they care about doing things right.
3. Ask what the main thing is that they are looking for from a cleaning company.
4. Listen to what they say they want.
5. Position your service as the solution to that need.
6. Move into an assumed close.
7. Offer an easy first cleaning with no commitment if appropriate.
8. Ask for the next booking detail, like their email address.
9. If they still want other quotes, do not force it.
10. Follow up later that night and the next day so you stay top of mind.

- "So what is the main thing you guys were looking for from a cleaning company?"
- "You're saying that reason you want to get off the phone, that's actually the perfect reason to stay on the phone."

- "With this offer, you can go ahead, try it out, and then make a better decision after you've given us a try."

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not argue with someone for wanting multiple quotes.
- Do not treat quote shopping as a dead end.
- Do not offer discounts for no reason.
- Be careful with discounts tied to Google reviews; the lesson notes this is technically not allowed.
- Do not wait passively for the client to call you back.
- Do not leave unsolved objections or unanswered questions.
- Do not expect to book 100% of people on the first call.

## 2. It's Too Expensive

# Handling "It's Too Expensive" (How to uncover the real price concern and sell value without haggling)
## Module
It's too expensive

## Course
Objection Handling Course

## Skool URL
Not provided.

## Best For
Cleaning business owners, VAs, and salespeople who get price pushback after quoting a cleaning and need to handle it without sounding defensive, discounting too quickly, or attracting bad-fit clients.

## Problems This Solves
- Leads saying the cleaning is too expensive.
- Salespeople assuming price is the problem but not asking what the lead expected to pay.
- Dropping the price too fast and making the original quote feel fake.
- Getting pulled into haggling or bidding wars.
- Not knowing when a lead is simply not your client.
- Failing to connect the price to the result, guarantee, quality, and convenience.
- Offering discounts without a reason.

## Quick Summary
This lesson covers the "it's too expensive" objection, which often sits underneath other objections like "let me talk to my spouse" or "let me think about it." The goal is not to immediately discount, but to ask open-ended questions and uncover the root concern. One key question is, "What price were you ideally looking to pay here?" because it shows whether the lead is even in the same ballpark and often reveals why they expected that price. If a discount is needed, it should have a reason, like excluding unused rooms or areas from the clean. The lesson also teaches how to re-anchor the conversation around quality, background-checked and insured cleaners, and the 100% satisfaction guarantee. If someone only wants the cheapest price, they are probably not your client. The point is to sell value and fit, not win a race to the bottom.

## Core Teaching
The "it's too expensive" objection comes up often, and it is usually at the root of other objections too. Sometimes people say they need to talk to their husband, wife, or think about it, but what they really see is an issue with price.

Almost every objection can be overcome by asking open-ended questions and getting to the root concern. The goal is not to guess what they mean. The goal is to pull more information out of them so you can address the real issue.

A strong first response is:

"I see, thanks for being honest with me. What price were you ideally looking to pay here?"

This helps you figure out whether you are even in the same ballpark. If you quote $350 and they expected $75, you are probably wasting your time. Not everyone is your client. No cleaning is better than a bad cleaning, and no client is better than a bad client.

But most people will not just give a number. They usually explain why they expected that number. They may say their old cleaner charged that amount, or they called a couple other companies and got similar prices. That gives you information you can work with.

Discounts can be used, but they should not be the first move. You should not make your price feel flexible. The lesson uses the example of going to Walmart to buy AirPods: you do not expect to negotiate with the person behind the counter. The price is the price because it is a legitimate business selling legitimate products. Your cleaning service should feel the same way.

If you do give a discount, attach a reason to it. For example, maybe the home is 3,000 square feet, but it is two elderly empty nesters and they only actively use the bedroom, living room, kitchen, and one bathroom. In that case, you can ask:

"Are there any rooms in the home, any areas in the home that are unused or that we don't really need to clean because they're just not that active?"

That lets you reduce the scope instead of randomly reducing the price.

Another strong response is to acknowledge that cheaper options may exist, then bring the conversation back to quality:

"Got it, yeah, you may be able to get cheaper prices elsewhere to be honest with you, but at the end of the day, we prioritize quality with all of our cleanings. That's what we're here for. All of our cleaners are background checked and insured and when you book with us, you have a 100% satisfaction guarantee. That means that we will do whatever it takes to make it right with you if you're not 100% satisfied. Were you just looking for the cheapest price or is there something I'm missing here?"

Most people will say they are not just looking for the cheapest price. That lets you dig into what they actually want: good value, good quality, peace of mind, less stress, or a clean home without worrying about it.

The lesson also mentions a small Google review discount as a final push, but clearly warns that you are not allowed to offer discounts for Google reviews and that Google can take down reviews or flag a GBP. The broader teaching is not to lead with discounts, and if a discount is used, make it sound like a final, thoughtful push rather than an easy price drop.

## Key Principles
- Price is often the root concern behind other objections.
- Ask open-ended questions instead of assuming what the lead means.
- Use "what" questions to get more information.
- Find out what price they expected and why.
- Not everyone is your client.
- No cleaning is better than a bad cleaning.
- Do not lead with discounts.
- Do not make your price feel flexible.
- If you discount, attach a reason to the discount.
- Reduce scope before randomly reducing price.
- Bring the conversation back to quality, trust, insurance, background checks, and satisfaction guarantee.
- If they only want the cheapest price, they are probably not your client.

## Step-by-Step Process
1. When they say it is too expensive, thank them for being honest.
2. Ask what price they were ideally looking to pay.
3. Listen for whether their expected price is in the same ballpark.
4. Listen for why they expected that price, like old cleaner pricing or other quotes.
5. If they are extremely lowballing, recognize they may not be your client.
6. If a discount makes sense, look for a real reason, such as unused rooms or reduced scope.
7. Ask whether there are any rooms or areas that do not need to be cleaned.
8. Reposition your service around quality, background checks, insurance, and the 100% satisfaction guarantee.
9. Ask whether they are just looking for the cheapest price or if you are missing something.
10. If they say they want value, connect your service to their specific life, home, kids, work, or pain points.
11. If they still only want cheap, let them go.

## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep

- "No cleaning is better than a bad cleaning."
- "No client is better than a bad client."
- "We don't really want to make the price seem flexible in their mind."
- "Are there any rooms in the home, any areas in the home that are unused or that we don't really need to clean because they're just not that active?"
- "You may be able to get cheaper prices elsewhere, to be honest with you, but at the end of the day, we prioritize quality with all of our cleanings."
- "All of our cleaners are background checked and insured."
- "When you book with us, you have a 100% satisfaction guarantee."
- "Were you just looking for the cheapest price or is there something I'm missing here?"
- "I mean, that totally makes sense."
- "You get to stay in your office, focus on your work, be unbothered."
- "What are your thoughts?"

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not immediately offer a discount the moment someone pushes back on price.
- Do not get into a bidding war.
- Do not make the client feel like the original price was fake or inflated.
- Do not haggle like the price is random.
- Do not chase people who only want the cheapest possible cleaning.
- Do not ignore the reason behind their expected price.
- Do not use discounts without a reason.
- Be careful with Google review discounts; the transcript says you are not allowed to offer discounts for Google reviews.
- Do not read these responses like a script. Learn the idea and make it natural.

## Quick Summary
This lesson covers how to handle the "let me talk to my husband/wife" objection. The main point is that most of the time, the spouse is not the real issue; the person on the phone has a concern and is using the spouse as a reason to delay. The response should start by agreeing and making it feel normal, not confrontational. Then ask what they think their spouse's main concern would be. ## Core Teaching
When someone says, "Let me talk to my husband," "Let me talk to my wife," or "Let me talk to my roommate," the goal is not to instantly back off. Most of the time, they are not saying this because their spouse has a concern they cannot see. They are saying it because they have a concern themselves and do not want to say it directly.

The first move is to validate them. Do not jump straight into a challenging question. If you skip the validation, it can sound confrontational and make their walls go up.

A good response is:

"Got it, that totally makes sense. No worries at all. What do you think would be your husband's main concern here?"

The tone matters. Smile while saying it. Make it friendly. The difference between "What's your wife's concern here?" and "Got it, yeah, that totally makes sense, man. If you don't mind me asking, what is your wife's main concern here?" is huge.

This question usually gets them to reveal the real objection. A lot of the time it is price. Sometimes it is timing, trust, or another concern. Once the real concern is out, you can address it.

If they genuinely do need to talk to their spouse because they share a bank account or have an agreement about purchases, then ask whether the spouse is already on board with the house cleaning:

"Yeah, that makes sense. Is your wife on board with the house cleaning or is this something that you were just personally looking into here?"

Most of the time, they will say the spouse is on board with the cleaning. Once they confirm that, you can go for a soft commitment:

"Got it. Okay. Well, I don't normally do this, but here's what I can do for you. We can get this appointment booked like we talked about for Monday at 3 p.m. And if for any reason your wife does object, something doesn't work for her and she wants to cancel, then we can go ahead and get that canceled for you free of charge. We'll completely waive that cancellation fee. What's a good email address for you?"

The key is to assume the close. Do not ask, "Does that work for you?" Ask for the next booking detail. If it does not work, they will tell you, and that gives you another chance to address the real concern.

Ideally, get them booked, get the card on file, and treat it like a real booking while still giving them the option to back out if the spouse objects. If they absolutely will not book, the next best thing is to send a quote, but most people will not accept a quote on their own without follow-up.

## Key Principles
- The spouse is often not the real objection.
- Validate before asking a follow-up question.
- Ask what the spouse's main concern would be.
- Use a friendly tone so it does not feel confrontational.
- Most of the time, the real issue will be price or another concern from the person on the phone.
- If the spouse is already on board, get a soft commitment.
- Booking with a free cancellation option is better than letting them disappear.
- Assume the close by asking for the next booking detail.
- If they later say the spouse objected, call and uncover the specific issue.

## Step-by-Step Process
1. When they say they need to talk to their spouse, validate it.
2. Say that it makes total sense and there are no worries.
3. Ask what they think their spouse's main concern would be.
4. Listen for the actual objection underneath the spouse excuse.
5. Address that concern directly.
6. If they truly need spouse approval, ask whether the spouse is already on board with house cleaning.
7. If the spouse is on board, offer to put the appointment on the calendar.
8. Waive the cancellation fee if the spouse objects.
9. Ask for the next booking detail, like their email address.
10. If they refuse to book, send a quote and follow up.
11. If they later cancel because of the spouse, call and ask what did not work.

## 4. Let Me Think About It

# Handling "Let Me Think About It" (How to ask for the real concern instead of letting the lead ghost)
## Module

## Course

## Skool URL

## Best For

## Problems This Solves
- Leads saying they will think about it and then ghosting.
- Salespeople accepting vague delays with no next step.
- Not knowing how to ask what the actual concern is.
- Asking "why" in a way that makes the lead defensive.
- Handling objections without enough rapport.

## Quick Summary
This lesson teaches how to handle the "let me think about it" objection. The main idea is that people usually say this because they want to get off the phone and avoid saying what their real concern is. There is probably only a very small chance they will actually reach back out on their own. The response is to validate them, then ask an open-ended question like, "What's your main concern here that you need to think on?" The lesson emphasizes using "what" or "how" instead of "why" because "why" can make people defensive. It also stresses that rapport makes objection handling easier. If you have built a human conversation earlier in the call, this question lands much better.

## Core Teaching
When a lead says, "Let me think about it and I'll get back to you," they will almost never get back to you. Most of the time, they are saying it because they want to get off the phone. They have a concern, but they do not want to say directly, "This does not work for me."

The goal is to dig deeper and uncover the real concern.

A strong response is:

"Absolutely, no worries. What's your main concern here that you need to think on?"

This works because it is open-ended and soft. You are not asking a yes/no question. You are not asking "why," which can sound accusatory.

The lesson stresses that questions should usually begin with "what" or "how." If you ask, "Why do you need to think about it?" it can make the person feel like they need to defend themselves. They may think, "I don't have to explain myself to you." But asking, "What's your main concern here?" lands much softer.

Tone and rapport matter. Put a smile on your face while handling objections. Sound happy. Be human.

The lesson contrasts two types of calls. One salesperson is flat and transactional: beds, baths, square footage, price, date, do you want to book? That person has not built enough rapport, so objection handling will feel awkward.

The better approach is to build conversation earlier in the call. Ask how they are doing, what they are up to, what they do for work, where they live, or something natural about their area. Get them to laugh if you can. Show them you are human.

Then when they say, "Let me think about it," your question lands much better:

"Oh Mary, what do you have to think on here? What's your main concern?"

A majority of the time, the real concern will be price, which is covered separately in the course.

## Key Principles
- "Let me think about it" usually means there is an unspoken concern.
- Do not assume they will call you back.
- Ask for the real concern while keeping the tone friendly.
- Use "what" or "how" instead of "why."
- Avoid yes/no questions when uncovering objections.

## Core Teaching

"But would moving the appointment due to another date or month help instead of canceling the entire appointment with us?"

That question gives the client an easier option. Instead of making them decide between keeping the current appointment or canceling forever, it gives them the option to pause and come back. The client says two or three weeks later should work, and the VA offers June 1 as a specific date.

The reason this works is because the client already likes the company. They are not trying to leave because of a bad clean. They are dealing with life circumstances. So the goal is to preserve the relationship, make the next step easy, and keep the appointment on the calendar.

The call also shows that when a client is happy and says they would leave a review, you should take the opportunity to ask for it and make it simple by sending a link.

## Step-by-Step Process
1. Answer the call warmly and let the client explain what is going on.

3. If the client is not upset with the company, do not accept the cancellation immediately.
4. Ask whether moving the appointment to another date or month would help.
5. Suggest a specific date that could work.
6. Confirm that the new date works for the client.
7. Update the booking.
8. If the client says they are happy with the company, ask for a review and tell them you will send the link.
