# Cleaning Biz 101 — Convert

## 1. Convert: Turning Leads Into Bookings

This part of the business is where you make the money. Acquire gets people to raise their
hand; Convert makes sure those raised hands actually turn into booked jobs.

Most cleaning businesses don't lose deals because they're bad at cleaning — they lose
deals because they respond too slow, forget to follow up, don't have a clear sales
process, or don't have a system that makes booking feel easy.

The goal of Convert: no lead gets lost. Every lead should have a source, a status, a next
action, and a follow-up plan. If Convert is weak, marketing feels "expensive" because
you're wasting leads you already paid for.

The team's VA had a 60% close rate in December. At $40 cost per lead: 10% close = $400 per
booking; 30% close = $133 per booking; 60% close = $66 per booking.

Side note: after running many weekly sales trainings breaking down live sales calls from
other cleaning business owners, most cleaning biz owners aren't good at sales — objection
handling ("it's too pricey," "I'm just looking around for quotes") is a common weak spot.

## 2. Speed-To-Lead: The First 5 Minutes

The fastest way to increase bookings without spending another dollar on ads: respond
faster. In home services, the first company to respond wins a lot of times. A lead that
doesn't hear back quickly either books someone else or cools off and becomes harder to
close.

Even if you can't personally respond instantly, the CRM can: an immediate auto-text, a
missed-call text, an email confirmation, and a task/reminder that forces follow-up.
Speed-to-lead isn't a "nice to have" — it drastically increases close rate. Delaying CRM
adoption to save money cost hundreds if not thousands of dollars in missed bookings. Even
without a CRM, respond to new leads within seconds and call ASAP.

## 3. Sales Happen on the Phone!

Instant quotes and long forms aren't used because the sale happens on the phone. A phone
call builds trust and connection fast, uncovers what the prospect actually needs, sets
expectations, avoids the cheap-quote-shopper trap, and guides a good fit into recurring
service.

When the first thing someone gets is a price, they compare you like a commodity. When the
first thing they get is clarity, confidence, and professionalism, they compare you like a
premium service. The goal of the call isn't pressure — it's leading the conversation so
booking is the natural outcome.

## 4. You NEED a Sales Pipeline!

Relying on memory, a notes app, or random texts to track leads makes Convert feel
chaotic. A pipeline is a visual way to answer: where is this lead right now, and what
should happen next?

A simple cleaning pipeline: New Lead → Follow-Up → Quoted → Booked (One Time) → Booked
(Recurring) → Long Term Follow Up.

The magic isn't the stages — it's the clarity and discipline. An accurate pipeline stops
you forgetting people, stops random re-texting, keeps you always knowing what to do next,
makes bottlenecks visible (e.g. tons of quotes sent but no bookings = a follow-up
problem), and is what allows scaling to 100+ bookings a month without chaos. Every
serious business with a real sales process uses a CRM that tracks a lead's current status.

## 6. Follow-Up: Where Most Bookings Actually Come From

Most bookings don't happen on the first message. A lead saying "let me talk to my
spouse," "I'll get back to you," "we're still deciding," or "maybe next week" isn't dead —
they need follow-up. A real Convert system includes a follow-up cadence (day 0, day 1,
day 3, day 7…), a way to separate hot vs. cold leads, and automation so it happens even
when you're busy.

This is why people think marketing "doesn't work." Marketing works — the gap is follow-up
or sales training. Better follow-up closes more of the same leads.

## 7. Old Leads Are Not Dead Leads

Some of the easiest bookings come from leads that didn't book two months ago, old
one-time clients who rebook later, and leads that go quiet and respond months later. This
is why long-term follow-up matters — the CRM should be checking in automatically over
time. If a lead was paid for once, there's no reason to throw it away. The database is an
asset; most cleaning businesses treat it like trash.

## 8. Your CRM = Heavy Lifting

Convert shouldn't rely on mood, memory, or motivation. The CRM should be auto-texting new
leads instantly, sending missed-call texts, running follow-up sequences, reminding for
manual follow-up calls, moving pipeline stages automatically when a booking happens, and
tagging lead sources automatically. Most CRMs are just a follow-up machine — Harvest CRM
goes further, using AI throughout. Without a follow-up machine, there's no real sales
system — just a "respond when I remember" system.
