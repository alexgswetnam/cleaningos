# Labor 101

36 lessons on running the Labor Engine day to day: cleaner pay (percentage-of-job vs.
hourly, and the legal reasoning against hourly for 1099s), the 1099 vs. W-2 decision and
its operational boundaries, the full hiring pipeline (Indeed screening, interview script
and rubric, background check, subcontractor agreement, onboarding, first-job pairing),
retention and quality tracking (three-strike system, red flags, keeping your best
cleaners), client-facing scope and expectation-setting (what's included/excluded,
handling a dirtier-than-expected home, cancellations), and a block of real operational
edge cases (a cleaner breaking something, insurance, gated communities, job notes, firing
a client, whether clients should have a cleaner's number).

This course sits directly on top of the vault's most active contested item —
[[Cleaner Pay Structure]] and [[Subcontractor Vs W-2]]'s unresolved dispute over whether
cleaners have a guaranteed hourly floor ($28/hr MEA vs. $25/hr non-floor DPS). Two
lessons here ("Determining Cleaners' Pay" and "Paying Cleaners Hourly vs Fixed Job
Price") go straight at it, and a third source found while reading the .txt exports — the
actual interview script inside "How to Conduct an Interview" — surfaces a **third
distinct position** in the company's own materials. See Conflict History on both
concept pages; full account in the ingestion report.

Much of this course sits directly on top of existing Labor Engine material —
[[Hiring SOP]], [[Cleaner Handbook]], [[Hiring Channels]], [[Backup Cleaner]],
[[Testing A New Cleaner]] — and updates or fleshes out those pages rather than
duplicating them. Several lessons were genuinely new decisions with no existing page:
see Concepts Created below.
