The Faithful Cleaners Subcontractor Agreement 
I. The Parties. This Cleaning Subcontractor Agreement (“Agreement”), effective as of 
_____________ (“Effective Date”), is by and between The Faithful Cleaners, LLC 
(“Contractor”), 8720 Silverado Trail #073, McKinney, TX 75070, and __________________ 
(“Subcontractor”). 
Recitals. Contractor engages Subcontractor from time to time to perform residential and/or 
commercial cleaning services for Contractor’s clients under accepted work orders, on the terms 
below. 
II. Term; Commencement. This Agreement begins on _____________ (“Commencement 
Date”) and continues until terminated by either party on written notice. Either party may 
terminate without cause on 7 days’ written notice, and for cause immediately for material 
breach, safety violations, intoxication on site, data/privacy breach, or criminal activity. 
III. Services & Autonomy. 
Subcontractor performs cleaning services for Contractor’s clients under accepted work orders 
(each, a “Job”). Subcontractor may accept or decline Jobs in its discretion, determines its own 
methods and sequence, and provides its own labor, tools, supplies, and protective 
equipment, except as otherwise stated in an individual work order. Subcontractor may engage 
qualified helpers at its expense, provided all helpers meet Contractor’s background and 
client-safety standards and are disclosed in writing before entering a client property. 
Subcontractor will follow any site-specific client requirements and reasonable safety rules 
stated in the work order. 
Subcontractor will, to the best of its ability, meet Contractor’s quality standards, schedule 
commitments, and communication expectations applicable to independent contractors. 
IV. Compensation 
a) Job Fees (Fixed-Fee per Job). For each accepted Job, Subcontractor will be paid the Job 
Fee stated in the Work Order. Unless otherwise specified, the Job Fee equals 40–60% of Net 
Job Revenue for that Job. “Net Job Revenue” means amounts actually collected from the client 
excluding sales tax, payment processing fees, discounts, refunds, and chargebacks. Job Fees 
are per-Job project payments, not hourly wages. 
b) Discretionary Project Supplement (DPS). From time to time, Contractor may issue a 
one-time project supplement to address material mis-scoping, client-driven changes, or unusual 
on-site conditions that materially impacted Subcontractor’s project economics. Contractor’s 
Compensation Assurance Schedule (Exhibit A) describes when Contractor will consider a 
DPS and the reference targets it uses to evaluate whether a Job was materially mis-scoped. 
Supplements are discretionary and project-specific, not an hourly guarantee or weekly 
minimum. Any DPS will appear as a separate line item on the statement for the affected Job. 
The decision to issue (or not issue) a DPS on any Job does not create an entitlement for other 
Jobs. 
c) Add-Ons, Upsells & Tips. Approved add-on services and scope increases are paid per IV(a) 
(same percentage methodology) unless the Work Order states otherwise. Tips paid through 
Contractor’s system are passed through to Subcontractor on the normal pay schedule. 
d) Travel, Cancellations & No-Access. Travel/commute are Subcontractor’s responsibility 
unless a written travel stipend is listed on the Work Order. Same-day client 
cancellations/no-access are compensated per the percentage methodology in IV(a) if stated on 
the Work Order, and any such amounts count toward Net Job Revenue. 
e) Statements, Documentation & Event Logs. Contractor may provide statements of 
completed Jobs showing Job Fees, add-ons, refunds/chargebacks, and any DPS. 
Check-in/check-out event logs and photos are collected for client records, QA, and billing 
verification only and do not convert compensation into hourly wages or determine the amount of 
compensation. 
f) Offsets. If (i) a client refund or chargeback is issued due to Subcontractor’s workmanship, or 
(ii) Contractor property loaned to Subcontractor is not returned (per any property schedule), 
Contractor may deduct those amounts from sums otherwise payable to Subcontractor. Any 
deduction will be itemized on the statement. 
g) Autonomy & Methods; No Employment. Subcontractor accepts/declines Jobs at its 
discretion, supplies its own tools/supplies, determines the manner and sequence of work, and 
may use qualified helpers as permitted elsewhere in this Agreement. Amounts paid are project 
compensation, do not guarantee any number of Jobs or earnings, and do not create an 
employment relationship. 
h) Prospective Changes. Contractor may update compensation parameters (including 
percentage ranges and Exhibit A) prospectively by written notice, effective for Jobs accepted 
after such notice. 
V. Payment Method & Schedule. 
a) Schedule. Contractor will pay weekly on Friday via ACH (or Subcontractor’s designated 
method) for Jobs completed and approved through the current Pay Week (ending Friday). 
Itemized statements will show Job Fees, any DPS, tips, and offsets. 
b) Required Information. Subcontractor must maintain a current W-9 and payment details on 
file. Disputed items will be identified in writing and paid upon resolution. 
c) No Guarantee of Hours or Earnings. Contractor does not guarantee any minimum Jobs, 
hours, or earnings. 
VI. Quality & Re-cleans. Contractor may inspect or solicit client feedback after each Job. If a 
material deficiency within the contracted scope is reported within 48 hours, Subcontractor will 
perform a no-charge re-clean within 48 hours (or the client’s requested time) to correct the 
deficiency. Items outside the original scope are billed as a new Job. 
VII. Return of Property. Upon termination or on request, Subcontractor must return any 
Contractor-provided property within 10 days. Contractor may deduct unpaid replacement costs 
from amounts otherwise due (with itemization). 
VIII. Time Is of the Essence. Subcontractor acknowledges that time is of the essence 
regarding performance of the Services under accepted Jobs. 
IX. Confidentiality & Non-Circumvention. 
“Confidential Information” includes Contractor’s pricing, operations, client lists, work orders, 
and Client Information (names, addresses, access codes, schedules, payment data). 
Subcontractor will not use or disclose Confidential Information except to perform Jobs. 
a) Non-Solicitation / Non-Circumvention. During this Agreement and for 12 months after the 
last Job performed, Subcontractor will not directly or indirectly solicit or perform cleaning 
services for any client introduced by Contractor, except through Contractor. If breached, 
damages would be difficult to calculate; therefore Subcontractor shall pay liquidated damages 
equal to the greater of (i) $2,500 or (ii) 40% of gross revenue received from such client during 
the first 12 months after the breach, plus injunctive relief. The parties agree this is a 
reasonable pre-estimate of loss and not a penalty. 
b) Client Data. No photos/video of client premises may be posted or shared publicly without 
written client consent. Any suspected data breach must be reported to Contractor within 24 
hours. 
c) Survival. Subcontractor’s obligations in this Section survive termination. 
X. Taxes. Subcontractor is solely responsible for all taxes and withholdings applicable to its 
business, including federal income taxes, self-employment taxes, and any sales/use taxes, if 
applicable. 
XI. Independent Contractor Status; Non-Exclusivity. Subcontractor is an independent 
contractor, not an agent, partner, joint venture, or employee of Contractor, and has no 
authority to bind Contractor. Subcontractor is free to perform services for others and is 
non-exclusive. No benefits are provided. If Contractor suffers loss due to a misrepresentation 
of authority by Subcontractor, Subcontractor will indemnify Contractor for such loss. 
XII. Safety, Backgrounds & Insurance. Subcontractor is solely responsible for safety of its 
personnel and compliance with applicable law and OSHA. All personnel entering client 
premises must have passed a background check to a reasonable standard acceptable to 
Contractor. 
a) Insurance. Before accepting Jobs and throughout the term, Subcontractor shall maintain at 
its expense: (i) Commercial General Liability of $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 
aggregate, naming The Faithful Cleaners, LLC as Certificate Holder (additional insured if 
available); (ii) if driving to or between Jobs, Auto Liability not less than $300,000; and (iii) if 
Subcontractor has employees, Workers’ Compensation as required by law. Certificates of 
Insurance must be provided upon request. 
b) Incidents. Any property damage, injury, or security incident must be reported to 
Contractor within 4 hours with photos and a written description. Subcontractor will cooperate in 
any investigation or insurance claim. 
XIII. Alcohol & Drugs. Intoxication or possession/use of illegal drugs on a Job site is grounds 
for immediate termination for cause. 
XIV. Successors & Assigns. This Agreement inures to the benefit of and is binding upon the 
parties and their respective heirs, personal representatives, successors, and permitted assigns. 
Neither party may assign without the other’s prior written consent, except Contractor may assign 
to an affiliate or in a merger/sale. 
XV. Indemnification. Subcontractor shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless Contractor 
and its clients from and against claims, damages, fines, and expenses (including reasonable 
attorneys’ fees) to the extent caused by Subcontractor’s or its personnel’s negligence, willful 
misconduct, or breach of this Agreement. Contractor will provide prompt notice of any such 
claim. 
XVI. No Waiver. No waiver of any provision is a continuing waiver unless stated in a signed 
writing. 
XVII. Governing Law; Venue; Jury Waiver. Texas law governs. Exclusive venue is Collin 
County, Texas. The parties waive jury trial to the extent permitted by law. 
XVIII. Severability. If any provision is held invalid or unenforceable, the remainder remains in 
full force. 
XIX. Entire Agreement; Amendments; E-Signatures. This Agreement is the entire 
agreement on its subject and supersedes prior understandings. Amendments must be in 
signed writing. Electronic signatures are valid and binding. 
XX. Acknowledgments. Each party signs voluntarily, has read and understands this 
Agreement, and has had the opportunity to consult counsel.

Exhibit A — Compensation Assurance Schedule (Project 
Supplements) 
Purpose (what this is): This schedule explains when Contractor considers adding a 
Discretionary Project Supplement (DPS) to a specific completed Job if the Job’s actual 
conditions made the project materially different than reasonably expected at dispatch. It is 
designed so a Subcontractor doesn’t end up on a mis-scoped Job (e.g., 8 hours of 
unexpected grime) for an obviously mismatched fixed fee. 
What this is NOT: 
● Not hourly wages, not a weekly floor, not an employment benefit. 
● Not automatic—each Job is reviewed on its facts. 
● No averaging across multiple Jobs. 
Reference Targets (what “fair project economics” means to us): For review purposes only, 
Contractor uses the following Reference Effective Rate (RER) target as a benchmark to 
assess mis-scoping on a specific Job: 
● Standard/Deep/Move-In/Move-Out Clean (reference): RER ≈ $25.00 per on-site hour 
How we use it: If a completed Job’s implied effective rate (Job Fee ÷ on-site time 
from check-in/out events) falls materially below the applicable RER due to 
mis-scope or client-driven changes, we may add a DPS to bring the project 
closer to that reference outcome. These RERs are targets to evaluate 
mis-scoping—not wages and not guarantees. 
When a DPS is considered (examples): 
● Client adds rooms/tasks on site after dispatch (and fixed fee wasn’t updated). 
● Severe build-up or unusual conditions not disclosed or not visible at booking. 
● Safety constraints or access issues that materially slow normal workflow. 
● Documented mis-estimate at dispatch despite reasonable diligence. 
When a DPS is typically not considered: 
● Re-cleans or rework resulting from Subcontractor workmanship issues. 
● Time unrelated to on-site performance (travel, supply runs, breaks, waiting time). 
● Tasks outside the booked scope where Subcontractor proceeds without approval. 
● Jobs where the Work Order fee was updated on site to reflect added scope. 
How time is referenced (for review only): 
● On-site time is taken from check-in/check-out events and photos. 
● These logs are for QA and billing verification only and do not determine pay; they’re 
used solely to evaluate mis-scoping for a possible project-specific DPS. 
Caps & documentation: 
● DPS applies per Job and only to completed Jobs. 
● A short DPS Review Request (notes + 2–4 photos) should be submitted within 3 
business days of the Job’s statement if the Subcontractor believes the Job was 
materially mis-scoped. 
● Contractor will review the request and respond within 5 business days (approve, adjust, 
or decline with reason). 
Prospective updates: 
● RER targets and examples in this Exhibit may be updated prospectively by written 
notice and will apply to Jobs accepted after the effective date.
