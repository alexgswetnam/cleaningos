## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that a cleaning sales call should follow a clear structure while still feeling like a human conversation. Relationship-building is the number one goal, but the call still needs to collect the right information and guide the client toward a booking.

Before taking the call, the rep should have the right screens open: Booking Koala booking form, Booking Koala calendar, Notion with the Sales Call Outline and Pricing Guide, and the GHL or Thumbtack window with customer information.

The rep should greet the client by name, explain why they are calling, and build rapport throughout the call. If the client mentions pets, moving, work, or family, the rep should ask natural follow-up questions. The goal is to be personable without forcing conversation.

Next, the rep confirms information and gathers the details needed to quote accurately. If the client has not had their home cleaned in more than six months, a deep clean should be recommended. If the dirtiness level is more than a 6, a deep clean is required. The rep should not ask what type of clean the client wants; they should recommend the right type based on the answers.

Before giving price, the rep should ask when the client wants the clean done and confirm availability. This gets micro-commitments and prevents giving a quote before knowing whether the company can actually serve them.

When giving the quote, the rep should recap the details, quote before tax, say “plus tax,” use charm pricing, and then pause. Do not fill the silence. If needed, mention the satisfaction guarantee, background-checked and insured cleaners, English-speaking cleaners, and years of experience.

If the client books, collect details, enter structured Booking Koala notes, explain the card hold and payment process, and set expectations. If the client objects, begin objection handling with questions that get to the root concern.
## Key Principles
- Relationship-building is the number one goal.
- Do not sound like a robot.
- Have the right screens open before answering the call.
- Confirm availability before quoting.
- Recommend the clean type instead of asking the client to choose.
- Ask for dirtiness level and then ask what areas they are most concerned about.
- Quote clearly, then pause and let the client respond.
- Use open-ended questions to uncover the real objection.
- Set expectations before ending the call.
- Keep Booking Koala notes structured and useful for cleaners.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Set up screens: Booking Koala form, Booking Koala calendar, Notion outline and pricing guide, and lead source/customer info window.
2. Greet the client by name and introduce yourself.
3. Build rapport naturally around their home, pets, job, move, or situation.
4. Confirm and collect required details: bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage, last clean, dirtiness level, clean type, frequency, pets, and add-ons.
5. Ask move-in questions if the client requests a move-in clean and escalate post-construction cases to a manager.
6. Confirm availability before quoting by asking when they want the clean done.
7. Recap the details and give the quote before tax, then pause.
8. If they book, collect contact info, address, payment method, and enter job notes.
9. If they object, use the relevant objection response and ask open-ended questions.
10. Set expectations around confirmation emails, reminders, cancellation policy, card hold, payment after cleaning, walkthroughs, clutter, furniture, and ladders.
11. Ask if they have any final questions.
12. End positively and confirm the booking time.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not force rapport if the client does not want to talk.
- Do not ask the client what clean type they want when you should recommend it.
- Do not quote before confirming availability.
- Do not fill the silence after giving the price.
- Do not show private notes or discount lines when sending a quote.
- Do not pressure the client; focus on relationship and trust.
- Do not skip expectation setting after booking.
- Do not ignore red flags like nickel-and-diming, wanting to remove too many rooms, asking for standard when the condition requires deep, or giving a bad gut feeling.

## Cleaned Lesson Notes
The Sales Call Outline is the main call flow for turning a cleaning lead into a booked job. If the client asks for something unique, like only three bathrooms or only an oven clean, the rep should collect the information and tell them a manager will provide the final quote.

Before answering calls, the rep should have the right setup: Booking Koala booking form, Booking Koala calendar, Notion with the Sales Call Outline and Pricing Guide, and the lead source window showing the customer information.

The call starts with a greeting and relationship-building. The rep introduces themselves, says why they are calling, and uses the client’s name. Rapport should happen throughout the call, not just at the beginning. If the client mentions a dog, ask what kind. If they just moved, ask where they moved from. Relationship-building is the number one goal, but it should not be forced.

The next step is collecting information. Confirm any details already provided, then gather bedrooms, bathrooms, square footage, last cleaning date, dirtiness level, type of clean, frequency, pets, and add-ons. If the home has not been cleaned in more than six months, recommend a deep clean. If they decline, do a standard clean with an upcharge for the first clean. If the dirtiness level is more than 6, a deep clean is required. Always ask what areas they are most concerned about and leave detailed notes in Booking Koala.

Do not ask what type of clean they want. Make a recommendation based on their answers. For move-ins, ask whether anyone is working in the home, whether surfaces are new or still curing, whether the home is empty, and whether debris removal is needed. If yes, it may be post-construction, not move-in, and a manager should quote it.

Before giving price, confirm availability. Ask when they were hoping to get the clean done. If they give a general answer, push for specifics. If they avoid committing, clarify that they are not committing yet and you are just making sure the company can serve them.

When giving the quote, recap the details and use the pricing guide. Quote before tax and say “plus tax.” Use charm pricing where appropriate. After giving the number, pause. Do not fill the silence. Let the client respond first.

If the client books, enter structured Booking Koala notes exactly: Preferred Cleaner, Clean Exclusions, Estimated Total Hours, and Unique Notes. Collect name, email, address, and phone number. Explain that the card is placed on hold before the clean and charged only after the clean and satisfaction call. Ask for the card number, or send a payment link if they do not want to give it over the phone.

If they object, use objection handling. Almost every objection can be overcome by asking a question to find the root concern. For “talk to my spouse,” ask what they think the spouse’s main concern would be. For “let me think about it,” ask what the main concern is. For “too expensive,” ask what price they were ideally looking to pay, check if unused rooms can be removed, or reposition the service around quality, background-checked and insured cleaners, and the satisfaction guarantee. For “my old cleaner charged less,” ask what made them stop using the old cleaner. For “getting quotes,” affirm that doing research is good and ask what they are mainly looking for in a cleaning company.

Before ending the call, set expectations. Tell the client they will receive a confirmation email, expectation email, and reminders. Explain cancellation policy, recurring cancellation difference, no heavy lifting, no ladders, card hold two days before, payment after cleaning, and the post-clean satisfaction call. If the client is home, they should be honest during the walkthrough so cleaners can touch up anything while still there.

End by asking if they have any final questions, then confirm the booking and close warmly.
