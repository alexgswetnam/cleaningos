## Core Teaching
The handbook teaches that cleaner onboarding should not just be “here is the job, go clean.” Cleaners need a clear understanding of the client experience, the checklist outcomes, and how communication should happen before, during, and after the job.

The first major teaching is that cleaners should prepare before they arrive. They should double-check their schedule, mark “on the way” in Booking Koala, check in and out in Booking Koala, read job notes, and notify the team immediately if parking, access, timing, or gate code issues come up.

The second major teaching is that the walkthrough is one of the most important parts of the clean. It sets the tone, builds trust, catches scope issues early, and helps avoid misunderstandings. Cleaners are encouraged to greet the client warmly, ask them to walk through the home, ask questions, build a personal connection, and raise concerns before starting if the home is dirtier than expected.

The third major teaching is that documentation protects everybody. Before-and-after photos are especially important on first-time cleans. If something cannot be cleaned or appears damaged, the cleaner should take pictures for reference in case the client later mentions it to the office.

The fourth major teaching is that cleaners should work calmly, professionally, and with respect for the client’s home. No loud music, speakerphone, guests, unapproved team members, smoking, vaping, or eating on site. If the client is home, the cleaner should invite a final walkthrough and touch up anything the client notices before leaving.

The fifth major teaching is that the pay model must be clear. Cleaners are paid as independent contractors based on job revenue, not hourly wages. Tips go to the cleaners who performed the job unless otherwise specified. Workmanship re-cleans are unpaid and expected to be corrected within the stated window. Add-ons must be approved and priced before work.

The last major teaching is that clean type and home condition matter. Standard cleans are for regular upkeep, deep cleans are required for heavier conditions, move-ins are for nearly empty homes that were already cleaned, and move-outs are for empty homes needing a deeper reset. The dirtiness scale helps the office and cleaners plan the right scope, pricing, and expectations.

## Step-by-Step Process
1. Before arriving, double-check the schedule, read Booking Koala notes, and mark “on the way.”
2. Arrive professionally with supplies ready, knock first, and call the office if there is no response after a few minutes.
3. Ask the client to walk through the home and clarify preferences, exclusions, order of cleaning, beds, pets, and special concerns.
4. If the home is much dirtier than expected, take photos and text the office before starting.
5. Take before photos once cleaning starts on first-time cleans.
6. Complete the clean calmly and efficiently while respecting pets, clients working from home, and personal belongings.
7. If extra tasks exceed the approved scope, message the office before continuing.
8. Do a personal final walkthrough and, if the client is home, invite them to inspect the work.
9. Take after photos and upload them to Job Media in Booking Koala.
10. Check out in Booking Koala and notify the office when the job is complete.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not ask the client how long you are supposed to stay; check Booking Koala or call the office.
- Do not use client supplies unless requested or listed in the notes.
- Do not eat, smoke, vape, or take loud personal calls on site.
- Do not bring guests or unapproved team members.
- Do not leave early without confirming the clean is complete and client-approved when the client is home.
- Do not ignore a home that is significantly dirtier than expected; document it and contact the office before starting.
- Do not skip photos on first-time cleans or when something is damaged or impossible to clean.
- Do not perform add-ons or extra scope without approval.
- Do not treat unpaid workmanship re-cleans like new paid work.

## Cleaned Lesson Notes
The Cleaner Onboarding Handbook is built to help cleaners deliver warm, professional, consistent service while still respecting the contractor relationship. It starts by clarifying that contractors choose which jobs to accept and choose their own methods for meeting the published checklist outcomes. This is not an employment handbook.

Before each job, cleaners should double-check their schedule, read the Booking Koala notes, mark “on the way,” and check in and out in Booking Koala. Those timestamps verify arrival and departure and support quality and pay-related calculations, but they do not turn the pay model into hourly wages. If a cleaner may be late, has a gate code issue, cannot find the entrance, or has trouble parking, they should contact the team right away.

Arrival should be professional and friendly. Cleaners should bring supplies to the door, knock first, ring the bell if needed, and call the office if nobody responds. The walkthrough is one of the most important parts of the clean. It builds trust, helps the client feel taken care of, and gives the cleaner a chance to catch scope problems before work begins. Cleaners should ask if the client wants to walk through the home, ask about preferences, clarify rooms or areas to avoid, and make a real human connection.

If the home is dirtier than expected, the cleaner should set expectations early. If it is only one or two small areas, spend the extra time and tell the team it took longer. If the issue is significant, take photos and text the office before starting so the scope and compensation can be handled correctly.

Photos matter. On first-time cleans, take before photos once cleaning begins in each room and after photos during the final walkthrough. Use similar angles so the improvement is visible. If something cannot be cleaned or appears damaged, take photos for reference. Upload photos to Job Media in Booking Koala so the office can access them later.

During the clean, work calmly, efficiently, and carefully. Keep noise low. Respect pets, clients working from home, and personal belongings. If extra work goes beyond the approved scope, message the office before continuing.

At the end, cleaners should do a full personal walkthrough. If working with another cleaner, check each other’s areas. If the client is home, invite them to look around and point out anything that needs a touch-up. If the client is not home, text the office that the clean is complete and send after photos.

The handbook also gives a clear “do not do” list: do not ask the client how long to stay, do not use client supplies unless instructed, do not smoke or vape on site, do not bring guests or unapproved helpers, do not take loud personal calls, and do not leave early without confirming the work is complete.

For scheduling, cleaners should keep their default availability and specific date availability updated in Booking Koala. Friday is the requested day to review the upcoming week, and cleaners should avoid changing availability within 72 hours of a scheduled clean unless there is an emergency.

For pay, the handbook explains that cleaners are paid 40–60% of net job revenue, tips go to the cleaner unless otherwise specified, and the company aims for a target effective rate based on on-site time. Re-cleans caused by workmanship issues are not paid and should be completed within the stated window. Add-ons must be approved and priced before work is done.

Clean types are defined clearly. Standard cleans are for regular upkeep and homes up to about a 5/10 dirtiness level. Upkeep cleans are smaller specialty cleans. Deep cleans are required for homes rated 6/10 or higher or homes that have not been cleaned in a long time. Move-in cleans are for mostly empty homes that were already cleaned and need a final sanitization. Move-out cleans are for empty homes that need a deeper reset.

Add-ons include fridge cleaning, oven cleaning, interior window cleaning, and hand-wiped baseboards. The cleaner should understand what each add-on includes and what it does not include so the client experience stays consistent.
