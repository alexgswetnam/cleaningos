## Quick Summary
This resource is a subcontractor agreement for The Faithful Cleaners. It defines the relationship between the company and the subcontractor and makes clear that the subcontractor is an independent contractor, not an employee, partner, agent, or joint venture. The agreement says subcontractors perform accepted work orders, can accept or decline jobs, determine their own methods and sequence, provide their own tools and supplies, and may use qualified helpers if disclosed and approved under the safety standards. Compensation is structured as fixed-fee per job, typically 40–60% of net job revenue, with add-ons, tips, offsets, and discretionary project supplements addressed separately. The agreement also covers confidentiality, non-circumvention, client data, taxes, safety, background checks, insurance, incident reporting, re-cleans, termination, governing law, and e-signatures. Exhibit A explains when discretionary project supplements may be considered for materially mis-scoped jobs. The overall purpose is to protect the company, the client relationship, and the subcontractor relationship with clear written terms.
## Core Teaching
The agreement teaches that subcontractor relationships should be documented clearly. The cleaner is not just “helping out.” They are a subcontractor performing accepted jobs under work orders.

The agreement emphasizes autonomy. The subcontractor may accept or decline jobs, determine their own methods and sequence, and provide their own labor, tools, supplies, and protective equipment unless a work order says otherwise. Helpers can be used if they meet background and client-safety standards and are disclosed before entering a client property.

Compensation is tied to the job, not hourly wages. The agreement defines job fees as fixed-fee project payments, typically 40–60% of net job revenue. It also defines net job revenue by excluding sales tax, processing fees, discounts, refunds, and chargebacks. Add-ons and scope increases are paid using the same percentage methodology unless a work order states otherwise.

The agreement includes quality and re-clean language. If a material deficiency within the contracted scope is reported within 48 hours, the subcontractor is expected to perform a no-charge re-clean within the required time window. Items outside the original scope are billed as a new job.

The agreement also protects client relationships and information. Subcontractors cannot use or disclose confidential information except to perform jobs. They cannot solicit or perform cleaning services directly for clients introduced by the contractor during the agreement and for 12 months after the last job.

Insurance and safety are also core parts of the agreement. Subcontractors are responsible for safety, compliance, insurance, background checks for personnel entering client homes, and reporting property damage, injury, or security incidents quickly with photos and written details.
## Key Principles
- Put subcontractor terms in writing.
- Make clear the subcontractor is independent, non-exclusive, and not an employee.
- Use accepted work orders to define jobs.
- Subcontractors may accept or decline jobs.
- Subcontractors determine their own methods and sequence.
- Pay is per job, not hourly wages.
- Net job revenue excludes taxes, processing fees, discounts, refunds, and chargebacks.
- Re-cleans for material workmanship deficiencies are corrected without a new charge.
- Client data and client relationships must be protected.
- Insurance, background checks, and incident reporting need to be clear.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Fill in the effective date, commencement date, contractor information, and subcontractor information.
2. Confirm the term and termination rules.
3. Define services, autonomy, job acceptance, tools, supplies, helpers, and safety expectations.
4. Define compensation: job fee, net job revenue, add-ons, tips, travel, cancellations, statements, and offsets.
5. Confirm payment method, weekly schedule, W-9, and payment details.
6. Set quality and re-clean expectations.
7. Define return of property requirements.
8. Include confidentiality, non-circumvention, client data, and survival language.
9. Define taxes, independent contractor status, non-exclusivity, and no benefits.
10. Set insurance, background check, safety, incident reporting, and drug/alcohol rules.
11. Include governing law, venue, severability, entire agreement, amendments, e-signatures, and acknowledgments.
12. Attach Exhibit A for discretionary project supplement review.

## Cleaned Lesson Notes
The Subcontractor Agreement is a written agreement between The Faithful Cleaners, LLC and a subcontractor who performs residential or commercial cleaning services for the company’s clients under accepted work orders.

The agreement begins by defining the parties, effective date, and commencement date. It continues until either party terminates it. Either party can terminate without cause with seven days’ written notice, and the company can terminate immediately for cause for issues like material breach, safety violations, intoxication on site, data or privacy breach, or criminal activity.

The services section makes the contractor structure clear. The subcontractor performs cleaning services under accepted jobs. The subcontractor may accept or decline jobs, determines their own methods and sequence, and provides their own labor, tools, supplies, and protective equipment unless a specific work order says otherwise. The subcontractor may use qualified helpers at their own expense if the helpers meet background and safety standards and are disclosed before entering client property.

Compensation is paid as a fixed-fee per job. Unless otherwise specified, the job fee is 40–60% of net job revenue. Net job revenue means the amount collected from the client excluding sales tax, processing fees, discounts, refunds, and chargebacks. These are project payments, not hourly wages.

The agreement also includes discretionary project supplement language. If a job is materially mis-scoped or unusual on-site conditions affect project economics, the company may consider a supplement. The supplement is discretionary, project-specific, and not an hourly guarantee.

Approved add-ons and scope increases are paid using the same percentage method unless the work order says otherwise. Tips paid through the company’s system are passed through to the subcontractor on the normal pay schedule.

Travel and commute are the subcontractor’s responsibility unless a travel stipend is written into the work order. Same-day cancellations or no-access situations are only compensated if stated in the work order. Statements may show completed jobs, job fees, add-ons, refunds, chargebacks, and any project supplements. Check-in/check-out logs and photos are collected for client records, quality assurance, and billing verification, not to convert pay into hourly wages.

Offsets are allowed if a client refund or chargeback is issued due to workmanship, or if company property loaned to the subcontractor is not returned. Any deduction should be itemized.

The payment schedule is weekly on Friday via ACH or the subcontractor’s designated method for jobs completed and approved through the pay week. The subcontractor must keep a W-9 and payment details current. The agreement states that no minimum jobs, hours, or earnings are guaranteed.

Quality and re-cleans are addressed directly. If a material deficiency within scope is reported within 48 hours, the subcontractor will perform a no-charge re-clean within 48 hours or the client’s requested time. Items outside the original scope are billed as a new job.

The confidentiality and non-circumvention section protects pricing, operations, client lists, work orders, client information, names, addresses, access codes, schedules, and payment data. The subcontractor cannot solicit or perform cleaning services directly for a client introduced by the contractor during the agreement and for 12 months after the last job performed, except through the contractor.

The subcontractor is responsible for taxes and withholdings. The agreement states that the subcontractor is an independent contractor, not an employee, agent, partner, or joint venture, and has no authority to bind the company. The subcontractor is non-exclusive and free to perform services for others.

The safety and insurance section requires the subcontractor to be responsible for safety, compliance, background checks for personnel entering client premises, general liability insurance, auto liability if driving between jobs, and workers’ compensation if they have employees as required by law. Property damage, injury, or security incidents must be reported within four hours with photos and written details.

The agreement also covers alcohol and drugs, successors and assigns, indemnification, no waiver, governing law, venue, severability, entire agreement, amendments, e-signatures, and acknowledgments.

Exhibit A explains the compensation assurance schedule for discretionary project supplements. It is meant for cases where a job was materially different than expected, such as severe buildup, added rooms, unusual conditions, safety constraints, access issues, or documented mis-estimates. It is not hourly wages, not automatic, not a weekly floor, and not an employment benefit.
