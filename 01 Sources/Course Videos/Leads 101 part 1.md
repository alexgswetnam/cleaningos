# Leads 101 part 1

35 lessons on the Acquire (marketing) side of the Leads Engine: what a lead engine is and
why free posting isn't one, the free-vs-paid split, the actual channel-testing history of
the business (Thumbtack → GBP → Facebook bust → Google Ads), a four-lesson "Marketing
Basics [Season] Coaching" set — one per Season — a Paid Marketing Methods breakdown with
Q4 cost/close/booking figures per channel, healthy CPL/CPA ranges by platform, tracking
and attribution discipline, ICP and USP definitions, a client-learnings lesson on which
customer types are actually the best fit, a six-lesson website cluster (need one? build
one? which platform? does it get leads by itself?), an outcome-over-features copywriting
lesson, a "why most cleaning businesses are stuck" diagnosis, a marketing-as-testing book
recommendation, and a short lesson on why the owner should learn marketing before
outsourcing it.

**This source overlaps very heavily with material already in the vault** — expected going
in, per the ingest brief, since this is the vault's most developed area. Three
already-ingested sources cover much of the same ground: `leads 101 — Marketing Basics
series` (three of this course's four Season-coaching lessons, near-verbatim — see below),
`Cleaning Biz 101 — Acquire` (the Paid Marketing Methods breakdown, the Q4 PPC/Thumbtack/
GBP numbers, and the "website builds trust, not sales" lesson all reappear here as the
same underlying content in a different course wrapper — same pattern as `Leads 101 Part
2`'s relationship to `Cleaning Biz 101 — Convert`), and `Google Biz Profile Setup and
Growth` (the "$6k/month from one GBP" figure, verbatim). Most updates below are
corroboration — adding this source to an existing page's `sources:` list — rather than new
claims. See "Concepts Updated" for exactly which lessons duplicate which prior source.

**Retired Season name, per standing precedent.** "Marketing Basics: Sustain and Sell
Season Coaching" uses the retired fourth-Season name. Per [[Four Seasons]]'s own
Superseded note and the precedent already set on `leads 101 — Marketing Basics series`,
this is translated to **Harvest** and is not treated as a fresh Canon conflict — it's the
same retired term the vault has already handled once.

**Two Type 1/3 audit obligations from the ingest brief.** Does anything here bear on the
contested **Reciprocity**, **Drop Scope Not Price**, or **Meta close rate (3–5% vs. ~10%)**
claims? **No**, on all three. None of the 35 lessons mention discounting-as-reciprocity or
scope-reduction as a pricing lever, and none give a Meta-specific close-rate figure (the
CPL ranges given for Meta in "What Is a Healthy CPL and CPA" — $5–10 good, $15–25 rough —
are cost figures, not close-rate figures). The course also carries no date of its own
(`date_translated: unknown`), so even a bearing claim couldn't settle either contested item

## Concepts Created

**New page:** [[Website Strategy]] — consolidates six lessons (do I need one, how do I
build one, when do I get one, which platform, will it get me leads by itself, and the
existing "builds trust not sales" material) into the single decision an Owner actually
faces: what role does a website play and when do I invest in it. Distinct from
[[Website Technical SEO Basics]] (the technical checklist once you have a site) and
[[PPC Landing Page Strategy]] (the narrower question of where *paid* traffic should land,
already covered).

**New page:** [[Ideal Customer Profile]] — consolidates two near-duplicate ICP lessons
plus the client-learnings lesson's archetypes (busy professionals, pet owners, elderly
clients) into one page. No existing page defined ICP as its own concept.

**New page:** [[Sell The Result Not The Service]] — the outcome-over-features copywriting
lesson (TurboTax/Cancun analogies). Distinct from [[Cleaning Is Holy Work]] (a different
claim — cleaning as meaningful work, not a copywriting technique) and from
[[Differentiation And Trust Building]] (differentiators, not the benefits-vs-features
framing).

**New page:** [[Owner Learns Marketing First]] — thin but genuinely undocumented decision:
whether to outsource marketing before understanding it yourself. Directly echoes
[[Philosophy]]'s belief that tools and delegation can't substitute for the owner's own
understanding, applied specifically to marketing.
