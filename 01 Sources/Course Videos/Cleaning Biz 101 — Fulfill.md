# Cleaning Biz 101 — Fulfill

## 1. Fulfill: Delivering the Service

This part of the business is where sales promises become reality. Acquire gets leads,
Convert gets bookings, Fulfill turns bookings into a smooth experience, happy clients, and
clean operations.

If Fulfill is weak, the business feels stressful even while "growing" — last-minute
reschedules, no-shows, confused clients, cleaners showing up unprepared, inconsistent
quality, constant fires. The goal of Fulfill is predictable delivery: consistent pricing,
clean scheduling, automatic confirmations, cleaners who know what "done" looks like, and
issues handled fast and professionally.

## 2. The Fulfillment Flow

Once someone books, the business should run almost automatically. The fulfillment
timeline: Booked → Confirmation (date/time window, service type, address, what to expect,
sent automatically by Harvest CRM) → 5-Days-Out Reminder (confirms they still need it,
gives time to update entry instructions, reduces last-minute cancellations) → 24-Hours-Out
Reminder (locks it in, reduces no-shows, catches day-of issues) → On-the-Way Text
(increases professionalism, reduces "where are you?" messages) → Payment Charged
Confirmation (clean communication, reduces awkwardness).

Side note: only book times a cleaner has actually marked as available — otherwise you're
going back and forth with cleaners and clients confirming a promise you can't necessarily
keep.

## 3. Fulfillment Is Where Retention Suffers

Most people think retention starts after the clean. It doesn't — it starts the moment
someone saw the website or spoke on the phone. A smooth client experience (clear
confirmations, consistent reminders, professional arrival, set expectations)
automatically increases repeat bookings, reviews, and referrals. Fulfill is one of the
biggest silent drivers or killers of retention. Clients have specifically praised
outstanding communication as a differentiator that gives a more luxury feeling.

## 4. Pricing + Packages

Most scheduling chaos starts with pricing chaos. Pricing shouldn't be guessed on each
call — quoting should be systemized. The goal isn't a perfect price, it's consistency:
a base price model, add-ons, a recurring discount structure (if used), and minimums so
small jobs don't drain the business.

The method: charge a flat rate, reverse-engineered from a target hourly rate. Target
$60/hour per cleaner; a 3-bed/2-bath, 1,800 sqft monthly clean for a couple with one kid
averaging 3 hours becomes $179 + tax. A 3,000 sqft deep clean averaging 10 hours at a
$55/hour target becomes $550. Every cleaner works at a different pace — track sqft/hour
per cleaner in a spreadsheet for about a month to find real averages. A "condition of the
home" parameter (e.g. dirtiness level 1–10) is a real time driver worth building into the
price. Everyone prices differently — no universal number is prescribed.

## 5. BookingKoala's Real Job (and Why We Use It)

BookingKoala is the fulfillment source of truth: if a job is quoted or booked, this is
where scheduling and pricing details live and are trusted. Trying to schedule in your
head, in texts, or in random calendars is when the business starts breaking.

BookingKoala's responsibilities: pricing and package consistency, calendar availability
and scheduling rules, booking confirmations, reminder texts/emails, payment-charged
notifications, recurring scheduling structure, and cleaners knowing their pay and
schedule through the app. In short: it prevents the business from being run by memory.
