Core Teaching
A USP is a unique selling proposition, and it is unique to your business. The speaker cannot tell you exactly what your USP should be because it depends on the DNA of your own cleaning business.
The lesson gives several possible USPs a cleaning business could use. The speaker’s company includes being Christian as part of its identity. They also use a satisfaction guarantee. Other possible differentiators include licensed and insured cleaners, English-speaking cleaners, background-checked cleaners, cleaners with previous experience, and owner involvement in the early stage.
When the owner is still taking calls, that can be a USP: the prospect is talking directly to the owner, not just a VA. The speaker notes that they no longer have that USP because VAs now take calls.
The lesson also explains that not every common differentiator matters to every business. For example, they do not push eco/green cleaning because customers rarely ask for it in their market. In another area, like somewhere with more health-conscious customers, that might matter more.
The goal is to find real differentiators and use them as ammo in calls, on the website, and in marketing.
Key Principles
Your USP must fit your business’s DNA.
Do not copy a USP that does not apply to you.
A satisfaction guarantee is a strong USP most students can implement.
Licensed, insured, English-speaking, background-checked, experienced cleaners can all be differentiators.
Owner involvement is a useful USP while the owner is still taking calls.
Eco/green should only be emphasized if your target clients care.
USPs give you ammo for sales and marketing.
Step-by-Step Process
Identify what is actually true and different about your business.
List possible differentiators such as guarantee, insurance, background checks, language, experience, values, or owner involvement.
Decide which differentiators matter to your target clients.
Avoid using a USP just because other businesses use it.
Put the strongest USPs into your sales calls, website, and marketing.
Update your USP as the business changes, such as when VAs take over calls.
