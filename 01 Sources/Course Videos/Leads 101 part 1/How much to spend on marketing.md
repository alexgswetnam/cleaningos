Core Teaching
There is no exact marketing spend number for everyone. The recommendation seen across the industry is about 10% of revenue.
When you are small and do not have much revenue, marketing is an investment. The speaker’s example was doing about $6,000 in cleans in the first month while putting around $4,000 of ad spend on a credit card. The next month they spent around $2,000–$3,000. Then they slowed marketing for a few months, retained clients, and used the cleans to pay off the credit cards.
But the reason they were confident spending early was that both partners came from sales and marketing backgrounds. They knew how to handle multiple leads per day, call immediately, follow up consistently, and convert leads into cleans.
The warning is that you should not spend a bunch of money acquiring leads if you are not confident you can convert those leads into bookings. You also should not spend heavily if you are not confident your cleaning team can take care of and retain those clients.
The lesson also discusses using credit cards for marketing spend. With 0% APR, it can let you grow now and pay down the debt later. Without 0% APR, it is like renting money, so the marketing needs to be profitable enough to justify the cost.
Key Principles
Around 10% of revenue is the general marketing spend benchmark given.
Small businesses may need to treat marketing as an investment.
Do not spend heavily on leads if sales is not dialed in.
Do not spend heavily if the cleaning team cannot retain the clients.
Marketing and sales are described as the hardest parts of the cleaning business.
Cleaner hiring, VAs, systems, and logistics are easier to solve once leads and sales work.
Credit card ad spend can be useful, but only when the marketing is profitable and the owner understands the risk.
Step-by-Step Process
Start with the 10% of revenue benchmark as a general reference point.
Decide whether you are in a stage where marketing needs to be an investment.
Check whether your sales process is dialed in before increasing spend.
Check whether your cleaning team can deliver and retain clients.
Only spend aggressively if you can call leads quickly, follow up, and convert them.
If using a credit card, understand whether it is 0% APR or whether you are “renting money.”
Use profits from cleans and retained clients to pay down the marketing debt.

Cleaned Lesson Notes
How much should you spend on marketing? I cannot give you an exact number. A common recommended amount across the industry is about 10% of revenue.
When you are smaller and do not have much revenue, marketing is going to be an investment. In our first month, we did about $6,000 in cleans and put the ad spend on a credit card. We spent around $4,000 in the first month and around $2,000–$3,000 in the second month. Then we stopped marketing heavily, kept those clients, spent less than $1,000 for the next few months, and paid down the credit cards from the cleans and retained clients.
We still had some of those clients from when we started in February 2025, including weekly, biweekly, and monthly clients. We also messed up some relationships early because we did not know what we were doing on the cleaning side. We sent bad cleaners, priced incorrectly, quoted incorrectly, promised things, and underdelivered.
I would not spend money on marketing to get leads if your sales process is not dialed in. Elijah and I came from sales and marketing backgrounds, so we came into this with that part nailed down. The hardest thing to nail down in the cleaning business is marketing and sales. It is not that hard to find cleaners, use systems, find a VA, or handle logistics compared to marketing and sales.
We were confident spending a lot early because we knew we could handle multiple leads a day, call them immediately, follow up consistently, get cleans, and have a good conversion rate.
Do not spend a bunch of money acquiring leads if you are not confident you can turn those leads into bookings. Also do not spend heavily if you are not confident your cleaning team can take care of those clients and retain them.
I recommend using a credit card because you can spend the money now and pay it off later, especially if you have 0% APR. If you do not have 0% APR, then it is like renting money. You might pull out $2,000 or $3,000, put it into profitable marketing, and pay the cost of borrowing while the business grows. The profits from the cleans should then help pay down the debt.
