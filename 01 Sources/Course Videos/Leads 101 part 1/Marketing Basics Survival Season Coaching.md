Quick Summary
In the Survival season, having a marketing problem is normal. It usually means the business has no leads coming in, does not know how to get leads, has not invested in marketing, or tried marketing that did not work. The priority is not to build elaborate systems or do a billion different things. The priority is to find one channel that brings in leads at a profitable cost per acquisition. The lesson warns that many people try to save money by posting in Facebook groups, messaging people, or doing free activities, then wonder why they get bad customers or no real interest. To move into Stability, the business needs to test until it finds one channel that can produce leads at something like $20–$40 per day and convert them into bookings. You are not going to grow without leads coming in and leads turning into bookings.
Core Teaching
In the Survival season, a marketing problem is typical. It looks like having no leads, not knowing how to get leads, not having invested in marketing, or having tried marketing that did not work.
The priority is to pick one channel that can find leads profitably. You do not need to do a billion things. You do not need elaborate systems. You need one channel that brings leads at a profitable cost per acquisition.
A common mistake is trying to avoid spending money by posting in Facebook groups, messaging people, and doing free activities. Then owners wonder why they get bad customers or why people do not reach out.
One of the most important things in business is getting people interested in buying your product or service. If you are in Survival and do not have leads coming in, you likely have a lack of marketing knowledge, which the rest of the videos are meant to solve.
To get from Survival to Stability, test until you find one marketing channel where you can spend something like $20 or $40 per day, get leads, and convert those leads into bookings.
Key Principles
In Survival, the priority is lead flow.
You only need one profitable channel to start.
Do not overcomplicate the marketing system too early.
Free posting often brings poor control and poor customers.
You may need to test multiple channels to find the one that works.
Leads must become bookings for the business to grow.
A profitable cost per acquisition matters more than simply getting attention.
Step-by-Step Process
Accept that the immediate problem is lack of leads.
Choose one channel to test.
Spend enough to see whether leads come in, even if it is $20–$40 per day.
Track whether those leads can be converted into bookings.
Measure whether the cost per acquisition is profitable.
If the channel does not work, test another one.
Move to Stability once one channel reliably brings profitable bookings.
