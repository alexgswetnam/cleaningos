## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that there is no one lead source that is guaranteed to work everywhere. The philosophy is to test everything, see what works, and then put more money and effort into the highest-ROI channel.

The first place to start is free marketing. Google Business Profile is the biggest recommendation because it can bring in free leads through Google Maps and the Map Pack. The lesson gives examples of bookings coming in from Google Business Profile even while paid marketing was paused. Because it is free and can compound through reviews and optimization, it should be one of the first things a cleaning business sets up and improves.

The lesson also mentions that there is a 30-minute video on how to set up and optimize Google Business Profile, and an entire classroom module on setting up, scaling, and optimizing it. Jackie Chu is mentioned as a YouTube resource for deeper learning, though the course has condensed the information for cleaning businesses.

Other free or low-cost methods include posting in Facebook groups, referrals, calling existing customers, asking customers to post on Facebook or Instagram stories, and tapping into old leads or one-time cleans. Facebook groups may produce leads, but often the quality is lower because people there may be looking for cheap options and the business may be competing with many other comments.

The lesson also highlights the value of CRM follow-up. A Facebook ad lead from seven months earlier eventually booked because Harvest CRM continued automated follow-up. That one clean can pay for the system, and there are usually more than just one.

For paid lead sources, Meta ads are not recommended as the first choice based on this cleaning business experience, although they can work in other industries or areas. Google Ads worked well in the example, with $43 cost per lead, a 41% close rate, and about $104 cost per booking. But setup matters, because another person spent over $1,000 with no bookings after setting it up wrong. LSA can work if cost per call makes sense, but in the example it was $70 to $90 per call and did not make sense. Thumbtack worked well for the business but may not be good in every area.

The final point is to test different channels, identify the one with the highest ROI, max it out, and then move to the next one.
## Key Principles
- Test everything and see what works in your area.
- Start by capitalizing on free lead sources.
- Google Business Profile should be one of the first lead sources to build.
- Free marketing can still bring in leads while paid marketing is paused.
- CRM follow-up can convert leads months later.
- Facebook groups can work, but may attract lower-quality, price-focused leads.
- Meta ads may work in some areas, but were not strong in this cleaning business experience.
- Google Ads can work well if set up correctly.
- LSA only makes sense if the cost per call works.
- Pick the channel with the highest ROI, max it out, then move to the next.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Start with free marketing before relying heavily on paid ads.
2. Set up and optimize Google Business Profile.
3. Begin collecting reviews and using GBP to drive calls, website visits, and leads.
4. Use referrals by calling existing customers and asking who they know that may need cleaning.
5. Ask happy customers to post about the company on Facebook, Instagram, or stories in exchange for a small incentive if desired.
6. Follow up with old leads and one-time cleans inside the CRM.
7. Test Facebook groups, but understand the lead quality may be lower and competition may be high.
8. When testing paid marketing, test channels like Google Ads, LSA, Thumbtack, and Meta based on the area and numbers.
9. Track CPL, close rate, cost per booking, and ROI for each channel.
10. Max out the channel with the highest ROI before moving heavily into the next one.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Skipping free lead sources and going straight into paid ads.
- Ignoring Google Business Profile setup and optimization.
- Assuming Facebook groups will bring premium leads without competition.
- Running Meta ads and expecting them to work the same as other industries.
- Setting up Google Ads wrong and then concluding Google Ads does not work.
- Using LSA when the cost per call does not make sense.
- Failing to follow up with old leads long term inside a CRM.
- Not testing multiple channels before choosing what to scale.
- Trying to scale a channel before knowing its ROI.
