## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that marketing decisions need to be based on tracked numbers, not feelings. A channel can feel like it is working because it recently produced a lead, or feel bad because it seemed expensive, but the numbers may tell a different story.

At minimum, every lead source should be measured by cost per lead, close rate, and cost per booking. Cost per booking is calculated from CPL and close rate. Without those numbers, the business cannot confidently decide where to put time or money.

Attribution means tagging every lead with the source it came from. The lesson names PPC, Thumbtack, GBP or Map Pack, referral, past client, organic website, Yelp if being tested, and other channels. This matters because memory and emotion are unreliable. The brain will favor whatever happened most recently.

The hidden win is follow-up attribution. A lead source should not only be judged by where the lead came from, but also by how long it took the lead to convert. Some leads book the same day, some two weeks later, and some respond months later to an automated follow-up text. Without tracking inside the CRM, a business may shut off a campaign too early and kill future bookings it did not realize were coming.

Good tracking is simple: every lead gets a source tag, every booking traces back to the original source, the tracking sheet is updated daily, and performance is reviewed weekly or monthly so the budget can be reallocated.

## Step-by-Step Process
1. Create source tags for each lead source, such as PPC, Thumbtack, GBP, referral, past client, organic website, and Yelp if testing.
2. Make sure every lead enters the CRM with the correct source tag.
3. Track cost per lead for every lead engine.
4. Track close rate by dividing bookings by leads.
5. Track cost per booking by using CPL and close rate.
6. Trace every booking back to the original lead source.
7. Update the tracking sheet daily with leads and results.
8. Review performance weekly or monthly and move budget toward what is working.
