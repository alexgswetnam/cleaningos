## Quick Summary
This lesson defines ICP as ideal customer profile. Knowing the ideal customer lets the business tailor marketing, wording, and phone conversations to that person. The example ICP is a busy professional with a family who makes good money and wants reliability, great communication, English-speaking cleaners, background checks, insurance, and a clean that is done right before guests or clients come over. The lesson contrasts this with Homeaglow, whose ideal customer is described as budget-focused and looking for the cheapest cleaning. For this type of business, the ideal customer is not the cheap person with a super low budget. The best clients are people looking for a full white glove service and willing to pay full price.
## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that a cleaning business needs to know exactly who it is trying to attract. ICP means ideal customer profile. Once the ICP is clear, marketing and sales language can be tailored to that person’s wants, needs, pains, and desires.

The example ideal customer is a busy professional with a family who makes a lot of money. This person wants cleaners to show up on time, do good work, speak English, communicate well, be background checked, be insured, and make sure the clean is done right. They want to be able to have guests or clients over without worrying about how the home looks.

When that ICP is known, the business can speak directly to that person in marketing and on the phone. The message changes because the business understands what matters to them.

The lesson compares this to Homeaglow, whose ideal customer is described as not luxury, not premium, and not high quality. The lesson says their ideal customer is someone very cheap and budget-focused, so their marketing is built around low-price offers.

For this cleaning business model, the ideal customer is not the cheapest person. The goal is to attract people looking for a full white glove service. These clients are easier to sell to, easier to manage, and less likely to create constant discount-related headaches. The best clients are the ones who pay full price.
## Key Principles
- ICP means ideal customer profile.
- The ICP changes the words used in marketing and sales.
- Marketing should speak to the ideal customer’s wants, needs, pains, and desires.
- A busy professional who values reliability and quality can be a strong ICP.
- The cheapest buyer is not the ideal customer for this model.
- Different companies target different ICPs, and their marketing reflects that.
- A full white glove service should target people willing to pay full price.
- The right ICP makes sales and management easier.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Define the person the business most wants to attract.
2. List that person’s wants, needs, pains, and desires.
3. Identify what they care about, such as punctuality, communication, background checks, insurance, English-speaking cleaners, and quality.
4. Write marketing that speaks directly to those priorities.
5. Use the same understanding during phone sales conversations.
6. Avoid targeting the cheapest customers if the business is built around premium service.
7. Focus on attracting full-price clients who want a white glove experience.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Trying to market to everyone.
- Targeting cheap, budget-focused customers while trying to sell a premium service.
- Not adapting marketing words to the buyer’s real concerns.
- Ignoring how the ICP affects targeting.
- Bringing in broke or discount-focused clients who create headaches.
