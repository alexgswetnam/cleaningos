Another mistake is deciding that marketing, Google Ads, or Facebook does not work when the real issue is how the campaign was executed. ## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that marketing should not be guessing. Many people throw money into ads and hope something works, then conclude the business or platform is broken when the real issue is their execution.

The lesson makes a clear distinction between a platform not working and a person's version of that platform not working. It is not that Google Ads does not work; it may be that the way it was set up was wrong. It is not that Facebook does not work; it may be that the way it was tried did not work in that area or with that creative.

Another major mistake is not tracking the numbers. Owners need to know how much they are spending, cost per lead, cost per acquisition, average ticket, and how many people move from one-time cleans to recurring. Without those numbers, they are just guessing.

The lesson also says that many owners blame marketing when the real problem is sales. A lead may not answer the first call, the second call, or the first text. That does not mean the lead is bad. The business needs to keep following up, track where everyone is in the pipeline, and get to a clear yes or no. Harvest CRM is mentioned as a system for keeping track of leads and follow-up.

Finally, the lesson warns that what worked for one person is not guaranteed to work for another. Still, it is smarter to find what is already working for someone else and test it than to create ad creatives or PPC campaigns completely from scratch with no experience.
## Key Principles
- Do not throw money at marketing and hope it works.
- A failed attempt does not mean the platform itself does not work.
- Track spend, CPL, CPA, average ticket, and recurring conversion.
- Many marketing problems are actually sales follow-up problems.
- A quote is not the end of the sales process.
- Follow up until there is a definitive yes or no.
- What works for one business may not automatically work for another.
- It is smarter to model proven campaigns than to invent from scratch.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Stop guessing and start tracking the performance of every marketing channel.
2. Record spend, cost per lead, cost per acquisition, average ticket, and recurring conversion.
3. When a channel fails, diagnose the setup and execution before deciding the platform does not work.
4. Follow up with every lead multiple times instead of assuming no answer means no interest.
5. Use a system like Harvest CRM to track where leads are in the pipeline.
6. Continue following up after a quote until there is a definitive yes or no.
7. When testing a new channel, study what is already working for someone else and model it.
8. Test what works for others in your own market instead of assuming it is guaranteed.
