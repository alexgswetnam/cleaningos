Quick Summary
This lesson explains that people do not buy services; they buy results, feelings, and outcomes. Most marketing should not focus on the technical details of the service. The lesson uses TurboTax as an example: the ad sells easy, simple, stress-free taxes, not the buttons inside the software. It also uses a flight to Cancun as an analogy: people are sold on the vacation, beach, culture, food, and escape from work, not the airport process. For a cleaning business, that means selling the relief of having the dog hair gone, feeling proud when friends come over, or removing stress from a household. The lesson warns against saying “we do these 72 things” because that is not what the customer is buying. A satisfaction guarantee helps because it lets you sell the customer on being happy with the final result.
Core Teaching
The point of your marketing is not to explain every technical detail of your cleaning service. The point is to help the customer see the result they want and the feeling they are trying to buy.
When TurboTax advertises, it does not sell the software buttons or each step of the tax process. It sells the result: your taxes are done easily, simply, quickly, and without stress. The customer buys the outcome.
The same thing applies to a cleaning business. You are not just selling “we mop floors, clean baseboards, and make it smell good.” You are selling relief from dog hair everywhere. You are selling the feeling of not being embarrassed when friends, family, or in-laws come over. You are selling the ability to sit in a clean home without stress.
The Cancun flight analogy makes the same point. A travel company sells the vacation: the beach, the food, the culture, time with friends, and getting away from work. They do not sell the TSA line, boarding process, luggage storage, and airplane steps. Cleaning business owners should not market as if customers are buying the process. Customers are buying what the process gives them.
This is also why a satisfaction guarantee matters. It supports the promise that the customer will be satisfied and happy with the service at the end of the clean.
Key Principles
People buy outcomes, feelings, and results.
Features are less powerful than benefits.
The customer is not buying your checklist; they are buying relief.
Your copy should speak to the customer’s pain points.
Cleaning can solve emotional problems, not just physical mess.
The process matters internally, but the outcome matters in marketing.
A satisfaction guarantee helps sell the final result.
Step-by-Step Process
Identify what the customer is actually stressed about.
Turn the service feature into the emotional or practical result it creates.
Write marketing around the result, not the checklist.
Use examples the customer recognizes, like dog hair, guests coming over, or not having enough time.
Show how the service makes them feel after the clean.
Use the satisfaction guarantee to reinforce the outcome.

Cleaned Lesson Notes
A common issue in marketing, not just in cleaning businesses, is that people get confused about the marketing message. When you watch a commercial on TV, they usually are not showing every feature and why the product is technically better. They show the end result.
TurboTax is a good example. Their ad does not say, “Look at this software, this button is here, the tax thing is done here, and here are steps one through three.” They show that your taxes can be done easily, simply, quickly, and without stress. Your taxes are taken care of. They sell the end result and the way you will feel.
Put that philosophy into a cleaning business. You are not selling, “We will come in, clean your home, mop your floors, clean the baseboards, and make it smell good.” You are digging into the customer’s pain points. If they have a dog, they know dog hair gets everywhere. Maybe they are embarrassed about how messy their home is when friends, family, or in-laws come over. Maybe it is a stress factor, and they know it needs to be done, but they do not have time.
The message is: let someone else take care of it so you can sit at home happy in a clean place, not feeling judged. People might even say, “Wow, your home is so clean. How do you keep it so clean?”
You are selling an outcome, a feeling, and a result. You are not selling the technicals or the features. You are selling the benefits of the service.
The same thing happens when someone sells a vacation. If someone sells you a flight to Cancun, they are really selling the vacation: fun, getting away from work, time with friends, culture, food, the beach, and the experience. They are not selling TSA, waiting at the gate, boarding the plane, and putting luggage away. That is not how you sell.
For a cleaning business, do not write emails or sales scripts that just say, “We do these 72 things in your home.” That is not what someone is buying. Someone might be trying to take strain off the relationship with their spouse because nobody is getting to the cleaning and both people are blaming each other. So you can say, somewhat jokingly, “This will save your marriage by having a cleaning.”
The point is to sell the benefits and the outcome, not the technicals or the features. This is also why a satisfaction guarantee matters. It lets you sell the idea that the customer will be satisfied and happy with the service at the end of the clean.
