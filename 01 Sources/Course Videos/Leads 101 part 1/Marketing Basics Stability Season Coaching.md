Core Teaching
In the Stability season, you probably already have one or two marketing channels bringing in leads. The issue is usually how profitable those channels are.
If the problem is close rate, that is a sales problem, and the student should look at the material on converting leads into bookings. But if the marketing channel itself needs work, the goal is to make it more reliable and profitable.
To move from Stability to Scale, you need a reliable marketing channel and a reliable process for getting leads at a cost per lead that makes sense. The leads need to be good quality, and you should not be capped on how much you can spend.
Thumbtack is used as an example of a platform that may be capped depending on the area. In some areas, there may not be enough lead flow to spend even $50 per day.
The work in this season is tracking cost per lead, tracking close rate, making sure leads are profitable, lowering cost per lead, improving lead quality, and turning marketing into an equation.
Key Principles
Stability means you have one or two channels producing leads.
Profitability is the next problem to solve.
Close rate issues belong to the sales/conversion side.
Lead quality matters as much as lead cost.
Some channels may be capped by local demand.
To reach Scale, marketing cannot feel like a guessing game.
Track cost per lead and close rate until the numbers are clear.
Step-by-Step Process
Identify the one or two channels currently producing leads.
Track cost per lead for each channel.
Track close rate for each channel.
Decide whether the issue is marketing or sales conversion.
Improve cost per lead and lead quality.
Check whether the channel has enough volume to scale.
Keep optimizing until the channel becomes reliable and profitable.
Move toward Scale when marketing feels like an equation.
