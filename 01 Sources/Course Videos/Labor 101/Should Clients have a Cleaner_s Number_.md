## Problems This Solves
- Clients bypassing the business and messaging cleaners directly.
- Cleaners having access to client contact information unnecessarily.
- Opening the door to poaching.
- Losing control of communication and client experience.
- Not knowing when exceptions are reasonable.
## Quick Summary
This lesson teaches that clients should generally not have the cleaner’s number and cleaners should generally not have the client’s number. The business should be the communicator between the client and the subcontractor because the client is doing business with the company. The lesson acknowledges that rare exceptions happen, such as a weekly client who only uses one cleaner and is sometimes not home. Even then, the cleaner should relay communication back to the company and the company should keep trying to direct the client back to the proper channel. The main reason to avoid direct contact is that it opens the door to poaching and makes communication harder to control. In the business’s experience, only around one or two percent of cleans involve this kind of direct contact.
## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that communication should flow through the business. The client hired the company, not the individual cleaner, and the company needs to remain the central point of contact for scheduling, issues, preferences, and expectations.

There are rare situations where a cleaner and client end up communicating directly. For example, a client may only accept one cleaner, have a weekly recurring service, or not be home during the clean. But those should be exceptions, not the system.

The lesson’s concern is poaching and communication control. If clients and cleaners are directly connected, it becomes easier for side deals, confusion, missed updates, or client preferences to bypass the business. Strong cleaners should want to keep doing their job well without taking on the business owner’s responsibilities.
## Key Principles
- The business should be the main communicator.
- Clients are doing business with the company, not just the cleaner.
- Direct cleaner-client contact should be rare.
- Exceptions can happen, but they should not become the norm.
- Direct contact opens the door to poaching.
- Good cleaners usually do not want the extra business-owner headaches.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Do not give client numbers to cleaners by default.
2. Do not give cleaner numbers to clients by default.
3. Route scheduling, issues, and preferences through the business.
4. When a rare exception happens, have the cleaner relay communication back to the business.
5. Keep reminding the client to contact the business instead of the cleaner.
6. Use this policy to reduce poaching risk and keep the client experience controlled.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Clients should not have a cleaner’s number.”
- “Cleaners should not have a client’s number.”
- “You need to reach out to us, not the cleaner.”
- “You should always be the communicator between client and your subcontractors.”
- “It opens the door for poaching.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Making direct contact the normal workflow.
- Letting clients bypass the company with scheduling or complaints.
- Ignoring poaching risk.
- Assuming exceptions should become the standard.
- Failing to centralize client preferences and updates.
