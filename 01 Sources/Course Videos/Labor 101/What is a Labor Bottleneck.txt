Labor bottleneck is when you have demand, but staffing is unstable. Turnover, no-shows, inconsistent quality. Or you just can’t find cleaners, period.



Labor problems look different based on your season.



I have a student named Shane in California. He was in that survival season. He couldn’t find cleaners for three months. Three whole months.



So if you’re reading this and you’re like “yeah, that’s me,” here’s a method I teach that works way better than people think:



Use Google Maps to call other small cleaning companies and subcontract their cleaners.



And you don’t call the big dogs with 200 reviews.



You go to like page three or page four of Google… or you zoom into a small neighborhood on Google Maps… and you call the cleaners with less than five reviews.



Check this out to see me do it live, it took less than 10 minutes to find a cleaner that was a good fit.



They’re usually hungry. They usually want extra work.



And you can build your business with subcontractors, that easily.



How do you solve labor, depending on your season?







Survival season: get one or two reliable helpers, or you do the cleanings yourself until you’ve got steady cash.



Stability season: onboarding checklist + onboarding packet. Reliability comes from standards. This is where a Client Experience Guide and clear expectations stop chaos.



Scale season: backup coverage and team leads. Growth collapses the second one person quits if you don’t build redundancy.



Sustain/Sell season: reduce risk by onboarding new cleaners every month and putting them through a quality testing phase. You’re building an asset, not a hustle.
