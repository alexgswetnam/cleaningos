You’re in a really dangerous spot if you pay hourly for both business and legal reasons:

You’re telling cleaners “If you work longer, I’ll pay you more.” So naturally, cleaners are going to make a job slower than it needs to be. The client has a longer, slower clean - and you are paying more for it.

This also penalizes your best, faster cleaners by saying “You got a hard, 4 hour job done in 3 hours. Now I’m going to pay you less.”

You will push away great cleaners with this pricing model. It’s not ideal - whether you’re small or at scale.

From a legal perspective, you are also straddling the line between 1099 and employee unless you have extremely tight documentation and invoicing with specific language. It’s very tough to do this legally because there’s a lot of factors that the government considers to determine if you are treating a contractor like an employee - and if you research, there’s cleaning companies that have been forced to cough up hundreds of thousands because they did this (and other similar things that shouldn’t be done)

I would talk to ChatGPT and ask it to audit your payment structure and how you treat your contractors, as it will likely be a shock to realize that if the government were to audit your business; you’d be in a lot of trouble.

Regardless of legal ramifications, if you’re running a 1099 model - It’s likely a bad business decision. I’ve talked to dozens of cleaning business owners over the past year who make more than $30k/mo in their business - and I don’t know a SINGLE one that pays contractors to clock in and clock out for hourly pay… There’s a reason for that
