## Problems This Solves
- Onboarding cleaners without clear documents.
- Misunderstandings around scope of work, pay, safety, and communication.
- Not protecting client privacy.
- Not having subcontractor agreements, W-9s, proof of insurance, or background check consent.
- Spending extra money on separate signing tools when the CRM can handle it.
## Quick Summary
This lesson explains the documents the business gives cleaners and why those documents matter. Good documents protect the business, clients, and cleaners while preventing misunderstandings. The lesson recommends a cleaner packet or handbook that includes expectations, policies, scope of work, pay, safety, communication, checklists, and reference guides. It also mentions confidentiality and client privacy agreements so cleaners do not share client information or post client homes. The business also uses an independent contractor agreement, W-9, proof of insurance, and background check consent. Harvest CRM is mentioned as a way to send and sign documents without paying for a separate tool like DocuSign.
## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that cleaner onboarding should be documented. Verbal expectations are not enough because misunderstandings create problems later. Cleaner documents should clarify what is included and excluded, how communication works, how pay works, how policies work, and what the cleaner is acknowledging.

Some documents are for signatures, while others are reference guides. The cleaner handbook can include home expectations, scope of work, cleaning checklists, communication expectations, pay information, safety, and how to use the business systems.

The lesson also emphasizes protecting client privacy. Cleaners should not be sharing client information, posting client homes, reaching out directly to clients, or soliciting work from clients. The business also includes terms around client or cleaner solicitation to discourage poaching.

Important onboarding documents mentioned include an independent contractor agreement, W-9, proof of insurance, and background check consent. The speaker also mentions using Harvest CRM to send and sign these documents instead of paying separately for DocuSign.
## Key Principles
- Good documents protect the business, the client, and the cleaner.
- Clear documents prevent misunderstandings.
- Cleaner onboarding should include both signed acknowledgments and reference guides.
- Client privacy needs to be protected in writing.
- Non-solicitation and anti-poaching language should be addressed.
- The business should collect contractor documents before assigning work.
- A CRM can make document signing easier.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Create a cleaner packet or handbook.
2. Include expectations, policies, scope of work, pay, safety, communication, and checklists.
3. Have cleaners sign the documents that require acknowledgment.
4. Use a confidentiality and client privacy agreement.
5. Include independent contractor agreement terms.
6. Collect a W-9, proof of insurance, photo ID, and background check consent.
7. Use Harvest CRM or another signing system to send, sign, and store documents.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Onboarding cleaners with no written expectations.
- Not collecting insurance or W-9 information.
- Skipping privacy and non-solicitation language.
- Assuming cleaners will remember everything verbally.
- Letting cleaners post or share client information.
