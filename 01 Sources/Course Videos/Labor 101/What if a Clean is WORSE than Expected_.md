# What If a Clean Is Worse Than Expected? (what to do when the home is dirtier than the client described or the job was underquoted)

## Best For
Cleaning business owners and cleaners who walk into homes that are much dirtier than expected and need to handle it professionally before the clean goes sideways.
## Problems This Solves
- Clients under-describing how dirty the home is.
- Not asking enough questions during booking.
- Starting the clean before realizing the full scope is bigger.
- Running late to the next job because the home is worse than expected.
- Not knowing when the cleaner should call the business before continuing.
## Quick Summary
This lesson explains how to handle a home that is dirtier than expected. The first protection is asking better questions before booking, especially using a 1 to 10 dirtiness scale and then asking what makes the home that number. Because one person’s 4 can be another person’s 7, the follow-up question matters more than the number itself. When the cleaner arrives, the initial walkthrough is critical and should happen before cleaning starts. If the home is significantly worse than expected, the cleaner should not just keep cleaning and hope it works out; they should identify the issue during the walkthrough and contact the business. The business can then talk to the client about the scope, the price, the time, and what can realistically be done. The major warning is not to discover the problem an hour into the clean after the client already thinks everything is included.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that worse-than-expected cleans are usually a qualification and walkthrough problem. Before booking, the business needs to ask how dirty the home is on a scale of 1 to 10 and then ask what makes it that number. The number alone is not enough because clients define dirtiness differently.

A home that is truly a 7 is not a standard clean; it needs to be priced and scoped differently. The business needs to understand clutter, pets, kids, mud, neglected areas, and any other reason the home will take longer.

Once the cleaner arrives, the walkthrough has to happen before cleaning starts. The cleaner should look at the whole job and identify whether the home matches what was booked. If there is a small extra item, the cleaner can often just do it. But if the extra work is significant or will add 15 to 20 minutes or more, the cleaner should reach out to the business.

The business can then call the client, clarify expectations, adjust scope, adjust price, or explain what can be completed with the available time. The key is to handle it before the cleaner is already an hour in and the client is expecting the whole home to be done at the original price.
## Key Principles
- Ask the dirtiness scale question before booking.
- Always ask what makes the home that number.
- A client’s 4 or 5 may not match your 4 or 5.
- Do an initial walkthrough before starting the clean.
- Do not start cleaning a clearly under-scoped job without addressing it.
- Small extras can be handled; bigger extras need communication.
- Cleaner and business need to protect the schedule and the client expectation.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. During booking, ask where the home falls on a 1 to 10 dirtiness scale.
2. Ask what makes the home that number.
3. Price and scope the clean based on the explanation, not just the number.
4. When the cleaner arrives, perform a full walkthrough before cleaning.
5. If the clean is only slightly worse, decide whether to do the small extra work.
6. If the extra work is significant, have the cleaner contact the business before starting or continuing.
7. Call the client and clarify the added scope, time, price, or exclusions.
8. Proceed only once expectations are clear.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “On a scale of 1 to 10, with 10 being the dirtiest, where do you think your home fits on that scale?”
- “What makes your home a 2?”
- “What makes it a 7?”
- “We’re not doing a standard clean for 7.”
- “This is what is included, and this is what is not included in this clean.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Taking the client’s dirtiness number at face value.
- Failing to ask what the number means.
- Skipping the initial walkthrough.
- Starting the clean before checking the whole home.
- Waiting until halfway through to mention the clean is worse than expected.
- Letting an underquoted job ruin the rest of the schedule.
