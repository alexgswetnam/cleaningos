# What to Include in Cleans, and What to Exclude (setting cleaning scope, exclusions, and expectations so clients are not surprised)

Cleaning business owners defining what their service includes and excludes, especially for move-outs, deep cleans, clutter, walls, windows, and special requests.
## Problems This Solves
- Clients expecting wall washing, window tracks, organizing, or heavy moving.
- Cleaners being blamed for things outside the service scope.
- Not setting expectations before the clean.
- Getting complaints because the client assumed more was included.
- Not knowing how to balance doing a lot with avoiding unnecessary risk.
## Quick Summary
This lesson explains that every cleaning business needs clear includes and exclusions. The speaker cannot tell every business exactly what to include, but shares exclusions used in their company: inside window tracks, wall washing, organizing, tall ladder work, exterior windows, heavy furniture moving, porches, and similar extras. These exclusions exist because they create risk, extra time, or expectation problems. The lesson emphasizes that client frustration usually comes from expectations not being met, not from the task itself. The business uses pre-clean emails and phone conversations to set expectations before the cleaner enters the home. The goal is to set an expectation you can meet and then surpass, rather than letting the client assume the clean includes everything.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that the scope of the clean needs to be clear before the job. If you do not define what is included and excluded, clients may assume wall washing, organizing, window tracks, exterior work, heavy furniture moving, or other risky tasks are part of the clean.

The lesson specifically excludes inside window tracks because they can be more complicated and create problems. Wall washing is excluded because clients may confuse paint damage, marks, or new scratches with cleaning failure. Organizing is not included because clutter can dramatically increase time. Tall ladders, outside windows, heavy furniture moving, and porch work are also excluded.

The deeper lesson is about expectations. People get upset when expectations are not met. A one-hour drive is not inherently a problem; it becomes a problem if the person expected it to take 20 minutes. The same is true with cleaning. If the client expects wall washing and you do not do wall washing, they may feel disappointed unless the expectation was set clearly beforehand.

Use the phone call, pre-clean email, policies, and in-home communication to make the scope clear. Then meet or exceed that expectation.
## Key Principles
- Scope should be clear before the clean.
- Exclusions protect the business and the cleaner.
- Wall washing creates expectation and liability problems.
- Clutter and organizing should be handled with clear policies.
- Clients get upset when expectations are not met.
- Set expectations early, then exceed them.
- Pre-clean emails help prevent surprises.
- If complaints happen, first ask whether the expectation was set correctly.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Decide what your cleaning company includes and excludes.
2. Clearly exclude risky or time-heavy items like wall washing, window tracks, exterior windows, heavy furniture moving, and organizing if that is your policy.
3. Explain clutter expectations before the clean.
4. Send a pre-clean expectation email before the appointment.
5. Set expectations on the phone while booking.
6. Have cleaners clarify scope in the home when needed.
7. After complaints, determine whether the issue was unrealistic expectations or the cleaner missing the promised outcome.
8. Update policies and notes so the same confusion does not repeat.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “We will not do wall washing.”
- “If there’s a bunch of clutter on the floor and it impedes our work, we will either go around it or we will charge extra for it.”
- “People are only upset when their expectations are not met.”
- “The problem wasn’t the hour-long drive. The problem was the expectation that was set.”
- “The first time we’re in someone’s home, we’re getting to know your home.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Letting the client assume everything is included.
- Doing wall washing and then being blamed for paint or wall marks.
- Not charging or adjusting scope when clutter adds time.
- Moving heavy furniture without clear agreement.
- Skipping the pre-clean expectation email.
- Not using complaints to improve expectation-setting.
