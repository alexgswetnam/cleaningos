# Keeping Organized Job Notes for Cleaners (using structured job notes so cleaners know preferences, exclusions, and unique details for each client)

## Problems This Solves
- Job notes becoming messy and inconsistent.
- Cleaners not knowing client preferences or exclusions.

## Quick Summary
This lesson explains the importance of keeping organized job notes for cleaners inside the scheduling system. The business uses Booking Koala, but the speaker says the same idea applies to ScheduleDrop, ZenMaid, or any system that stores job notes. The structure they use is preferred cleaner, clean exclusions, and unique notes. This keeps the information clear instead of letting every booking become a messy free-form note. Examples include flexible scheduling, strict day/time needs, using the client’s cleaning supplies, cash left on the counter, gate codes, excluding bedrooms, or only cleaning one bedroom and common areas. The goal is to make recurring cleans easier for the business, easier for the cleaners, and better for the client.
## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that job notes need structure. If every note is typed randomly, bookings become messy and cleaners miss important details. The business keeps notes under clear categories: preferred cleaner, clean exclusions, and unique notes.

Preferred cleaner tells the scheduler and team who the client likes or expects. Clean exclusions say what is not being done, such as bedrooms or specific rooms. Unique notes hold special details like gate codes, using the client’s supplies, cash on the counter, special mop preferences, flexible scheduling, strict scheduling, or specific areas of focus.

Notes should be updated as more client preferences are discovered. Not every client will have a lot of notes, and many typical cleans may have none. But when a client does have preferences, those notes protect the client experience and make the cleaner’s job easier.

The lesson applies to Booking Koala, ScheduleDrop, ZenMaid, or whatever scheduling system the business uses. The tool matters less than the discipline of keeping notes clean and current.
## Key Principles
- Job notes should be structured, not random.
- Use categories like preferred cleaner, clean exclusions, and unique notes.
- Update notes as you learn more about the client.
- Notes help cleaners serve recurring clients consistently.
- Not every client needs long notes.
- Gate codes, supplies, payment details, and room exclusions belong in the job notes.
- Organized notes make scheduling and cleaning easier.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Choose where job notes live in your scheduling system.
2. Use a repeatable structure for every recurring client.
3. Add preferred cleaner when relevant.
4. Add clean exclusions for rooms or tasks not included.
5. Add unique notes like gate codes, supplies, strict timing, flexible scheduling, cash on counter, or special preferences.
6. Update notes whenever a client gives new information.
7. Keep notes concise and easy for cleaners to read.
8. Review notes before assigning or sending cleaners to the job.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Preferred cleaner.”
- “Clean exclusions.”
- “Unique notes.”
- “This client’s flexible, we can do him any day of the week.”
- “She will leave cash on the counter.”
- “Clean only the one bedroom downstairs and the common areas.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Typing random notes with no structure.
- Not updating notes after learning a client preference.
- Forgetting gate codes or access details.
- Not recording excluded rooms or tasks.
- Making notes too messy for cleaners to use.
- Assuming recurring clients do not need documentation.
