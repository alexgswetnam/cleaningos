## Problems This Solves
- Depending on one or two cleaners for all fulfillment.
- Losing revenue when cleaners get sick.
- Having no backup for recurring clients or move-outs.
- Scrambling last minute to service booked clients.
- Not building relationships with other cleaning companies before needing help.
## Quick Summary
This lesson explains why the business needs a roster of 1099 cleaners instead of relying on one or two people. The speaker gives an example from January where two cleaners got sick in the first week and the business missed the revenue it should have done that month. Even though other cleaners could take some work, many clients specifically liked those cleaners and moved the clean to the next week. The danger of a thin roster is that one sick cleaner, unavailable cleaner, or cleaner who leaves can make the business unable to fulfill bookings. The lesson recommends backups for backups and building relationships with other cleaning companies ahead of time. Sometimes the business may make only 10 or 20 percent on a job by subcontracting, but the priority is servicing the client and not leaving them hanging.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that labor redundancy protects the business. One cleaner is not a labor system. Two cleaners are still risky. If either gets sick, becomes unavailable, or quits, the schedule can collapse.

The lesson uses a real revenue example: the business should have done around 30K in January, but two cleaners got sick the first week. Other cleaners were available, but some clients only wanted their assigned cleaner and moved the clean instead. That directly affected revenue.

A roster means having multiple cleaners and backups for backups. It also means building relationships with other cleaning companies before a crisis. If a move-out clean needs to happen and your cleaner calls out, having another company to call can save the client experience even if your margin is only 10 or 20 percent.

The business owner’s job is not just to find one good cleaner. It is to build enough coverage that the client still gets served when normal human problems happen.
## Key Principles
- One cleaner is not enough.
- Backups need backups.
- Sickness and callouts are part of the business.
- Client service matters even when margin is lower.
- Relationships with other companies should be built before emergencies.
- A roster protects revenue.
- Recurring clients may still prefer their assigned cleaner, so redundancy needs planning.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. List your current cleaner coverage.
2. Identify what happens if each cleaner gets sick or leaves.
3. Start onboarding additional 1099 cleaners before you desperately need them.
4. Build relationships with other small cleaning companies in your area.
5. Create a plan for emergency coverage and move-out callouts.
6. Be willing to make lower margin on emergency jobs to protect the client experience.
7. Keep strengthening the roster over time.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Relying on one or two cleaners.
- Waiting until a cleaner calls out to find backup.
- Not building relationships with other cleaning companies.
- Protecting margin at the expense of the client experience.
- Assuming every client will accept a different cleaner easily.
