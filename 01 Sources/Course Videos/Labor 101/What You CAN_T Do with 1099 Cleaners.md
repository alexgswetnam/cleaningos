## Quick Summary
This lesson covers the things the business says you cannot do with 1099 cleaners if you want to avoid treating them like employees. You cannot control their schedule, require specific days or hours, force them to take jobs, punish them for declining jobs, or keep them on call. You also cannot control the exact means and methods of their work like step-by-step SOPs, mandatory tools, or mandatory products. Uniforms, branded vehicles, company supplies, employee-style training, non-competes, hourly employee-style pay, and formal discipline are also called out as issues. The lesson explains that you can define the scope, outcome, price, and expectations of a job, and you can give recommendations or guidelines. The key distinction is that the contractor owns the method while the business defines the result needed.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that 1099 cleaners need to be treated like contractors, not employees. The business can define the job, the outcome, the price, and the requirements of the service being sold. But it cannot control the cleaner like staff.

You cannot require contractors to work specific hours, take assigned jobs, be on call, or be punished for declining work. You cannot dictate the exact step-by-step process, products, tools, or techniques they must use. You also cannot require employee-style appearance and branding like uniforms or branded vehicles.

You can give recommendations. You can say what you prefer, what has worked best, and what outcome the client needs. For example, you can say the shower glass must be clean, but you cannot force a specific product or exact scrubbing method.

The lesson’s distinction is simple: you can define the outcome, but you cannot micromanage the method. If you want that level of control, that is employee territory.
## Key Principles
- 1099 cleaners control their own schedule.
- You cannot force a contractor to take a job.
- You cannot require a contractor to be on call.
- You can define outcomes, not micromanage methods.
- Recommendations are different from mandatory training.
- Contractors should use their own tools and supplies.
- Employee-style discipline and branding are warning areas.
- Treat contractors like vendors.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Review how you currently communicate with cleaners.
2. Remove requirements around specific days, hours, on-call availability, or mandatory job acceptance.
3. Replace step-by-step mandatory methods with outcome-based checklists.
4. Turn supply and product preferences into recommendations unless the service itself requires a specific category.
5. Do not require uniforms, branded cars, or company-owned tools.
6. Pay by job or outcome rather than employee-style hourly pay.
7. Use vendor-style agreements, scopes, prices, and expectations.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Requiring specific hours every week.
- Punishing contractors for declining work.
- Forcing a specific product, tool, or process.
- Requiring company uniforms or branded vehicles.
- Supplying all tools and supplies like an employer.
- Using write-ups and discipline like employee management.
- Trying to ban them from working for other clients.
