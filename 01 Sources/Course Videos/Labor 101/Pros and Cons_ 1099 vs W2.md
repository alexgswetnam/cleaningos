## Quick Summary
This lesson compares 1099 subcontractors and W-2 employees from an operational perspective, with the disclaimer that the speaker is not a lawyer or CPA. A W-2 model gives more control over schedule, process, training, uniforms, standards, routing, and team culture, but it also creates more payroll, insurance, tax, admin, and employment responsibility. A 1099 model is easier to start with, easier to scale capacity, and pays by job outcome instead of hourly, but the owner has less control over methods and must be careful not to treat contractors like employees. The lesson recommends 1099 for starting and scaling, while acknowledging that a core W-2 team plus 1099 backup may make sense at a larger stage. The main warning is that many owners want contractor advantages but then operate as if cleaners are employees. That is where compliance trouble can happen.
## Core Teaching
The main teaching is that the labor model is foundational. If you choose the wrong model or operate it incorrectly, the business can get stuck operationally or run into compliance problems.

A W-2 employee means the company controls the schedule, process, training, standards, payroll taxes, and more. This gives the owner more control, but it also creates more responsibility and cost. Employees can be trained, coached, routed, required to wear uniforms, and built into a team culture. The downside is payroll, workers comp, onboarding pay, admin costs, and the reality that employees expect stable income even when the business slows down.

A 1099 subcontractor is different. The contractor is running their own business, sets their own terms, and is paid for an outcome. This model is easier to start and can scale faster because you are not building a full payroll operation from day one. You are also not paying hourly, so a cleaner cannot stretch a six-hour job into eight hours and get paid more. If a contractor is not a fit, you can stop assigning jobs.

The tradeoff is less control. You cannot train them like employees, force uniforms, control every method, or manage their schedule like staff. The owner must find good cleaners who already know what they are doing and then give outcome-based expectations.

The lesson’s recommendation is to start with 1099 because it is easier to start and scale, while being careful not to treat contractors like employees. At a larger stage, a hybrid model may make sense: a core W-2 team with 1099 capacity to fill in gaps.
## Key Principles
- 1099 cleaners are businesses, not employees.
- W-2 gives more control but creates more responsibility.
- 1099 is easier to start and easier to scale capacity.
- Contractors should be paid for outcomes, not clocked hours.
- You cannot take the advantages of 1099 while controlling people like W-2 employees.
- If a contractor is not performing, find a better cleaner instead of trying to train them into one.
- A hybrid model may make sense later, but 1099 is the recommended starting point in this lesson.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Understand what control you need over schedule, methods, uniforms, routing, and training.
2. If you want high control, recognize that you are moving toward a W-2 model.
3. If you use 1099, find experienced cleaners who already know what they are doing.
4. Pay contractors per job or outcome rather than hourly.
5. Avoid treating subcontractors like employees.
6. As the business grows, consider whether a core W-2 team plus 1099 backup makes sense.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Trying to control a subcontractor’s schedule, process, and training like an employee.
- Hiring W-2 employees before the business has stable demand.
- Thinking less control with 1099 is automatically bad.
- Using 1099 cleaners who do not already know how to clean professionally.
- Ignoring compliance because the business is small.
