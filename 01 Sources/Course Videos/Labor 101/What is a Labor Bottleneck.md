## Quick Summary
This lesson defines a labor bottleneck as having demand but unstable staffing. The symptoms include turnover, no-shows, inconsistent quality, or not being able to find cleaners at all. The lesson gives a specific method for survival season: use Google Maps to call smaller cleaning companies and subcontract their cleaners, especially companies with very few reviews instead of the big companies with hundreds of reviews. These smaller companies are usually hungrier and may want extra work. The solution changes by season: survival season is about finding one or two reliable helpers, stability season is about onboarding packets and checklists, scale season is about backup coverage and team leads, and sustain/sell season is about onboarding new cleaners monthly and testing quality. The big point is that labor has to be built like an asset, not handled reactively.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that a labor bottleneck happens when demand exists but staffing cannot support it. You may have leads, booked clients, or recurring work, but if cleaners are unreliable, unavailable, or inconsistent, growth stops.

In survival season, the fastest solution may be very practical: call other small cleaning companies on Google Maps and ask about subcontracting their cleaners. The lesson says not to call the biggest companies with hundreds of reviews. Instead, go deeper into Google results or zoom into small neighborhoods and call companies with fewer than five reviews. They are often hungry and open to extra work.

The solution depends on the business season. Survival season is about getting one or two reliable helpers or continuing to clean yourself until cash is steady. Stability season is about onboarding checklists, onboarding packets, and clear expectations. Scale season requires backup coverage and team leads. Sustain/sell season means reducing risk by onboarding new cleaners monthly and putting them through a quality testing phase.

The larger point is that labor cannot be a last-minute scramble. The owner needs redundancy, standards, and quality testing before one sick cleaner or one resignation collapses the schedule.
## Key Principles
- Labor bottleneck means demand exists but staffing is unstable.
- Turnover, no-shows, and inconsistent quality are labor problems.
- Small cleaning companies can become subcontractor partners.
- Do not rely only on one or two cleaners long term.
- Labor solutions change by business season.
- Standards and onboarding create reliability.
- Redundancy protects growth.
- Sustain/sell requires building an asset, not a hustle.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Identify whether the real constraint is leads or labor.
2. If in survival season, look for one or two reliable helpers.
3. Use Google Maps to find small cleaning companies with very few reviews.
4. Call them and ask about subcontracting or extra work.
5. In stability season, create onboarding checklists and packets.
6. In scale season, build backup coverage and team lead structure.
7. In sustain/sell season, onboard new cleaners monthly and test quality.
8. Keep building labor capacity before demand overwhelms the business.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Assuming leads are the issue when the real issue is cleaner capacity.
- Only looking for cleaners through normal job posts.
- Calling only the largest cleaning companies for help.
- Waiting until a cleaner quits to build backup coverage.
- Trying to scale without redundancy.
