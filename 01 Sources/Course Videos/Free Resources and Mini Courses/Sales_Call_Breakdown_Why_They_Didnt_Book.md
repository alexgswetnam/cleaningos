## Quick Summary

This lesson breaks down a real sales call that did not convert. The main issues identified were lack of energy, weak connection-building, unclear call direction, overly defensive questions, and pricing that did not match the customer's actual needs. The teaching emphasizes making a friend first, guiding the conversation confidently, listening carefully, and customizing pricing based on the scope of work. The lesson also covers objection handling and the importance of practicing sales conversations.

## Core Teaching

Sales is about connection before pitching.

Build rapport:

* Use energy and personality.
* Look for opportunities to connect outside of cleaning.
* Follow interesting details the prospect mentions.

Guide the conversation:

* Have a clear structure.
* Avoid random questions.
* Explain why you are asking questions.

Understand the customer's situation:

* Ask what makes the home a certain level of dirty.
* Clarify what rooms actually need service.
* Repeat information back so the customer feels heard.

Handle objections properly:

* Avoid “why” questions.
* Ask softer questions.
* Stay in the conversation when resistance appears.

Price accurately:

* Price based on actual workload.
* Consider unused rooms and limited-use areas.
* Monthly service should be cheaper than an initial deep clean.

## Key Principles

* Make a friend, help a friend.
* Connection matters throughout the call.
* Confidence and fluidity improve with repetition.
* Customers want to feel heard.
* Pricing should reflect actual scope.
* Do not put prospects on the defensive.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. Start with energy.
2. Build rapport before pitching.
3. Ask qualifying questions.
4. Clarify cleaning priorities.
5. Understand actual scope.
6. Present pricing.
7. Handle objections calmly.
8. Continue the conversation instead of ending at first resistance.

## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep

* “Make a friend, help a friend.”
* “On a scale of one to ten, with ten being the dirtiest, where would you say your home is right now?”
* “What makes it a seven?”
* “I do have a couple boring questions I've got to ask you real quick.”
* “What is your main concern here?”

## Common Mistakes / Warnings

* Sounding robotic.
* Asking accusatory questions.
* Using too many “why” questions.
* Ignoring opportunities for connection.
* Pricing solely from square footage.
* Failing to acknowledge customer comments.
