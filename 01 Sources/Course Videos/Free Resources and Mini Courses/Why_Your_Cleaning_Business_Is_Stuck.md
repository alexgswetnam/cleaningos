## Quick Summary

Most cleaning businesses do not have one bottleneck—they have several that show up at different times. The key is identifying the current bottleneck and solving it based on the season of the business. The lesson breaks bottlenecks into Leads, Labor, Logistics, and Leadership. Each bottleneck requires a different solution depending on whether the business is in Survival, Stability, Scale, or Sustain/Sell. Growth comes from solving the right constraint rather than trying to perfect everything. The same issue may require a different solution as the company matures.

## Core Teaching

The biggest problem changes as the business evolves. Focus on the bottleneck that is currently limiting growth.

### Leads

* Survival: Pick one marketing channel and a simple sales script.
* Stability: Track cost per booking and improve conversions.
* Scale: Add a second channel and use capacity-based marketing.
* Sustain/Sell: Delegate marketing and protect lead quality.

### Labor

* Survival: Get one reliable cleaner/helper.
* Stability: Use onboarding checklists and onboarding resources.
* Scale: Build backup coverage and team leads.
* Sustain/Sell: Continuously recruit and quality-test cleaners.

### Logistics

* Survival: Use essential checklists and scheduling rules.
* Stability: CRM, automated follow-ups, and one source of truth.
* Scale: Build operating processes and weekly operations cadence.
* Sustain/Sell: Formal documentation, audits, and SOP library.

### Leadership

* Survival: Set and enforce standards.
* Stability: Accountability through scorecards and coaching.
* Scale: Team leads and recurring meetings.
* Sustain/Sell: Install leaders and audit the machine.

## Key Principles

* Most businesses have multiple bottlenecks.
* The biggest bottleneck changes by season.
* You do not need perfection to grow.
* Solve the constraint that matters right now.
* Systems and leadership become increasingly important over time.

## Step-by-Step Process

1. Identify your current bottleneck.
2. Determine your business season.
3. Apply the appropriate solution for that season.
4. Reassess as the business grows.
5. Solve the next bottleneck.

## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep

## Common Mistakes / Warnings

* Assuming there is only one problem.
* Solving a scale-stage problem while still in survival.
* Treating every season the same.
* Ignoring leadership bottlenecks.
