Quick Summary
Objections are usually uncertainty, not hostility. Build an Objection Library in 10 minutes: list your top objections and script one clean, repeatable answer for each. Use the simple flow: Acknowledge (“That makes sense”), Clarify (“Is price the main thing?”), Solve (“If price felt right, would you book now?”). If they commit, make a smart concession—small incentive for a review, trim scope, or a recurring discount with a two-clean minimum—then confirm time and take the card. Keep it calm and direct. Memorize basics; you don’t need to be a “sales wizard.”

Core Teaching
Preparation beats improvisation. Script the common objections once and deliver them consistently with acknowledge–clarify–solve. Use fair concessions that protect margins and tie to action.

Key Principles
Objections = uncertainty; don’t get defensive.
Acknowledge → Clarify → Solve.
Concessions should be small, specific, and conditional (“Sound fair enough?”).

Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
Acknowledge: “Yes, that makes sense — I get that.”
Clarify: “Is it just the price that’s keeping you from booking?”
Solve: “So if you were happy with the price, you would book with us right now?”
Concessions:
“We can do a one-time $15–$30 courtesy if you’re thrilled and leave a 5-star review.”
“If we remove [area], we can meet your budget.”
“Recurring bookings come with a discount after at least two cleans.”
Close line: “Sound fair enough?” then confirm time and take the card.
Common Mistakes / Warnings
Rambling or rapid discounting that trains customers to haggle harder.
Offering concessions with no condition or commitment.
Taking “think about it” at face value without clarifying the real blocker.
