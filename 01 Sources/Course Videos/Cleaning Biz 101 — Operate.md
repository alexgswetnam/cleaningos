# Cleaning Biz 101 — Operate

## 1. Operate: Scale the Business Without Chaos

This part of the business is the internal operating system. Acquire gets leads, Convert
gets bookings, Fulfill delivers the service, Operate is what makes the business stable.

If Operate is weak: everything depends on the owner, cleaners need help for every
question, mistakes repeat, quality is inconsistent, and the owner is always putting out
fires. The goal: the business runs the same way every time, even when nobody's watching.
Operate is built on communication, SOPs, quality control, and reporting rhythms.

Proof point: after nose surgery in mid-December, the business wasn't checked for almost
three weeks — confidence that the VA and cleaners would follow the systems already in
place, and would ask if they genuinely needed help.

## 2. Systems: Motivation or Memory

Most businesses rely on motivation or memory — "I hope cleaners remember," "I hope the VA
knows," "I hope they do it right." Systems exist so the business doesn't have to rely on a
human that heavily, eliminating error and forgetfulness: documented standards, clear
communication channels, a repeatable workflow for problems, checklists with verifiable
completion proof.

Operate isn't about more tools — it's about eliminating unnecessary fluff so work is as
easy and simple as possible. Test: if something is done over and over, templatize or
systemize it. Example: a repeated explanation of standard-clean vs. deep-clean scope
became a saved template in Harvest CRM instead of being retyped. Small fixes like this
compound when handling ten bookings in a day.

## 3. Why Should You Use SOPs

Most owners avoid SOPs thinking it means writing a novel. It doesn't — a "minimum viable
SOP library" is just the handful of documents that prevent 80% of problems. If only a few
could be kept: new client intake + expectations, cleaner hiring, before/after photos + QA,
what's included vs. not (scope), reschedule/cancellation policy, complaint/redo/refund
process, payment failure process, and access issues (lockbox, pets, alarms).

They're how you stop solving the same problem 50 times and scale without the stress. The
hiring SOP specifically was turned into a documented process once it had been done enough
times, so the VA could handle it instead of the owner.

## 4. Internal Communication: Discord

Discord is used for internal communication and daily monitoring: one place for cleaner
communication, one place for daily reporting, one place for issues and escalations.
Scattering communication across texts, calls, and random DMs loses visibility,
accountability, and consistency. Discord gives a single "ops room."

Zapier connects BookingKoala and Harvest CRM into this space so daily leads, clean
outcomes, and new bookings are visible without checking multiple tools manually. A
written SOP exists for onboarding cleaners onto Discord and is given to every new hire.

## 5. How to Get Cleaners on Discord (SOP)

The switch is from Google Voice, which didn't reliably send notifications, causing missed
messages. Discord notifies instantly across devices and organizes conversations into
topic channels (job updates, supplies, scheduling). Google Voice stays in use for phone
calls only, since it's faster for that specific purpose.

Setup steps: install the app → register/log in with a work email or phone, using an
identifying username (e.g. "Maria C. – Cleaner") → join the team server via invite link →
turn on notifications for all messages, especially Announcements and Daily Jobs, at both
the app and phone OS level → learn the basics (send messages in a channel, send photos
via the "+" button, tag someone with @Name, reply by holding a message) → keep Google
Voice installed for calls only. Daily habit: check Discord before and after jobs; nothing
is lost since messages stay scrollable in the channel history.
