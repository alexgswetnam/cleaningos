## Core Teaching
Systems matter because you cannot scale efficiently or get consistent results without them.

A system is not only a CRM. A system also includes SOPs, which are standard operating procedures. That means there is a normal, standard, repeatable way that the business does something every single time.

For example, if every sales call is handled differently, then you cannot really determine what is working and what is not working. One call might use one approach, another call might use different objection handling, and another call might go in a totally different direction. Because nothing is consistent, there is nothing useful to compare.

But if you use the same sales call SOP and the same objection handles over and over, then after doing it 50 times, you can look at the results and ask, "What is working here? What is not working?" Then you can test a different process against the first process and see which one produces better results.

That is the first reason systems matter: they create repeatable processes that can be measured and improved.

The second reason systems matter is scale. If every important task relies on you manually doing it, the business is personally attached to you. If you take a vacation, take a break, or step back, the business suffers because you are no longer there doing the work.

But if the important work is handled by systems that are separate from you, then you can step back without harming the business. The business can keep operating because the success is based on whether the system works, not whether you personally are doing everything.

A VA can then oversee those systems and make sure the right things are getting done. That is how systems help the business grow without the owner being personally connected to every result.

## Cleaned Lesson Notes
Systems matter because you are not able to scale efficiently or get consistent results if you are not using a system.

And when I say "system," I do not just mean a CRM. I am talking about systems in the context of SOPs too. SOPs are standard operating procedures, which means you have a normal, standard way of doing things that is repeatable and that you follow every single time.

For example, look at sales calls. If we do something different on every single sales call, then we cannot determine what is working and what is not working. Every call is different. Every approach is different. Every objection handle is different. So there is no clean way to compare results.

But if we use the same sales call SOP, treat each sales call the same way, and use the same objection handles, then after doing that 50 times we can look back and ask, "What is working here? What is not working here?"

Then we can test another way of doing it and compare. Does this process work better than the first one? Do we get better results doing it this way or that way?

You can only split test and compare one process against another if both processes are consistent. This has to be the same every single time, and that has to be the same every single time. Otherwise, you are not really comparing anything clearly.

That is one reason systems are important: they create repeatable processes so you can determine whether one process is better than another.

The other reason systems matter is scale. If everything in the business relies on you manually doing things, then the business is attached to you personally. If you need to take a vacation or take a break, the business crumbles because you are not there doing all the things that need to be done.

But if the things that need to be done are handled by a system that is separate from you, then you can step back without harming the business. The business can continue running because the work is being done through the system.

Then the question becomes: who oversees those systems and makes sure they are working correctly? That is where having a VA comes in. A VA can operate the systems and do the things that need to be done in the business.

So that is why systems matter.

One, they allow you to compare one process against another and see what works better.

Two, they allow you to scale and grow without being personally connected to every single result, because success is no longer based only on you. It is based on whether the system is successful.
