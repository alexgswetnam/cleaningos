## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that a CRM is the system for managing client relationships, leads, follow-up, and communication. It is not just a contact list. In a cleaning business, the CRM should show who reached out, who was quoted, who needs follow-up, who booked once, and who should be nurtured long term.

Harvest CRM is the system the speaker built and uses in their own business. It is based on GoHighLevel, but the distinction is that GoHighLevel comes blank. The speaker compares it to being handed car parts and told to build the car. Harvest CRM is the cleaning-specific car already built for the way they run residential cleaning.

The CRM handles texting, calling, lead pipeline, follow-up, review/reputation management, documents, AI response help, and integrations with scheduling software like Booking Koala or ScheduleDrop. It should help the business make more money, reduce chaos, and allow VAs to operate inside one system.

The lesson also makes the point that tools should do their main job well. Scheduling software is for scheduling and job notes. CRM is for sales pipeline, client communication, and follow-up.
## Key Principles
- CRM stands for Client Relationship Management.
- A CRM should manage leads, follow-up, and client communication.
- GoHighLevel is powerful but blank without setup.
- Harvest CRM is built specifically around residential cleaning operations.
- A CRM should make more money than it costs.
- Scheduling software and CRM software serve different roles.
- A central CRM reduces chaos and supports VA operation.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Understand that CRM means Client Relationship Management.
2. Use it to track leads, quotes, bookings, follow-ups, and long-term nurture.
3. Keep communication in one place through text, phone, and email.
4. Use CRM automations for old leads and one-time clients.
5. Connect the CRM with scheduling software such as Booking Koala or ScheduleDrop.
6. Use scheduling software for calendar, cleaners, and job notes, and CRM for pipeline and communication.
7. Evaluate the CRM by whether it creates more money and less chaos.
