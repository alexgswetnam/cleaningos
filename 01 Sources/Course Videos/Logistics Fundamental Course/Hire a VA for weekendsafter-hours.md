## Quick Summary
This lesson says weekend and after-hours VA coverage can absolutely be worth it if the numbers make sense. The speaker explains that even one additional booked clean per week can justify the cost. They use an example of a VA working 6 PM to 9 PM at $5 per hour, costing $75 Monday through Friday, compared against average booking revenue. They also show that weekend calls can turn into real bookings, especially when clients are urgent and want to get it done. The speaker's weekend model is not necessarily paying someone to sit at a computer all day; they pay a smaller daily amount for the VA to be available to pick up calls, respond to booking texts, and handle urgent client or cleaning changes. The key is to run the numbers instead of guessing.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that after-hours and weekend coverage should be judged by cost versus return. If having a VA answer after work or on weekends produces even one extra booked job per week, the math can easily make sense.

The speaker gives a simple example: a VA working a few evening hours at a low hourly rate might cost around $75 during the week. One booked clean can bring in significantly more revenue than that, and if that client becomes recurring, the value is even higher.

The same applies on weekends. People call when they are ready, and if nobody answers, especially on urgent bookings, they may call the next company. The best clients are often the ones who want to book, pay full price, and get it done.

The weekend setup does not have to mean the VA is actively working every second. The speaker pays a small daily amount for the VA to be available to pick up calls, respond to texts, and handle urgent client or cleaner issues. The decision should come from real numbers, not from a gut assumption that after-hours coverage is too expensive.
## Key Principles
- After-hours coverage should be evaluated by ROI.
- One extra booking can justify the cost.
- Urgent callers often book with whoever responds.
- Weekend coverage does not have to mean full active work all day.
- The VA's job after-hours can be limited to calls, booking texts, and urgent issues.
- Run the numbers before deciding.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Calculate what after-hours or weekend VA coverage would cost.
2. Estimate how many extra bookings are needed to cover that cost.
3. Look at average booking revenue and labor cost.
4. Compare one extra booked job to the VA expense.
5. Define the VA's after-hours role: answer calls, respond to booking texts, and handle urgent issues.
6. Track whether calls and weekend leads turn into bookings.
7. Keep coverage if the return is higher than the cost.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- "I consider the pros and the cons."
- "What does it cost and what is my return?"
- "We absolutely made more money by having our VA work longer hours."
- "Otherwise they would have called someone else, especially if it was urgent."
- "You're just here to pick up calls when people dial in."
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Assuming after-hours coverage is not worth it without running numbers.
- Letting weekend and evening calls go unanswered.
- Paying for unnecessary active work instead of defining availability-based coverage.
- Ignoring recurring value from a client booked after-hours.
- Not tracking which calls came in after hours.
