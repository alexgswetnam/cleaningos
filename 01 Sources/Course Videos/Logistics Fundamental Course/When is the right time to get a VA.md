## Quick Summary
The right time to get a VA depends on the owner, the business, and the return on cost. The speaker explains that they hired a VA earlier than they absolutely needed because they wanted time to train before the business got slammed. They brought in their first VA around $9k/month while preparing to scale to $15k, $20k, $25k, and $30k. That timing helped because the first VA did not work out, and they still had time to replace and train another one before the business grew. The lesson frames the decision as a cost-benefit question: will the VA make more money, free up time, prevent missed leads, or help the owner step back? A VA can start with calls, follow-ups, weekend coverage, tracking, analytics, customer support, or post-sale work instead of immediately running everything. The warning is to bring a VA into clear work they can succeed at, not undefined chaos.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that there is no one universal revenue number where every business should get a VA. The right time depends on whether the VA's return is greater than the cost and whether the business is about to need more capacity.

In their own business, they hired earlier than strictly necessary because they wanted to prepare before things got slammed. They were around $9k/month, knew they were trying to scale quickly, and wanted time to train a VA. That mattered because the first VA did not work out, and they still had room to find and train the next one.

A VA does not have to run the entire business on day one. The first role could be sales calls, follow-up calls, weekend coverage, tracking, analytics, customer support, or post-sale operations. The job should match where the business is actually losing money or time.

The decision is a cost-benefit analysis: if the VA makes more money than they cost, frees up the owner's time, or prevents dropped leads, it can make sense. But if there are no leads, no work to delegate, and no systems, it may be too early.
## Key Principles
- The right time depends on the business and owner.
- Hire before chaos if you know growth is coming.
- Training a VA while slammed is harder.
- The VA should produce a return greater than the cost.
- Start with a focused VA role if needed.
- Give yourself time to replace a VA if the first one does not work.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Look at where you are losing time or money.
2. Decide whether a VA could make more than they cost or free up meaningful time.
3. If growth is coming, hire early enough to train before you are slammed.
4. Start the VA on a clear role such as calls, follow-up, tracking, analytics, weekend coverage, or customer support.
5. Track performance and decide whether the VA is working.
6. If the VA is not the right fit, replace them before the business suffers.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Waiting until the business is already overwhelmed.
- Hiring without knowing what the VA should actually do.
- Ignoring whether the VA will create financial or time return.
- Expecting the first VA to automatically work out.
- Letting emotion guide the decision instead of business performance.
