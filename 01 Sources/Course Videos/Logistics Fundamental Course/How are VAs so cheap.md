## Quick Summary
This lesson explains that VAs are cheaper primarily because of cost-of-living differences, not because their work is automatically low value. The speaker mentions countries like Bangladesh, Pakistan, Colombia, Nicaragua, Brazil, and the Philippines. In these countries, rent, food, and everyday costs can be much lower than in the United States, so a wage that seems low in the US can be very strong locally. The speaker says some VAs have master's degrees, years of experience, and skills that would cost $20 to $40 per hour in the States. The lesson also warns against the belief that third-world country equals lower skill. The point is that the global labor market lets a cleaning business get skilled support at a cost that makes sense for both sides.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that VA pricing is mostly about cost of living and labor market differences. A VA in the Philippines, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Colombia, Nicaragua, Brazil, or other countries may charge far less than a US worker because their rent, food, and expenses are much lower.

The speaker pushes back hard on the idea that low cost means low skill. Some VAs have degrees, years of experience, and real ability in business, marketing, admin, software, and operations. Paying $7.50 or $10 per hour can be very strong pay in their local market.

This is why VAs can be both affordable and skilled. The owner should not judge value only by the hourly number. They should judge by whether the person can do the job well and produce results.
## Key Principles
- VAs are cheaper because of cost-of-living differences.
- Low cost does not mean low skill.
- Some VAs have strong education and experience.
- $3 to $10 per hour can be meaningful pay in many countries.
- Judge the VA by performance, not geography.
- The VA market is competitive because international work can be highly valuable locally.
