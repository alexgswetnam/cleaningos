## Quick Summary
This lesson teaches that SOPs should not be so long that they stop being useful. A long, over-explained document may look thorough but often will not be followed day to day. The speaker gives a bad example of an SOP that explains every tiny step of logging into Booking Koala and clicking around, even when that detail is not needed. The test is whether someone can use the SOP correctly with the information included. If they cannot, add the missing piece; if the information is fluff, cut it. Some SOPs should be checklists used every day, while others are only needed until the process is memorized or when a special nuance comes up. The goal is maximum usefulness with minimum fluff.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that an SOP has to be useful, not just detailed. If it is too long, the VA or team member will not actually use it in daily work. The SOP becomes a giant document instead of a tool.

The speaker's standard is simple: can someone else read it and do the task correctly? If not, add the missing instruction. If yes, remove the fluff. The goal is as useful as possible and as short as possible.

The lesson gives a bad SOP example: explaining every obvious step of going to Booking Koala, entering the URL, checking the internet, clicking calendar, and so on. That level of detail can make the SOP harder to use unless the step is truly necessary.

Some SOPs should be followed every time, like daily checklists. Others can be learned and then referenced only when needed. Important warnings or nuances should stay in the SOP, especially if missing one small thing could break the process.
## Key Principles
- Long SOPs often do not get used.
- SOPs should be useful, not bloated.
- Include enough detail for someone to do the task correctly.
- Cut words that do not help the person complete the task.
- Daily checklists may be followed every day.
- Some process SOPs are used until memorized, then referenced when needed.
- Important nuances and warnings should stay visible.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Write the SOP based on the actual task.
2. Give it to someone else or ChatGPT to test whether the task can be done correctly.
3. Add missing instructions only where needed.
4. Remove fluff and over-explaining.
5. Use checklists for repeated daily work.
6. Use short process guides for tasks that can be memorized.
7. Keep important warnings or one-step nuances in the SOP.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- "You do not want them to be super long to the point of them not being useful."
- "Can they do it with everything that's here?"
- "How can I make this as useful as I can while cutting as much fluff as I can?"
- "The shorter the SOP and without the fluff, the more likely it is to actually be used by your VA."
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Writing 50 pages for a simple process.
- Overexplaining obvious login or navigation steps.
- Making the SOP too intimidating for the VA to use.
- Cutting necessary details just to make it short.
- Not distinguishing between daily checklists and reference SOPs.
