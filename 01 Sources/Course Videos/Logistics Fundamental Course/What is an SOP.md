## Quick Summary
An SOP is a standard operating procedure, meaning the standard way the business handles a task or situation. The lesson explains that SOPs can be simple checklists, like an end-of-day checklist, or detailed guides for specific situations. The speaker emphasizes that their SOPs are not theoretical or generated from scratch; they came from what they actually did day to day in the cleaning business. SOPs allow a VA to follow the same process the owner used, and they also reveal when the process is unclear. The lesson introduces the idea of a feedback loop: when the VA finds an unclear part, the SOP should be improved. SOPs are not only for maintenance; they help the business run smoothly and continue growing.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that an SOP is the standard way the business operates. It can be a checklist, a detailed guide, or a process for handling a specific situation. The point is not to create a fancy document; the point is to create a repeatable way of doing the work.

The speaker stresses that SOPs should be based on what is actually done in the business. Their SOPs were built from real day-to-day operations, not from ideal theory. When they hired a VA, the VA could use those processes to operate the business.

SOPs also need a feedback loop. If a VA runs into a situation and the SOP does not answer the question clearly, that is not just a VA problem. It means the SOP needs to be improved. The owner explains the decision, the reasoning, and then the SOP gets updated so the next situation is easier.

SOPs help maintain consistency, but they also support growth because the business can keep improving without the owner being the only person who knows what to do.
## Key Principles
- SOP means standard operating procedure.
- An SOP is the standard way the business handles something.
- SOPs can be simple checklists or detailed situation guides.
- Good SOPs come from real operations, not theory.
- A VA should be able to follow the SOP and run the process.
- Every unclear SOP needs a feedback loop and improvement.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Notice a repeated task or decision in the business.
2. Write down the normal way you actually handle it.
3. Turn that into a checklist or process guide.
4. Give it to the VA or team member who will use it.
5. When they find unclear parts, explain the decision and why.
6. Update the SOP so the next person can handle it more clearly.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Creating SOPs that sound good but do not match real operations.
- Expecting a VA to know what to do without a process.
- Leaving SOPs vague after they cause confusion.
- Treating SOPs as one-time documents instead of living processes.
- Using SOPs only to maintain the business and not to support growth.
