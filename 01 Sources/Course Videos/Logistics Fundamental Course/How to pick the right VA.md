## Quick Summary
This lesson teaches that the right VA is picked through practical testing, not just resume review. The speaker says people can look great on paper but not pan out, and the only way to know if they can do the job is through a test run. Their process includes a one- or two-week trial and practical roleplays, such as pretending to be a customer calling for cleaning and giving objections. They also test system ability by having the VA show how they would make or adjust a booking in Booking Koala and learn pipeline tools. The lesson includes a warning example of a VA who sent an unprofessional text after a call disconnected, which violated company values and led to immediate termination. The point is to hire for demonstrated ability, speed to lead, close rate, follow-up, tracking, and communication—not just degrees or past experience.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is that a VA should be chosen by what they can actually do, not how good they look on paper. Resumes, degrees, and past experience matter less than whether the VA can perform the specific work the cleaning business needs.

The speaker recommends a test run. Tell the VA the business will try one or two weeks to see whether the fit makes sense for both sides. During that time, watch performance, communication, honesty, and alignment with company values.

The interview should include practical tests. Roleplay a sales call immediately. Give objections. See if the VA keeps control, asks questions, and pushes toward a booking. Also test systems: show Booking Koala and ask how they would create or adjust a booking. If they do not know a tool but can learn quickly from a video, that is different from not being capable.

The speaker's bigger hiring philosophy is that results matter more than credentials. They would rather have someone with no fancy resume who can produce results than someone with impressive experience who cannot close, follow up, track, or communicate well.
## Key Principles
- Do not hire only from a resume.
- Use a one- or two-week test run.
- Test the exact work the VA will do.
- Roleplay sales calls and objections.
- Check system ability with Booking Koala, CRM, or pipeline tasks.
- Company values matter; unprofessional client communication is a hard no.
- Performance beats credentials.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Review the VA enough to decide if they are worth testing.
2. Set expectations for a one- or two-week test period.
3. Run a live sales roleplay during the interview.
4. Give easy and hard objections to see how they respond.
5. Test practical software tasks like creating or adjusting a booking.
6. Check whether they can learn tools from process videos.
7. Watch real performance during the test period.
8. Let them go quickly if they violate company values or repeatedly drop the ball.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep

- "Can you do the job? Can you do the job well?"
- "We're gonna do a week or two weeks, see if you fit."
- "Ring, ring… hey, I'm looking for a cleaning."
- "I don't care if this is your first job, I don't care if this is your 50th job."
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Hiring the best-looking resume instead of the best practical performer.
- Skipping roleplay.
- Not testing Booking Koala or CRM ability.
- Letting a bad-value communication incident slide.
- Assuming experience automatically means good close rate or follow-up.
- Promising long-term work before the test period proves fit.
