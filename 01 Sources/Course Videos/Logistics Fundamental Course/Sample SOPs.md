## Quick Summary
This file gives two sample SOPs. The first is an end-of-day checklist covering assigned upcoming cleans, collected payments, first-time-clean follow-up calls, lead calls and texts, reviews and GBP posts, tracking sheet updates, Discord updates, and Friday cleaning-note checks. The second is a re-clean SOP that defines the re-clean guarantee window, eligibility, limitations, and a process for triage, collecting photos, scheduling the re-clean, checking cleaner availability, creating replacement bookings, and confirming with the client. The lesson shows that SOPs do not need to be abstract; they can be direct checklists and decision processes. It also makes clear that re-cleans apply when checklist standards were not upheld, but may be limited by misrepresentation, declined add-ons, pre-existing issues, clutter, access problems, or unavailable utilities. This resource is best used as an example to adapt, not as a generic theory lesson.
## Core Teaching
The core teaching is shown through examples rather than a normal lesson. The EOD checklist shows the daily operational rhythm: assign upcoming cleans, confirm payments, call first-time clients, contact leads, manage reviews and GBP, update tracking, and send status updates through Discord.

The re-clean SOP shows how to handle service issues consistently. It defines a 48-hour re-clean window, explains when the guarantee applies, and lists limitations such as declined add-ons, pre-existing damage, misrepresented property condition, limited access, contractors, pets, clutter, or utilities not working.

The re-clean process starts by triaging whether the issue is minor or larger. If minor, apologize, add a note, and handle it next time or offer a small discount if needed. If bigger, collect photos and details, send the context to the cleaner, offer re-clean windows, confirm access and pets, and create a replacement re-clean booking if the original cleaner cannot return.

The lesson shows what practical SOPs look like: not long theory, but checklists, decision rules, and specific next actions.
## Key Principles
- SOPs can be checklists or decision processes.
- Daily operations should be checked before clock-out.
- Re-clean eligibility should be defined clearly.
- Photos and details help resolve issues quickly.
- A re-clean process should protect the client and the business.
- Refund authority should have limits.
- Replacement cleaner pay and original cleaner pay should be handled intentionally.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Use the EOD checklist before clocking out each day.
2. Confirm upcoming cleans are assigned and payments are collected.
3. Call first-time cleans and follow up with leads.
4. Update tracking and send Discord status updates.
5. For re-cleans, first triage whether the issue is minor or significant.
6. Collect photos and details when needed.
7. Offer 2–3 re-clean windows within the guarantee window.
8. Check if the original cleaner can return.
9. If not, create a replacement booking and adjust pay as outlined.
10. Confirm the solution with the client.

## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Not checking assignments and payments daily.
- Letting leads go without two call/text touches.
- Not updating tracking sheets.
- Offering refunds or re-cleans without a process.
- Demanding photos in a way that feels hostile.
- Not defining when the guarantee may be limited.

Example 2: How to Handle a Re-Clean SOP

Re-Clean Guarantee Policy

Re-Clean Window: 48 hours from the original service completion (unless the client specifies otherwise)

Eligibility

A re-clean is eligible when our cleaning checklist standards were not upheld (i.e., missed areas or incomplete work based on the agreed scope).

Limitations (Guarantee May Not Apply)

A re-clean and/or refund may be limited or denied if any of the following are true:

The client declined recommended add-ons / extra service fees needed to address the issue (ex: heavy buildup, inside appliances, deep detail work)

The issue involves permanent stains, damage, or pre-existing conditions

The property was misrepresented during booking (size, condition, access, level of mess, etc.)

It was explicitly stated on the phone that, due to the nature of the work, a service guarantee would not be possible

Our ability to clean was hindered by contractors, pets, excessive clutter, limited access, or utilities not working (water/electricity)

Process

1) Triage: How big is the issue?

Ask yourself: Are the issues truly minor?

Example: one trash bag left in a bathroom, a small missed wipe-down, a single spot.

If very minor:

Thank them for letting us know, confirm we'll handle it on the next clean, and tel them you will add a note to the next booking in BK so it's not missed again. Apologize for the inconvenience! Just saying sorry helps a lot!

If they're still unhappy, offer a discount using the Pricing Guide .

If it will take more than a few minutes:

Move to Step 2.

2) Collect context (photos + details)

Politely request photos so we can resolve it quickly:

Ask for photos of the missed areas (and which rooms they're in)

Do not demand photos - keep it low-friction and client-friendly

Send the photos/details to the cleaner and briefly explain what was missed

3) Offer and schedule the re-clean

Tell the client we can send someone to re-clean the missed areas and propose times:

Recommend 2–3 time windows within the next 48 hours

Confirm access method (home, lockbox, code, etc.)

Confirm whether pets need to be secured, and any special instructions

If the client insists on a refund instead of a re-clean:

Use discretion to approve refunds up to $150

Any refund over $150 requires owner approval

If the client can't/won't schedule within 5 days:

Recommend a partial refund instead (after 5 days the home will naturally start accumulating dust/use again)

4) Check the original cleaner's availability

Ask the cleaner if they can return for the re-clean:

If yes:

Create a new booking in BK with $0 price, estimate the needed time, and add clear notes on exactly what must be addressed.

If no / unwilling:

Inform them their pay will be reduced by the amount paid to the replacement cleaner. Proceed to Step 5.

5) Create a replacement re-clean booking (if needed)

Create a new BK booking and assign a new cleaner:

Pay enough to make it worth their time to go back out

Minimum pay to new cleaner: $30

Reduce the original cleaner's pay in BK to reflect the replacement cleaner cost

6) Confirm with the client (final confirmation)

Send a simple confirmation message:

Date/time of arrival window

Who is coming (if you share cleaner name)

Access instructions

What will be re-cleaned (missed areas only, unless otherwise approved)

7) Log everything (for accountability + pattern tracking)

Log the issue in the TFC Tracking Sheet
