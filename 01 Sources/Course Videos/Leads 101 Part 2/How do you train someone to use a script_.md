## Core Teaching
A script does not work just because someone has it in front of them. You have to practice it until it is comfortable. That means repetition, roleplay, recordings, and trying the same parts over and over until the words feel natural.

Pay attention to the places where the script gets mumbly jumbly. If a sentence has too many words or does not fit the person’s mouth, change it. The goal is not to sound like you are reading. The goal is to know what you are trying to ask and have a word track that comes out clean.

Also train tonality, pace, confidence, and how much the person talks. Speaking too fast hurts the call. Talking too much hurts the call. Weak tonality hurts the call. Confidence matters most. A trained person should know the outline of the script and the main word tracks without needing to stare at the script.
## Key Principles
- Repetition builds comfort.
- Roleplay is necessary for script training.
- Word tracks should sound natural when spoken.
- Tonality and pace matter.
- Do not talk too fast or too much.
- Confidence is the most important part.
- The script should become an outline the person understands, not a page they read robotically.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Practice the script repeatedly out loud.
2. Roleplay with a friend, team member, or someone in the group.
3. Record attempts and listen back.
4. Find any lines that feel mumbly jumbly or unnatural.
5. Simplify or rewrite those lines so they are easier to say.
6. Work on tonality, pace, and confidence.
7. Keep practicing until the person knows the outline and key word tracks without looking.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Practice it, practice it till you’re comfortable.”
- “On a scale of one to ten, where would you say your home is?”
- “What makes you say that?”
- “You don’t want to stumble over your words.”
- “Confidence honestly is the most important.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not hand someone a script and expect them to perform well immediately.
- Do not keep awkward, overly wordy lines just because they are written down.
- Do not speak too fast.
- Do not talk too much.
- Do not ignore tonality and confidence.
