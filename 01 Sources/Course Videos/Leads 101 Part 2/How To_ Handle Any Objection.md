## Quick Summary
This lesson gives a simple approach for handling objections: agree first, then ask deeper questions. When someone objects, start by validating that what they said makes sense. Then ask what they mean specifically so you can understand the real concern. People often say one sentence, like “I need to think about it,” but that does not reveal what is actually stopping them. By probing with curiosity, you can uncover whether the real issue is price, timing, a decision maker, travel plans, or something else. Only after understanding the problem should you offer a solution. The analogy is being like a cleaning doctor: diagnose first, then prescribe.
## Core Teaching
When someone gives you an objection, do not panic, argue, or immediately try to overcome it. First, agree with them. Say something like, “Yeah, that makes total sense,” or “No, I totally get it.” That keeps the conversation human and lowers defensiveness.

Then ask deeper questions. You need to understand what they actually mean. If someone says, “I need to think about it,” ask, “What is it that you need to think about specifically?” If they need to talk to their husband, ask what they think he might be concerned about. If price comes up, do not get weird. Ask questions and understand what is really going on.

The goal is to diagnose before prescribing. Like a doctor, you ask enough questions to understand the real problem, then you offer a solution that actually fits their situation.
## Key Principles
- Agree first.
- Probe deeper second.
- Do not assume the first objection is the real objection.
- Stay curious instead of getting defensive.
- Understand the situation before offering a solution.
- Price objections should not make you weird.
- Diagnose like a doctor before prescribing.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Listen to the objection fully.
2. Agree or validate the concern.
3. Ask what they mean specifically.
4. Keep asking curious follow-up questions.
5. Identify the real issue behind the objection.
6. Offer a solution that fits the actual problem.
7. Move toward the booking once the concern is addressed.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Yeah, that makes total sense why you think that.”
- “Oh yeah, no, I totally get it.”
- “What is it that you need to think about specifically?”
- “What do you think your husband might be concerned about?”
- “What’s making you think about doing the cleaning before?”
- “Well, how about this? What if we put a time on the calendar…”
- “You’re kind of like the cleaning doctor.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not argue with objections immediately.
- Do not assume you know what is in the customer’s head.
- Do not offer a solution before understanding the problem.
- Do not get awkward when price comes up.
- Do not make the tone weird or interrogating.
