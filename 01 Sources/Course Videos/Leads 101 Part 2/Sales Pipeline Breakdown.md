## Quick Summary
This lesson breaks down the six CRM stages needed for the cleaning business sales pipeline. The stages are New Lead, Follow-Up, Quoted, Booked - One Time, Booked - Recurring, and Long Term Follow Up. Each stage has a specific goal, and each stage should have automatic and manual actions attached to it. New Lead is about instant contact and not letting someone slip. Quoted is where follow-up matters because most bookings happen there when the process is strong. Booked - One Time is about delivering a great first experience and converting later. Booked - Recurring is about retention, and Long Term Follow Up turns old leads and past clients into future bookings.
## Core Teaching
The CRM needs six main stages: New Lead, Follow-Up, Quoted, Booked - One Time, Booked - Recurring, and Long Term Follow Up. This is the core of the pipeline.

New Lead starts the exact moment someone reaches out. The goal is instant contact and not letting them slip. They should have a marketing source tagged, an immediate auto text, optional email confirmation, and missed call text if needed. Manually, you call ASAP.

Follow-Up is for leads you have not gotten on the phone yet or leads who told you to call at a certain time. Quoted is for anyone who has received pricing. That is where you follow up hard until they book, say not now, or are marked as a bad fit.

Booked - One Time is for first cleans and should include reminders, expectations, job notes, and a later one-time clean follow-up sequence. Booked - Recurring is where the business becomes stable, so the focus is retention, preferences, and handling issues quickly. Long Term Follow Up is for old leads, ghosted quotes, past one-time clients, and paused or canceled clients.
## Key Principles
- Every lead needs a clear stage.
- The CRM stage should define the next action.
- New leads need instant contact.
- Quoted leads need consistent follow-up.
- One-time clients need a path back to rebooking.
- Recurring clients need retention systems.
- Long-term follow-up creates bookings without extra mental strain.
- Automation should handle repetitive reminders and messages.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Set up the New Lead stage for instant source tagging, auto text, email, missed call text, and immediate call tasks.
2. Use Follow-Up for leads who need a scheduled call or have not been quoted yet.
3. Move anyone who receives pricing into Quoted.
4. Follow up with quoted leads every 1–2 days until they book, say not now, or are marked DNC.
5. Move one-time bookings into Booked - One Time and make sure reminders, notes, and rebooking automations are in place.
6. Move recurring clients into Booked - Recurring and track preferences, reminders, and quality checks.
7. Move ghosted leads, not-right-now leads, one-time clients, and canceled clients into Long Term Follow Up.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- Immediate auto text: “Hey this is ___ with ___. Saw you reached out for cleaning - when is a good time to call you?”
- Quoted stage soft close: “I can get you booked in 2 minutes.”
- Quoted stage mindset: “You are NOT bothering them! You need a yes or no.”
- Long-term follow-up examples: “Still need help?” and “Want to get back on the schedule?”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not let a lead enter the CRM without a source tag.
- Do not leave quoted leads without follow-up.
- Do not treat one-time clients as finished forever.
- Do not let old leads sit without a long-term cadence.
- Do not keep bad-fit people in active follow-up; mark them DNC.
