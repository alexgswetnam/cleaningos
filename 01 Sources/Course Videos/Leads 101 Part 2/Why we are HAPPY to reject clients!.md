## Problems This Solves

- Dealing with clients who haggle, complain, refuse to pay, or leave bad reviews.

## Quick Summary
This lesson explains why we are happy to reject certain clients before they ever get on the schedule. It is better to have no client than a bad, upset client who refuses to pay or leaves a bad review. Some people show signs early that they will be difficult no matter how good of a job the cleaners do. If someone is haggling hard, angry, or clearly not a fit, it is better to deal with that now instead of after the home has already been cleaned. Rejecting bad-fit clients protects your cleaners, your business, your reviews, and your schedule. These people are not the ideal customer profile. The schedule spot is better saved for someone who is a better client and worth more long term.
## Core Teaching
We are happy to reject clients because no client is better than a bad, upset client. If someone is going to be a problem, haggle hard, refuse to pay, or leave a bad review, it is better to catch that on the front end.

The worst version is accepting the client, cleaning the home, and then realizing they are unhappy, they do not want to pay, and now you have a bigger issue to deal with. At that point, the work is already done and the business is exposed.

Some people are just angry, mad, upset-at-life people. No matter how good of a job you do, they are going to find a problem. Something will not be good enough. Those are not the clients we want.

Rejecting them upfront is not a loss. They are not our ideal customer profile. There are better people out there who can take that spot on the schedule and become better long-term clients. We would rather save the spot for a client who fits the business and makes more sense long term.
## Key Principles
- It is better to have no client than a bad client.
- Red flags on the front end usually become bigger problems after the clean.
- Bad-fit clients can cost money, time, reviews, and peace.
- Haggling and difficult behavior can be a sign someone is not a fit.
- Not everyone is your ideal customer profile.
- Schedule spots should go to better long-term clients.
- Rejecting bad clients protects the business.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Pay attention to how the prospect behaves before booking.
2. Look for red flags like haggling, anger, unreasonable expectations, or problem energy.
3. Ask whether this person fits your ideal customer profile.
4. If they seem likely to become a payment, review, or satisfaction issue, do not force the sale.
5. Reject the client on the front end instead of accepting a bad-fit job.
6. Save the schedule spot for a better client who is more likely to be profitable long term.
