## Quick Summary
The lesson says yes, you should use a sales script, but not by reading every word robotically. A good script has an outline for the overall call and exact word tracks for key moments like objection handling. Some phrases need to be memorized exactly with the right tonality, such as a money-aside objection question. Other parts of the call can be said different ways as long as you understand the purpose behind them. The example in cleaning is asking about calendar availability before giving price to create micro-commitments. Micro-commitments make the final booking feel like the last small step rather than a huge decision. The script is really a structure that guides the conversation while leaving room to sound human.
## Core Teaching
Yes, use a sales script, but probably not how you think. Do not copy every word and read it like a robot. A good script has structure and philosophy in some places, and exact word tracks in others.

For objection handling, certain word tracks should be memorized exactly. In another offer, the phrase after a price objection is: “Okay, yeah, money aside, do you feel like this could be the answer for you?” That exact line matters because the words and tonality are designed for that moment.

Most of the cleaning sales script is more of an outline. You understand the goal, then say it naturally. For example, before giving price, you ask about availability to create micro-commitments: what day, morning or afternoon, specific time. The point is to get them 90% of the way there before the final commitment.
## Key Principles
- Use a sales script, but do not sound robotic.
- Some word tracks should be memorized exactly.
- Other parts should be an outline guided by the goal.
- Understand the philosophy behind each question.
- Micro-commitments make the final booking easier.
- Tonality matters on exact objection-handling lines.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Create an outline for the full sales call.
2. Identify key moments that need exact word tracks.
3. Memorize those exact phrases and practice tonality.
4. For the rest of the call, understand the goal of each section.
5. Before price, ask for availability and create micro-commitments.
6. Use the script to guide the call while still sounding natural.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Okay, yeah, money aside, do you feel like this could be the answer for you?”
- “Just to see if I even have a spot available on the calendar for you, do you have a certain day in mind?”
- “Tuesday, Thursday, morning or afternoon?”
- “I want more micro-commitments from them.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not read the whole script word for word.
- Do not ignore exact word tracks where the exact words matter.
- Do not ask random questions without understanding the goal.
- Do not give price too early if you can get micro-commitments first.
- Do not confuse a script with a robotic conversation.
