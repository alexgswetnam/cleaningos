## Quick Summary
This lesson focuses on turning cleaning jobs into recurring clients by creating a great experience and making the next step easy. Recurring clients are what make the business stable, so the sale does not stop when the first clean is booked. The first clean needs to go well, expectations need to be clear, and the client should feel taken care of. After that, the business should follow up and ask about getting them on a recurring schedule. If a recurring client needs to cancel, the goal should often be to reschedule or pause rather than lose them entirely. The lesson also shows through a call example that asking one more question can save a cancellation and move the client to a later date. Recurring cleanings are secured through service quality, clear communication, and follow-up.
## Core Teaching
Recurring cleanings are where the business becomes more stable. A one-time clean is good, but the goal is to create a great experience and move the client into a recurring schedule where it makes sense.

The first clean matters. Expectations should be clear, the cleaner should have good notes, and the client should feel like the business is organized and professional. After the clean, follow up. Do not assume they will ask for recurring on their own.

When someone wants to cancel or pause, do not immediately accept it as final. Ask whether moving the appointment to another date or month would help. Sometimes the client is not canceling because they are unhappy. They may have family changes, travel, or timing issues. A simple reschedule can save the client.
## Key Principles
- Recurring clients create stability.
- The first clean sets up the recurring relationship.
- Follow-up after a one-time clean matters.
- Cancellations should be explored, not instantly accepted.
- Sometimes the solution is rescheduling or pausing.
- Clear communication and service quality make recurring easier.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Deliver a strong first clean with clear notes and expectations.
2. Follow up after the first clean.
3. Ask about getting the client on a recurring schedule.
4. If the client hesitates, understand whether the issue is timing, price, or service.
5. If a client wants to cancel, ask whether moving the appointment would help.
6. Reschedule or pause when appropriate instead of losing the client.
7. Keep communication clear and professional.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Would moving the appointment to another date or month help instead of canceling the entire appointment with us?”
- “Maybe we can reschedule the cleaning starting June 1?”
- “We’ll see you on June 1, okay?”
- “Nothing at all negative and I’ll be happy to give you a good review.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not treat a cancellation as final before asking one more question.
- Do not assume the client is canceling because they are unhappy.
- Do not forget to follow up after one-time cleans.
- Do not rely only on the cleaning itself to create recurring clients.
