## Problems This Solves
- Sounding unsure during sales calls.

- Having upward inflection that makes statements sound like questions.
- Knowing the service is good but not communicating that confidence.
- Not listening back to calls to hear how the tone actually lands.
## Quick Summary
This lesson explains that confidence changes how people receive the same words. If you sound unsure, the customer feels unsure about your ability to help them. If you say the same basic message with confidence, the offer lands completely differently. Confidence reduces the need to overthink every word or tonality trick because the customer can feel that you believe what you are saying. Upward inflection can weaken authority because it makes you sound like you are asking instead of telling the truth. To sell confidently, you need to actually believe your cleaners do great work, that your guarantee is real, and that the client will be taken care of. Then you should listen back to calls and check whether you sound confident.
## Core Teaching
Confidence sells because people can feel whether you believe what you are saying. If you say, “I think we could maybe help you,” the customer does not feel much confidence in you. If you say, “Absolutely, we can get two cleaners to your home and get a deep clean knocked out,” it lands differently.

You do not have to overthink every word when you are confident. The confidence itself communicates authority. But if you sound unsure, use filler words, or end statements with upward inflection, you weaken the sale. It sounds like you are asking a question instead of stating the truth.

The answer is not to fake confidence. Get confident in the fact that your cleaners really do good work, that you really have a satisfaction guarantee, and that you really are going to take care of the client. Then listen back to your calls to see if that confidence is coming through.
## Key Principles
- Confidence changes how the exact same words land.
- Uncertainty makes the customer feel uncertain.
- Upward inflection can make statements sound weak.
- Confidence comes from believing in the service.
- Call review shows whether you actually sound confident.
- Confidence matters more than memorizing perfect tonality tricks.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Identify where you sound unsure on calls.
2. Replace weak language like “maybe” and “I think” with clear statements when appropriate.
3. Build real confidence in your cleaners, guarantee, and client experience.
4. Practice saying the offer clearly and directly.
5. Listen back to calls and check your tone, pacing, and authority.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- Unsure version: “Yeah, we could clean your home, a deep clean, I think.”
- Confident version: “Absolutely, yeah. We can get two cleaners to your home and get a deep clean knocked out.”
- “I’ve got availability on Thursday, it looks like.”
- “I’m telling you the truth.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not use upward inflection on statements that should sound certain.
- Do not say “maybe” and “I think” when you actually know you can help.
- Do not try to fake confidence if the real issue is that you do not trust your service.
- Do not avoid listening back to your calls.
