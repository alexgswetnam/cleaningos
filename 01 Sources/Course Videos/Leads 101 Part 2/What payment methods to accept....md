## Problems This Solves
- Clients asking to pay by check, cash, Zelle, Venmo, or Cash App.
- Getting burned when a client refuses to pay after a clean.
- Not being able to charge cancellation or lockout fees.
- Making too many payment exceptions while trying to scale.
- Messy bookkeeping from mixed payment methods.
## Quick Summary
This lesson strongly recommends taking card payments as the default. Checks, especially out-of-state certified check situations, can be scams and should raise concern. When you do not have a card on file, you can do the clean and then run into problems if the client refuses to pay, wants a fix first, is not home, or violates cancellation and lockout policies. Cash, Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, and checks give you less recourse when something goes wrong. The business has made some exceptions for trusted long-term clients or special cases, but does not recommend making exceptions going forward. Card payments also make the business feel more professional and easier to track. As the business scales, moving away from non-card methods reduces headaches.
## Core Teaching
The default should be card. Be very wary of checks, especially if someone says they are out of state and wants to mail a certified check. That is usually a scam.

The bigger issue is control and recourse. If you do not have a card on file, you can clean the home and then have the client refuse to pay until something is fixed. Or the cleaner shows up, the client is not home, and you cannot charge the cancellation or lockout fee because there is no card to charge.

Cash, Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, and checks can work in certain trusted situations, and we have made special exceptions before. But going forward, especially when scaling, we do not recommend making those exceptions. We are a real business, and real businesses take card. It is also cleaner for tracking finances.
## Key Principles
- Card should be the default payment method.
- Card-on-file protects the business.
- Checks can create scam risk.
- Non-card methods reduce recourse when a client causes a problem.
- Cancellation and lockout policies only work if you can charge them.
- Special exceptions may happen, but they should not become the norm.
- Scaling requires cleaner, more professional payment systems.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Require card payment as the standard.
2. Keep the card on file before the clean.
3. Use the card to enforce cancellation, lockout, and payment policies when needed.
4. Be extremely cautious with checks, especially out-of-state certified check requests.
5. Avoid new exceptions for Zelle, cash, checks, Venmo, or Cash App.
6. If legacy clients pay another way, track those payments meticulously.
7. Use accounting software to keep the numbers accurate.
