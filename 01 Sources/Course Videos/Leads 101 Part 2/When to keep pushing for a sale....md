## Problems This Solves
- Ending the sales conversation too early.
- Not knowing when persistence becomes too much.
- Letting price objections end the call.
- Failing to ask questions after a prospect says they need to talk to someone else.
- Not knowing how to soft-close without being aggressive.
## Quick Summary
The lesson teaches that you keep pushing for the sale as long as the person is still willing to talk and there are no red flags. The point is not to be aggressive or weird, but to stay in the conversation and keep asking questions. Door-to-door salespeople are trained the same way: as long as the door is open, keep talking. If a lead says they need to talk to their husband, ask what they need to talk about. If they say the price is high, ask what they expected to pay and why they are looking to switch cleaners. You are not just asking questions for no reason; your eye stays on the prize, which is getting them to book. The close can be soft, like putting something on the calendar and letting them adjust if needed.
## Core Teaching
Keep pushing for the sale as long as someone will talk to you and there are no red flags. If someone is a bad fit, not qualified, or showing clear red flags, do not push. But if the person is still in the conversation, do not be the one who ends it.

This does not mean hard-closing in a weird way. It means staying curious, asking probing questions, and keeping your eye on the goal. If they say, “I need to talk to my husband,” ask, “What do you need to talk to your husband about?” If they say price is the issue, ask what they were expecting to pay. If they had an old cleaner, ask why they are changing from that cleaner.

The point is to understand the real objection. Many people stop after the first price objection or after the lead says they need to think. That is where a lot of sales are lost. Keep asking questions, keep helping them think through it, and use soft asks that move the booking forward.
## Key Principles
- Keep talking as long as the lead keeps talking.
- Do not push bad-fit clients or red-flag clients.
- Objections are invitations to understand more.
- Ask probing questions instead of ending the call.
- Always keep your eye on the sale.
- Use soft asks instead of awkward hard closes.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Confirm the lead has no major red flags.
2. Stay in the conversation as long as they are willing to talk.
3. When they give an objection, ask what they mean specifically.
4. If they mention another decision maker, ask what that person may be concerned about.
5. If they mention price, ask what they expected and why they are switching cleaners.
6. Offer a soft next step, like putting a time on the schedule.
7. Keep moving toward a yes or no instead of leaving the conversation vague.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “What do you need to talk to your husband about?”
- “What do you think might not work?”
- “What were you expecting to pay?”
- “Why did you change from your old cleaners?”
- “What about a satisfaction guarantee? Does that help make the decision easier?”
- “What if we put it on the schedule and you can let me know if your husband changes anything?”
- “Always be closing.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not push someone who has red flags or is not qualified.
- Do not end the conversation just because they mention price once.
- Do not sell yourself the whole time; ask questions instead.
- Do not go down a rabbit hole with no goal.
- Do not make the ask harsh or awkward.
