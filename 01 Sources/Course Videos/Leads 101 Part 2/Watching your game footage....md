## Quick Summary
This lesson compares sales call review to athletes watching game footage. Roleplay matters, but reviewing real calls is just as important because it shows how you performed in a live situation. Listening back helps identify what you said, how you said it, the words you used, and what could have been done better. You can catch issues yourself, but it is also valuable to get feedback from people better than you. The golf analogy appears again: if you are bad at golf, you should not rely only on yourself to fix your swing. The same applies to sales, where a coach, group, or live call review can help. The action item is to record calls, review them, and post them for feedback when needed.
## Core Teaching
Just like a good athlete reviews game footage, you should review your sales calls. Roleplay is important, but live calls show what actually happens under pressure.

When you review a call, you can hear what you said, how you said it, your tone, your wording, and where the call could have gone better. You will notice things yourself, but outside feedback is also valuable.

Get feedback from people who are better than you. If you are bad at golf, you go to a golf coach. If you want to improve sales, get feedback from a sales coach, the group, or live call breakdowns. Record calls, review them, and ask for help on specific moments.
## Key Principles
- Sales calls are game footage.
- Roleplay and real call review both matter.
- Recordings reveal what actually happened.
- Self-review helps, but outside feedback is valuable.
- Go to people better than you for coaching.
- Always keep learning and improving.
## Step-by-Step Process
1. Record your sales calls.
2. Listen back to the full call or key moments.
3. Identify what you said and how you said it.
4. Mark what went well and what could improve.
5. Post recordings in the group or bring them to live calls for feedback.
6. Use the feedback to roleplay and improve the next call.
## Scripts, Examples, or Phrases to Keep
- “Just like any good athlete, you are going to be reviewing your game footage.”
- “What did you say, how did you say it, the way you said it, the words you used?”
- “If I suck at golf, it doesn’t help for me to try to make myself better at golf when I’m the one that sucks at golf.”
- “Drop them here and ask for help.”
## Common Mistakes / Warnings
- Do not rely only on memory to evaluate a call.
- Do not skip call review and expect sales to improve quickly.
- Do not only ask people at your same skill level for feedback.
- Do not roleplay without checking how you perform live.
- Do not be too proud to post calls and ask for help.
