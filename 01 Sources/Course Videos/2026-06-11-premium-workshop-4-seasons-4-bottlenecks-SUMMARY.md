# Premium Workshop — 4 Seasons, 4 Bottlenecks

**The master framework.** Per the source: *"the master framework that all other lessons
plug into."* Primary source for the Canon.

> [!danger] Uses two retired terms
> This workshop predates two renamings. It says **"Sustain & Sell"** (now
> [[Four Seasons|Harvest]]) and **"Bottlenecks"** (now [[Four Engines|Engines]]).
> Expected in older material. Do not re-flag as a contradiction. Do not teach either term.

## The Four Seasons

| Season | The question |
|---|---|
| **Survival** | *"Is this even going to work?"* You might be the cleaner. Leads inconsistent. |
| **Stability** | It works but feels fragile. One cancellation or call-out creates chaos. Making money but it doesn't feel controlled. |
| **Scale** | Repeatable systems. Growing intentionally. Can spend more and handle volume. Doesn't depend entirely on you. |
| **Sustain & Sell** *(now Harvest)* | The business is an asset. Someone could buy it tomorrow and run it off your SOPs. |

> "The goal isn't to scale faster. The goal is to turn a working business into a
> repeatable machine."

## The Four Roles — new to the Canon

Each Season has a role, and **the role is the lever**:

| Season | Role |
|---|---|
| Survival | **Self-Employed** — you do everything |
| Stability | **Supervisor** — you have cleaners but you're the glue |
| Scale | **General Manager** — you manage managers, systems run things |
| Harvest | **Owner** — you own an asset |

> "You pull the business to the next stage. The business doesn't pull you."

If you keep acting self-employed, you stay in Survival.

## The Four Engines (called Bottlenecks here)

- **Leads** — marketing (getting them) + sales (closing them)
- **Labor** — finding, hiring, retaining, and backup cleaners
- **Logistics** — systems, SOPs, CRM, scheduling, communication
- **Leadership** — delegation, ownership culture, accountability

## The Diagnostic

Rate each Engine by Season. **The one lagging behind is your constraint.** Fix it,
re-evaluate, find the next. This is [[Business GPS]].

Worked examples: Rick (Leads, specifically sales — 20–25% close rate), Courtney (Leads
primary, Logistics secondary), Alex's own business (Labor only — can't spend more on
marketing without more cleaners; Logistics and Leadership both at Sustain & Sell, owners
working ~1 hour/week).

## Foundational Belief

> "Your business is limited to who you are as a person — your beliefs and your skillset."

Bad at marketing → the business has bad marketing. As a small owner you're the jack of all
trades. To break through: improve the skill, or hire someone who has it.

## Price Objections

Not every client is your client — $249 against a $90 expectation is a different league,
not an objection. **20 clients at full price beats 50 discounted** — a Fort Worth owner
averaged $110/clean from constant discounting: 3x the bookings, 1/3 the revenue.

Don't get on your heels. Push back: *"What happened to your old cleaner?"*

The price is the price — the Snickers bar at the stadium. **Drop scope, not price.**

Courtney's receptionist technique: *"I'm Laura, the receptionist. That's the company's
price — out of my hands."*

Only discount: $10–15 off the first clean in exchange for a review, at the finish line.

## SOPs

*When this happens, we do this.* Don't build 50 in Survival. Start with a daily checklist.
Build them from situations you've actually encountered — don't ChatGPT a lockout SOP on
day one. Hiring SOP lives in Notion: Indeed post → screen → interview (warmth,
communication, professionalism, reliability, red flags) → same-day onboarding
(subcontractor agreement, BookingKoala provider account, cleaner handbook). Book rec:
*The Checklist Manifesto.*

## VAs

Hire when you're losing money by not having one — a missed $400 call while at the movies
costs more than a $200/week VA. $3–6/hour, Philippines or Bangladesh; more than a nurse
earns in the Philippines, nearly double a doctor in Bangladesh. Full-time 9–6 plus
on-call, or part-time 3 hours in the morning. **Female VAs close 45–60% vs. 30% for male.**

## Hiring Cleaners

A numbers game. Indeed, Housekeeper.com, Care.com, Facebook groups, Marketplace, Nextdoor,
Craigslist, Google Maps (call small cleaners with few reviews). Talk to 10 → find 1. Talk
to 50 → find 3–4. First two weeks are a test period. Courtney runs a second-round
interview in the field with an existing cleaner — the team gets a say.

## Scheduling

Two cleans per cleaner per day, morning and afternoon. Arrival windows 8–10 AM and 1–3 PM.
Cleaners don't need four cleans a day when pay is strong (~$45/hour). Lead cleaner: 22
hours clocked, $950 in a week.
