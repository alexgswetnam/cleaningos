### Building SOPs From Hard Situations
A member (Jack) had a nightmare client — a move-in clean where the client sat in a camp chair in a 600 sqft apartment, wouldn't let cleaners enter without her, made a cleaner cry, had the support cleaner removed mid-clean, and left a one-star review despite an immaculate job. She had a history of leaving one-star reviews for small businesses. There were no red flags on the phone call.

The coaching response: (1) You did the best you could, and that was the right thing. (2) What do you learn from it? (3) Turn it into an SOP. The host didn't have an SOP for this situation either and committed to making one. The key insight: you can't write an SOP for every specific scenario ("if a cleaner cries, then..."), but you can write generalized guidelines for what to do when a customer is difficult, a cleaner feels unsafe, or a cleaner wants to leave before the job is done.

Why this matters at scale: at $15K/month, this happens occasionally. At $50K/month, it's three to four times more likely. Your VA needs a system to handle it — you can't rely on the owner's intuition. The homework assignment: write the SOP for handling difficult customers, post it in the Skool community so everyone benefits.

Additional context from other members: low-cost jobs ($99 specials) are the ones that almost always give trouble. $150+ clients rarely cause problems. The juice isn't worth the squeeze with litigious or aggressive clients — send your cleaners home, eat the cost, and move on.

Cancellation policy: one free reschedule for recurring customers; charge the cancellation fee for non-recurring. These are the kinds of things that should live in SOPs.

### Calling Your Database (Free Organic Strategy)
Before turning ads back on (especially when labor-constrained), call people already in your database: one-time cleans who never rebooked, leads who never booked, people who went quiet. Don't call them like a cold call trying to sell something. Call like a human — check up on them, have a conversation, maybe reference something personal, then naturally mention a promotion or availability.

Example approach: "Hey Lindsay, I remember you had [something about their home]. How's it going? We actually have a clean promotion right now..." It's a numbers game — call enough people and some will book. The VA can be assigned to do this during certain seasons or around holidays.

The host's point: Courtney's database potential is "untapped." She's just starting to use it, and calling old leads is another free organic source before spending on ads.

### Automated Hiring Pipeline (Harvest CRM)
Rick and Melissa walked through their hiring pipeline built in Harvest CRM. The stages and automations:

**Active Cleaners** (left column) — tagged "active cleaner," automation sends a text every Thursday at 5 PM: "Check your schedule for the next week or two. Make sure it's up to date."

**New Application** — comes in from the website (not directly from Indeed; Indeed applicants are directed to apply on the website as a proving ground — if they won't fill out the form, they're not worth it). Application questions: valid driver's license, own vehicle, consent to background check, years of professional cleaning experience, insurance status.

**Schedule Interview** — drag the card here and it sends an email/text asking the applicant for a good time to call.

**Interviewed** — moved here after the interview. The interview is the one part that can't be automated — you need the human feel to determine fit. Melissa does ~30 minute interviews.

**Good Candidate** — moving here triggers automated emails and texts requesting: photo of driver's license/government ID, and then the contract/agreement to sign. Once signed, a notification is sent to the team: "They're ready for a background check."

**Background Check** — the actual background check has to be sent manually (can't be done inside Harvest). Once it comes back clean, this stage kicks off onboarding: setting up BookingKoala, Discord, etc.

**Bad Candidate** — sends an automated rejection email: "Thank you for applying. We decided to move on. Good luck."

**Deactivated Cleaners** — sends a farewell email: "Thank you for working with us."

Setup time: about a week, a little at a time. The main bug Rick hit: duplicating an automation and forgetting to change the trigger, which fired all emails at once. That was user error, not a system issue.

For Courtney's setup: the video tutorial is in Harvest CRM's section in Skool. The main work is uploading your W9, agreement, and background check consent form. Indeed applicants can be routed by sending them the Harvest form link or embedding the form on the website. A Zapier integration from Indeed directly to Harvest is possible but the form is simpler.

Hiring tip: incentivize existing cleaners with a referral bonus (e.g., $200 after the referred cleaner completes five cleans). They vouch for someone who fits the team. One member found that caregivers/home healthcare workers transition well into cleaning — they're patient, kind, experienced with demanding people, and cleaning is easier than what they've been doing.

### BookingKoala Double-Booking Workarounds
BookingKoala has a known quirk: recurring cleans can double-book the same cleaner, and holidays don't block bookings even when marked as holidays. Workarounds:

- **Proactive checking:** At the beginning of every month, review the full calendar and fix any double bookings. Check at least a week ahead.
- **VA end-of-day checklist:** One of 8 items the VA checks off daily is reviewing this week's and next week's cleans for double bookings. Catching it every day prevents last-minute chaos.
- **Holidays:** The VA should be looking two weeks ahead for holidays and preemptively moving cleans.
- **Moving clients:** If you only move a client by one day, you usually don't need to call — just move them. If it's more than a day, call and explain: "Your cleaner accidentally got double-booked. Is there another day that works?"
- **Week view vs. month view:** Use the week tab for daily scheduling work; keep the month view for overview.

Courtney also mentioned a bug where recurring clients disappear from the calendar after about a year and have to be re-added. No known fix — just monitor for it.

### Delegating What You Hate
Ask: what does the business need? Then: can someone else do it? If you hate doing the lead sheet, updating bookings, calling to reschedule, dealing with double bookings — those are exactly the tasks to hand to a VA. A VA at $5/hour can handle the things you emotionally don't want to do but the business needs done.

Rick's automation example: he built Zapier automations that update the lead sheet automatically when a lead status changes (new lead, quote sent, booked, no booking, canceled). He used a webhook triggered only by an "update contact" tag — reducing zaps from 1,500/month to 200/month and cutting costs.

The mindset: "What does the business need? Put emotions aside. If the business needs it and you hate it, pay someone else to do it. Make your VA's life easier too."

## Step-by-Step Process

### Hiring Pipeline Stages (Harvest CRM)
1. Application comes in (from website form — Indeed applicants are directed to apply on the website).
2. If interested, drag to "Schedule Interview" → automated email/text asks for a good time to call.
3. Conduct the interview (~30 minutes, human feel, assess fit).
4. If good, drag to "Good Candidate" → automation sends requests for driver's license photo and contract/agreement to sign.
5. Once documents are submitted, team gets notified: "Ready for background check."
6. Send background check manually. If clean, drag to "Background Check" stage → triggers onboarding (BookingKoala setup, Discord, etc.).
7. If bad candidate at any stage, drag to "Bad Candidate" → automated rejection email sent.
8. Deactivated cleaners get moved to their stage → automated farewell email.
9. Active cleaners get an automated text every Thursday at 5 PM: "Check your schedule for the next week or two."

### Calling Old Leads From Your Database
1. Pull up past one-time cleans, leads who never booked, and leads who went quiet.
2. Call them like a human — not a cold sales pitch.
3. Check up on them, reference something personal if possible ("Hey Lindsay, I remember you had...").
4. Naturally bring up availability or a current promotion.
5. It's a numbers game — call enough and some will book.
6. Have the VA handle this during slow seasons or around holidays.
