## Core Teaching

### Record Months and the Labor Constraint

Courtney is at $18.5K on April 20th with 10 days left, $1,500 from $20K. Previous record was $16.5K last month, $14K before that. Records breaking every month. But the constraint isn't leads — it's labor. She had to put a new booking into May because no one was available for the client's preferred day in April. Sales are still coming in organically (GBP, CRM drip sequences — one text turned into two recurring bookings). The headwind is purely hiring.

### Solving the Labor Bottleneck

Courtney is doing four to five interviews a day on Indeed but can't find the right people — not for cleaning ability (that can be taught) but for customer service and personality, since her cleaners are in clients' homes.

Tactics to increase the hiring pipeline:

* **Post on Facebook and Nextdoor** (not just Indeed). On Nextdoor, make a short post: "We pay at least \[X] per hour-ish. If you're a cleaner, reach out. We're super slammed and need help." Alex found a cleaner this way.
* **Follow up with applicants who didn't respond to the form.** Send a mass blast: "Have you given up on this?" — nothing else. The best candidate might have meant to respond but life happened. Same principle as sales follow-up — hit them three to four times over four to five days.
* **Pair a less-impressive hire with a strong lead cleaner.** Don't send new or weaker cleaners alone. A quiet cleaner who gets the job done paired with a strong, client-facing cleaner works — the lead cleaner handles client interaction, the support cleaner handles the work. This lets you hire people you wouldn't send solo.

### Onboarding a Brand-New Owner (Keeley)

Keeley feels overwhelmed — she sees the whole elephant and doesn't know where to start. She's great with people, leading, training, and coaching, but the business/numbers/systems side is all new ("CRM — I still don't even know what that means").

Alex's guidance: eat the elephant one bite at a time. He walks her through the five business pillars that apply to virtually any business:

1. **Acquire** — get leads, get people interested.
2. **Convert** — turn leads into bookings/sales.
3. **Fulfill** — deliver the service.
4. **Finances** — know the money (spending vs. making, what makes sense).
5. **Operate** — the glue that makes everything work smoothly (systems, SOPs, people).

He uses the Buffalo Wild Wings analogy: they put a sign out and do events (acquire), make the menu look great and push desserts (convert), make great food and atmosphere (fulfill), have someone running numbers in the back (finances), and have a GM, AGM, employees, and systems making it all run (operate).

**Homework for Keeley:** Go through Cleaning Business Systems 101 in Skool (the short, potent write-ups under each pillar), then post in the community what she learned with journal notes. Also watch Alex Hormozi's beginner-level content for simple business breakdowns.

**Timeline:** Keeley asks if she can be up and running by August (four months out). Alex says yes — if she takes bites out of the elephant. If she lets it sit, it'll sit for another four years, like Courtney's business did before she decided to grow three months ago.

### GBP Results: Live Walkthrough

Alex walks through Courtney's Google Business Profile performance live on screen:

* **February (before verification):** \~15 total interactions for the whole month.
* **March 17th:** GBP got verified (address + name change with keyword).
* **April (through the 18th only):** Already at 40+ interactions — a 4x increase with only 18 days of data.

This proves the GBP optimization (address + keyword in name) works and is a major reason Courtney is hitting $20K — free leads with zero ad spend. Sales are coming in and she's not even doing any paid marketing right now.

**To-dos on GBP:** Post consistently (not once a month — at least every other day), get the logo uploaded (it was missing), and set up the business email properly in Harvest CRM (DNS records — Alex's new VA Ash will help on a call).

### Subcontracting vs. W-2

Alex's recommendation for early-stage: subcontract. W-2 comes later when you have money saved — the cost difference is significant (taxes, and eventually insurance if you choose to offer it). In Philadelphia, both the business and the employee get taxed again if the employee lives in the city. Subcontract keeps it simple and cheaper.

### VAs for Small Tasks (Even Early On)

You don't need a full-time VA to benefit from one. Alex has a VA doing random two-to-four-hour/week tasks: editing, Go High Level / Harvest work, Skool management, sending texts, calendar stuff, social media editing. $6/hour. Even Courtney — who resists doing the tracking sheet — could pay someone $10/week to spend 15 minutes a day updating it.

The distinction: hire a VA for tasks you know how to do but don't want to do. Don't hire a VA for tasks you don't know how to do yet — you can't tell if they're doing it right, and they can lie to you. Learn it first, then delegate.

For Keeley (brand new), a VA is premature — she needs to learn the business first. But she can still use a small-hours VA for things like GBP posting once she knows what she wants.

### Commercial Cleaning Logistics

Courtney runs commercial jobs with one cleaner per job, capping at about three hours (three hours fifteen max). Anything longer isn't worth it margin-wise. One cleaner on a consistent weekly schedule works well — it's reliable income for the cleaner and doesn't burn them out. Examples: one commercial client pays $375/week for 1.5–2 hours, another pays $265/week for 3 hours (plus a separate Friday visit for 30 minutes of trash).

### The Tracking Sheet Conversation

Courtney hasn't been doing the tracking sheet. Alex doesn't shame her but is direct: it's fine now at $18K, but at $50K or $100K you can't make financially responsible decisions without knowing your numbers. Fix the future problem by choice now, or get to the future and it breaks you. If you won't do it yourself, pay a VA $10/week to do it.
