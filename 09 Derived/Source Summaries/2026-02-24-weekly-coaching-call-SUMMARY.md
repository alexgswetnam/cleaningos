## Core Teaching

### Managing Cleaner Availability
The Friday text system: send a text every Friday telling cleaners to update their availability in BookingKoala for the upcoming week. When you're smaller and cleaners are "squirrely" (working for multiple companies), call them on Fridays to confirm: "What's your availability, seriously? I need to know."

When you book a cleaner, immediately message them: "Your calendar is available, you're booked for this — that's good." This prevents the situation where you book someone Tuesday for Thursday and they say they can't do it.

At scale, when multiple cleaners are available for the same time slot, message all of them with the details: "Here's the job — would you like to take it?" First to say yes gets the clean. This treats them like contractors (which they are) and creates a competitive dynamic.

If a cleaner is constantly unreliable despite empathy and working with them, they're not your cleaner. Don't build an entire automation system to solve a bad-cleaner problem — fix the cleaner or find a new one.

### Transitioning Clients From You to New Cleaners
When you're a solo cleaner growing into an owner, your existing clients are used to you. Some will resist a new cleaner. The approach:

Phase 1: Have a real conversation. "I'm growing the business. The girls are wonderful. I can guarantee you'll be happy." Assign the cleaner to willing clients first. Keep the resistant ones yourself for now.

Phase 2 (months later): "I'm at a point where I can't do your home anymore. This is exciting for me. I have wonderful cleaners and I can guarantee you'll be happy." You have to be willing to have that conversation eventually.

For new cleaners wanting a full schedule immediately: "I can't just give you 10 houses right away. There's always a testing period. The better job you do, the more cleans I'm going to give you." It's like any job — you don't become manager on day one.

### When to Get a VA
There's no single right answer — it depends on your situation:
- If you work a 9-to-5 and can't answer calls during the day, get a VA immediately (like Rashawn and Desiree did).
- Alex got a VA at ~$15K/month because they knew they'd scale fast and the first VA would have kinks to work out, so they wanted to start early.
- A guy in Canada at $100K/month has no VA because he runs everything through instant quotes on his website — totally different model.
- Jack at ~$10K/month is feeling like it's becoming a second job with 8 hours of work daily — that's a sign.

Key insight: Alex and Elijah built the business knowing someone else would operate it. SOPs, CRM, Notion — all built with handoff in mind from day one. Your first VA won't be perfect — expect kinks. That's why starting earlier is better than later.

Where to find VAs: onlinejobs.ph, Fiverr (message people directly), Facebook groups (BookingKoala group), and the train-and-place model Alex is developing (train a VA on their systems/SOPs and place them with a member).

### Raising Prices on Existing Clients
Know your number — at what price does this client make sense? If the answer is $120 and you're charging $90, that's the new price.

The conversation: "We love serving you, but we gave you a very heavily discounted rate. As we've grown, unfortunately this is what it would have to be. I totally understand if it doesn't work out for you. Is there any way we can work this out?"

You have to be willing to lose them. If they threaten to cancel: "I really hate to hear that. We'd love to keep you." But if their counter-offer is below your number, hold firm. Sometimes you don't change the cleaner's pay — you just increase your margin.

Also talk to the cleaner: "We're raising prices on this client. Do you like this client? How do you feel about the pay?" If the home is dirtier than expected after the first clean: call the client, don't change the price for this clean, but set the new rate going forward.

In Toronto, many higher-end restaurants pay for nightly cleaning rather than having closing staff do it. They found their niche through personal networks, not SEO.

Alex recommended instantly.ai for scaling this: it finds your ideal prospect, warms up an email domain, and lets you run an email drip campaign to bars/restaurants in the area. ~$50 to send a bunch of emails; best case, new accounts.

### Referral Program for Existing Clients
The fastest way to get new clients when you can't do paid marketing yet: call existing clients, check in, and ask for referrals. "I'm trying to take on more clients. I'm doing a referral thing — if you refer someone, you both get $30–$40 off your next clean." Simple, free, and leverages the trust you've already built.

### BookingKoala Scheduling Walkthrough
Alex showed how scheduling works in BookingKoala: look at a specific cleaner's week view, see what's available (Wednesday afternoon, Thursday late morning, Friday afternoon), and offer clients those specific time slots. "When are you looking for the clean? I have Wednesday afternoon or Friday afternoon — those are your options." Book based on your availability, not theirs.

For recurring clients: set the booking as weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly. BookingKoala sends automated reminders (set to 25 hours before and 24/48 hours before), so you never have to manually text clients again. This alone solves Nicole's biggest pain point.

If you have a reliability problem, find a better cleaner — don't engineer around a bad one.
