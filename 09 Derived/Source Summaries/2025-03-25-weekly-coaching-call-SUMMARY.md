## Core Teaching

### Objection Handling: "I'm Still Shopping Around" / "Let Me Think About It"
These are the two most common objections. Everyone on the call confirmed they lose deals here.

**Word track for "let me think about it":**
"Sure, no worries. What's your main concern you need to think about?"

This is the exact line every time. When you know the words, you don't have to think — you can actually listen to the person and adjust your tone to their personality. Type A person: direct and quick. Granny who talks a lot: softer, warmer, "of course you want to think about it, no pressure at all."

**"Money aside" technique (used in another business for $1,600/month offers):**
The moment you drop price and they give any resistance — "I want to talk to my partner," "I want to look it over," "I want to think about it" — the response is: "Yeah, I mean, money aside, do you feel like this could be the answer for you?" This separates the money objection from the fit objection. If they say yes to fit, money is the only thing left to solve.

**Affirm the shopper:**
When someone says "I'm calling around, you're the first person I talked to, I just want to do my research" — don't fight it. Affirm it: "Honestly, I'm just like you. I call five people before I decide who I'm going to go with. That tells me you really care about your home. Our best clients are the ones who talked to five people and then ended up calling us back."

**The soft close — hold the spot:**
"I just don't want you to lose your spot. What I usually do in this situation is just put a time down. We're not going to charge you. You can give me a call back. Let me just save this spot for you." If they need a card: "I have to put a card down — it will not charge you, it's encrypted, the bank protects you." Something in their head clicks — they've committed. Many end up staying.

**What didn't work:** The "helpful friend" approach of giving tips about what to look for when calling other companies ("a lot of places don't have insurance, they don't have English speakers," etc.). Alex tried it and fewer people came back. Don't coach them on how to shop — close them.

### Labor Before Leads (The Sequencing Argument)
Courtney has two bottlenecks: leads and labor. She wanted leads first ("I can hire all I want, but I need leads"). Alex argues the opposite:

**Leads-first scenario:** Turn on marketing → leads come in → you can't fulfill them because you don't have cleaners → you waste money → then you scramble to hire → hiring takes longer → you're stuck with a labor bottleneck while paying for leads you can't serve.

**Labor-first scenario:** Hire first → test the new cleaner (partner them up, get your best cleaners' opinions) → confirm they're good → then turn on leads → no bottleneck, you can scale. You're not wasting marketing dollars while waiting for labor.

Hiring takes longer than turning on leads. So start the slower process first. Even without growth plans, you need at least one backup cleaner — if someone leaves, you're in big trouble.

Rick reinforced this: "We were afraid of hiring when we had no work. We hired first, told them it'll be a slow start. Two days later, we had work for them."

### PPC Pitfall: Content Display Network
Rick spent $1,700 on PPC and got only 6–7 leads. The problem: the campaign was deployed through the content display network (CDN), not search. CDN shows your ads as banners across random websites — not to people actively searching for cleaning. Alex tested CDN on his own account and cost per lead went from ~$40 to $200+.

**How it happened:** Rick set up the campaign following Google's recommendations, which asked for videos and pictures (that's CDN). He also used ChatGPT's suggestions. Neither led to a search-focused campaign.

**Warning about Google reps:** If Google calls with "recommendations for your ad account," hang up. They will ruin your account. Google reps are incentivized for you to spend more, not get better results. Alex, Rick, and Courtney all had bad experiences following Google rep advice. "It's shameful how much money I spent."

**The fix:** A properly set up PPC search campaign (not display). Alex and Elijah can replicate their setup but can't guarantee results in a different market. Different setups work in different areas — Jack pays ~$53 CAD per lead with a different structure and it works for him.

### Rick's IVR Setup (Cutting Robocalls)
Rick built an IVR (Interactive Voice Response) in Harvest CRM using an 11 Labs AI-generated voice. When someone calls the business, they hear: "Hi, this is [business]. Press 1 to connect." Robocalls can't press buttons, so they're eliminated.

The system has four levels: during business hours, it rings through to the team; after hours, it goes to voicemail. Weekends are handled separately. Rick used the "go-to" function in the CRM workflow builder to route all paths to a single voicemail or call-forward endpoint — reducing management to one place.

### CRM One-Time Follow-Up Working
Courtney turned on the one-time booking follow-up sequence in Harvest CRM for the first time. Sent 20 messages to past clients. Results: two bookings and two inquiries for late April. "The CRM paid for itself." She also got some "do not disturb" opt-outs, which the system handled automatically (one person had moved — it auto-stopped). This validates the "Old Leads Are Not Dead Leads" principle.

### Emotional Detachment From Reviews
Alex shares a friend's story: five stars all the way to 200 reviews, then cleaners didn't work out, got 5–10 bad reviews in three months, and it crushed him. He was too emotionally connected — "his business is a reflection of him." Alex's learning: "This was my baby. It's not my baby anymore. It's an asset that brings money in for me and one day my family." Care about people (customers, cleaners, VAs), but don't let reviews drive you insane. Put the care in the right areas.

### Business Valuation: Why Alex Declined $50K
A cleaning business owner in California (~$100K/month revenue, 300+ GBP reviews) offered $50K for Alex's business. Alex declined. The math:

At $60K/month revenue with 25% profit = $15K/month profit × 12 months = $180K annual profit. Sell at 2–2.2x = $360K–$400K. Why take $50K now when the infrastructure (VAs, systems, GBP) is set to scale without additional owner effort?

The buyer even said: "I know where you guys are at. You're about to explode. I can't offer you enough." The four stages: survive → stabilize → scale → sustain and sell. Alex is at sustain — the business will keep growing even without aggressive marketing. They'll sell eventually, but only when the numbers justify it.

### Airbnb Clients: Still Not Worth It
Jack got two Airbnb calls (FIFA World Cup coming to Toronto). Alex's position unchanged: Airbnb owners optimize for cheapest possible cleaning. The turnaround pressure is high (checkout at 11, must be done by 3, no room for error). If your cleaner calls out, you get a one-star review and a chargeback. Alex's business does occasional Airbnb cleans but charges full price — clients keep coming back because cheaper cleaners keep failing.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Handling "Let Me Think About It" / "I'm Shopping Around"
1. They say: "I need to think about it" / "I'm shopping around."
2. Respond: "Sure, no worries. What's your main concern you need to think about?"
3. Listen to their answer — adjust tone to their personality (direct for Type A, warm for chatty).
4. If it's a money concern: "Money aside, do you feel like this could be the answer for you?"
5. If they're shopping: "Honestly, I'm just like you. I call five people before I decide. That tells me you really care about your home. Our best clients talked to five people and ended up coming back to us."
6. Soft close: "I don't want you to lose your spot. Let me just put a time down — we won't charge you. You can call me back."
7. If they need a card: "I have to put one down, but it won't charge you. It's encrypted, the bank protects you."
8. Follow up per your cadence if they don't commit on the call.

### Deciding: Hire First or Get Leads First
1. Ask: which process takes longer — finding/testing a cleaner, or turning on a marketing channel?
2. Almost always: hiring takes longer. Start there.
3. Hire → partner with existing cleaners → test on 2–3 cleans → get your best cleaners' feedback.
4. Once confirmed good: turn on leads.
5. If you go leads first: you waste money on leads you can't fulfill, then scramble to hire under pressure.
