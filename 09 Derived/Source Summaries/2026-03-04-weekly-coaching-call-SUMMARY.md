## Core Teaching

### Handling Cleaner Professionalism Issues
Jack's situation: a cleaner ate lunch at a client's dining table while the client was away. The client called to cancel the weekly service. Alex's take: this isn't a fire-the-cleaner situation — it's a slap on the wrist. The cleaner came from another clean, the client wasn't home, and she thought it was a good time for a quick bite. Understandable, but unprofessional.

What to tell the cleaner: don't eat in a client's home. Microwave food somewhere else (a food court, your own house, or ask to microwave on the way out), eat in the car, and leave the house. Make it a clear standard going forward.

What to tell the client: apologize sincerely, acknowledge it shouldn't have happened, and ask for another chance. "The girls love cleaning your home. I understand this was unprofessional. We talked to them. Would you be willing to give us another chance? I'd love to offer a discount on the next clean as an apology." The worst they say is no. The best case: they valued the cleaning quality and come back.

Alex's own stories: a cleaner sat on a client's couch eating while Alex (co-owner) was fixing her work. Another cleaner had body odor complaints from clients — her friend/co-cleaner had to buy her deodorant. A subcontractor scratched shower glass in a $2M home ($650 repair). These things happen. Handle them with heart but make business decisions.

### Same Side of the Table (Upset Client Technique)
When a client is upset, you must get on the same side of the table immediately. Don't defend, don't minimize, don't talk at them. Get shoulder-to-shoulder and look at the problem together.

Phrases: "I totally agree with you. This is unacceptable. This should never have happened. You shouldn't have to go through this. Thank you for being so kind and patient about this. Tell me more."

You're reaffirming their frustration, not fighting it. You're both on the same team, problem-solving together. Never say "it's not that big of a deal."

Elijah's example: a subcontractor scratched shower glass in a $2M home. On the call, Elijah said: "I can't even imagine. I'm in disbelief this happened. I'm so sorry. Thank you for being so honest and patient." The client calmed down because she felt heard and validated.

### The Angry Boat (Hormozi Technique)
Only one person can be in the angry boat. If a client is angry and you match their anger (directed at the problem, not at them), they'll actually talk themselves down.

The tire shop analogy: you walk in angry because they scratched your tire. If the counter person is meek and apologetic, you stay angry. But if the counter person says "Are you KIDDING me? They scratched your tire? Let me see it. I might FIRE the guy who did this!" — suddenly you're like "No no, it's not that big a deal, I'm sure it was a mistake." They jumped in the angry boat and kicked you out.

Alex has used this two or three times with very angry clients. You amplify the outrage ("I CANNOT BELIEVE that cleaner did that. What? Oh my God.") and the client starts defending the cleaner: "No, she was really nice, I'm sure it was a mistake." It's counterintuitive but it works — you diffuse by amplifying, and they bring you both back down.

Use sparingly. Same-side-of-the-table is the default. Angry boat is for the really heated ones.

### GBP Verification: Always Do the Live Video Call
When Google asks you to verify your Business Profile, they offer a pre-recorded video option. Do NOT use it. The pre-recorded submission gets denied almost every time for vague, unexplained reasons. You resubmit, get denied again, with no feedback. One person went back and forth four times over a month.

Instead: open a support ticket with Google, email them, and request a live video call. In the module, Alex walks through exactly how to do this. On the live call, verification takes about 10 minutes. This is in the GBP setup module in Skool.

Courtney's situation: Google told her to pre-record. Alex said ask for the live call — he got the option within 24 hours of requesting it.

### BookingKoala: Pairing vs. Teams (Fixing Double-Booking)
The root cause of most BookingKoala double-booking: using "teams" instead of individual providers with pairing. When you create a team, BookingKoala doesn't properly account for individual availability — it still shows team members as available even when they're on a team clean.

Fix: delete all teams. Add every cleaner as an individual provider. When you need two people on a clean, use the "pair provider" function (assign one provider, then pair the second). This way BookingKoala correctly shows each person's individual availability, and pairing adjusts for both.

For spotting double bookings visually: go to Settings → General → Store Options → "How do you manage colors in your admin booking calendar?" → Set to "Provider based." Now each cleaner is a different color. If you see colors overlapping on the calendar, someone's double-booked. Part of the VA's SOP is checking this week and next week for overlaps daily.

Scheduling workflow: use the week view with sidebar layout. Have two BookingKoala tabs open — one with the calendar/availability, one for making bookings. On mobile, the CRM is for messaging/notifications only; booking and scheduling should be done on a laptop.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Winning Back a Lost Client
1. Acknowledge the issue sincerely — don't minimize.
2. Get on the same side of the table: "I totally agree, this shouldn't have happened."
3. Thank them for their honesty and patience.
4. Tell them what you've done to fix it ("we talked to the cleaner, set new standards").
5. Ask for another chance: "Would you be willing to give us one more try? I'd love to offer a discount as an apology."
6. If they say no, respect it. If yes, deliver flawlessly.

### The Angry Boat (For Very Heated Clients)
1. Recognize the client is in the angry boat and won't come out through normal calming.
2. Jump in the angry boat — be MORE upset than them (directed at the problem/cleaner, not the client).
3. "Are you kidding me? I cannot BELIEVE that happened. I might have to let that cleaner go."
4. Watch the client start defending: "No, she was really nice, it was probably a mistake."
5. You've kicked them out of the angry boat. Now you're both calming down together.
6. Transition to same-side-of-the-table problem-solving from there.

### Fixing BookingKoala Double-Booking
1. Delete all teams in BookingKoala.
2. Add every cleaner as an individual provider.
3. When booking a two-person clean, assign one provider, then use "pair provider" to add the second.
4. Go to Settings → General → Store Options → set calendar colors to "Provider based."
5. Check the calendar weekly (VA SOP): look for overlapping colors = double bookings.
6. Use the week view with sidebar layout for best visibility.
