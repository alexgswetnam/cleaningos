### VA Onboarding: Leadership and First Week
**Treat the lead VA like an equity partner.** Give them buy-in to decisions and thoughts. When someone has input into a decision, they own it and take responsibility. Ask about their goals, life, situation. Project a future with them: "We want to grow. You're part of that. One day you could have a team of VAs under you." Getting to know them as a person is integral — Alex's VA loves playing Roblox on weekends, is always by the computer, and wants to earn more for her family.

**Pay structure.** Start at $6/hour. Do small frequent bumps — a month in, bump to $6.50. Then $7, $7.50 over time. They get the dopamine hit and work toward the next bump. Give surprise bonuses: the VA closed five or six people in one day, Alex sent $80 ("You killed it. I want you to keep killing it."). Optional incentive structure: base rate ($200/week) plus $10 per booking, $10 per review. Estimate what it looks like on the bad and good end. Note: some VAs prefer steady pay over variable — Alex's VA wanted more stability.

**Cleaner communication transition.** Cleaners are used to texting you directly. Don't just disappear. Check in with them personally (pull up to a job, have coffee, call them): "How's it going? Is the VA taking care of you? Do you have any issues?" Let them know: "I'm not handing off the responsibility of making sure you're happy. You can still message me. But the VA will handle day-to-day client communications and scheduling." Over time, the VA earns trust and you naturally step back. Alex hasn't talked to a single cleaner in months except when one asked for career advice.

If a cleaner notices the VA's accent or says something odd: "They're from another country — that explains it." Most cleaners understand and move on.

**First day / first week.** Have the VA follow you through everything you do — calls, bookings, pipeline management. After showing them, have them do it while you watch. "Does that make sense? Show me how you'd do this. Let's role play a call." It's a mentorship in the beginning. The VA takes on your personality and approach based on how you train them. If you're customer-centric and curious on sales calls, they'll be that way too.

Daily structure is governed by the end-of-day checklist SOP (in the Logistics course in Skool): ~10 items checked off before clocking out. The master operations manual ties everything together: scheduling dispatch, failed payments, lockouts, cancellations — each references the relevant SOP.

### Lead Attribution: UTM Tracking + Zapier + CRM
To know where each lead comes from, you need UTM campaign tags on every link pointing to your website. When someone hits your site from GBP McKinney, the URL includes a UTM tag. When they fill out the form, the UTM data is captured and submitted with it.

The Zapier setup: website form submission → email → Zapier scans for that email every two minutes → JavaScript code parses the email → creates a contact in Harvest CRM + sends a Discord notification ("new lead"). The UTM campaign tag (e.g., "GBP McKinney" or "PPC1") shows the source.

Alternative: embed a Harvest CRM form directly on the website, which skips the Zapier email-parsing step and sends leads straight into the CRM.

Jack's form pop-up generated 33 submissions in the remainder of the month vs. 2 from the original PPC form — a massive increase that raised attribution questions (are pop-up submissions from PPC visitors or organic visitors?). Solution: make sure the pop-up does NOT appear on the PPC squeeze page so you can cleanly separate PPC leads from organic leads.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Monthly Revenue Tracking
1. On the 1st of each month, screenshot BookingKoala showing total projected revenue and recurring revenue.
2. At month end, screenshot again.
3. Compare: how much new business was added? How much recurring was kept vs. lost?
4. Track month over month to see your recurring growth trajectory.

### VA First Week
1. Day one: log them into all systems (CRM, BookingKoala, Discord, Notion).
2. Walk through the daily checklist SOP together.
3. Do every task yourself while the VA watches and listens (calls, bookings, pipeline management).
4. After demonstrating, have the VA do the same task while you watch. Give feedback.
5. Role play sales calls.
6. End-of-day debrief: "How did it go? What questions do you have?"
7. Repeat for 1–2 weeks, gradually reducing your involvement as they prove competency.

### Lead Attribution Setup
1. Set up UTM campaign tags for every link pointing to your site (use Google's UTM Campaign Builder).
2. Tag each source: GBP location, PPC campaign, LSA, Thumbtack, etc.
3. Website form captures the UTM data along with name and phone.
4. Zapier scans for the form-submission email every 2 minutes, parses it with JavaScript, creates a contact in Harvest CRM, and sends a Discord notification.
5. Alternative: embed a Harvest CRM form directly on the site (skips the Zapier email step).
6. Ensure pop-up forms don't appear on the PPC squeeze page to keep attribution clean.
