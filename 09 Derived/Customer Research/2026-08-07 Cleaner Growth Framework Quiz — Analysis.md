## 1. Executive summary

**Who answered.** Owners who are past the very beginning but not out of the woods. Most
are not the ones cleaning — **13 of 30 never clean, 3 rarely do.** But **23 of 30 still
personally handle quoting and sales** (14 unaided, 9 with a basic script). They have
production capacity. They do not have a demand engine.

**What they want.** More customers, overwhelmingly. Given a spare $1,000/month,
**23 of 30 would spend it on marketing or sales.** No other category reached 3.

**What keeps showing up.** One thing, across six differently-worded questions:

| Question | Leads-flavoured answer | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest profit killer | leads / close rate / follow-up | **24 / 30** |
| Which feels most true | "if we could just get more ideal customers, we'd explode" | **18 / 30** |
| What breaks first when you grow | "we just don't get enough inquiries / bookings" | **17 / 30** |
| Weekly stress | "how will we get new cleanings?" | **15 / 30** |
| Extra $1,000/month | marketing / sales | **23 / 30** |
| 30 days without marketing | "all new business stops; panic mode" | **12 / 30** |

**What surprised me — three things.**

**"I bought myself a job" does not mean they're cleaning.** 14 people chose it. Of those,
**6 never clean and 2 rarely do.** The trap they're describing isn't the mop. It's being
the only person who can produce revenue. That reframes the phrase entirely, and it is the
single most useful finding in this dataset.

**The people most confident that leads are the problem have the least data.** Of the 18 who
said "if we could just get more ideal customers, we'd explode," **16 do not track weekly
KPIs** — 9 do basic monthly bookkeeping, 7 are "checking the bank; random surprises
happen." Only 1 tracks CAC and churn. They are confident about a diagnosis they cannot
measure.

**Labor looks healthy, and that may be an illusion.** Only 3 of 30 said finding cleaners
breaks first; only 2 named turnover as the profit killer. [[Four Engines]] says Labor
becomes the first serious growth ceiling *as soon as marketing starts working.* For most of
this sample marketing isn't working yet — **so Labor hasn't been stress-tested.** Reading
this as "Labor is fine" would be a mistake.

**What the data does not support.** It cannot tell us whether these owners have a lead
*volume* problem or a lead *conversion* problem. The instrument bundles them. That
distinction is the whole of [[Leads Problem Or Sales Problem]], and this quiz cannot
resolve it.

## 2. Goals

Grouped from what people said they'd buy, what they'd fix, and what they call success.

### Goal A — More qualified inquiries — strongest signal

**Frequency:** 23/30 would spend $1,000/month here · 17/30 say inquiries break first
**Their wording (Alex's options):** *"We just don't get enough inquiries / bookings"* ·
*"We do great work — if we could just get more ideal customers, we'd explode"*
**Engine:** Leads · **Season:** Leads in **Survival** for most — 20/30 said 30 days
without marketing means panic or feeling out of control

### Goal B — Stop being the person who sells

**Frequency:** 12/30 say they can't take time off because *"if I'm not marketing/selling,
we won't get many new bookings"* — the single most-selected barrier to time off
**Engine:** Leads → Leadership · **Season:** Leads Stability at best; Leadership Survival
**Note:** this is Goal A's twin. They want more leads *and* to stop personally converting
them. Those are different builds.

### Goal C — A business that runs without them

**Frequency:** 26/30 describe the business as owner-dependent — 14 "I bought myself a job,"
12 "it works, but I'm still essential." Only 4 chose "it's a machine; I steer it"
**Engine:** Leadership · **Season:** Leadership **Survival** for most
**Caveat:** aspiration, not an active project. Only 5/30 named growth decisions or team
ownership as their blocker.

### Goal D — Predictable operations

**Frequency:** 10/30 stress about *"gaps… better systems and team members who take
ownership"* · 16/30 say scheduling is *"manageable with templates but I'm still the hub"*
**Engine:** Logistics · **Season:** Stability
**Note:** consistently second place. Real, not urgent.

### Goal E — Knowing the numbers

**Frequency:** only 2/30 track weekly KPIs. **Nobody asked for this.**
**Engine:** cross-Engine measurement
**This is a need, not a goal.** See §6 — it's the gap between what they want and what would
actually tell them whether they're right.

**Two cautions.**

signal, not a reading. A real [[Business GPS]] pass needs the owner's goal first — this
quiz never asks for one.

The `Season` and `Role` columns in the export are **numeric scores** (11–33, 12–41), not
names. Whatever scoring the quiz applies is not visible in the data and is not reflected
above. **NEEDS ALEX.**

## 5. Customer language bank — cannot be delivered

**There is no customer language in this dataset.**

All 18 questions were multiple choice. Every phrase in the export was written by Alex. A
language bank built from it would be a mirror, and using it to write copy would sound
persuasive to us and like nothing to a customer.

**What can honestly be salvaged: which of Alex's phrasings people selected most.** That is
tested internal language — useful, and different from customer language. Do not present it
in copy as "what owners tell us."

|---|---|

| "I bought myself a job" | 14/30 |
| "We just don't get enough inquiries / bookings" | 17/30 |
| "How will we get new cleanings?" | 15/30 |
| "All new business stops; panic mode" | 12/30 |
| "Manageable with templates but I'm still the hub" | 16/30 |
| "Checking the bank; random surprises happen" | 11/30 |
| "Mostly verbal; inconsistent" (SOPs) | 12/30 |

**To get real language, one change:** add two open-text questions to the quiz.

> *"In your own words, what's the most frustrating part of running your business right
> now?"*
> *"If this were fixed 12 months from now, what would be different?"*

Thirty answers to those two would be worth more for copy than everything above. This is
the highest-value, lowest-cost action in this entire analysis.

## 6. Objections and beliefs

### Stated explicitly

| Belief | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **"More leads would fix this."** | 18/30 chose "we'd explode" · 23/30 would spend $1,000 on marketing |
| **"I'm the reason it works."** | 26/30 owner-dependent · 23/30 personally sell |
| **"Free/cheap channels first."** | Most-requested resource is free Google leads (10) |
| **"Our work is already good."** | 18/30 open with "we do great work" · only 4/30 admit quality depends who shows up |

### My inference — flag as inference

**They will resist a diagnosis that isn't "leads."** They have pre-diagnosed. Content
opening with systems, leadership or measurement likely loses them. **Enter through leads,
then widen.** This is a positioning conclusion, not a fact from the data.

**They may not buy measurement.** 27/30 lack operating numbers and none asked for help
with it. Selling "track your KPIs" to this group is selling homework.

**Price sensitivity is untested.** Nothing in the quiz asks about budget beyond a
hypothetical $1,000/month. Do not read that as willingness to pay for coaching.

**Free-resource takers are not buyers.** 29/30 requested something free. That is opt-in
behaviour, not purchase intent, and it should not be treated as pipeline.

## 9. Free product opportunities

*What could we solve in Survival that makes the value obvious immediately?*

**Strongest: the Business GPS diagnostic itself.** This quiz is 80% of it. It already
collects Engine-by-Engine signal — it just never returns a diagnosis. Adding an output
turns a lead-capture form into a demonstration of the core method.

The output should say: **here's the Season of each of your four Engines · here's the one
Engine blocking your goal · here's your one next step.** That is the product, given away.
It also fixes the missing goal question — [[Business GPS]] step 1 is *define the goal*, and
this quiz never asks.

**Second: the Google leads sequence.** 10/30 asked. Material exists across four pages and
five lessons. Complete, checkable, free, and produces a visible result.

**Third: a two-number scoreboard.** Not a KPI system. Two numbers — inquiries this week,
booked jobs this week. 27/30 have nothing. Cheap to make, and it's the on-ramp to
[[Five Laws|Stop Guessing]] for people who would never buy "measurement."

**Not free-tier material:** anything Leadership-framed. They won't click it.
